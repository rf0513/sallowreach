# Set Piece — The Long Anchorage *(the wreck-yard)*

**In plain words:** Deep in the swamp there is a drowned basin where every boat the Mire has ever taken eventually ends up — the currents all pull one direction, and what sinks anywhere arrives here, slowly, over months or years. Two hundred years of hulls lie lashed together by root and current in a slow spiral: newest wrecks at the rim, oldest at the center. The party's own wrecked boat is in there — and so is the physical proof that its explosion was staged. The lizardfolk clans work the yard's edge as a feeding ground, the villain harvests along its shelves, and an expedition that vanished thirty-one years ago left its answers in one of the hulls. This is the campaign's dungeon crawl: rooms are wrecks, the corridor is black water, and the clock is the tide. Built as bones (2026-07-19) — run it as a full boat-day whenever the party points deep, S6 onward.

*House rule 18 compliant: this is the named scene; its engine is the room structure below plus [[the-deep-mire]] as ambient law. No new environment stat block.*

---

## Why this scene matters *(the payoffs, codenames glossed)*

- **The wreck's seam.** The Ridgeback — Remy's boat, the one the party went down with in Session 1 — is here, and her keel tells the truth: the blast came from *inside*. Proof the wreck was **manufactured** — the same lesson as the snake's seam, in the party's own hull. *(Two-stage rule: the evidence proves* staged*, never* who. *Caius's name stays out of the water's mouth.)*
- **Remy's deepest water.** His pride and joy is in the yard — and maybe, somewhere, an answer about **Seve**, the client he lost three years ago and has never gone back for. *(Deliberately open — see the floating beat. Don't canonize Seve's fate; let the table decide how hard to look.)*
- **The Cuvier survey.** The Dry Store's thirty-one-year unclaimed locker gets its story: the surveyors' punt is here, and their chart is the yard's biggest clue — everything the Mire keeps drifts toward the **same heading**. *(Water-truth: the chart shows it, nobody explains it.)*
- **The villain's shadow.** The yard sits where Gault's harvest-lines and the Tessak's wreck-larder meet — the party walks through the supply chain they've been hearing about, and nobody has to say his name.
- **The empty yard.** The most haunted-looking place in the campaign has **zero ghosts** — the swamp's dead cross clean ([[cosmology]]; the ghost rule). Isotta's beat is the silence.

**Prerequisites:** the deep kit — which means passing **Odette's Refusal** ([[the-dry-store]]) — and a route: Remy's guiding, the Tessak marks (if earned at [[set-piece-the-tessak-camp|the camp]]), or Odile. **When to run:** any deep-water session from S6 on; pairs naturally with a [[set-piece-the-cold-cache|Cold Cache]]-style boat day.

## The Shape of the Place *(how the dungeon works)*

**What you see, arriving:** the channels widen into a basin the maps don't have, and the water goes green-dark and *patient*. Hulls — dozens, then more — lying half-sunk in a slow spiral you can only see from the fallen mast you'll climb: newest at the rim, oldest at the hub, every bow pointing the same way. No birds. No insects. The quietest water in the campaign.

- **Rooms are wrecks; the water is the corridor.** The party boats or wades between hulls; boarding is a scene. Six rooms below, rim to center — run 3–4 per visit, not all six. The yard keeps.
- **The tide is the clock.** Dynamic countdown, **Progress 4 / Consequence 4** *(SRD-verified — [[STATUS]] §SRD Facts; tick by outcome: crit 3 · Hope-success 2 · Fear-success 1, and on the consequence side failure-with-Fear 3 · failure-with-Hope 2 · success-with-Fear 1)*: Progress = a mapped route deeper (climbs, reads, marks); Consequence = the tide turning — gates between hulls close, boarding ramps shift, the way out lengthens. At Consequence full: nobody's lost forever, but they're spending the night in the yard, and the night has its own Fear menu.
- **The tide replaces the Bilge.** The trip *out here* runs on [[the-living-mire]] §Travel With Teeth (the difficulty ladder, the Bilge, the stakes rule). The moment they enter the basin, **the tide is the only clock** — one clock at a time. Pick the Bilge back up on the way home.
- **⭐ What the Mire took is here.** Anything the party lost to a Swamping on any previous trip has drifted into this yard. If they've been bitten before, put one of their own things in a hull and don't announce it — let them find it.
- **The yard is not hostile.** The Mire keeps; it doesn't hunt. Every danger here is physics (settling hulls, rot, deep water), residents (gar, the Tessak), or visitors (the harvest). Play the place itself with *courtesy* — it is, in its way, a collection lovingly kept.

**Rolls at a glance** *(house ladder: 11 baseline · 13–14 hard · 15 reach)*: reading the spiral from the mast **Instinct 12** · boarding a settled hull quietly **Agility 12** · the keel dive at the Ridgeback **Agility or Strength 13** · the chart reconstruction **Knowledge 13** · larder etiquette **Presence 13** (marks give advantage) · the Grandmother's hold **Finesse 14**.

## The Rooms *(rim → center)*

### 1 · The Wrackline *(the rim — the teaching room)*

Fresh debris rafted against the outermost hulls: crate-wood, cork floats, a punt pole still bright at the cut. **What it teaches (free, no roll):** everything here *arrived* — nothing wrecked in this basin. Fletcher reads it in one look: drift-sign, all of it, months of travel, one direction. Climb the fallen mast (Agility 11) and the spiral resolves — and someone will say the yard's first true thing: *"They're all facing the same way."*

### 2 · The Ridgeback *(the party's own hull — the evidence room)*

Half-sunk at the rim, a season's growth on her rails, and Remy goes completely still on the water (his tic — S1's wreck, three sessions of not talking about it). **The find, keel-side (the dive, 13):** the blast scar **petals outward from inside the hull** — Tenson's auditor read, and his line to hand him: *"Explosions from outside push in. This pushed* out. *Somebody shipped this blast as cargo."* Nearby, snagged in the same growth: **stone anchor-weights with cut rope** — the kind that hold a wrecker's net. *(The rumor was true, and now they've held it.)* **Guardrail:** the hull proves *staged*. It does not know a name. If the players say "Caius," smile and confirm nothing.
**The small thing:** the doorframe Tenson's shield-door was pulled from ([[items/loot-ledger|the Ridgeback Door]]) — the empty hinge-straps, home at last. Let whoever carries it stand in front of it a moment.

### 3 · The Larder *(three hulls, lashed — the residents' room)*

Feeding racks between the hulls — Tessak work, same craft as the camp — hung with smoked gar and eel. Fresh wakes. This is clan larder, worked respectfully: they take from the yard's *edge*, per their own season-law. **The scene is a toll, not a fight:** a Tessak scout surfaces to watch (stats: [[adversaries/sallowreach-roster]]); the marks — if the party earned them — cut both ways here (Presence 13 without them; with them, advantage and a countable courtesy). Steal from the racks and the yard's whole visit re-prices. **If the détente is already broken** ([[set-piece-the-bad-season]] board state): the racks are stripped, the wakes are many, and the yard is no longer neutral ground — say so with the water, and shorten the visit.

### 4 · The Lantern Nursery *(the wonder room — spend the flourish here)*

A grain barge, decades down, hull like a cathedral rib-cage — and inside, denning **lantern rays**: dozens, dim-pulsing in their winter rhythm, light moving over the party like slow weather. No roll to watch; it's a gift. The sting is on the way out: along the older shelf, a **harvest-line scar** — hooks, spaced regular, industrial — and a shelf of den-hollows, empty. Brin's arithmetic, if she ever sees the count. Leavy's line if she takes a water sample: the charged water here is *rich* — the yard sits in the reaching's path. *(Nobody says his name. The spacing of the hooks says it.)*

### 5 · The Cuvier Punt *(the thirty-one-year answer)*

A guild survey punt, kit still stowed with a professional's neatness — the two who never came back for their Dry Store locker. In the oilskin tube (Knowledge 13 to reconstruct the damp-eaten sheets): **their drift-chart.** Weeks of float-markers, plotted honest — every object they tracked, every current they logged, converging on one heading. The last entry stops **mid-line**. *(What took them is not written and not findable — the Mire keeps its own counsel. The chart is the treasure: water-truth, drawn by careful hands. Where the heading points is for the party to lay against their own maps — and their own tether.)* **Clue discipline:** the chart is *shown*; the *said* is whoever reads maps at the table — Fletcher or Tenson — saying the heading out loud.

### 6 · The Grandmother *(the hub — the oldest keeping)*

The center hull: a deep-sea trader of a build nobody's seen in living memory — first-fleet old, founder-era old. And she is **whole.** Below the waterline: preserved, clean, *kept* — the way the great root heals saw-cuts ([[the-kept-root]]). Her mooring lines are the yard's quietest horror shown, never said: **root — grown, not tied.** Leavy's line: *"Nobody tied these. They* grew. *"* Aboard (Finesse 14 through the root-web): trade iron worth honest weight, glass beads, a navigator's lens worth a Bag to the right buyer — and the ship's bell, bright as new, which rings true if struck and which — one Fear, once, on the way out — rings **once, unstruck**, soft, like an hour being kept. **Isotta's beat, the room's last:** even here — the oldest hull, two hundred years of taken boats — *nothing lingers.* Her casebook: *"The yard is full and the yard is empty. Whoever keeps this collection keeps it without them. Query: then what is being kept?"* *(She's brushing the truth — the Mire keeps* things *; the dead cross; only the tethered are held living. Say none of that.)*

### ★ Floating — Seve's Question *(GM discretion — read the table before arming this)*

Remy knows what the yard is. Somewhere between rooms, quietly, to whoever's nearest: *"Three years I've told myself there was nothing to find. And now I'm standing in the place where everything ends up."* Whether he asks to look, refuses to look, or asks the party to look *for* him — and whether anything is found — is left deliberately open. If you open it: no body, no ghost (the dead cross clean out here); at most an object that settles the question gently. This is a wound scene, not a dungeon room. One knife per PC per session still applies, and this is Remy's.

## 😨 Fear Menu *(performed — the yard's own moves)*

- **The Settle** *(1 Fear)*: the hull they're standing in shifts — one groan, three inches, then still. Everything on the walls is now at a slightly different angle. Agility Reaction 12 or mark a Stress.
- **The Wake** *(1 Fear)*: between two hulls, exactly where they're about to pole, water moving that nothing is moving. It passes. The Tessak are counting, that's all.
- **The Bell** *(1 Fear, once ever)*: the Grandmother's bell, unstruck, one soft note across the whole basin. Nothing follows. Do not explain it.
- **The Closing** *(2 Fear)*: the tide takes a gate early — tick Consequence and reroute them through a room you wanted them to see.
- **The Count from the Dark** *(2 Fear — night only)*: from somewhere unlit, in trade-cant, a voice counting the party. It reaches four and stops. *(Tessak courtesy: you are seen, you are numbered, you are — for now — not taken.)*

## 🎒 Loot *(honest salvage — the [[the-dry-store|Dry Store]] buys it; signature pieces stay in [[items/loot-ledger]])*

Rope, hardware, unbroken jars, trade iron by weight *(a purse of honest work per trip)* · the navigator's lens *(a Bag, to the right buyer — a coin BEAT, not a windfall; one per session rule holds)* · the Cuvier chart *(worthless for coin; priceless laid against their own maps — and Odette will want to know, gently, what they found near her locker's owners)*.

## Walking out *(states to log)*
