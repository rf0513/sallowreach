# Set Piece — The Maintenance House *(meeting Oswin Cray)*

*Tenson's arc-opener ([[tenson-arc|The Reconciliation]]). Paper-forward, zero combat, runs 30–50 min. **Re-cut 2026-07-24** from the unplayed S4 version: Oswin's register changed from pitiable to **contemptuous**, and he now gives away two hundred years of the campaign's answer because he finds it boring. Handouts live in [[oswin-papers-handout]].*

---

## 🚨 Panic Panel *(open here, run off this)*

**What this is:** Tenson goes looking for the Mages Guild's offices and finds one bitter, brilliant man with a wheelbarrow. Tea, papers, a tour, and a box of old journals shoved at him to make him go away.

**Say this if you need a beat:** *"You're looking for the Guild offices. Everyone you ask gives you the same directions and the same face."*

## 🧭 In Plain Words *(no jargon — read this first if you're cold)*

There's a salt wall along the southwest edge of the city that keeps the swamp from eating it. One wizard maintains it. His name is Oswin Cray, he's in his fifties, he was sent here four years ago for what was supposed to be six months, and he has been furious about it ever since — quietly, politely, and to nobody.

He is not a sad man. He is a superior one, stuck. He was good at his job and nobody noticed, so he stopped being good at it and nobody noticed that either. He gambles at the crime boss's casino because it's the only room in this city where anything is at stake, he's lost badly, and he has been stealing from the wall's maintenance budget to cover it. He thinks of that as getting paid what he's owed.

He is not afraid of the crime boss — his Guild credentials mean nobody's allowed to touch him. He is afraid of Tenson, because Tenson reads paperwork for a living.

So he does two things at once all scene: he's *thrilled* to finally have an educated person to talk to, and he's steering that person away from one drawer in his desk. Both are completely sincere. The way he steers is by handing over other things — including, eventually, a crate of two-hundred-year-old journals he considers worthless, which are not.

---

## Beat 1 — Looking for the Maintainer *(the joke that isn't one)*

Tenson asks around the civic quarter for who runs the salt wall. **Everyone he asks gives him the same directions and the same face** — pointing him all the way out southwest to the lonely stone tower on the mud.

> You ask around the civic quarter for who runs the salt wall, and the clerks point you all the way out southwest, past where the boardwalks thin out.
>
> When the timber finally ends, there’s a three-story stone tower sitting right on the mud. Actual granite, built two hundred years ago when the Guild thought this posting was going to be important. The base is crusted white with salt, six feet up, like an old kettle that boiled over.
>
> You can smell dark, bitter coffee twenty feet from the door—really strong, cutting right through the swamp air.
>
> Inside, an apprentice with massive purple circles under his eyes is hand-cranking a brass bean grinder. And out front on the wall, there’s one older guy in stiff robes packing salt into a crack with a trowel.

- **Let them watch before he notices.** The image is the whole thesis: *an institution, staffed by one annoyed man and a vibrating apprentice.*
- **Fletcher's job (free, no roll):** green shoots pushing through cracks in fresh salt. *"That regrowth is days old. Days, not weeks. He's re-salting ground he already salted."* **(Clue #10, shown.)**
- **The salt shed** stands open by the door — **half empty**, early in the season. Show it. Don't explain it. **(Clue #7/skim, first channel.)**

### ⭐ His opening line — he thinks Tenson is his replacement

This is the introduction. Play it straight and let it be a little humiliating.

> He clocks you from thirty feet and he *straightens up.* Drops the trowel in the barrow. Wipes both hands on the robe, twice, which does nothing.
>
> "You're Guild." Not a question. He's already walking. "Or Citadel — that's a Citadel coat, that's — " and he stops about six feet off, and he says it plainly, and there's four years underneath it:
>
> "Are you here to relieve me?"

- **Whatever Tenson says, let the answer land before anything else happens.** Two seconds of naked hope from a proud man. That's your hook — everything after this scene is the table remembering he asked.
- Then he recovers **instantly and gracefully**, because he's good at rooms: *"No. No, of course not. Forgive me — come in, you've walked a way. I have coffee. Real southern fleet roast, which is the only sentence I can say about this posting without lying. Aldric, bring the heavy mugs!"*

## Beat 2 — The Tower *(What You See)*

**Indoors. Three floors, built for a staff of six.**

- A **ground-floor hall with four desks.** Three of them are being used as shelves. The fourth is his, facing the door, immaculate.
- A wall of **pigeonholes**, maybe sixty. Four have anything in them.
- **Everything is correct.** Logs squared, ledgers current, instruments cleaned and racked. *He is not a lazy man — he's a contemptuous one, and that's worse.* A lazy man's tower is a mess. Oswin's tower is in **perfect order**, kept that way by someone who despises every hour of it.
- Objects from home, maintained with unreasonable care: **the tea case**, a **pair of good boots kept oiled** in a city where boots rot, a **deck of cards** worn soft at the corners.

### ⭐ The tally *(the single detail to make sure you show)*

Scratched into the plaster beside the stairs, at hand height, where he passes it every day.

> The first stretch is neat. Grouped in fives, evenly spaced, the marks of a man with a plan — a hundred and eighty of them, near enough. Six months' worth.
>
> Then it keeps going. Different pressure. Tighter. It runs the length of the stairwell and around the corner and out of sight.

**If anyone asks, he's ready — he's told this one before:**

> "I stopped counting at a thousand." *[not bitter; he does the joke well]* "Not despair. It was taking up too much wall."

*(He'll never say what the six months were. If someone asks — "That's what the posting was. Six months, assess the situation, write it up, hand it to somebody junior." A beat. "I wrote it up.")*

## Beat 3 — The Word "Auditor" *(the flinch)*

He talks the whole way up the stairs. Where are you from, what do you do, and then *actual follow-up questions* — the hunger of a man four years starved of anyone who can hold a complex thought. The tea is real. He apologizes for it twice and is fishing for a compliment both times.

**Then Tenson says what he does.** (Or hands over [[silence|Magistra Silence]]'s letter, if you're running that thread.)

> He goes still for a second with the cup halfway up.
>
> "An auditor." And then he *laughs* — a real one, delighted, the best sound he's made in a year. "Finally. Gods, finally. Do you know how long I've been asking for someone to actually *look* at this?"
>
> And underneath it, fast, gone again: something else crosses his face.
>
> The smile stays. But it's working now.

🎲 **Instinct 13 — read the flinch.** On a hit, say it plainly to the table: **he is genuinely thrilled you're here, and something about you being here frightens him, and both of those are completely true.**

- **The math he just did:** an auditor is the answer to four years of petitions *and* the only person alive who can find what's in his lower desk drawer. He asked for this. He can't un-ask for it.
- **On a miss:** Tenson just gets the joy. The fear surfaces on its own in Beat 6 — fail forward.

## Beat 4 — The Papers *(Handouts 1 & 2)*

Run this as written; it's the arc's engine and it still works.

- **He volunteers the correspondence** — doesn't wait to be asked, *wants* to be pointed at Caius. Folder tied in cord, worn soft. **→ Hand the player [[oswin-papers-handout|Handout 1]] and shut up.** Silence while he reads is the set piece working.
  > "Five petitions. Five denials. Read the denials — read them *twice*, they're beautiful. Not one fact in dispute and not one coin released. I've stopped being able to tell whether I'm being refused or *managed*."
- 🎲 **Knowledge 13** — the on-the-spot audit, **fallback only** if the player doesn't find the tells himself. Give him real time first.
- **Free, no roll — the routing.** The player can't know imperial administrative law and his handout calls Caius "the ambassador." The moment Tenson reads a signature block, hand him his own expertise plainly: *"A cultural liaison holds no fiscal authority anywhere in the Empire. This channel shouldn't exist. Someone built it."* **(Clue #4.)** The deduction — *built by whom, for what* — stays his.
- **The stamps get a mouth,** Oswin refilling the tea, almost to himself: *"You'll notice they never take long. Eleven days for a petition to crawl across this city. The refusals come back before my tea's cold. Efficient office."* **(Clue #3.)**
- **⚠ New — the Caius pattern, if Tenson's report is already at the Embassy.** He'll ask, conversationally, how Tenson found the man. Then, dry: *"Ask him for something and it's a fortnight of forms. Ask him to say no and he's the fastest desk in the Empire."* **(Free confirmation of Front 6 from a second victim, four years deep.)**
- **Getting Handout 2** — Tenson asks for the books. 🎲 **Presence 12.** Hit: he produces the annual expense summary because refusing a Citadel auditor looks worse than complying, **and his face does the thing again, harder**, and he over-narrates the whole time Tenson reads. Miss: tomorrow, "once I've tidied it" — and the tidying is itself a clue.
- **The contradiction, from his own mouth. Fire this while the player is physically holding Handout 2** — twenty minutes of table time between the line and the ledger row is how this connection dies:
  > "Research? Gods, no. I stopped in year two. You need a reason to research, and the reason is somebody somewhere *reading it*." *[the small laugh]* "I file quarterly observations. That's my entire scholarly output."

  *(The summary in his hands bills the Guild for field research every quarter for eighteen months. If the player connects the line to the laugh — that's Rung 2, and **it must be his**, never an NPC's. If he doesn't, it keeps.)*
- **Party jobs:** **Leavy** — brewer's eye, free: the salt in the new barrels is **coarser, cheaper grade** than the old stock crusted on the shed floor. Same invoice line, worse salt. **Isotta** — the thin stretches she felt at the wall map to the stretched intervals in his logs, if she and Tenson compare notes.

## Beat 5 — The Tour, and the Thing He Finds Boring ⭐

**This is the re-cut's payload. Two hundred years of the campaign's answer, handed over as small talk.**

Give him two minutes of being genuinely excellent first — what the salt does, why the intervals matter, why the southwest face eats twice the attention. This is his one remaining domain and he holds it with both hands.

**Then someone asks what the wall is actually *for*.** He answers completely, immediately, and with no drama whatsoever.

> "A spirit. A big one — the biggest thing in that swamp by an order of magnitude. It comes at the city from the southwest and the salt disrupts it. That's the posting. That's the whole posting."
>
> He says it the way you'd describe a drain.
>
> "It's in the records. It's been in the records for two hundred years — every maintainer writes it down, every one of them writes it down the same way, and not one person up the chain has ever asked a follow-up question." *[he tops up the tea]* "Including me, eventually. You can only ask so many times."

**⚠ Deliver this *bored*.** No hush, no significance. The horror is that a credentialed professional has been holding the outside half of the campaign's secret for four years and finds it about as interesting as drainage. Two institutions, same blindness: **Adaline doesn't ask out of reverence. The Guild doesn't ask out of contempt.**

**Then the direction — his one act of genuine curiosity, and where his contempt was born:**

> "Here's the part I did chase, once. It isn't spreading. It's not growth, it's *approach* — it comes from the southwest and only the southwest, and it has done for two hundred years. Something in this city has its attention."
>
> "I wrote that up properly in my first year. Surveys, figures, the lot. Best work I've done since I got here." *[a beat]* "Nobody replied. Not 'no.' Not 'interesting.' Nothing at all."
>
> He looks out at the wall.
>
> "I've stopped being surprised by that."

*(**That's the origin story.** The gambling and the intellectual surrender have the same birthday. Don't underline it — the table will feel it.)*

### ⭐⭐ The Interlock — the biggest thing he'll say all night

Drop it as a **scheduling footnote**, mid-sentence, while explaining his maintenance intervals. He is not offering a theory. He does not have one.

> "— and I get a bit of slack after the Celebration, which is the one mercy in the calendar. The wall's always easier the morning after. Settles. Holds better for a day or two."
>
> "No idea why. Something in the acoustics, probably. Anyway —"

**Say nothing else. Move on immediately.** Let the party sit with it.

*(Clue-map hint **#24**. Given what the table now believes about the Celebration and their own cage, this may be the loudest bell of the session. **Oswin offers no interpretation and cannot be made to** — press him and he shrugs: "It's a hundred people singing at a wall for an hour. Sound does things." He genuinely believes this and finds the question tedious.)*

**🚫 Hard guardrail — the words he never says:** *grief · mourning · sad · lonely · looking for · calling.* He's an engineer describing a load. He never wonders whether the thing outside feels anything. **All of the pathos belongs to the players.** And he does not know, cannot know, and must never hint at: the binding, the Tree Spirit, or that there are *two* spirits at all.

## Beat 6 — The Pivot, as an Object ⭐

If Tenson pushes toward the money — the difference, the research line, *where does it go* — Oswin does what he does. He doesn't lie. He **avoids**, expertly, and now he avoids with his hands.

> Whatever was open in his face closes like a ledger.
>
> "The figures are with the Guild, of course. If the Citadel wants a full accounting that's a formal process, and I'd welcome it. Truly." And then he's already standing, already moving, already somewhere else entirely —
>
> "Actually. Actually — you're an auditor, you're exactly the right — hold on."
>
> He goes into the back and comes out carrying a crate.
>
> It lands on the desk hard enough to jump the teacups. Journals. A dozen, maybe more, bound in cracked leather, the salt-damp of two centuries in them.
>
> "Vane. First maintainer, two hundred years ago. Left his papers to whoever held the post next, which by an unbroken chain of nobody caring is *me*." He pushes the crate an inch further toward Tenson, and there it is — a man getting something *away* from himself. "I've never opened them. Take the lot, take them tonight, take them for good. Two hundred years of men writing down the weather."
>
> "You'll want something to read. There's nothing else in this city."

**What just happened, GM-side:**

- He is buying his desk drawer back with a crate. **This is the hard pivot from his file, made physical** — and by his lights it's a *clean trade*: he's out a box of worthless paper and the auditor now has homework for a month.
- **He does not know the party already has volume one.** If they say so, he's *pleased* — it makes the distraction more likely to stick. *"Then you're already started. All the more reason."*
- **He has never read them.** If asked why not: *"Because I'd have to care about this posting to care what a dead man thought about it."*
- 🎲 **Instinct 13 — read the pivot** (optional): it lands as **avoidance, not innocence.** A man steering around something with the practiced motion of somebody who's steered around it a thousand times. **That's as far as tonight goes** — no interrogation crack, ever ([[tenson-arc]] guardrail; the paper is the confessor).

**📚 What's in the crate — GM only, don't decide it here.** The later volumes are unbuilt on purpose ([[the-first-maintainers-journal]] §The Other Volumes). Treat it as a **stocked shelf you can draw from between sessions**, not a document dump. Nothing in it names the binding or the Tree Spirit; it's two hundred years of one observation getting quieter.

## Beat 7 — Marcel, If They Raise Him *(the register that changed)*

**⚠ Read this before the scene — it's the biggest departure from the old file.**

Oswin is **not afraid of Marcel Dray.** His Guild credentials are a hard wall; Marcel is practical and has never once threatened him with anything but paperwork. Their arrangement is a mutual inconvenience, and Oswin has rationalized it into *a manageable understanding between businessmen.*

So the tells invert:

- **Ask about Marcel the man →** he's *breezy*, almost admiring. *"Dray? Perfectly civil. Better company than most of this city, and I include the Guild in that."* **No flinch. None.**
- **Ask about Oswin's own account →** *there's* the flinch. He goes light and fast and changes the subject. He's not protecting Marcel; he's protecting the chain **gambling → debt → the drawer.**
- **Ask directly whether he plays there →** a small self-deprecating shrug: *"I've been. It's a bit rich for me these days."* Which is true in a way he'd rather you didn't examine.

*(This is scarier than fear. A frightened man reads as a victim. A comfortable one reads as complicit — and the party will be right, and it will still be more complicated than that.)*

**The gambling invitation** stays banked as a warm button for a later visit ([[oswin-cray#The Gambling Invitation|his file]]). He's good at cards. He'd like the company.

**If anyone goes for the desk:** the locked iron box is findable as an object — 🎲 **Finesse 14** just to get a private moment — but burgling a Guild wizard's lockbox mid-tea, on day one, in front of him, is a bridge the table should feel the weight of. If they do it anyway, [[oswin-cray#If He Realizes the Party Intends to Expose Him|his file governs]]. **Design intent: tonight plants the drawer. A later visit opens it.**

## Button — "Say What's True" *(at the door)*

He's holding the door and not looking at anyone.

> "When you write it up. Whatever you've found in here." *[a beat, and for one second the clever face and the tired face are the same face]* "Say what's true. All of it. It'll be the first honest paperwork this wall's had in fifty years."
>
> Then, back to himself, dry: "And if it gets me recalled — I'll owe you a very good bottle."

*(He means every word. He is also including himself in the indictment and hoping nobody notices — and he's just told an auditor that getting caught and getting **out** are the same door. That's the arc's whole dilemma, handed over in a doorway, and the player doesn't know it yet.)*

## 🚫 Guardrails

- **No confession.** Suspicion and paper, not proof. The box stays shut.
- **He's not afraid of Marcel.** He's afraid of Tenson. Everything else follows from that.
- **The spirit lore is free and boring.** Never mysterious, never hushed, never a theory.
- **He never says "grief."** Weather and tide, not a mourner ([[cosmology]]).
- **The Remainder holds** — Tenson proves the size of the anomaly; he never narrates it ([[tenson-arc#The Remainder (guardrail — the audit's edge)|the Remainder rule]]).
- **The Interlock line fires once, unexplained, and you move on.** Recognition, never exposition.
