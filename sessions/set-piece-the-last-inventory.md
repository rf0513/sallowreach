# Set Piece — The Last Inventory

*The end of [[gault|Gault]], whenever it comes. **His stats, canon, and the first two encounters live in [[gault]]; the deep-water staging rules live in [[the-deep-mire]]** — this file is how you perform the last one: the doors in, the ground, the fight, and the ending menu. Built open on purpose — the party picks the door and the ending. This file only guarantees the room.*

> ⚠ **Re-scoped 2026-07-19 — a door, not the destination.** The arc's default end is now the Double Knot convergence: [[set-piece-the-bad-season]] (his win) → [[set-piece-beyond-the-season]] (the war). This file fires only if the party corners him alone — *before* the raid — or as the **epilogue** if he slips the final battle: no crew, no fortune, no buyers, still too stubborn to stop. Run it as written either way; the invariants below hold in every timeline.

---

## 🧭 In Plain Words *(the whole set piece — no jargon)*

Gault is a poacher — the campaign's human villain. For eight years he has stripped the swamp of its living treasures and sold them in jars, and the party has crossed him before: the unhurried man loading sealed containers, always gone before anything sticks. He is protected by an eerie fact: the swamp's predators will not touch him — years of wading in spirit-charged water changed his scent, and animals avoid him the way they avoid carrion. This scene is the last time anyone meets him. He has followed the deep water's strange lights into the most remote part of the swamp, convinced he has found the biggest score of his career. He is wrong — the light is not coming from animals, it is coming from something vast and old whose home he is standing in. He is alone, out of his depth, and too greedy to know it. However the party gets there — hunting him, delivering the spear, or crossing his wake once too often — the fight happens here: small, human, and ugly, in front of something enormous that does not notice either side.

**Two goals:** the party gets the fight they earned — no speech saves him, no trick escapes them this time — and the scale of the place makes the fight feel exactly as small as it is.

## ✅ What's True No Matter What *(the invariants — canon, whatever else has happened)*

- **He is alone.** Eleven hired hands in eight years; none of them this deep. Nobody left to cover his exit.
- **He has no exit the party doesn't know about.** First time in eight years. He doesn't know it yet.
- **He's misread the light.** He thinks the concentrations are an undiscovered lantern-ray population. They're not rays. He is in the wrong place for the wrong reasons.
- **The Mire ignores him.** Predators pass him. Hazards part around him. The water lets him through. The party should find this infuriating — it's a clue, not a power.
- **He fights to survive, not escape** — and the difference is visible. The smugness is gone. Nobody at the table has seen him like this.
- **He does not beg. He does not bargain. He does not explain.** If it's over, he knows it's over.

## 🎬 The Ground *(Hunt door only — skip for Threshold/Collision; 15–20 min)*

Finding him is a short tracking leg through worked water. Run [[the-deep-mire]] rules underneath (navigation at disadvantage past the known channels).

- **The trail** — Instinct (tracking), **Diff 13**, Fletcher's lane: worked pools, cut nets, a stripped nesting shelf. Success: they find his staged cache a half-day ahead of him. Failure forward: they find it as he's leaving it — cut straight to The Fight, his terms.
- **The cache** — no roll, just let them open it: sealed containers in neat rows, each labeled in a small careful hand. Manifest numbers, no names. One jar still faintly glowing. *(If anyone frees what's living, note it — it pays off in The After.)*
- **His charts** — Knowledge, **Diff 12**: his soundings of the light concentrations, meticulous, and *wrong*. Whoever reads them realizes it before he did — the light he's chasing isn't scattered like a population. It's arranged like something single, and huge, and he's rowing toward the middle of it. **Say-aloud line (hand it to the reader):** *"He thinks he's found a thousand rays. He's found one thing. And he's headed for its heart."*

## ⚔️ The Fight *(stats: [[gault]] — Tier 2 Solo, Diff 16; a reach fight below Tier 2, on purpose)*

**The arena:** spirit-adjacent dark. The water is lit from below by something vast — slow sheets of color, no lantern rays anywhere. Footing is roots and his beached skiff. The light moves on its own schedule and belongs to neither side.

**Open on him working.** He's loading containers by the wrong light, unhurried, right up until he sees them. Then — for the first time — he counts exits and doesn't like the number.

- **His opener, if anyone talks first:** *"You're not the worst thing I've dealt with in here."* — and he means it, which is unsettling. That's his whole negotiation. Then the harpoon.
- **Run his features as written** ([[gault]]): the weighted net, the shove into black water, Channel Read. **When He Commits** at half HP — perform it: the exit-math stops, the voice stops, and something older than greed takes over his hands.
- **The light is his weapon:** he knows how the deep glow disorients; the party doesn't. Once per round he can move through a dying patch of light — treat as his Channel Read repositioning, described as the dark swallowing him. Counter: any PC who spends a beat reading the light's rhythm (Instinct, **Diff 13**) strips this for the rest of the fight — the light doesn't follow *his* schedule either.

### The clue that lands the theme *(fires twice, mid-fight)*

- **Shown:** something big passes through the fight — a wake, a shadow under the boat — and it avoids *him*. Curls around him like water around a post, and comes toward the party instead.
- **Said aloud (Fletcher's lane, free, no roll):** *"Nothing out here will touch him. They're not scared of him — they're treating him like carrion."*
- *(NPC fallback if the door was the Threshold and [[odile-verret|Odile]] poled them in: "The water doesn't bother hating him. That's your job.")*

### The Name *(optional beat — only if the party carries Brin's grievance)*

If anyone throws the dead mentee at him — [[brin-sevarre|Brin]]'s young hunter, the one of his eleven who didn't make it out — he stops. Genuinely tries. Then, with real effort and no cruelty:

> *"...Which one was that?"*

Don't decorate it. That answer is the whole man. *(Brin gets her line in The After.)*

### 😨 His Fear menu *(spend 3–5; he's one man — the bursts are desperate, not grand)*

- **1 Fear — The Mire Knows Me** *(his feature, performed)*: "A root lets go under you — not breaks, *lets go* — and the current hands you to him like the swamp did it a favor. 1d8, and you're beside him." *(The horror: it works HERE too. Whatever tracks him is still tracking.)*
- **1 Fear — the light dies:** the glow beneath the fight guts out for one breath — his silhouette is just *gone*. Next attack against him at disadvantage; he reappears at range, already throwing.
- **1 Fear — the net, again:** he pins the PC who's hurt him most. No line, no gloat. He's done performing.
- **2 Fear — the last of the smug** *(once, at Severe)*: he wrenches a container off his back and hurls it — glass, preserved things, oil that burns cold across the water between him and them. Everyone Close to the burst: Agility **Reaction 13** or 1d8 and pushed back. It buys him ten feet. It costs him the score. Watch a player realize he's spending his *inventory* to live.
- **What you never spend Fear on:** the vast thing intervening. It doesn't. The Mire ignores him to the end — nothing out here fights for either side, and the fight stays exactly as small as two boats and one harpoon.

## 🏁 The End — a menu, not a script *(the table chooses; log the choice in [[world-in-motion]])*

- **They kill him.** The canon coda, as written: *the Mire goes still. The bioluminescence disperses slowly. No one mentions it. No one needs to.* Give the table a full quiet minute. If this was the Threshold door — then cross, per [[set-piece-the-delivery]].
- **They spare him / maroon him.** He doesn't thank them and doesn't promise anything — canon: he doesn't bargain. Two dials, decide *later*, not tonight: he walks out (he always has), or the deep finally keeps him. Either way he's finished as a threat — the workforce is gone, the buyers are far, and the party has seen him small.
- **They bring him back.** Sallowreach has no cell for a man like this ([[justice-in-sallowreach]] — the ladder ends at casting *out*, which for Gault is a homecoming). The real teeth are elsewhere: **Brin's three years of Guild files** finally get a living face to attach to, and **[[caius-maren|Caius]]** develops a sudden, courteous interest in a courier who moved unmarked imperial cargo — the manifest is paper, and paper is his country. Handing Gault to Caius is handing Caius a lever. Say nothing; let them notice.

## 🌊 The After *(regardless of ending — short scenes, big weight)*

- **The containers.** Whatever's still alive in his cache needs deciding. A release at the brightfin basin is a five-minute scene and the campaign's quietest win. *(Villain loot beyond this: [[items/loot-ledger]].)*
- **The standing order dies.** Word moves down the corridor: no more coin for the two-headed one, no more triple for a trail. If the Double Knot hasn't played, the serpent is now *nobody's* cargo — which isn't the same as safe.
- **Brin's line** *(underplay it)*: she doesn't celebrate. She asks one question — *"Did he say which one?"* — and whatever the answer, she nods once, like a ledger balancing, and goes back to work.
- **If the Tessak still fed from his deal:** the easy supply stops. The clan gets hungry on a schedule the party can now predict. Tick it ([[world-in-motion]]).

## 🎯 Win Condition

Nobody at the table said "boss fight" — they said his *name*, the whole time; the fight felt like cornering a man, not slaying a monster; and afterward at least one player looked at the vast slow light and understood that the campaign's human villain was never the campaign's biggest thing — and never mattered less than at the end.
