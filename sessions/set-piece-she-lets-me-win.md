# Set Piece — She Lets Me Win *(the Basile case: the name and the crossing)*

*Opens S7 mid-scene from S6 ([[session-06-recap]]): **Isotta and Basile are on the road together — they've already done the red house** (charcoal rub, the elm crest, the Morel name). **They're walking now to the elm house** with his cane going tap… tap. **Basile is with her the whole way** — don't stash him. ⭐ **Re-cut 2026-09-01: the scene now runs to FOUR beats** — the Tallykeeper arrives at the crossing to collect him, and the grace terms (which S6 never actually set) land there. **The grace cord is retired.** **Basile's canon lives in [[vael-miroux]] §The Quest**; the Tallykeeper's canon in [[the-tallykeeper]]; her promise to help someone cross is the engine from [[isotta-arc]]. Built 2026-08-25, re-cut 2026-09-01. **3 rolls — and the deduction is not one of them. ~35–45 min for all four beats.***

## 🧭 In Plain Words

One of the player characters — Isotta — is secretly a ghost who bought herself time by promising a death-spirit she'd help someone cross over properly. The someone is Basile: a beloved old man at the neighborhood café who has quietly been an immortal spirit for about a hundred and fifty years. He stayed because he loved this city and one family in particular; now his memory is failing, almost everyone who ever knew him is dead, and the one thing he still wants is the full name of the girl who claimed him as her big brother when he first arrived. She died seventy years ago, and nobody alive remembers her — not even her own descendants. Isotta walks a paper trail through two houses — and at the second one she finds that three different women in this family had names starting with M, their belongings all jumbled into the same trunks. **She works out which one he means**, from a family register, a scorecard, and the ages he keeps mentioning without realising they are clues. Then she says the name to him, and walks him to the veil, gently. Nobody else in the world can witness any of this. It's a detective story where the reward at the end is a funeral, and the funeral is a good one.

## 🔴 What She Already Found — the red house, banked *(don't re-run it)*

She's been there. Charcoal rub on parchment, elm crest under the new paint — and the pantry doorway beside it:

> Under the paint on the pantry doorframe, low down where the light falls wrong: scratches. Rows of them, painted over but still proud of the wood. Dates climbing one under the other, six years old — seven — eight. And beside the column, one more mark, cut fresh over and over. Same height. Every single year, the same height.

Somebody grew up in this doorway. Somebody came to every birthday and never grew an inch. The domino tile in the windowsill crack made the bridge back to the cafe. **That work is done. The name is still ahead — at the elm house.** If she never looked inside past the doorway, the rest keeps for any future visit; tonight it doesn't gate her.

## 🟢 Beat 1 — The Elm House ⭐ *(the puzzle: which M. Morel?)*

**The shape of this beat, for the GM:** Yvette is warm and useless. The trunks hold **three** women's lives mixed together. Isotta's job is to work out which one Basile loved — and she can do it in about ninety seconds from evidence that is already in this file. **Let her.** Don't help, don't hint until the whiff-proof fires, and above all don't have anyone say the name before she does.

> Same crest you saw under the paint — carved fresh over the lintel, facing the big old elm in the courtyard. It's a good omen, and it means you're at the right door. Basile slows down a step when he sees the tree. Doesn't say why.

Inside: **Yvette Morel**, seventies, the eldest. Hospitable the way this whole city is hospitable, and she can offer nothing about the woman Isotta is looking for — because the family's memory ran out before she was born:

> "Auntie's trunks? Lord, those came to *us*. When the cousin's house sold, boxes came with it — family things. You don't sort a thing like that, you just carry it." *[beat]* "There's names on some of it, I expect. Never had the heart to go digging through somebody's whole life for them."

☎ **Basile is right there.** Slightly behind Isotta, cane in both hands, smiling the polite smile of a guest who doesn't want to be a bother. He does not get invited in first — let Isotta hold the doorstep. She's his person tonight, not his chaperone.

🎲 **Presence 13** — getting the trunks down *(Isotta's ask; Basile hangs back)*:

| Roll | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Success w/ Hope** | Yvette lights up — "Oh, you knew Auntie? Nobody's asked in *years*" — trunks come down fast, tea appears, she wants to help sort. |
| **Success w/ Fear** | She brings them, but her grandson does the talking first — polite, curious what you want with Auntie's things. He watches Basile a beat too long. |
| **Failure w/ Hope** | Yvette hesitates — "Those aren't really *ours* to open, cher" — but Basile, quiet, says "Please." She looks at his face and relents. She doesn't know why. |
| **Failure w/ Fear** | Longer hesitation — she needs a real reason before she'd let strangers through Auntie's life. Not refusal; a cost. Offer to earn it, or come back tomorrow — except Basile won't keep well till tomorrow. **Spend the Fear:** the trunks come down, but Yvette stays in the doorway, watching. |

### ⭐ The problem: it isn't one woman's trunk. It's three.

> Three trunks, and none of them are sorted. Somebody's christening gown folded on top of somebody else's reading glasses. A wedding photograph face-down on a school slate.
>
> Yvette shrugs at it, cheerful. "Told you. You just carry it."

🎲 **Instinct 12** — sorting three lives into three piles *(the tactile detective beat — Basile hovering, useless and delighted)*:

| Roll | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Success w/ Hope** | Three clean piles by wear, fabric, and hand. One pile is *much* older than the others, and the dominoes set is in it. |
| **Success w/ Fear** | The piles come out right, but Yvette starts helpfully putting things back in the wrong ones. Isotta has to be gently rude to a kind old woman. |
| **Failure** | It stays a jumble. **Not a wall** — she can still get there, she just has to reason it out loud instead of seeing it laid out. Slower, no other cost. |

### ⭐ The register: three M. Morels

The **prayer book** is the key object, and it's a period family register — births and deaths written on the flyleaf in three different hands, decades apart.

🎲 **Knowledge 12** — reading the flyleaf. **Success:** the names *and* the dates. **Failure:** the names, but the ink's gone — she has to get the dates from Yvette (who knows the recent ones) and Basile (who knows the old one, if she asks right).

> Inside the front cover, in three different hands and about a hundred years of ink:
>
> **Marceline Morel** · **Mathilde Morel** · **Marguerite Morel**

**Hand the player the three names and the dates. Then stop talking.**

| Name | Died | Aged |
|---|---|---|
| **Marceline** | 15 years ago | 81 |
| **Mathilde** | 40 years ago | 22 |
| **Marguerite** | 70 years ago | 87 |

### 🧩 The Deduction *(no roll — this is hers, and it is the whole point)*

**GM crib — the three eliminations, each from a different kind of evidence.** She already has every piece:

1. **Marceline is out — because Yvette remembers her.** She's who the family means by "Auntie." Basile's woman is one *nobody* remembers. *(Yvette's own hospitality is the first clue — she'll happily tell stories about Marceline all night.)*
2. **Mathilde is out — because of the domino lid.** Two columns of scores running *decades*, hundreds of games. Mathilde died at twenty-two. She didn't live long enough to play them.
3. **Marguerite is the one — arithmetic.** Died 70 years ago at 87, so born about 157 years ago. **[[vael-miroux|Vael]] already told Isotta Basile has been here around 150 years** — which makes Marguerite about *seven* when he arrived. And Basile has been saying *"she was seven"* and *"gap tooth till she was nine"* all evening without knowing they were numbers.

**Everything in that list is already on the table before this beat starts.** Nothing new is invented, nothing is withheld.

✅ **Make sure she has the 150 figure** — the arithmetic needs it and canon never confirms Vael said the number out loud. **Basile can just tell her, free, no roll**, because his firewall only covers the Celebration and the founders, never his own arrival: *"A hundred and fifty years, near enough. I stopped counting when it got rude."* Ask-and-receive. If she never asks, Vael says it at the crossing.

🛟 **Two eliminations already leave one name** — the arithmetic is a third lock, not a requirement. If she gets there on Yvette + the domino lid alone, she is **right**, and you should say so. Do not withhold the win because she skipped a step.

⚠ **The wrong answer is live.** Mathilde is genuinely tempting — died young, the sad one. If Isotta settles on the wrong name, **let her.** But when she says it to Basile, he hesitates. Half a second, then he'd take it politely and go, uncorrected and wrong. **The hesitation is the tell** — if the player is paying attention, she can pull it back. Don't flag it; just play the pause.

🛟 **Whiff-proof — it's already the scene's first line.** He keeps starting the name and losing it: *"Marg—"*. If she stalls, play that stutter again, closer, twice. It narrows the field for free and costs nobody anything. **Never let the table grind here.** This beat's job is to make her feel clever, not to be hard.

### ⭐ The click *(she says it first, and then the object agrees with her)*

When she says **"Marguerite"** out loud — and only then — the confirmation:

> Folded into the shawl with the dominoes, small as a playing card: a portrait painted on ivory. Two children at a table. The girl mid-laugh, gap-toothed, missing nothing.
>
> And right beside her, a boy.
>
> You know that face. You had coffee with it yesterday.
>
> Turn it over. On the back, in a careful hand:
>
> **M. M. — her seventh year.**

**It does not name her.** It confirms the *seven*. Which confirms her arithmetic. Which means **she** solved it and the object only agreed — that's the difference between being told and being right, and it's the whole reason this beat exists.

*(Painted the year she turned seven — the two children done together, because the family had already stopped asking where he'd come from and started setting his place.)*

### The lid *(the emotional payoff, now that she's earned which M)*

> The dominoes set. Bone tiles, worn yellow, the felt gone through where hands held them.
>
> Inside the lid, scratched deep — not once, over and over. Two columns. **B** and **M**. Scores going back decades.
>
> And at the bottom of the last page, rounder, younger:
>
> **"M. 47 — B. 41. he lets me win."**

That's the name in her own handwriting. Say it out loud. Make the family's own daughter the first word Isotta gives him back.

## ✋ Beat 2 — Saying It Back *(roll-free — his beat; she's already holding the name, and he's right there)*

Takes thirty seconds. Wherever she is — elm house parlor is fine if Yvette's given her space, or the lane outside, or back at the Still Cup if she wants her goodbye to have a night inside it — she says the name. Basile is beside her. Don't make him wait for a curtain.

> And it's like watching a fire catch. He comes up out of the slump — eyes clear, back straight, seventy years arriving all at once.
>
> "Marguerite." Twice, careful, like carrying something full to the brim. "Marguerite Morel. Gap tooth till she was nine — kicked it out on her brother's knee, and swore me to secrecy—" *[stops. His hand goes flat on the cane's head.]*
>
> "Her stone," he says, to nobody. "She put it there herself. So I'd remember I was tall enough to reach things."

Then, plainer than he's been since she met him:

> "You want to know how I've stayed. I stayed because she asked. She's been gone seventy years, and the coffee stopped tasting like anything a long time ago."
>
> He looks at her — properly, the veil looking out from behind his eyes.
>
> "Walk me home."

## 🕯️ Beat 3 — The Crossing *(roll-free — three minutes, quiet)*

Dawn, before opening, next morning if she wants her goodbye to have a night inside it — or tonight, if she doesn't. [[vael-miroux|Vael]] unlocks the door and says nothing, which for Vael is a speech. Three coffees. One in the third-place setting — the good cup, the one that has always been Basile's.

> Basile squares the dominoes. All twenty-eight, corners lined. Pats the lid once.
>
> "Keep the score."
>
> And between one breath and the next, the chair holds more stillness than it did. His coffee steams. Nobody drinks it.

**Do not end the scene here.** Something came to take him, and it is still in the room. Go straight into Beat 4.

## 🪶 Beat 4 — Who Came For Him ⭐ *(roll-free — the Tallykeeper arrives; 10–15 min)*

**In plain words:** the heron-thing that has been following Isotta is the spirit that collects the dead. Basile just died properly, so of course it is here — that is its whole job. It has never met her under good circumstances before. Tonight she has just done its work for it, well, in front of it. This is where the grace period actually gets agreed, and where it lets slip three things it does not explain.

⚠ **This replaces the S6 cord-tying that never happened** *(2026-09-01: the grace cord is retired — see §The Terms below).* The knock and the time-freeze fired in S6; the terms did not. They land here instead, and they land better, because she is negotiating on **delivery** rather than on a promise.

### The arrival — let Vael tell her what it is

> The chair holds more stillness than it did. And there's someone else at the table.
>
> There wasn't, a second ago.
>
> Vael doesn't jump. Vael stands up — slow, the way you stand up for somebody older than you — and steps back from the chair.

☎ **Vael can see it.** His white eye reads *spirits* ([[vael-miroux]]) — it just can't read the town's uncrossed human dead. So he sees this perfectly well, and his deference is the tell. He does not introduce it. He does not sit back down.

### What it says about Basile *(the opener — it is complimenting her, in the only register it has)*

> "That was a clean one. Named. Anchored. Willing."
>
> *[beat]*
>
> "I close perhaps four in a year in this city."

### ⭐ Drop 1 — the older entries *(the number that will not hold still)*

Unprompted, or the moment she asks it anything at all:

> "I have older entries."
>
> *[beat]*
>
> "There are some here I have carried since before the pilings were cut. I cannot close them."

If she asks **how many** — and she will:

> "Tonight? Nine."
>
> *[beat]*
>
> "Ask me tomorrow. It will be wrong."

**GM, the reason — never said:** it has never had a clean look at them. It counts them **from outside**, from the ring, through the binding, and it has been doing that for two hundred years. Nine, eleven, thirteen. In two nights she will be standing ten feet from them and *her* count won't hold still either ([[session-05-first-celebration]] Beat 5.5b — **the Eleven**). That recognition is the payload. Do not help her get there.

### ⭐ Drop 2 — the place it does not enter *(the limit, never the location)*

If she asks why it cannot close them:

> "There is a part of this city I do not enter."
>
> *[beat]*
>
> "I have not entered it in two hundred years. I do not intend to."

*"Why?"*

> "Because I would not come back out."

🚫 **And it will not tell her where.** This is the hard guardrail — the location of the drowned tree is **her deduction**, derived from her own tally sheet ([[isotta-arc]] §The Standing Count), and an NPC handing it over kills the best beat in her arc. The line that hands the work back:

> "I will not point at it."
>
> *[beat]*
>
> "You have a route. You have a sheet. You will find it, or you will not."

She leaves with **a hole and no coordinates.**

### ⭐ Drop 3 — the city empties *(the fact, then Vael's image, then the button)*

If the Celebration comes up — and it's two nightfalls out, so it will:

> "On that night, they go. The whole city empties of its dead by dusk. It has been so for two hundred years."

Then **Vael**, who talks like a person. *(He's never seen the ghosts go — he can't see them. He's watched his own side empty.)*

> "Forty years I've worked this city, and one night a year my eye's just… got nothing in it. Nothing at all. Cleanest night of the year, and I've never liked it once."
>
> *[beat]*
>
> "You ever seen birds go up before a squall? All at once, all one way — and then it's quiet like somebody put a lid on it. That's this. Every year."

⭐ **Then the button. Vael says everything leaves. It corrects him:**

> "Not all."
>
> *[beat]*
>
> "Nine stay."

**End the scene there if you can.** That's the best last line available and nothing improves it.

### 📜 The Terms *(no cord, no countdown — what she actually walks out with)*

**Baseline, always granted if it engages at all: it waits.** It says why, flatly, and the reason is true and cold — a frayed Isotta is worth nothing to it. It needs her line *closeable*, not closed today.

- **The only dial is her [[isotta-arc|Slipping Clock]]** — 6 segments, already in her sheet, already tickable. Fill it and she frays toward dissolution, and dissolution is the one outcome the grace exists to prevent. **Draw that clock on scrap where the player can see it.** One object, not two.
- **Closed cases don't tick anything.** They change what it will *grant*. It keeps the count itself and states the balance when asked — which is the most on-tic thing this creature can possibly do.
- **Her tally sheet counts as collateral too.** Nobody has counted this city's open lines in two hundred years. It will say so flatly, and it is the closest thing to a compliment it has ever paid anyone.

**Still arguable, if she asks plainly** *(it respects plain asks; it states prices only if asked)*:

| She can win | By | The price |
|---|---|---|
| **The full night's notice** | Asking for it plainly | Before any collection, one whole night's warning, guaranteed. Price: *she comes to that meeting willingly.* |
| **The Guarantor** | Another PC co-signing her line | Their promise becomes an entry — if her line goes to dissolution, the guarantor's account carries the open debt. It states this plainly **if asked.** Never let it be signed casually. |

**Terms, never whether.** No roll here ends in collection. Bad rolls end in worse terms — an omission read back, a rash promise recorded at face value. It never punishes. It *records*, which lasts longer.

### 🚫 Guardrails for Beat 4

- **It never names the Celebration, the binding, or what's in the middle.** It knows the way a predator knows a snare, not the way an archivist knows a document. It has only ever seen the edge.
- **It does not editorialize** ([[cosmology]]). It states counts. The dread is the arithmetic.
- **It is not menacing and not comic.** Courteous, patient, absolutely unmoved.
- **Still held, do not spend tonight:** the ranked line — *"You are not the oldest open account in this city. You are the second."* That one puts **her** on the same list as the nine, and it's a different, more personal turn. Save it for a direct question later.
- **No tenderness, ever.** It waits because a dissolved Isotta is a closed door. That's all it will ever admit.

## 💡 If They Get Clever

- **Basile IS with her** — that's the shape, not a variant. At the elm house he's slightly behind her, hands on the cane, letting her hold the doorstep. The trunks are her find; the name is her mouth. His beat is *after*.
- **Asks him directly for the name, on the walk:** fragments only, and **keep them useless this early** — *"Ma— no. That's the street."* Confirms urgency, solves nothing. ⚠ **Don't let him get as far as "Marg—" until the whiff-proof.** That syllable is the safety net; spend it late or the puzzle evaporates.
- ⭐ **Asks Yvette about all three aunties:** she's *delighted*. Marceline she can talk about for an hour — recipes, a bad husband, the good chair. Mathilde she knows only as a sad family fact. **Marguerite she has literally never heard of**, and finding that out in front of Isotta genuinely rattles her. That contrast is the first elimination, handed over free by a kind woman who doesn't know she's helping.
- ⭐ **Asks Basile to look at the piles:** he can't name anything, but he'll *touch* the right pile. Reaches for it without noticing he's doing it. Not proof — a nudge, and a good one. Give it to her if she thinks to ask; don't offer it.
- ⭐ **Asks Yvette how old the dominoes are / who played them:** she has no idea, but she'll say the true useful thing — *"That's older than this house, cher."*
- ⭐ **Tries to date the trunk contents by fabric or print:** free, no roll, and generous. The oldest pile is a full generation older than the other two.
- **Ghost-sight / Connects with Spirits on the objects:** objects hold nothing. Spirits aren't *in* things — anchors are people. Free flavor, zero information, and its own small lesson.
- **Goes to Vael for answers first:** even Vael doesn't know. Decades of protective silence cut both ways: *"His to tell, child. Not mine."*
- **Wants to open the red-house pantry again:** still there, keeps for any future visit. Not needed tonight — the name is at the elm house.

## 🚫 Guardrails

- **The firewall holds:** Basile arrived ~150 years ago — *after* the Celebration began. "It was already old when I came" is his entire testimony on the subject, and he never offers more. No founders, no binding, no chant-lore, ever.
- **No lecture.** The mirror — a spirit clinging to humanity watching one let go — is never spoken by anyone. Her player does that math alone.
- **Family amnesia is total.** No Morel suddenly remembers lore. Yvette learns her great-aunt existed *from Isotta* — that flip is the scene's gift, don't give it away early.
- **The crossing stays quiet.** No dice on the goodbye, no supernatural weather, no last-minute complication. Small and finished.
- **He is not sad company.** Once the name lands he's *present* — funny, sharp, seventy years of stored-up noticing. Grief gets one plain line ("walk me home"), not a register.

## 🎒 Walking Out

- ⭐ **Her first closed case, and the grace is now actually agreed** — log the terms she won (night's notice? guarantor?) and anything she promised. No cord, no loops: **the Slipping Clock is the only dial** ([[isotta-arc]]).
- ⭐ **Log the two things it let slip** — the count that won't hold still, and the place it won't enter. She should be carrying both into the Celebration.
- **The protocol's first field success.** Clear a Stress, gain a Hope — the casebook earns its keep.
- **Did she tell the Morels what their aunt was?** Their call, her risk. Log it — if yes, small future texture: gate left open, an extra place at feast tables.
- **Does she tell Vael?** He protected Basile in silence for decades. Telling him his friend crossed well is a gift — log his reaction.
