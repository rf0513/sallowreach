# Set Piece — The Cold Cache

*Fletcher's Side-Quest 2 ([[fletcher-arc]]), built table-ready 2026-07-15. Rewritten same day for cold-open readability.*

## In plain words *(read this first — no jargon)*

The party's boat used to be a smuggler's boat. It belonged to Gault's operation, and it ran supplies on a fixed route through the swamp. In this scene, someone finds a hidden compartment in the boat. Inside is a route card — proof that the boat has scheduled stops. The party follows the card to one of those stops: a hidden supply cache deep in the swamp. On the way there, lizardfolk who control that stretch of water block the channel; the smuggler markings on the hull can buy passage. At the cache they find four things: shipping papers proving the two-headed snake was made in a lab and escaped during transport, a crate of stolen alchemist jars, old snake sheds with cage scars on them, and a drowned smuggler's personal effects. On the trip home, the snake itself surfaces and watches the boat. It does not attack. It watches, and then it's gone. Back in town, two follow-ups: a fence at the docks confirms that someone has been paying for three years to catch the snake *alive*, and the drowned smuggler's ghost is waiting at the night berths — Isotta's next case.

That's the whole scene. Everything below is tools for running it.

## Why this scene matters *(the payoffs, with the codenames explained)*

- **Fletcher's hunt advances one rung.** His arc gates the truth about the snake in steps ([[fletcher-arc]]). This trip delivers **Rung 2: the snake was cargo — shipped by someone, and it escaped in transit.** (Rung 1 — "it's an abomination, someone made it" — landed on the Wrong Skin trip. Rung 3 — "the courier was Gault" — comes later, from Gault's own mouth, in the scene the arc calls **the Offer**.)
- **Isotta gets her next case.** Her arc is holding a saved-up scene: she finally *witnesses* a ghost cross over, and hears a specific parting line ([[isotta-arc]] calls this the banked jewel). The drowned smuggler at the night berths is where that scene fires.
- **Leavy gets leverage.** She owes the Alchemists' Consortium a debt — Orna's written Lastlight recipe, which Orna doesn't know about yet ([[leavy-arc]]). The stolen jars in the cache are Consortium property. Returning them is a bargaining chip.
- **Tenson gets the paper.** Off-the-books imperial freight is exactly the rot he came to Sallowreach to prove exists. The waybill is his find, and the big line is his to say.

**When to run it:** S6 is the natural slot, but it really starts whenever someone finds the compartment (Beat 1). Remy crews the boat. Brin pays Guild rate afterward for proving the smuggling route — the payday rides the quest, per [[coin-in-sallowreach]].

> **Canon note (2026-07-15):** the original arc sketch put the smuggler's *ghost* at the cache. That breaks the ghost rule — ghosts stay in town and only Isotta sees them; the deep swamp belongs to spirits ([[cosmology]]). So the cache holds the man's belongings, and his ghost waits in town at the night berths.

## Beat 1 — Finding the compartment *(in town, any quiet moment)*

Fire this whenever someone cleans, repairs, or audits the boat. If nobody ever does, Remy finds it — it can't stay lost forever.

> It's a wet afternoon and somebody's finally doing the job everyone keeps putting off — clearing out the aft locker. And a board goes sideways. Not up. *Sideways.* It's not broken; it's built: a dry-slot under the locker floor, shallow as a book, lined in waxed cloth. Inside there's exactly one thing: a stiff waxed card, notched down both edges like a tally.

Then, for Fletcher:

> Fletcher — you've seen those notches before. They're carved into your own hull, low, under the rubbing strake. You took them for old repairs. Hold the card against the wood and the notches match, station by station. Your boat doesn't just have a home port. It has **stops.**

**Someone says the clue out loud** — Tenson if he'll take it: *"This is a route. Our boat is a route."* If nobody gets there, Remy runs a thumb down the notches and goes quiet: *"…Way-marks. This boat ran a schedule."*

**Why they go:** Brin will pay real coin to have the smuggling route proven. A cache in the deep channels means valuables. And it's *their* boat — they'll want to know what it was.

## Beat 2 — The checkpoint *(Tessak water)*

The route runs through water the Tessak control — the deep-channel lizardfolk, the ones who tried to eat the party in Session 1. Five of them surface across the channel like a toll gate. They read the hull markings first, then the people, then the markings again. The catch: the marks identify this boat as one of **Gault's** — and Gault's boats get through.

Three ways past, the table's pick:

- **Say nothing.** The marks speak for them; they're waved through *as Gault's people*. Free — but the Tessak now have the party on the wrong ledger, and that lie resurfaces if they ever visit the Tessak camp ([[set-piece-the-tessak-camp]]).
- **Tell the truth** — 🎲 **Presence 12.** Plain words work; the Tessak respect honesty, not flattery: *"We took this boat from the ones who ran it."* Riskier, but it earns the exchange below, and a better standing later.
- **Go around** — 🎲 **Finesse 12.** The flank channels cost the whole afternoon, which means the trip home happens in failing light, and the scavengers show up free.

If they told the truth, the lead scout gives them this — hissed trade-tongue, patient:

> "Wrong crew. Right boat. The boat may pass — it has passed before." *A beat.* "Past the double knot, turn back. That water belongs to the **Ssseason-Eater.** We do not fish it. It does not stop eating."

*(GM: "Season-Eater" is the Tessak's name for the snake. A clan of man-eaters is afraid of something in the water — let the table sit with that. Explain nothing further today.)*

## Beat 3 — The cache *(four finds, one per PC)*

The last notch on the card is a dry island behind a brush screen that's too tidy to be natural. Behind it: a flood-proofed crib of lashed crates under oilcloth. Somebody engineered this. And half the crates are wrong — torn open **from the outside**, lids levered off, contents raked out, and the damage spans years. The snake has been raiding its own old supply line for food this whole time.

🎲 **Knowledge or Instinct 12** for the search, then hand each find to the right player:

- **The waybill — Tenson's, no roll needed.** A shipping paper wedged under a lid, half-eaten by damp. He reconstructs it: specimen designation **2H-3** *(the brand from Fletcher's journal)* · a list of stops running upriver, one of them past a **Ribbit spawning-town** *(watch Fletcher's player; say nothing)* · an imperial facility stamp with the name **redacted** · and one handwritten line: ***"LIVE — DO NOT MANIFEST."*** Then he says the big line, and Rung 2 lands: *"This wasn't an animal that wandered in. This was a shipment. It escaped."*
- **The sheds — Fletcher's.** Old castings near the crates, and these carry **cage-wear**: scale growth scarred in crate-straight lines. This is fresh snake-sign, so his phobia rules apply — he marks a Stress, and someone should anchor him by saying his name ([[fletcher-arc]]). His line: *"This thing spent years in a box."* And one more thing: across the newest cracked lid, a casting has been **arranged** — draped corner to corner, squared. Like a signature. Like it wanted the owner to know.
- **The jars — Leavy's.** One crate intact: **four sealed Consortium-bound reagent jars** (listed in [[items/loot-ledger|the loot ledger]] as the Seal-Jars). Stolen goods with plot strings — entrée, leverage, or the thing that settles her Lastlight debt. Watch the literal automaton wrestle with whether she's allowed to fence things. Let it be funny.
- **The kit — Isotta's.** In the driest corner, hidden in a hurry: a drowned man's oilskin wallet. A pay-tally with eleven marks — the twelfth never made. Bone dice. Half of an unsent letter. Her read, no roll needed: **nothing lingers here.** The swamp's dead cross clean; that's normal out here. But the pay-tally names where his wages sit: **the night berths, Sallowreach.** If any part of this man is still waiting anywhere, it's waiting there. *(His case opens back in town — see below. He's named now: **Émile Thibodeaux**, and his full case — the wages, the half-letter, and the banked witnessed-crossing jewel — is [[lantern-docket]] Case 3.)*

## Beat 4 — Leaving *(the load choice)*

Frame the choice, don't script it: **take everything** (slow and low in the water — and the Tessak watch them leave; are they looters or owners?) · **take only the paper and the wallet** (fast and clean; the jars keep for a second trip, if the cache survives one) · **spoil or mark the cache** (a blow struck at the smuggling route itself — Gault's operation will notice).

🎲 One **[[mire-complications]]** roll on the way out, loaded down. If they stayed past dusk, add the scavengers: 4–6 Mire Gar working the shallows. A skirmish, not a set piece — the cargo is the real enemy.

## Beat 5 — The sighting *(no roll — never cut this)*

This is the first time the party sees the snake, and it's a look, not a fight. The arc calls it the watch-beat; it's the climax of the hunt's first stage.

> Return leg. Low sun behind you, channel wide and easy — and Fletcher, your skin has been cold for a full minute and you haven't said anything, because there's nothing to point at. Then Remy stops humming.
>
> Off the port side, a boat-length out: a wake-ripple with no wind in it. And two heads come up. Slow. A body-length apart. Periscope-still, just above the water — and both of them are pointed at *you*.
>
> They don't close. They don't circle. They watch — long enough for everyone on this boat to understand that this is not an ambush. Then both heads go under at once, no ripple. And nothing follows. Nothing follows you all the way home.

**GM:** this is Delphine's Session 2 warning coming true on-screen — *"don't let the hunter become the hunted."* Don't add anything. Cut to the Mooring lights.

## Back in town *(next day — half the scene's value lives here; don't skip the first two)*

- **Cauvin, the fence at the night berths** *(a live thread from [[brin-sevarre]]'s file)*: he confirms what the waybill implies. Someone has paid standing coin for three years for word of the two-headed snake — **alive, never dead, triple for a trail.** He doesn't know who pays; the coin arrives clean, which is itself a tell. *(This sets up the Offer — Gault's recruitment pitch to Fletcher, [[fletcher-arc#The Offer]]. If the players guess the name themselves, smile and confirm nothing.)*
- **Isotta's case — the man at the berths.** The wallet's owner is exactly where his pay is: at the night berths, uncrossed, waiting on wages that stopped mattering three years ago. Once she settles him, his testimony: ***"It came out of the water already knowing where the latch was."*** And the hearsay: a crate broke upriver three years back, and the standing order has been paying ever since. **Then run the saved-up scene from [[isotta-arc]]** — the witnessed crossing and the threshold line, exactly as banked there.
- **The jars go to the Consortium.** The clerk holding Leavy's Lastlight marker would much rather have the jars ([[leavy-arc]]). One door, three prices — entrée, leverage, or debt paid. Let her negotiate like paperwork is a love language.
- **Brin gets the route.** Card and waybill on her map table: the smuggling road she's suspected for years, proven. She pays like people the Guild intends to hire again — then goes very still at the redacted stamp: *"That's not a smuggler's mark. That's a government's."*

## 😨 Fear menu *(scripted moments — say "I'm spending a Fear" out loud, move the token, then read)*

Today's dread rule is the opposite of the Wrong Skin trip: today the snake **appears** — once, free, scripted, at Beat 5 — and no amount of Fear buys a second look. Spend Fear on the smuggling route being older and better-run than the party, and on the swamp watching them loot it.

**The checkpoint** *(one spend max)*
- **1 · The taps:** two flat-palm taps on the water — from *behind* the boat this time. (Only if the taps fired on the S5 trip. Same signal, different sender. Don't explain.)
- **1 · The toll leans in:** one Tessak head surfaces beside the hull, close enough to touch, reads the newest scratch in the wood — the one the *party* made docking last week — and sinks. The ledger out here is current.

**The cache**
- **1 · Fresh splinters:** one lid's tear is new. Sap still bright, splinters still pale. It has been back **since the last rain** — and Fletcher knows exactly what rain-aged wood looks like.
- **2 · The island empties:** every bird leaves at once, one direction, no sound but wings. Nothing arrives. Nothing at all. *(Never confirm what passed within smell.)*

**The trip home**
- **1 · Low in the water:** loaded down, the boat answers slow — add an Experience to one hazard or gar attack, and narrate the *cargo*, not a curse.

**Never today:** a second sighting · any attack from the snake · a face for whoever pays Cauvin.

## Guardrails

- **The GM-facing truth, plainly:** this is Gault's supply route, Gault dropped the crate, Gault pays Cauvin. *The party* doesn't get that confirmed today — the file's coyness is for them, never for you.
- The snake **never attacks today.** The sighting is free, single, and final.
- Ghosts stay in town. The cache holds belongings, not a haunting.
- Never a solo snake scene for Fletcher — standing rule, [[fletcher-arc]].
- No lab, no dungeon. The facility is a redacted stamp and pressure, not a place to raid.

- **Order:** compartment (free) → checkpoint (Presence/Finesse 12) → search (Knowledge/Instinct 12) → load choice + trip home ([[mire-complications]]) → the sighting (free, scripted) → town follow-ups.
- **Fear:** menu above. Boat-trip voiceovers: reuse **The Water Between** from [[set-piece-the-wrong-skin]], one per leg.
- **Party jobs:** Fletcher the sheds · Tenson the paper · Leavy the jars · Isotta the wallet.
- **Voices:** Tessak scout — hissed trade-tongue, patient, unhurried · Remy — sunshine until the notches, then his wreck-tic (he counts the party out loud on night water) · Cauvin — a fence's friendliness; prices where opinions should be.
