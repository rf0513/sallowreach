# Leavy — Arc: Seven for a Secret

> **Status: ACTIVE** *(designed 2026-07-01).* Supersedes the "they did it to themselves" GM-truth on [[the-witch|Leavy's sheet]] — sheet updated with a pointer; every word of the *player-facing* text there stays true. Tuned for the player: the table's comic relief, playing a super-literal automaton, whose one true love is **making potions.** So the arc runs through the cauldron — comedy on the surface, grief in the sediment. Opener set piece: [[session-04-gloamcap-run]].

## The Answer (GM only — the whole arc in one paragraph)

Leavy was the chosen heir of **the Nine Hedges** — a northern coven of nine hedge-witches whose seats carry plant-names that outlive their wearers. Their teacher, **Feverfew**, the coven's head, was dying; on coronation eve Leavy was to drink the cup and take the waiting name: **Rue**. **Bindweed** — the elder witch passed over for the succession — struck first: a curse that killed the heir, scoured the mind, and dissolved the ninth seat entirely, its share of the coven's power flowing to the surviving eight. What Bindweed never learned: Leavy, contingency-planner to the bone, had years earlier built a clank vessel in their own workshop — *in case* — and Feverfew spent the last hours of her life pouring her student's soul into it. She died completing the transfer; that's why no maker was waiting when Leavy woke. The curse took the *story* of the life. It couldn't take the **craft** (practice isn't stored as story — the hands remember), and it couldn't reach the **taste-vault** (the memory-backup Feverfew keyed to the tongue). And it left one mercy nobody intended: to scrying and sending, Leavy now reads as a smooth blank. **Bindweed believes the heir was destroyed. The curse is the only thing hiding them.**

In plain table language: **Delphine was exactly right — the curse was cast to stop a coronation.** Leavy has spent three years investigating a crime committed against a stranger. The stranger is them.

- **Orna, Sessions 2–3:** *"Every witch needs a coven"* — and Leavy has none; plus the carrot: *help me get the gloamcap, and I'll help you get a familiar.*

## The Truth, in beats (GM)

- **The Nine Hedges.** Nine witches, nine seats, each seat named for a plant — the name passes to the successor at coronation. The naming pattern is itself a clue a mystery-player can chew: **witches of the old covens wear plant-names.** *(Rhymes with the spell-named wizards — [[Magistra Silence]] — different tradition, same music. Have Lorne or Orna say the pattern out loud once.)*
- **Feverfew** — coven head, Leavy's teacher. Warm, exacting, always right, cheated of nothing but time. In her last weeks — dying, and already watching Bindweed — she and Leavy distilled the essential memories into draughts keyed to Leavy's tongue: a backup no one else could ever read. She left the recipes deliberately **unfinished** — a locked vault. The final fixing needs Leavy's own trained hands and reagents she didn't live to gather. *(Why the draughts are incomplete, and why they're theft-proof.)*
- **Rue** — the seat Leavy was to take. **They never received their name.** This is why the player "hasn't settled on a name": lean in, don't rush it. The name is *in* the arc — it's waiting at the bottom of the coronation cup. Rue: the herb of grief. Also called **herb-of-grace.**
- **Bindweed** — passed over. Her grievance is real: senior, decades of service, and Feverfew chose a student over her. The curse is unforgivable; the wound under it isn't. She is owed *an answer* she was never given. She stays offscreen (guardrails below).
- **The vessel.** Old Leavy built it themselves — an alchemist's contingency body, with a tasting tongue, because a mixologist would never build a body that couldn't taste. The sheet's *"search ends at a mirror"* survives as a half-truth: the maker they've hunted for three years **was them.**
- **Seven** — the magpie familiar. It was there that night. It has been searching ever since, carrying what it could: three of Feverfew's recipe-slips and a hoard of small stolen keepsakes from the life. It cannot say any of this.

## The Hidden Explanation (one truth under every symptom)

Per house rule — costs, gifts, and one discoverable explanation that unifies everything:

**The curse scoured the story. It couldn't touch the practice, and it couldn't reach the tongue.**

- *A clank that can taste* (Great Mixologist) — the vessel was built by an alchemist for an alchemist; the tongue is the vault's keyhole.
- *Hands that know technique nobody taught them* — craft is stored as practice, not story; the wipe missed it.
- *Plant Encyclopedia intact, life gone* — same law: procedure survived, narrative burned.
- *Grief with no object* — the story is gone; the shape of it isn't.
- *Three years unfound by anyone from the old life* — the smooth blank. Scrying slides off them. **The curse is the camouflage.**

### Costs & Gifts *(homebrew — pitch to the player before play; `[VERIFY]` each vs the SRD)*

**Costs**
- **Memory bleed.** On a roll with Fear during a brew — or while handling old-life objects — a fragment surfaces sideways: Leavy's voice drops into someone else's cadence, a phrase in a dialect they don't know, hands finishing a gesture mid-air. Mark a Stress; give the table the fragment. A cost that pays the player in clues. `[VERIFY Stress economy]`
- **The old training overrides.** When Leavy deliberately brews *against* the old school's method, the hands fight them — disadvantage. The dead life has opinions. `[VERIFY]`
- **The name-hole.** They can't give a true name because they don't have one. Workings that hinge on names gutter around them — mostly flavor, occasionally a locked door.

**Gifts**
- **The hands remember.** Advantage on any brew that touches the old repertoire — *if they let the body lead.* (Also the clue channel: letting the hands lead is how the fold gets seen.) `[VERIFY]`
- **The tongue is the vault.** The impossible palate isn't skill; it's the keyhole. Memory-draughts work on Leavy and only Leavy.
- **The smooth blank.** Scrying, sendings, magical detection — Leavy isn't there. In-fiction plot armor with teeth, because **the day the curse breaks, the blank lifts.** Restored means *findable.* The cure has a price tag no potion lists.

## The Memory Engine — brewing as archaeology

The player's favorite thing at the table is the arc's delivery mechanism. Potions stop being "you made one, good job" and become digs.

**The scoured grimoire.** The formulary's binding is decades older than its oldest page — it was *their* grimoire, wiped by the same curse. Leavy has spent three years refilling their own emptied book without knowing. Paper remembers: under a drop of **gloamcap distillate**, scoured pages raise **ghost-ink** — burned lines coming up as pale residue. Not deliberate messages; *residue* — headers, marginalia, fragments. (First page rises in [[session-04-gloamcap-run]].) Standing dilemma: gloamcap is controlled and Orna needs it too — every page raised costs something, which makes the Alchemists' Consortium a supply line worth cultivating.

**The three draughts.** Seven carries three unfinished recipe-slips in Feverfew's hand. Each, completed and drunk, returns **one whole memory** — the arc's three jewels. Each is a session-sized brew: a rare-reagent hunt (a hook into a location) plus a dice-forward brewing set piece.

| Draught | The memory returned | The reagent hook |
|---|---|---|
| **I — The Lesson** | An ordinary teaching afternoon with Feverfew. Nothing happens. That's the devastation — warmth, tea going cold, being *known.* Establishes what was taken. | Something under [[The Verdant Margin]]'s care — earned through the Orna bond. |
| **II — The Warning** | Feverfew near the end: *"one of the eight is hungry."* No face shown. And the distilling itself — Leavy watching their own memories go into glass. | Deep-Mire reagent — the new boat's first personal voyage. |
| **III — The Eve** | Coronation night. The cup on the table, undrunk. The curse striking; Feverfew's last working beginning. **The flip: the coronation was theirs.** | Rarest — gate behind the Consortium relationship or a [[Marcel Dray]]-adjacent source; land it just before the Tidebreaker push. |

**The cup.** The coronation draught's recipe isn't on any slip — it's the ghost-ink at the grimoire's heart, raised page by costly page. Brewing it is the finale of the craft-line (endgame menu below).

**Brewing set-piece skeleton** *(reusable; difficulties are Tier-1 suggestions — `[VERIFY vs SRD]`):* **the Base** (Knowledge, ~11) → **the Turn** (Finesse, ~13; advantage if the hands lead — and the hands doing something unlearned *is* a clue firing) → **the Fixing** (Instinct or Knowledge, ~13). Fear anywhere = memory bleed (above). Failure never wastes the session: the brew spoils, but the *attempt* surfaces one fragment anyway. Rolls forward, always.

## Seven — the magpie

> **Full table sheet — appearance, the One Rule, and the read-aloud phrasebook for ad-hoc scenes: [[seven]].** The arc-level beats live here; grab lines from the sheet at the table.

*(Named by old Leavy. Current Leavy will name it something devastatingly literal — let them; everyone at the table adopts it; the true-name payoff lands harder for it.)*

**How it arrives:** Orna's calling-ritual doesn't summon it. It's been on the roof for days. It lands before the circle is finished, ignores the rite entirely, and starts doing Leavy's mise-en-place. Orna, delighted and appalled: new familiars don't do that. **That bond is old.** *(Full beat: [[session-04-gloamcap-run]].)*

**What it knows and how it tells:** everything, and it can't say any of it. It remembers in *behavior*, not narrative:
- Completes Leavy's old rituals — nudges a cup over at the old tea hour, always the left shoulder, preens hair that isn't there.
- **Charades and theft.** It shows; it never says. Per the clue rules, every Seven-clue gets a second channel — an NPC or PC says the plain-words version aloud.
- **The hoard — physical props, handed across the table, on the bird's schedule (your pacing dial):** a pressed sprig of **rue** (first — it has been trying to hand Leavy their name since day one) · a **nine-leafed iron pin** (coven token) · a **hazel wand snapped clean in half** · a **teacup shard**, glaze crazed with age · last and latest, a **small portrait frame, empty**, the picture long since weathered out.
- **The true name.** The Mire has its own magpie counting-rhyme — have a boardwalk kid or Brin say it over the bird some session: *"…six for a debt, and seven for a secret never to be told."* A mystery-player can get there. The day Leavy calls it **Seven, the bird answers** — the first true name recovered, and proof the life is recoverable.
- **Banked beat — the face** *(mid-arc; do not rush, do not force):* some session, Leavy shapeshifts into an incidental disguise — and Seven goes berserk with joy. Lands on them. Won't leave. They reconstructed their own old face out of muscle memory and wore it as a costume. The only witness who can confirm it can't speak. Play it exactly as sad as it is.

## The Reveal — two stages (house shape)

**Stage 1 — eerie, and funny about it.** Leavy knows one fact: the curse was cast *to stop a coronation.* A super-literal automaton will investigate that **outward** — whose coronation? What successions are pending? **Encourage this.** Give the investigation real NPC scenes; never wink. Let them interview baffled Sallowreach fishmongers about regional royalty and take meticulous notes. Meanwhile the true evidence piles up on the side: a school of technique living in their hands, a coven that famously lost its ninth seat, plant-named witches, a bird that already knew them. **The comedy is load-bearing** — every laugh at the literal investigation is rope for the gut-punch.

**Stage 2 — the flip.** *Coven headship is conferred with a cup, and the ceremony is called a coronation. The ninth seat dissolved the night an heir vanished. The heir's hands folded ash inward — like yours do.* The deduction must be **available from evidence one full session before Draught III confirms it** — this player's table-kin get to *solve*, never get told. No NPC hands over the conclusion; Delphine and Lorne confirm evidence only. Then Draught III shows them the cup on the table, undrunk. And the horror inverts, house-style, into something cleaner and worse: they weren't collateral in someone's coup. **They were the point.**

## Bindweed — guardrails

- **Never onscreen this campaign.** No stat block, no confrontation, no clock. Her pressure is *implication*: the blank is Leavy's only cover, and the cure costs the cover.
- **Her claim stays legitimate even though her remedy was murder.** If her voice ever reaches the table — in Draught II's edges, a ghost-ink margin — it should sound *reasonable.* That's what makes it frightening.
- **Disambiguation:** "Bindweed" deliberately rhymes with the campaign's binding/bound language — same music, entirely different tradition. She has nothing to do with the [[Tree Spirit]]'s binding. If the table cross-wires them, Orna kills it in one line: *"Bindweed's a strangler vine, dear. Chokes what it climbs. Fitting name, whoever picked it."*
- **Epilogue-only hook** (player's call, post-campaign): a dissolved seat can re-form if the heir lives. That's a war for another table.

## Comedy channels (play the player)

- The outward coronation investigation (above) — the arc's signature bit.
- **The Consortium loves paperwork, and so does Leavy.** The front-door approach to anything Consortium is forms — and the literal automaton completes them perfectly, in triplicate, to the clerks' mounting awe. Tenson redlines; Leavy *files.* Comedy duo whenever both share a scene.
- **Seven's thefts:** mostly shinies, occasionally load-bearing. The party learns to check the hoard whenever anything goes missing.
- **The provisional name.** Whatever literal thing Leavy names the bird, every NPC adopts it without blinking — Orna most of all.
- **Potion side effects on Fear:** comedic-sideways early, eerie later. The register shift is the two-stage reveal in miniature.

## The Tidebreaker — division of labor

- **Draughts are photographs; the spear is the whole album.** Three memories come back through craft. The *life* — the years, the faces, their name in their mother's voice — stays under the curse, and only [[the-tidebreaker|the Tidebreaker]] unbinds it.
- **The price with teeth:** the moment the curse breaks, the smooth blank lifts. Restored means findable. Say it out loud before they choose — Delphine reads it plain: *"The thing on you is also the thing hiding you. Cut it, and you're lit up for whoever's looking."*
- **Sequencing with [[Isotta]]:** two PCs, one spear, two cure-scenes. Stage them as separate, weighty beats — never a shared stat-change montage (per the [[the-tidebreaker]] GM note).

## Endgame — a menu, not a script

*(Per the no-hard-coded-endings rule: live options, chosen at the table.)*

- **Restored.** The spear breaks it; the life floods back; they take the name **Rue** and wear it openly — findable, and done hiding. The person the party knows and the person who died negotiate cohabitation. Play the first hour of having both.
- **The new coven.** They learn everything and choose *not* to be restored — the photographs are enough; the clank who did the living keeps the life. Then they brew the cup anyway — not as inheritance, as **founding.** A coronation in a swamp garden: Orna, Delphine, Seven, the party. Orna's *"every witch needs a coven"* was the answer all along — Leavy has been assembling one since Session 2.
- **Both roads north.** Restored *and* the seat stirs — an epilogue hook, never an onscreen war.
- **The unwritten one** the player invents once they hold all the pieces.

## What this arc deliberately doesn't do

- **No lore-conduit relapse.** The Nine Hedges are personal canon from far away — zero pipe into the Turn, the binding, or Sallowreach's secrets. Leavy's mystery is *Leavy.*
- **No villain fight.** Bindweed is pressure, not a boss. The campaign hasn't the time, and the arc is stronger for it.
- **No collision** with the tether or with Isotta's spear scene — cure beats staged separately; the shared tether still holds everyone to the finale.
- **No memory dumps.** Nobody narrates Leavy's past at them. Memories arrive only through the tongue; knowledge only through deduction.

## Secrets & Clues — the coronation file

> **As played, S4:** the Consortium leg is done early — Leavy holds the **gloamcap distillate**, and the price was her promise to deliver **Orna's written Lastlight recipe** (debt live; Orna doesn't know yet). The Calling/familiar beats (4–7 of [[session-04-gloamcap-run]]) carry to S5. Table notes: **Nature's Tongue on the cub — now named Whimsy — was her highlight of the night** (the tied-up girl + sister-cub reveal was hers), and she's openly hungry for the deep-Mire boat trip. Feed both.

*(Ten, abstract from discovery, per the Lazy DM rules. Track shown/said in the set-piece ledgers.)*

1. Leavy's hands perform brewing technique no one taught them, and the style has a name and a home.
2. Witches of the old covens wear plant-names; the name belongs to the seat, and the seat outlives the wearer.
3. A northern coven called the Nine Hedges lost its ninth seat three years ago — it dissolved overnight, and its power flowed to the eight that remain.
4. Coven headship is conferred with a cup, and the ceremony is called a coronation.
5. The heir of the Nine Hedges vanished the night before their coronation and is presumed unmade.
6. The formulary's binding is decades older than its oldest page, and paper remembers what ink was burned away.
7. A witch's memories can be brewed — taste is the deepest vault — but these recipes were left unfinished on purpose, and only one tongue can receive them.
8. The magpie did not arrive in Sallowreach; it *caught up* — it has been searching for its witch for three years.
9. To every scrying eye in the world, Leavy does not exist — and that is the only reason they are still alive.
10. The witch who dissolved the seat was owed an answer she never got; her grievance was real, even though her remedy was murder.
