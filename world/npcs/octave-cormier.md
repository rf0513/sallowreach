# Octave Cormier

**Role:** The Pale Room's bar fixture — the man with the lifetime tab; the only person who ever beat [[Marcel Dray]] and got paid in full
**Location:** [[the-pale-room|The Pale Room]] — third stool from the end, every night the room is open
**Faction:** Independent (the house's living exhibit)
**Tier:** 1

*The GM function in one line: the party is in this building to win something enormous from Marcel. Octave won. Ask him how it went.*

## Appearance

Sixties, broad, gone soft the way a strong man goes soft when nothing has asked anything of him in a decade. Dressed like a man going to the same party for eleven years — good clothes, out of date, immaculately kept. In front of him: a row of full glasses, lined up neat as pins, untouched. Beside them, a small stack of coins the bar will not take.

## Personality & Mannerisms

Loud, warm, delighted to have company, and the best commentary in the room — he's watched this floor longer than anyone but Marcel. **The tic: he quotes odds on everything**, including feelings, including yours. *"Three to one she folds. Even money you ask me why."* Every third sentence has a number in it.

**Play the tell:** he orders drinks all night and never touches them — they're free, and that's the problem. The coins are for tips; the house won't take those either. The little standoff on the bar top is eleven years old.

**One warning for the table:** Octave is a commentator, not a confidant. Everything you tell him is said back to the room at volume, with odds attached. Talking to him is broadcasting.

## What He Wants

**To owe Marcel something again.** He has spent years trying to give the winnings back — offering double the worth of every drink he never paid for. Marcel declines, without heat, every time: *"Our business is concluded."* Not cruelty — code: the day one settled account reopens, every settled account in the city becomes negotiable, and the whole machine he runs is built on closed meaning closed. Octave is the one man in Sallowreach trying to force money **into** the Pale Room's ledger, and the house treats him like a man trying to pay twice for a funeral.

## What He's Hiding

What he actually won. The floor tells it a dozen ways — a barge of imperial silver, a night with death itself, Marcel's real name. The truth, which he'll only say late, to someone who lost tonight and took it well: **he named his own prize — "nothing in your house will ever cost me anything" — and Marcel asked him, once, plainly: *"Are you certain that is what you want?"*** Octave laughed and said yes. He was twenty-two hands into the best night of his life and thought he'd won paradise. It took him about three years to understand that cost is the thing that makes anything worth wanting. The one question was the only warning Marcel owed him, and Marcel gave it. Octave has never once claimed otherwise — that's the part that keeps him at the bar.

## The Night He Won *(the story he'll tell anyone; buy him nothing, he can't accept it anyway)*

Eleven years ago — one card table in the back of a distillery, before there were tablecloths, when "the Pale Room" was a joke about Marcel's complexion that nobody made twice. Octave was a barge-master then, flush and fearless, and he beat Marcel at Wake straight up. Fair game. Marcel paid the same night, completely, and has never sat a table with him since.

> "You want to know what losing looks like on him? I'll tell you for free — everything I do is for free. It looked like **gratitude.** He thanked me. Shook my hand — cold as a wet ledger — and thanked me. I've had eleven years to work out what he was thanking me for, and I'll tell you where I've landed: I'm the best advertisement he ever bought. The house pays out. Look how *paid* I am."

*(GM — Octave's theory is his bitterness talking, and it's wrong. The truth is simpler and greyer: it was the best hand of cards Marcel ever watched, honestly played, and he said so. There was no scheme. Marcel asked his one question, paid in full, and moved on. The ruin was in the wish, and the wish was Octave's.)*

## What He's Good For *(GM — three uses, all earned by sitting down)*

- **The floor read.** He knows every regular's tell and narrates them cheerfully with odds — including [[oswin-cray|the ward wizard's]] weekly game and exactly how deep in he is. Real intel, delivered loudly. *(Anything he says, the room hears — the floor files it with [[camille-babineaux|Camille]] like everything else.)*
- **The tutorial.** The only man alive who has negotiated Marcel from the winning side, and he gives the meta-rule away as bar wisdom: *"His word is iron once it's given. Before it's given? That's the only part of the man that moves. Four to one he offers you the first shape of the deal tonight. Even money you take it. Don't. And whatever you win off him — price it before you pocket it. I didn't."*
- **The exhibit.** He is the campaign's closed-account theme in a mortal key — a settled man, still breathing, hollowed out by having nothing left open ([[marcel-dray]] is the same shape supernaturally; [[isotta-arc|Isotta]] is its opposite, the account that won't close). If she looks at him with her sight: nothing. Ordinary, living, fine. The hollow in Octave isn't visible from the spirit side. That's what makes it frightening.

## Key Relationships

- [[Marcel Dray]] — the deal that made them both: Octave the proof, Marcel the payer. They have not played since, and Marcel has never once mentioned the tab. *(Whether Marcel keeps him as advertisement, warning, or the nearest thing he has to a peer — deliberately unanswered.)*
- [[camille-babineaux|Camille Babineaux]] — the one regular she's never had to read; he posts himself. He calls her *"the house's best odds."* She lets his coins sit on the bar and has never told him why that's kind.
- [[the-mooring|The Mooring]] — his old barge life; men he used to command still drink to him like he's dead. He knows the water-freight world of a decade ago, which sometimes matters more than the current one.

## Dialogue Hook

A PC takes the next stool. He doesn't look over:

> "Two to one you're here about the man in the back. No — don't tell me. I like the suspense. It's the only stake I've got left. Sit, order anything, it's on me — it's *always* on me, that's the tragedy, but it's a long one and you look thirsty."

## Stat Block

None needed. Octave hasn't been a danger to anything but a buffet in years. If it ever matters: Tier 1 social presence, one Experience worth borrowing — *Knows the Table +3.*
