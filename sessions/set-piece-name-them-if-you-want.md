# Set Piece — Name Them If You Want *(Gault, Encounter 1 — the worked basin)*

*The poacher, up close, at last. **His stats and canon live in [[gault]]** (§Encounter 1, §The Mire-Touched Quality); **the boat-fight rules, if it ever comes to that, live in [[set-piece-boat-ambush]]**. This file is only the staging. Built 2026-07-26. **Default placement: [[set-piece-the-wrong-skin]] Beat 2.5** — outbound, right after the lantern-ray voiceover.*

---

## 🧭 In Plain Words *(the whole scene — no jargon)*

The party is on a day trip into the swamp. Twenty minutes ago they watched a line of huge glowing rays glide under their boat, and their guide took his hat off without saying anything. Now they come round a bend into the bright shallow pool those rays were heading for — and there's a man standing knee-deep in it, netting them. His name is Gault. He's a poacher who's spent eight years stripping this swamp of its living treasures and selling them in sealed jars to collectors in far-off cities. The party has seen him twice before, both times across a room, never up close, never spoken to. He does not stop working when they arrive, and he will answer every question they ask honestly and calmly while he finishes. **He is not going to fight them** — four armed strangers over one pool of fish is a bad trade, and he knows where the other pools are. The only real question is what the party does with the ninety seconds before he's loaded and gone.

## 🚨 Run It In Sixty Seconds

| | |
|---|---|
| **Is there a fight?** | **No — not unless they start one, and even then he leaves.** He never draws first. See §If They Go At Him. |
| **The clock** | **4 containers left to seal.** Every beat they spend arguing instead of acting, one more gets sealed. **Say the number out loud.** |
| **What they can grab** | **The net** (a number of rays saved) **or him** (nothing — he leaves anyway). Not both. |
| **The one roll** | The net — **Finesse or Strength 12**, group. Graded as *how many get out.* |
| **How it ends** | He steps into the skiff, says the provenance line, and **looks back once.** |
| **The floor** | If they freeze, **Remy** goes into the water alone and cuts the net open with his hands. Somebody will help him. |

⚠ **The scene cannot fail. It can only cost.** Don't protect them from that.

## 👁️ What You See *(read it — spoken, unhurried)*

> You come round the bend and the channel opens out into a basin. Shallow, wide, bright — it's the one place out here where the sun gets all the way to the bottom.
>
> There are three corks in a line across the mouth of it. Floats. Spaced out like fence posts.
>
> There's a skiff pulled up on the roots. Flat-bottomed, low, containers strapped down in rows.
>
> And the light's wrong. All of it's crowded into one corner of the water, up against something, moving too fast.
>
> There's a man standing in it to his knees, back half to you, doing something with his hands. He hasn't looked up.
>
> Remy stops poling.

**Then, before anyone speaks:** he finishes sealing the container he's holding. Wax, a press, a tap. *Then* he looks up. He counts them, counts the boat, and goes back to work.

**That's the introduction. They are not an emergency.**

*(Sound note: rays don't scream. There is no thrashing, no cruelty theater. The only noise in this scene is wax and water. **The horror is that it's quiet and businesslike.**)*

## ⏳ The Clock — Four Containers

He has four left to seal and stow. **Tick one every time the party spends a beat without touching the water** — arguing, threatening, demanding, or asking a question he answers. Say it flat, like he's not doing anything at all:

> *"That's three."*  … *"That's two."*

At zero he steps into the skiff.

⭐ **This is not a punishment and it must not read as one.** Every tick buys them a true answer — and he gives very good answers. Talking is free for him; outrage is free for him. He is simply faster than their anger. *(Same engine as Adaline: the horror is that he's satisfied. Same rule as Marcel: no tricks, no gotchas — he just doesn't stop working.)*

**What he'll tell them, honestly, while he works:**

| They ask…                     | He says                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What are you doing?           | "Working." *[beat]* "Basin's good this time of year. They come up shallow to warm."                                                                                 |
| Do you know what those *are*? | "Lantern rays. Eleven so far. Two of them worth the trip on their own."                                                                                             |
| What are they *for*?          | ⭐ The provenance line *(canon)*: "They stop glowing after a few days in the jar. Doesn't matter. Collectors aren't buying the glow. They're buying the provenance." |
| That's wrong / how can you    | "The Mire was here before your city and it'll be here after. It doesn't need your protection." *(canon)*                                                            |
| Who's buying?                 | "Nobody you'd know." *[seals one]* "That's the point of them."                                                                                                      |
| Does anybody let you do this? | ⭐ His thesis *(canon)*: "You can't touch a tree in this swamp without the whole town knowing." *[a container goes down in the rack]* "Nobody counts the rays."      |
| Do you know who we are?       | He looks at them properly, for the first time. "No." *[back to work]*                                                                                               |
| Stop. / You're not leaving.   | **See the box below.**                                                                                                                                              |

## ✋ Remy *(the half that's never been written — this is the reason to run the scene)*

**Mire-Remy, per his card: he goes still, drops volume, and uses only imperatives.** His grief comes out as arithmetic — which is the same shape Old Plenty's will take four hours later, at the rookery, in the same day. Don't point at the rhyme. Just let both of them count.

**1 · When they come round the bend**, before anyone else speaks — quiet, not to anybody:

> "Three floats." *[beat]* "He's got three in."

**2 · The name — the whole scene turns on this.** He starts to say one and stops, because there are strangers in the boat and it's embarrassing:

> "That's Old L—"

*(He has names for individual rays. He has never told anyone. He is not going to finish that sentence.)*

**And Gault finishes the thought for him, without looking up:**

> ⭐ *"Name them if you want. Makes the inventory easier to track."*

**He means it practically. That is the worst part.** Do not have him smirk. Do not have him pause for effect. It's a scheduling remark.

**3 · The only demand he makes,** flat, three words, completely ignored:

> "Pull them."

**4 · Afterward, if anyone asks him why he didn't do more:**

> "Fourth time."

*(Canon: three confrontations in eight years, each one Remy unable to do anything. This is the fourth. He doesn't explain it and he doesn't want comfort.)*

## ✊ The Two Grabs *(pick one — their hands are full)*

Both things are visible from the boat. **Nobody has to explain the trade; the geometry does it.** The net's in the water in front of them; the skiff's on the root shelf past him.

- 🐟 **The net** → they get a **number**. Say it out loud.
- 🧍 **Him** → they get **nothing**. He was leaving either way, and the hands cut the float line while they do it.

**🎲 The net — Finesse or Strength 12, group** (one PC rolls, the rest **Help with Hope**). ⭐ *Spend a Hope for **+2**: Fletcher's **Swamp Specialist** (this is his water and his kit — unlike the town session, it fits cleanly) or Isotta's **Animal Whisperer** (keeping eleven panicking animals from tangling it worse).*

| Roll | How many get out |
|---|---|
| **Success w/ Hope** | **All eleven.** The line comes open clean and the basin lights up from underneath again, all at once. Nobody in the boat says anything for a moment. |
| **Success w/ Fear** | **Eight.** And **spend the Fear:** from the skiff, Gault tells them plainly that hauling them like that will kill two more by morning. He is right, and he isn't being cruel — he's the only person here who's done it before. |
| **Failure w/ Hope** | **Four**, and Remy gets those out with his hands after Gault's gone. It costs them the light and most of the rest. |
| **Failure w/ Fear** | **Two.** The float line goes under with the others. **Spend the Fear:** the last of the light goes down into the dark under their own boat, still moving. |
| **They never roll at all** | **Three.** Remy, alone, in the water, with a knife. |

⚠ **The Bilge does not tick here.** It's a passage clock; this isn't a passage. Keep them separate ([[the-living-mire]] §Travel With Teeth).

⚠ **There is no roll that stops him leaving. Don't offer one.** A die on an outcome that can't happen is a lie to the table.

## 🗡️ If They Go At Him

He doesn't draw. He doesn't posture. He uses **one** feature to break contact — **Weighted Net**, or **Into the Water** on whoever's closest — and steps into the skiff. The two hired hands never fight at all; they're port muscle paid by the run *(**Wending Runner Lackey**, [[sallowreach-roster]])*, and they pole off.

**His answer, delivered as a plain reply and not a taunt** — he does not stop working while he says it:

> "I'm not fighting four people over one basin." *[a container goes into the rack]* "There's more water."

**He isn't backing down and he isn't afraid. He just has more basins.** Their threat is irrelevant because the thing they're defending isn't scarce to him.

⭐ **If somebody lands a hit — let it land.** He takes it, looks at it, doesn't retaliate, and gets in the boat on schedule. A man who takes an arrow and leaves at the same speed is worse than one who fights back. His card already has the line for it *(the only honest expression they'll ever see on him)*:

> "…Huh. You're a *cost* now. That's new."

*(This quietly feeds [[set-piece-the-bad-season]]: he now knows these four will shoot. Part of why he eventually stops asking and starts taking. **Guardrail holds — he learns they're dangerous, not that Fletcher has a trail.**)*

**If it does turn into a real fight:** run it on [[set-piece-boat-ambush]] §The Boat Is the Battlefield. Gault **Difficulty 16** ([[gault]] §Stat Block). One round, maybe two, then he's gone. ⚡ **Fear spend worth having:** *Into the Water* shoves a PC into the basin — **into the net**, tangled up with the rays they came to save.

## 👀 One Free Read Each *(no rolls — hand these out)*

- **Tenson** — the containers carry the **same maker's stamps** Brin named at the dock four hours ago. The clue closes on screen, free. Then the knife: he reaches for the procedure and *there is no procedure.* No permit exists to be violated. There is no column for this.
- **Isotta** — the basin is glowing and **his eyes hold the light a half-second too long.** This is the S4 Ranging House read that never fired, in perfect lighting. *(Never explained. Not by him, not by the text — [[gault]] §The Mire-Touched Quality.)*
- **Fletcher** — old net-scars on the roots and sunk floats from previous seasons. **He's worked this basin before.** Year after year.
- **Leavy** — she reads what the containers are *for*: waxed, sealed, packed for a long journey to somebody with a shelf.

## 😨 Fear Menu *(performed — 2–3 spends max; it's a short beat)*

- **1 · The wax** *(extra move)* — he seals another one mid-sentence, while somebody's still talking. **That's a free tick.**
- **1 · Better placed** *(extra move)* — the two hands on the far bank shift. Not aggressive. Just *better*. They've stood in this exact spot before.
- **1 · Wrong to the Mire** *(the tell)* — something big moves through the basin and the party's boat rocks. It goes **around** him. Doesn't touch him. Nobody comments and neither do you.
- **1 · The look back** *(interrupt, as he goes)* — he stops the skiff. Turns. Looks at the four of them for three full seconds — reading, filing, pricing. Then goes. *(Canon: he looks back once.)*

**Never:** he doesn't kill anyone · he doesn't gloat · he doesn't threaten the party · nothing in the net screams.

## 🎒 Walking Out *(log these — two take seconds, one pays off later)*

- ⭐ **The number of rays that got out.** Write it down. **[[set-piece-the-bad-season]] empties this basin completely** — whatever they saved here is exactly what he takes later, and the GM gets to say the number back to them on the worst night of the campaign.
- **Did anyone touch him?** If yes, he knows they'll shoot.
- **Did Remy go into the water alone, or did someone go in with him?** It's a live dial on the campaign's first friendship.
- **How many answers did they buy?** (i.e. how far the container clock ran.)

---

## 🚫 Guardrails

- **No fight by default. He never draws first.** If they start one he disengages — he is *too smart to fight in the middle of the Mire*, and that's business, not fear.
- **He never lies. Not once.** Every ugly thing he says is a practical statement of fact ([[marcel-dray]] §Playing Him Grey, same register in a colder key).
- **No mustache-twirling.** Plain, once, unhurried. He does not argue and does not explain himself. The table's hate is earned by what he says *calmly*.
- **He does not learn Fletcher has a trail.** He clocks four capable strangers and a guide he already knows.
- **No roll stops him leaving.**
- **Remy stays useless, and the party watches it.** Don't rescue him with a lucky beat.

## 📝 GM note — the basin's name *(one decision, thirty seconds)*

[[gault]] §The Bad Season calls this place **the brightfin basin** — but *brightfin* is canonically a small hand-sized companion critter that punt families keep, not a ray. **The reconciliation that makes both files true, and makes the place hurt more:** the basin is *named* for the brightfins that live in it — it's where families have always gone to get theirs — and the lantern rays come up into the same shallow water to warm. So Gault is netting rays in the pool where half the city got their childhood pet.

**Take it or cut it** — nothing in this scene depends on it. If you cut it, the basin is just an unnamed bright shallow and Gault's file gets a one-word fix instead.
