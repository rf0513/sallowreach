# The Living Mire

**Tier 1 · Traversal** — *the swamp itself, anywhere. The default card for any scene on the black water.*

> ⭐ **THE table card.** Of all thirteen environments this is the one to physically bring. **Restructured 2026-07-24** so it survives a cold open: the panic panel is first, the teaching material is last. Mechanics swept vs [[STATUS]] §SRD Facts.

---

## 🚨 They're On The Boat — Run It From Here

### First, buy yourself thirty seconds

Say this to the table. It stalls, it's in character, and **it hands you both numbers you need:**

> "Before you push off — Remy's going to want to know where you're headed, and how long you think you'll be gone."

Their answer tells you *known channels or deep* and *day or night*. That's the whole setup. Now:

### The four lines

| | |
|---|---|
| **1 · The number** | Remy aboard **11** · without him, or after dark **13** · deep water **14, at disadvantage** |
| **2 · The Bilge** | daylight **6** · night or deep **4** · deep at night **3** — draw that many boxes on scrap paper |
| **3 · The rule** | **before every roll, say out loud what a miss costs** |
| **4 · The tick** | fail+Fear **3** · fail+Hope **2** · success+Fear **1** · success+Hope **0** · crit **0, and rub one out** |

**The costs, in order — take the next one down each time:**

> **time** → **a named thing gets wet** → **1 Stress each** → **Lost** → **the boat**

**Say the water level every single tick:** *ankles → shins → knees → thwarts.*

**On any Fear result:** draw from [[mire-complications]]. Every entry's bold **Teeth:** line is the price, already decided for you.

**Bilge full = the Swamping.** The boat floods. **Nobody drowns.** The trip is over, and **the Mire takes one thing** — whoever was holding it says what it was.

*That's the entire system. Everything below is why it works.*

## ⚔️ Travel With Teeth *(the detail behind the panel)*

**The problem it fixes:** in Session 5 you ran deep-Mire night navigation — the boat took damage, the party took Stress — and it played softer than the danger you'd promised. Here's why. You rolled, they missed, and *then* you decided what it cost. Deciding after the dice always produces a soft answer, because you're inventing a punishment for people you like. So decide before.

> **Say the cost out loud before anyone rolls.** *"If you miss this, the lantern goes in the water."* Then roll. Never invent the cost after seeing the die.

That one habit is 80% of the fix. The rest is bookkeeping.

### The number *(it already existed — it just wasn't on this card)*

The stat block's flat **12** is for *fighting the terrain* — sprinting, footing, hauling someone free. **Travel is a different roll**, with its own ladder ([[the-wending]]):

| Water | Difficulty |
|---|---|
| Known channels, **Remy aboard** | **11** — this is what a guide is *for* |
| Known channels, no guide **or** after dark | **13** |
| **Deep Mire** | **14, at disadvantage** ([[the-deep-mire]]) |
| Within reach of the **tether edge** | as above, **+ the sickness rider** below |

⚠ **Don't stack.** Deep water is *either* 14-at-disadvantage *or* the Wending card's flat 15 — **never both.** The SRD prefers disadvantage over raising the number, so use 14 + disadvantage and leave the 15 alone.

**The sickness rider** *(S5 canon, as played)*: near the line every PC is sick and weakening. All rolls at disadvantage, 1 Stress per hour, worsening. **They cannot cross.** That's a wall, not a hard roll — don't offer a number for it.

### The clock — the Bilge

[[the-wending]] already gives you a **Progress** countdown (*The Shifting Road*, 4). Nothing ever tracked the **cost.** The Bilge is that missing half — a consequence countdown, sized before you push off (**6 / 4 / 3**, per the panel).

**⭐ Give it a face.** The Bilge is *water in the bottom of the boat*, and you narrate the level every tick. Boxes on scrap, loops, coins, matchsticks — anything they can see. An abstract clock is furniture; a rising waterline is dread. Same lesson as the grace cord: they have to watch it tighten.

*(Tick values are the SRD dynamic countdown's consequence side — verified, [[STATUS]] §SRD Facts.)*

### What a tick costs

| # | The cost |
|:--:|---|
| 1 | **Time.** You arrive late and something at the far end moved. *Name what.* |
| 2 | **Wet.** One named thing is soaked — powder, rope, food, Leavy's chalk, Tenson's report. Name it *before* the roll. |
| 3 | **The body.** 1 Stress each. Deep water, make it 2. |
| 4 | **The route.** **Lost** — no Long Rest until it clears ([[the-wending]] §Lost). |
| 5 | **The boat.** *Root-Choke* or *The Hinge* from [[the-wending]], fired now. |
| **Full** | **The Swamping** — below. |

### The Swamping *(pre-decided so you never improvise it)*

**Nobody drowns.** This is a cost, not a TPK.

- The boat floods or goes over. Everyone's in black water, in the dark, hauling.
- **The trip is over.** They limp home wet, or spend the night out here — and the night has its own draws ([[mire-complications]]).
- **The Mire takes one thing.** Whoever was holding it says what it was. Take the one that hurts.

**And the door:** what the Mire takes is **not gone.** Every current out here pulls one way, and everything it keeps ends up in the same basin — [[set-piece-the-long-anchorage|the Long Anchorage]]. A loss with an address is a loss the players will chase.

### Guardrails

- **The Bilge is per passage.** Resets on arrival. Never carries between sessions, and it is **not** a doom timer — Iron Rule 4 stands. Teeth bite *inside* the scene; nothing hangs over the campaign.
- **One clock at a time.** If a set piece brought its own (the Anchorage's tide), run that one instead.
- **Don't double-charge** — one Fear, one event ([[mire-complications]]).
- **Teeth are for trips that matter.** A milk run is still texture. Bite on the trips they chose.

## Stat Block

**Impulses:** Swallow whatever stops moving · blur every landmark · shelter those who respect it · take back what's taken.

**Difficulty:** 12 *(for fighting the terrain: sprint across it, keep your footing, haul someone free. **Travel uses the ladder above.**)*

**Potential Adversaries:** [[mire-bestiary#Mire Gar|Mire Gar]] pack · [[mire-bestiary#Bloodmidge Swarm|Bloodmidge Swarm]] · glass snake (SRD) · a [[sallowreach-roster#Tessak Diver|Tessak]] in the water. *The Mire rarely needs an adversary — the water and the dark are the threat.*

## Features *(the Mire's own moves — use whichever fits the beat)*

**🟫 The Mire Underfoot — Passive** *(the baseline)*
No solid ground — only muck, roots, waist-deep black water. Any roll to **move fast, chase, or fight on your feet** is against **Difficulty 12**. On a failure you don't necessarily fall — but the swamp has you a beat longer than you wanted.

**🌿 Reeds, Mist, and Black Water — Passive** *(the toolbox — point this one out loud)*
The Mire hides things, including the party. A PC who stops and goes still rolls **Agility (12)** to become **Hidden**; rolls against a Hidden creature have **disadvantage**, lost the moment they're seen, move into the open, or attack. A PC can also **drop under the black water** to break line of sight entirely.

**🕳️ The Ground Gives — Action** *(the Mire takes a turn)*
What looked like ground is bog. Targeted PC makes an **Agility reaction roll (12)** or sinks to the waist — **Restrained** (can't move, can still act) until they or an ally spends an action to haul free (**Strength 12**).

**🩸 Blood in the Water — Reaction** *(the Mire answers you)*
When a PC spills blood, makes a loud splash, or **rolls with Fear**, the swamp responds: bloodmidges boil up, or something downstream wakes and turns toward the noise. Bring an adversary to **Close range**, or make every nearby roll **Vulnerable** to the bites.

**🌫️ The Mire Closes In — Action · Fear** *(the escalation)*
**Spend a Fear.** Mist drops to arm's length, water climbs. Any attack beyond **Very Close has disadvantage**, and **one PC is cut off** from the group, alone in the white.

### Temporary clocks live here too

Any **Countdown** you spin up in the swamp belongs to this card — like a **Breath Countdown (3)** for a PC dragged under (ticks each action; at 0 they mark Stress per action). Printed or improvised, both are "the environment acting." *(SRD parallel: the official "Raging River" environment runs exactly this way.)*

## First time you use an environment *(the S1 teaching plan — never fired yet)*

Still worth doing on the next boat day, **but not during a freakout.** Only run this when you have breathing room.

> **Say to the players once:** *"In Daggerheart the **place** is a character too. The swamp has its own stat block — a Difficulty and a set of moves, same as a monster. It can act against you. But you can also **use** it: cover to hide in, water to vanish under, terrain you can turn on your enemies. When I do something and say 'that's the Mire,' that's this card."*

Then teach it in **two moves, mid-scene:**

1. **Show the toolbox first.** When a PC wants to escape something, offer **Reeds, Mist, and Black Water** — "you can go under and they lose you; roll Agility." Let a player *win* using the environment. That's the hook.
2. **Then show it bites back.** Later, spend a Fear on **The Mire Closes In** and say out loud *"that's a Fear — the swamp is taking a turn."* Now they have the whole loop: the place helps, the place hurts, and Fear is the dial.

After that, the fancy environments just feel like *this, with a theme.*
