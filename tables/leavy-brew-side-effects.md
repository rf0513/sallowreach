# Leavy's Brews — the Sideways Table (d12)

*For any potion Leavy brews or serves. Fire it on a **roll with Fear** during a brew, as a GM move when a potion gets drunk at a dramatic moment, or whenever "the potion works" needs to be more fun than a stat line. The potion always still works — these ride on top.*

**The register slide (the whole trick):** the table runs comedy at the top, eerie at the bottom. **Early arc: roll d6** (entries 1–6). **After the flip ([[leavy-arc#The Reveal — two stages (house shape)|stage 2]]): roll d6+6.** Mid-arc, roll the full d12 and own what you get. The table darkening *is* Leavy's arc happening in the potions.

**Guardrails:** no entry ever returns a whole memory (memories come only through the draughts — [[leavy-arc#The Memory Engine — brewing as archaeology|engine rule]]) and none advances the flip without you choosing it. Fragments, sensations, gestures — never the story.

---

1. **The shine.** The drinker glows faintly for a scene — that oil-slick magpie iridescence, black-green-blue when they move. Seven finds them *fascinating* and will not leave them alone. Neither will moths.

2. **Flavor-speech.** For a few minutes the drinker can only describe things by taste. The guard up ahead is "burnt toast and old coins." The plan is "under-salted." Weirdly, everyone knows exactly what they mean.

3. **The lean.** Every plant within thirty feet slowly leans toward the drinker for the rest of the scene. Indoors, the cut flowers turn. At the Verdant Margin this is a *standing ovation*, and Orna takes notes.

4. **Sympathy dose.** The nearest animal gets the effect too, full strength. The owlbear cub is now also invisible. Seven is now also very fast. Nobody budgeted for this.

5. **The delay.** Nothing happens. Nothing happens. Nothing happ— it kicks in at the least convenient moment of the next scene, at full strength, GM's choice of timing. (Tell the player it worked. Don't say when.)

6. **The hum.** The drinker comes up humming two notes they've never heard. It's catchy. By nightfall the whole party's humming it. *(GM: it's from the old life — Seven goes very still the first time it hears it. Comedy that's secretly a clue; say nothing.)*

7. **Better than it should be.** The potion is *excellent* — one shade beyond what the recipe can produce. There's a technique in the residue Leavy has no memory of performing. Their hands did a step alone while they were talking. `[mark it in the casebook of anyone paying attention]`

8. **The steam.** For one second the steam off the cup holds a shape — a row of hedges? a long table? a hand reaching to steady the pour? — and it's gone before anyone can say which.

9. **The reflection.** In the surface of the liquid, Leavy's reflection stirs with the wrong hands. Flesh hands. Just for a beat. Only the drinker sees it, and only if they look.

10. **Someone else's afternoon.** The drinker gets a flash that isn't theirs — tea going cold on a windowsill, a woman's laugh from another room, the smell of a garden after rain. Warm, specific, gone. It wasn't the drinker's. *(It was Leavy's. Never confirm. Mark a Stress if the drinker chases it.)* `[VERIFY Stress]`

11. **The old cadence.** Leavy answers the next question in a voice that isn't quite theirs — someone else's rhythm, someone else's phrasing — a full sentence before they catch it. Mark a Stress; give the table the sentence. *(This is the memory bleed from [[leavy-arc#Costs & Gifts|the arc]], formalized — make the sentence count.)* `[VERIFY Stress]`

12. **The gesture.** As the brew finishes, Leavy's hands — unbidden — raise the vessel in a slow, formal, two-handed lift. A ceremony's gesture. Held one breath. Set down. And Seven **bows.** *(The coronation cup-raise. No one at the table knows what they just watched. Use once, late, and let the silence do it.)*
