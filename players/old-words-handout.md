# Handout — The Old Words *(Caspar's transcription + GM key)*

*The chant of the Celebration, at last written down — the physical page [[caspar-renne|Caspar]] hands over (or leaves in the archive, or sends in a dead man's handwriting) with the instruction that pays off at the Heart: **"ask it to read my fairytales back to me."** Print the boxed section as the prop; the GM key below it is the load-bearing part. This is the object that travels the whole campaign: planted in S4, argued over at feasts, and finally translated by the spirit itself at [[reveal-read-alouds]] §4.*

**Calibration guardrail (same law as [[the-first-maintainers-journal|Vane's journal]]):** the prop must never contain the words *binding, spell, prison, trapped* — and neither may Caspar's notes conclude anything. His marginalia get to be *baffled*, never right. The answers stay locked behind the Heart.

## The Prop *(print this — one aged page, close-written, forty years of different inks in the margins)*

> **THE OLD WORDS — as sung at the Celebration**
> *transcribed from performance, C. Renne — collated over many years; punctuation mine; meaning, regrettably, nobody's*
>
> **First movement** *(the welcome verse — the one the children learn first)*
> *Oroth sela, oroth sela,*
> *vhal-dou kess ammé.*
> *Oroth sela.*
>
> **Second movement** *(eight bars; identical in every family's rendering — the only movement that never varies)*
> *Sela. Ressa.*
> *Kess-dou né passa.*
>
> **Third movement** *(the long chant — sung facing the heart of the city; nobody can say why)*
> *Kess sela vhal-oroth,*
> *ammé-thara sela kess,*
> *dou-kess, dou-kess,*
> *né passa oroth kess-amméa.*
>
> ---
> *Marginalia, in Caspar's various hands and decades:*
>
> — *The dialect: not proto-imperial, not Mire-trade pidgin, no parent tongue I can find in forty years of looking. A liturgical register that appears in exactly one document tradition: this one. Odd.*
>
> — *The root "sela" occurs **eleven times** across the full cycle. In no family's rendering does any single word occur eleven times. Whatever it means, everyone is translating around it.*
>
> — *Cf. the founding fragment: "lights on the water, as the Mire gives them." The early hand renders "vhal-dou" as "the Mire gives." The oldest words we have and the oldest lights we have share a verb.*
>
> — *Movement II: asked forty different elders across forty years why these eight bars alone may never vary. Forty renderings of "that's how it's done." The bars themselves: same four words, every household, two hundred years. Everything else drifts. This does not drift.*
>
> — *M. Perrin insists Movement III is a preserving-recipe — for eels, she believes ("keep it in the heart of the home; do not let it pass out"). I no longer argue with Maud.*
>
> — *(newest ink, cramped)* — *Five translations collected this spring, each devout, no two alike. A younger colleague beat me to this method by fifty years and struck out his own conclusion. I begin to understand the impulse.*

## GM Key — what the words actually say

*(The spirit delivers this translation at the Heart — [[reveal-read-alouds]] §4. Never earlier, never from an NPC. The lexicon exists so YOU stay consistent if players start doing cryptography — and they will.)*

| Root | Meaning | The trap in it |
|---|---|---|
| **sela** | stay / hold / keep / remain | imperative *and* stative at once — "stay!" and "it holds" are the same word; every family translation diverges on exactly this ambiguity |
| **ressa** | remain (bound-couplet with *sela*) | the pair is a formal doubling — a *command said twice*, which is how old workings seal |
| **oroth** | guest / presence / the honored one | the city hears "guest." The word does not mean invited |
| **vhal** | the water / the Mire | |
| **kess** | we / ours / the city | |
| **dou** | give / receive / owe | bidirectional — "the water gives" and "we are given to" are the same verb; Maud's recipe and the founders' tribute both live here |
| **ammé** | home / hearth; **ammé-thara**: the great house = the heart of the city | families hear *thara* as "the year's bounty" — hence "keep the good year" |
| **né passa** | shall not pass / shall not cross / shall not leave | |

**The true rendering, movement by movement** *(matches the Reading word for word)*:

- **First movement:** *"Stay, honored guest — stay: the water gives you to our home. Stay."* — The doubled opening (*oroth sela, oroth sela*) is where "welcome the guests twice" comes from. The welcome IS doubled. It's a seal, not a greeting.
- **Second movement — the eight bars:** *"Stay. Remain. What we hold shall not pass."* — The lock's tumbler. This is why the variation-taboo guards these bars and no others: everything else in the cycle is ornament; **this is the mechanism** ([[fen|Fen's]] "why these eight bars?" — this is why).
- **Third movement:** *"We keep what keeps us. We hold the great presence in the heart of our home — it gives to us, we give to it — and it shall not pass out from among us."* — Sung by sixty thousand people, facing the Hall, hands over hearts, every week for two hundred years.

**Why every family is partially right** *(the payoff to stage after the Reading — each translation caught a true fragment)*:

| Family rendering | The real fragment it caught |
|---|---|
| "The promise holds." | *kess sela* — "we keep / it holds"; covenant grammar, correctly felt |
| "The water remembers." | *vhal-oroth* — "the water's honored one"; they heard the water had a mind in it |
| "Keep the good year." | *ammé-thara sela* — "hold the great house"; *thara* misheard as harvest |
| "Welcome the guests twice." | the doubled *oroth sela* — the seal, heard as hospitality |
| Maud's recipe | the imperative list — *stay, remain, keep, do not let it pass out* — because it IS a set of instructions. Maud is the closest of all of them. Never tell her. *(She keeps [[the-last-board|The Last Board]] — the campaign's deepest secret lives behind that bar, filed under eels.)* |

*(When the party works this table out — and after the Reading, a good table will re-derive it themselves — the horror compounds: nobody was wrong. Everyone was singing the truth in fragments, with love, for two centuries. That's the campaign's thesis arriving via grammar.)*
