# Set Piece — The Long Room *(sending word home; and the note under the chant)*

*Built 2026-07-24. The room and the man were built long ago ([[the-long-room]]) and have never once fired. **Zero combat, 30–50 min, one coin beat.** Two halves: the party finally sends word to the people waiting for them, and — if they earn it — they ask the one man in Sallowreach who could hear what the Celebration is doing to actually listen. He says no. Then he says he'll do it at the next one.*

---

## 🚨 Panic Panel *(open here, run off this)*

**What this is:** a narrow candlelit room with a wall of humming stones. You sit in the good chair and say what you want to say, and a quiet old wizard makes your words swim out of the swamp to somebody a thousand miles away. It costs. Your body stays here.

**Say this if you need a beat:** *"Lucien puts the kettle on before you've finished explaining what you want. He's already reaching for a slate."*

## 🧭 In Plain Words *(no jargon — read this first if you're cold)*

There's a shop in this city that sends messages. Not by boat — by magic. A man named Lucien Aubert sits you in a chair, you tell him what you want to say, and he pushes your words out over the swamp to a stone in a far-off city, where somebody local carries them the rest of the way. It costs real money, it takes most of a night, and it is the only way anyone here reaches the outside world.

The party has been trapped in this swamp for about a month. Every one of them left somebody behind who thinks they're dead or thinks they're late. This is the room where they get to fix half of that — words go, bodies don't.

Lucien is about sixty-five, plain, careful, and kind in a dry way. He is the only person born in this city who ever became a wizard. He left as a young man, trained, saw everything, and then came home to a one-room shop and has never explained it to anyone. He is the exact opposite of the party: he can leave whenever he wants and chose not to.

He is also the only person alive who could listen to the weekly city festival the way a trained wizard listens to a spell — and he has spent forty years refusing to. Years ago he heard something in the city's hum that was wrong, and it's part of why he left, and he has never named it to anybody.

The party wants to investigate that festival. He's the answer. He will say no. The scene is getting a man to agree to hear a thing he's spent his whole life not hearing — and what it costs them to ask.

## 📍 What You See

**Indoors. One long narrow room on stilts, maybe twelve feet by thirty. Warm, low light. Smells like cold iron, candle smoke, and the sharp air after lightning.**

- **The sending wall** — a slab of black Mire-wood, inlaid with wire in a pattern, and a shelf of maybe twenty **stones**, each humming a different note. Some are lit. Most aren't.
- **The good chair** — one armchair facing the wall, worn soft. Everyone who sits in it stays a while.
- **The ledgers** — floor-to-ceiling shelves of them, thirty years deep, in handwriting nobody but Lucien can read.
- **A kettle**, always on.

**The quiet in here has weight — the way a confessional has weight.** That's the note to hit. Not spooky. *Private.*

---

## Beat 1 — The Chair *(getting them in the room)*

Whoever walks in, he's already up and moving. He doesn't ask their business — he asks who they're trying to reach.

> He's past sixty, spare, plain clothes, nothing Guild about him. Ink to the second knuckle on both hands.
>
> "Sit. Not there — *there.*" The good chair. "Everyone stands the first time. It doesn't help."
>
> He sets the kettle without asking. Then he pulls a slate off the desk and a stub of chalk, and he waits, and he doesn't fill the silence at all.
>
> "Tell me what you want to say. Not the pretty version — the true one." *[a small shrug]* "I'll make it fit. I always make it fit."

**The stones, if anyone asks** — he'll name them down the row, unhurried. The capital. Two Guild hubs. **Vethmoor.** A handful of merchant houses. And, at the end, three that are dark: *"Those relays went down. Nine years, six years, and one I keep tuned because I'm sentimental and it costs me nothing."*

*(Don't explain the last one. That's [[the-long-room]] §What He's Hiding, shown and never said.)*

## Beat 2 — The Sending ⭐ *(the heart of it — give it real table time)*

**⚠ This is not a roll. Do not make it a roll.** The scene is the player saying the words out loud, in character, and cutting them down.

### The rule that makes it work

Lucien writes on the slate as they talk, and **a handful of coin buys about thirty words.** He says so plainly, without apology, because it's just the price:

> "A handful gets you thirty words, near enough. Distance eats some. The swamp eats more." *[he doesn't look up from the slate]* "Most people spend the first twenty telling somebody they're sorry. Say the thing first. Be sorry in the next one."

Then **make the player actually do it.** Count with them if you like. Let them cross things out. This is the whole beat.

### 💰 Tonight's coin beat *(the only one — don't stack a second)*

A sending is **a handful, or several for a long one.** Weigh, don't price ([[coin-in-sallowreach]]). The squeeze is real and it is not a trap: more words costs more, a reply costs again, and Lucien extends no credit — kindly, but he doesn't.

### ⭐ Tenson's, specifically *(the player asked for this — it's the knife)*

Tenson wants to write home to their parents. Play it warm and let it get quietly terrible on its own, because of what Tenson did last session.

- **Lucien's opening demand is the knife, and he has no idea.** *"Not the pretty version — the true one."* Tenson has just spent a week composing true sentences arranged to mislead. Now they're in a chair being asked for the true one, by a stranger, for free, about their own mother.
- **Ask the player the question directly, out of character if you need to:** *do they tell their parents they can't leave?* Thirty words does not have room for both "I'm alive" and "I'm never coming home."
- **Don't editorialize.** Say nothing about the report. The rhyme does the work. *(Knife per [[twist-the-knife]] — one per PC, softest version first.)*
- **The other envelope.** Tenson also owes [[silence|Magistra Silence]] a dispatch ([[tenson-arc]]). Let them send both in one visit — the personal one costs them, the official one is expensed ([[coin-in-sallowreach]] Payday Map). *An auditor's mother is out of pocket and their patron is reimbursed. Let that sit there without comment.*

### The other three — free jobs, no rolls

| PC           | The job                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Leavy**    | Lucien asks her, same as everyone: *who's waiting for you?* **She doesn't know.** A life was taken out of her clean ([[the-witch]]). The room is built entirely around a question she cannot answer. Ask it once, gently, and move on — **do not solve it tonight.**                                                                            |
| **Isotta**   | Her sight, free: **there is nobody here.** Not one lingering figure, in the one room in Sallowreach where thirty years of people came to say the last thing they had to say. Everybody who sat in that chair got it *said.* *(Her arc's thesis delivered as wonder, not exposition — [[isotta-arc]]. Let her make of it what she makes of it.)* |
| **Fletcher** | The stones each hum a pitch. One of them is the direction he was going when the ship went down — and the wrong-way pull in his chest ([[tether-manifestations]]) points somewhere else entirely, in the same room, at the same time. One line. Don't chase it.                                                                                  |

## Beat 3 — Business, If They Want It *(short — clear the desk before Beat 4)*

Run only what the table reaches for.

- **The Truth-Teller's Ink.** It lives here, and the price is a **favor owed to the Long Room, on the books, properly witnessed.** Full item in [[items/loot-ledger]] — don't restate it, hand it over and let Tenson insist on the paperwork.
- **The Bigsby fallback.** If the table still hasn't bitten on the attendant's transcript ([[tenson-arc]] three-brightness plant), Lucien delivers it dry over his ledger, exactly as written in [[session-04-redline-meeting]]: *"The spell is the record, man. Ask what else the attendant heard."* One line. Never twice.
- **⚠ If they ask him to look something up in the archive about somebody else** — the answer is **no.** Warm, immediate, absolute, and it costs them standing if they push. *"No. And I'd like you to not have asked me that, so we can go on being friendly."* **His discretion is not a difficulty number. It does not have one.**

---

## Beat 4 — The Ask ⭐⭐ *(the payload)*

The party has resolved to investigate the Celebration. Sooner or later somebody works out what Lucien is: a man who grew up chanting the old words before he could read, who then went away and learned what a working actually sounds like.

**He is the only person in the city with both halves.** He has never put them together on purpose.

### He refuses. Use the canon line.

> Whatever he was doing with his hands, he stops doing.
>
> "No."
>
> Not unkind. Just finished. Then, because he likes them and doesn't want it to land wrong —
>
> "I sing it like everyone sings it. I don't take it apart." *[he goes back to the ledger]* "Some things you leave whole."

🎲 **Instinct or Presence 13 — read him.** On a hit, say it plainly: **that was not reverence. That was a door he shut a long time ago and has been leaning on ever since.**

### 🎲 The bad path — arguing *(Presence 15, reach)*

If they debate him, reason with him, or invoke their curse as *leverage*, this is where it goes. **Even a success only buys** *"I'll think about it,"* and he means it and won't. **On a failure or Fear:** he's polite, he's finished, and the visit ends. He'll still send their letters. He'll be a little further away next time.

**Make the roll available and let it be the worse option.** That's the design.

### ⭐ The real lever — a true thing, given first

**No roll.** He is the man who has held every private grief in this city and been party to none. What moves him is not an argument. It's somebody handing him something back.

**The specific key: they tell him what happened to them.** They went under on a Celebration night, and the water was singing, and they can't leave. Nobody in this city has been told that plainly.

> He listens to all of it without moving. When it's done he sits back and looks at the wall of stones for a while.
>
> "I've been the room people say things into for thirty years." *[a beat]* "Nobody's ever handed me one to carry."
>
> Then, and this is the hard part for him: "I could be gone by morning. I came back. I've never explained that to anyone, and I'm not going to now."
>
> He turns the ledger over so the page is face down.
>
> "There's three days to the next one. I'll stand where I always stand." *[quiet]* "And I'll listen properly. Once. That's what I've got."

**⚠ THE COST — say it, don't hide it.** He keeps copies of everything. Whatever they just told him goes in that hand nobody can read, on that shelf, in this room, tonight. He'd never dream of misusing it. He also would never dream of *not writing it down.* Show them the pen moving. Say nothing about it.

**⚠ He does not listen tonight.** He listens at the **next Celebration**, which is in **three days** — the same clock Adaline names and Remy refuses on ([[session-06-run-sheet]]). *Three separate people now hang something on that date.*

## Beat 5 — Stage Two, Banked *(fires after the Celebration — not tonight)*

He comes to *them.* That's the tell: a man who never leaves his room, standing at Remy's door in the morning, having not slept.

> "It's a working."
>
> He says it like a diagnosis he'd rather have got wrong.
>
> "It's not a song with a shape. It's a shape with a song on it. Eight bars in the second movement that don't do anything a piece of music does — they *hold*. Everything else is decoration around those eight bars, and it has been for two hundred years, and I've sung it four thousand times."
>
> *[and then the part he came here to say]*
>
> "There's a note under it that nobody is singing. It doesn't move. It's been there the whole time." *[a beat]* "I couldn't tell you where it's coming from. Sound has a direction. That doesn't."
>
> "I don't know what it's for. I know it's *for* something."

**Then stop.** He has no theory, he refuses to invent one, and he'll tell them he's forty years out of practice and might be wrong. He is not wrong.

**🚫 What he must never say or hint:** binding · prison · prisoner · a spirit · two spirits · what's under the Hall · anything about a cage. **He does not know.** He has heard a *structure* and a *held note*, which is exactly as far as a trained ear gets. Recognition, never exposition ([[the-celebration-clue-map]] — this is L3 mechanism, deniable as acoustics, and the chant-pointer L2 lost in the journal rewrite).

**What it costs him:** everything. *He could have known.* He chose not to, for forty years, and an outsider had to ask him. Don't play that as a breakdown. Play it as a very tired man being extremely precise.

## 😨 Fear Tonight *(low-Fear night — Fear buys attention, not damage)*

- **1 Fear — the scatter.** Mid-sending the resonance breaks up, and one word of the message comes back out of the wall a second later, in the sender's own voice. Lucien doesn't flinch. *"It does that. It's not an omen. It's a swamp."*
- **1 Fear — the dark stone.** One of the three dead relays lights, faintly, for about two seconds. He looks at it. *"That's not for tonight."* He doesn't explain, and he won't.
- **1 Fear — the follow-up question.** He asks something about the message that's a shade too precise, and the table realizes he has been listening to *everything*, all evening, the way he always does.
- **1 Fear — someone asked first.** Conversationally, no malice: *"Funny — I had your friend in here. Tall fellow, talks like a landslide. Wanted to know if you four had sent anything out."* **That's [[sinope]]**, being cheerfully impossible ([[world-in-motion]] Front 11).
- **Banked, not spent:** the reply arriving at the worst possible moment ([[panic-button]]).

## 📮 GM Only — The Paper Fuse *(Front 11's sixth holder; do not fire tonight)*

Nobody has counted Lucien among the people who could unravel the Caspar lie. He should be.

- **Caspar floated out of the Mire on a balloon toward a world that has relay stones in it.** He is a lettered man with forty years of correspondents and a compulsion to catalogue. Sooner or later he writes to somebody. **Everything coming into this city lands in this room first, and the man who handles it keeps copies.**
- **The soft version (recommended):** a sending arrives for **Delphine** from a far relay, under a name that isn't his, and Lucien mentions it in passing because it's odd, not because it's suspicious. He'd never open it. He wrote it down.
- **The reverse door:** if the party ever wants to *reach* Caspar, this is the only one — and buying it means telling Lucien who they're writing to. **That's the price, and it's the good kind:** the confession is a lever, not a punishment ([[world-in-motion]] Front 11, the holders table — *the gift if the party tells them first*).
- **⚠ He cannot send to nowhere.** No relay, no sending. Flat, no roll, no workaround. *"I can put your words in a city. I can't put them in a man."*

**⭐ The crossover:** the party is carrying a bundle of letters that were written and never sent ([[caspar-letters-handout]]), and they are standing in front of a man whose whole life is delivering words that were meant to leave — and whose one private crack is *the message he never sent.* If they ever show him that bundle, he is undone, quietly, and he will do almost anything they ask afterward. **Spend that once.**

## 🚫 Guardrails

- **He is a clue, not a shortcut.** He never explains the Celebration. He describes what he heard and refuses to theorize.
- **He does not listen tonight.** Three days. The delay is the design — it hangs the Celebration on a third hook.
- **The lever is a true thing, not a good argument.** If they only argue, they only get *"I'll think about it."*
- **His discretion never breaks.** Not for a roll, not for coin, not for a good reason. If it ever breaks, it breaks *him* ([[the-long-room]]).
- **The archive is shown, never opened.** Not tonight, probably not ever without a whole scene of its own.
- **Never let the room become an escape hatch.** Words go. Bodies don't. Say it out loud if anyone gets hopeful.
- **One coin beat.** The sending is it.
- **Marcel is not a fuse here.** He'd pay a fortune for the archive and knows better than to ask. He stays grey and stays out of it ([[marcel-dray]] §Playing Him Grey).
