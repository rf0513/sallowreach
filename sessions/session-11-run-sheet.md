# Session 11 — Run Sheet: *Eleven*

*Prepped 2026-10-20, same day as play. Online table. One page: everything you say, in order.*

## 🧭 In Plain Words

Last session stopped in the middle of the town's big festival, right as everyone started singing. Tonight you finish that night. The whole city sings an old chant that nobody understands. During the middle part, all four PCs feel their curse pull tight. Isotta watches eleven very old ghosts in the front row, and they're singing too. Someone writes the words down, and one word turns up exactly eleven times. Then the town sends lanterns onto the water, and all four PCs feel exactly what they felt the night they drowned. They go home and sleep better than they have in months. Next morning, each PC gets one stop in town: Fletcher at Brin's map table, Isotta at the founders' book, Leavy stuck babysitting, Tenson at the barges.

**Tonight's one goal:** two things land. The chant is on paper (pasted in chat), and the click happens (four lines, one per player).

## 1 · The Song Picks Up (5 min)

> So. The whole city's on its feet. The drums drop out.
>
> And sixty thousand people start singing at once.
>
> It's not pretty, exactly. It's too big to be pretty. It's a whole city doing something it's done ten thousand times, right down to the kids who don't know the words and sing anyway.
>
> First part's the welcome verse. It rolls over you like weather.

**Tenson only. Say it, then say nothing else:**

> Across the water there's the Embassy. One window lit.
>
> And Caius is in it. He's singing. Old words, every one. And his eyes are wet.

## 3 · The Eight Bars (all four, ~5 min)

> Then the second part starts. It's short. Eight bars.

**Go around, one line each, no pauses:**

- **Fletcher:** The pull in your chest, the one that always drags you southwest, swings. For eight bars it points dead at the middle of the crowd, at the same thing everyone's facing.
- **Leavy:** For eight bars the song has a *taste*. And you know it. It tastes like a seal going onto a jar.
- **Isotta:** Everything that's been watching you tonight stops. For eight bars nothing's looking at you, because all of it's facing the same way you are.
- **Tenson:** The bell. *Your* bell, the watch bell from the boundary markers, is ringing here, four beats, right on the song. And it isn't a watch changing. It's a watch being *set*.

> And then it's over. The third part starts, and the whole city turns to face the middle of town and keeps singing.
>
> And it's gone. Remy's singing his lungs out two feet to your left. He didn't feel a thing.

🎲 **If they try to pin down exactly when it happened: Instinct 13.**
- **Success:** eight bars and four words, the same four repeated.
- **Failure:** just "the middle bit."
- **With Fear:** it hangs on three seconds too long, like a hand that won't let go of a handshake.

**If someone looks grey, Remy pats their arm:** *"First one, is it? That middle bit gets everybody. That's the city saying hello, cher. Don't fight it."*

## 4 · The Eleven (Isotta, ~5 min)

> The chant finishes. The whole square lets out this big warm breath. Everybody laughs and heads for the food.
>
> The eleven don't stop.
>
> Same tempo. Same words. Into a square that's emptying out around them.
>
> And you get this feeling that they haven't stopped since they started.

**Pick 1–2 extras:**
- When the living clap, they **sag** like people at the end of a shift. When the next part starts, they straighten up, and it looks like it hurts.
- They **all turn their heads at once**, toward the middle of town. They hold it, then turn back. Nothing's there.

**🔢 The count (it shifts, but never above eleven):** nine, then seven, then eleven again. If she rolls to get a true count, she succeeds and still gets a different number. **If she says it out loud:** *"Never more than eleven. Every time you check."*

**🁢 If Isotta uses Basile's Domino on them** *(once per long rest; the domino cuts through "ritual chants." It works.)*

> You put the tile down. Click.
>
> The nearest one stops singing.
>
> Its mouth closes. First time all night.
>
> It turns, slowly, and looks past you. Over the rooftops, toward the Kept Root. You can see the glow from here.
>
> It holds there.
>
> Then the next line starts, and it straightens up, like something pulled it. And its mouth opens again.

**It says nothing.** The payload is that they don't choose to sing, something makes them, and it looked at the tree.

🚫 **They never speak a plain sentence. They aren't sorry. She can't cross them. Never say "founders."** Let Isotta describe them to the other three.

## 4b · ⭐ The Paper Man (Tenson, at the feast, ~10 min)

*Right after the Eleven, while everyone's eating. Before the lantern bell. Full notes: [[session-10-run-sheet]] §4b.*

**Why it hits:** Tenson saw the real Caius singing at the Embassy window earlier tonight. Caius never leaves the Embassy, and everyone knows it.

> Tenson, somebody says your name, right behind you.
>
> It's Caius.
>
> Out here. At the feast. Good coat, holding a plate of food he hasn't touched.

*[Let that land.]*

> "Tenson. I hoped I'd find you."
>
> "The barges go out in the morning. My pouch goes with them." *[beat]* "I'd so like your report in it."
>
> "I know. Not tonight, of all nights."
>
> *[beat]*
>
> "And yet."
>
> He holds out a pen. "Keep it. The Empire has a great many pens."

**It's live.** Tenson can answer, push, or make a deal. Caius **never refuses**, and he **never finds out the death was faked.**

**When it ends:**

> He smiles. "Good night, Tenson."
>
> And he folds.
>
> Like, actually folds. Down the middle, then in thirds, quick and neat, like somebody tidying up a letter.
>
> There's no Caius. There's a sheet of paper on the bench where he was standing.
>
> It's the incident report form. A fresh copy.
>
> And you're still holding the pen.

**Said aloud (Tenson's math):** *"He spent more on that than the wall gets in a season, to ask me for a signature."* **Fallback, someone at the feast:** *"Was that the imperial man? I've never once seen him out."*

**This is tonight's one coin beat.**

## 5 · ⭐ The Lanterns + THE CLICK (all four, ~10 min)

> The bell rings once. And sixty thousand people go quiet at the same time. Mid-laugh, mid-song, just… hush.
>
> Then the lanterns go up. All of them. Every street, every window, thousands of blue-green lights lifting out over the black water. The water glows back.
>
> Solène's next to you. She lights one last lantern, holds it a second too long, and lets it go.

**Get everyone to the rail.** If anyone wandered off, Solène hands them a lantern.

🎲 **Anyone releasing a lantern: Instinct 13** `[VERIFY]`.
- **Success:** a clean moment of belonging. Clear a Stress.
- **Failure with Fear:** the lantern drifts back against the current, and for half a breath something huge out on the water looks *through* them toward the middle of town. One PC at most.

> The chant peaks.

**Say each line to its player: Fletcher first.**

**After Fletcher's line, you say:**

> And the cold spot where your curse sits, all four of you, *answers* it. It doesn't hurt. That's the terrible part. It answers the way your name answers when somebody calls it.

**Then Leavy, Isotta, Tenson. Don't talk between them. Then:**

> The last time you felt this, all four of those things, in exactly this order, you were drowning. Open water, black night, forty miles from this dock, the night the ship went down.
>
> And the entire city is singing.

**Stop.** Don't explain it. Never say "ritual," "binding," or "the Celebration did this."

## 6 · Night Button (end of the festival)

> It's late when you walk back. Families carrying sleeping kids. Somebody still singing three canals over. Remy's humming the middle bit under his breath, badly, and doesn't know he's doing it.
>
> And here's the strange thing. The curse, that cold, stuck feeling you've carried since the water closed over you, is still there. But tonight it's quiet. Almost settled. Like whatever's holding the leash is holding it gently, just for tonight.
>
> You sleep like the drowned. All four of you. No dreams at all.

**Then cut to morning (§7). Don't connect "it hurt at dusk" with "it tucked us in." Let them find it.**
