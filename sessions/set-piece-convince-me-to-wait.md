# Set Piece — Convince Me to Wait

*The [[the-tallykeeper|Tallykeeper]]'s collection notice, staged. **Its canon, titles, etiquette, and the grace-period terms live in [[the-tallykeeper]]; the arc engine lives in [[isotta-arc]]** — this file is how you run the parley at the table: the arrival, the courtesies, the sums, and the cord. It runs the same engine every time it's needed — the first firing (the Introduction) and any renegotiation after. Nothing here decides how her story ends; it decides how much time she has to write it.*

---

## 🧭 In Plain Words *(the whole set piece — no jargon)*

One of the player characters, Isotta, is secretly a ghost. She died on a ritual table years ago, and the magic yanked her soul back before her death could be completed — so she walks and talks, but her edges fray, and if she frays away entirely there'll be nothing left of her at all. The being that completes deaths in this swamp is an ancient, unfailingly polite spirit that wears the shape of a grey heron — the dead call it the Tallykeeper. It ties a knotted cord for every death; in two hundred years it has missed exactly one. Hers. It has been following her for weeks, and in this scene it finally introduces itself and states its business: it has come to collect her, while there's still enough of her to take. But it would *rather* wait — if she can become properly mortal again and die properly someday, its books balance better. So this is not a fight. It cannot be fought. It's a negotiation with a cosmic accountant about time: how much she gets, what it costs, and what she puts up as collateral. She walks out of this scene alive, with a cord of six loose loops tied around her wrist — her remaining grace, made visible.

**Two goals:** the horror inverts into arithmetic — it was never hunting her, she is *owed* — and the player leaves holding a physical cord whose loops the whole table will watch for the rest of the campaign.

## 🌒 The Arrival *(low tide, no wind — wherever she is; it comes to her)*

> Isotta — the light changes first. Not darker. *Stiller.* The sounds of the city drop away like the tide going out, and the water under the boards stops moving.
>
> And the heron is there. Not on the roofline. On the boards. Ten feet away.
>
> And then it isn't a heron — or it is, but it's also the *other* one, the veilside one: tall as a stilt-house, feathers like wet slate, and around its neck, on a cord, hangs a ledger of knots. Hundreds. Thousands. Every one tied off and finished.
>
> Except one. One cord hangs loose, no knot in it, smooth as riverglass. And as you look at it — it lifts. Toward you. Softly. Like a compass needle finding north.
>
> The heron bows.

Then it waits. It has waited two hundred years. It will win any silence the table starts.

**Delivery tic (canon, the whole performance):** no contractions. Short, ruled lines. Say a sentence. Let it land. Small pause. Next one.

## 🤝 The Three Courtesies *(live table rules — let the players discover them; enforce gently)*

1. **Return the bow.** It offers courtesy first, always. If she doesn't return it, it simply stands back up and *files her under not ready* — and the scene ends, possibly for a season. *(Mercy dial: if she catches it as it turns away, one bow salvages the night.)*
2. **Speak plainly.** You cannot lie to a ledger. A lie or a bargaining omission isn't punished — it's *read back to her, aloud, in full*, which is worse. Once.
3. **Promises are entries.** Anything anyone in this scene promises, it records. It will not warn them about this — canon: it cannot imagine needing to. **But it answers direct questions truly** — the protection is *asking* ("what would that cost?") before offering. Players who ask get plain answers. Players who don't, sign.

## ⚖️ The Shape — terms, never whether *(say this to yourself before running it)*

**This scene cannot be failed into collection tonight.** It has already done the arithmetic: dissolution is the one outcome it cannot accept, so waiting is *already* its preference — if the risk is covered. Every roll in this scene moves **terms**, never *whether*. What's on the table:

**The notice (its opening, canon — deliver as written, [[the-tallykeeper#The Grace Period]]):**

> "Two endings balance your line. You cross now. Or you make yourself mortal again — live your life — and I collect you at the end of it. Like everyone else. Either works.
>
> The third ending does not balance. You wear through to nothing. Nothing to collect. An open line, forever.
>
> So. Convince me to wait."

**The sums it counts** *(its three columns — let it name them, one ruled line each)*:

| Column | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **What steadies her** | Closed docket cases (proof she balances accounts) · the grounding technique · named anchors — a lit lantern, a held hand, her own name · a *credible* cure lead |
| **What frays her** | Every filled Slipping clock on record — it has them all · nights alone in the dark · veilstep overdraft · the thin veil itself, which is not negotiable |
| **The ask** | Time. Which, to a ledger, is risk. She has to make the risk worth carrying. |

## 🎲 The Rolls *(3, house-style — each moves a dial on the terms table below)*

1. **The Case Presentation** *(the scene's jewel — her player's whole lane)*: she opens her own casebook — the file labeled *Subject: myself* — and runs the Order's protocol out loud, to the thing itself: manifestations documented, anchor identified, what the spirit is owed. **Knowledge, Diff 13; advantage if the physical casebook is played as a prop.** Hope-success: pick one Terms dial free. Fear-success: the dial is won, but it adds one liability to the record, aloud. Failure: baseline terms stand — never worse, never collection.
2. **The Valuation** *(when she argues for a specific lever)*: **Presence, Diff 12.** She's not charming it — you cannot charm a tide — she's *pricing* herself. Frame every success as the ledger accepting a figure.
3. **Speaking for her** *(any other PC, optional — never solicited)*: **Presence, Diff 13.** Success: their testimony enters the assets column — a named anchor with a pulse. Failure costs nothing but this, said kindly: *"I have recorded your fondness. Fondness is not collateral."* **If anyone offers to co-sign — see the Guarantor dial. Let them ask the price first. If they don't ask, it doesn't offer.**

## 📜 The Terms *(baseline is canon; the dials are what the parley can move)*

⚰️ **THE GRACE CORD IS RETIRED** *(GM decision, 2026-09-01)* — it was a second six-segment counter stacked on the Slipping clock and wired to it. **Everything below that counts loops is dead.** The live version:

**Baseline (always granted if it engages at all): it waits.** No cord, no loops, no number. **The [[isotta-arc|Slipping Clock]] is the only dial** — fill it and she frays toward dissolution, the one outcome the grace exists to prevent. Draw that clock where the player can see it. **Closed cases and her tally sheet do not tick anything**; they change what it will *grant*, and it keeps the count itself. Revocable if her fraying outpaces the value of waiting — and it will say so, politely, in advance.

⭐ **As played:** S6 fired the knock and the time-freeze and stopped before any terms. The terms landed in **S7 at Basile's crossing** — [[set-piece-she-lets-me-win]] **Beat 4**, which is now the canonical terms scene. Use this file for a *reprise* or for a table that hasn't met it yet.

*(Historical, for reference only — the retired baseline: a cord of six loose loops, one drawn tight per filled Slipping clock, one loosened per closed case.)*

| Dial | Won by | The price *(it states prices only if asked)* |
|---|---|---|
| **The seventh loop** | A crit, or a case presentation that visibly moves it | None. Excellence is its own collateral. |
| **Double entry** | The Valuation, argued well | For a season, each closed case loosens *two* loops — but she may refuse **no case** that comes to her window. The docket owns her nights. |
| **The full night's notice** | Asking for it plainly — it respects plain asks | Before any final collection, one whole night's warning, guaranteed. Price: an entry — *she comes to that meeting willingly.* |
| **The Guarantor** | A PC co-signs her line | +1 loop now. Their promise is an **entry**: if her line goes to dissolution, the guarantor's account carries the open debt. What that means, it will state plainly — *if asked.* GM: price it true at the table; never let it be signed casually. |

**Worse terms exist too:** an omission read back costs a loop. A rash promise becomes an entry at face value. It never punishes — it *records*, which lasts longer.

## 🕯️ The Interview *(Forced-early door — it volunteers nothing; her questions are the scene)*

It answers every direct question truly, completely, once. Its likely answers, in register — use what she earns:

- *"Am I dying?"* → **"No. You cannot. Your death is already spent. What you are doing is fraying. Those are different ledgers."**
- *"What are you?"* → **"I keep the count. Every crossing in this Mire is tied by me. Yours was not. That is the whole of our business."**
- *"Are you here to take me?"* → **"You misunderstand me. I am not deciding whether to take you. I am deciding whether I can afford to wait. Help me with the sums."** *(canon)*
- *"How long do I have?"* → **"That is the wrong sum. Ask what waiting costs me. Then offer to carry some of it."**
- *"Can you fix me?"* → **"I sever. Severing is collection. The mending is not my office."** *(the cure stays hers to find — [[the-tidebreaker]])*

Whatever she doesn't ask, she doesn't learn. That's the investigation, made of nerve.

## 😨 Fear Tonight *(0–2 spends, arithmetic made visible — never menace)*

- **1 Fear — the cord lifts:** mid-sentence, her smooth cord rises off the ledger toward her again, and every other cord hangs dead still. She marks a Stress. Say nothing about why hers is worn smooth. **Never confirm it. Never.**
- **1 Fear — the office does not pause:** it stops mid-negotiation. Somewhere in the city, someone has died. It takes a new cord from somewhere that isn't anywhere, ties one knot — one motion, unhurried, two hundred years of practice — and returns to her. **"Forgive me. The office does not pause."** Whoever watched marks a Stress. *(This is the scene's scale-shot: her whole crisis is one line in a working ledger.)*
- **1 Fear — the read-back** *(only if she's earned it — see Courtesy 2)*: it recites her omission back, in full, in her own words, without judgment. Worse than any attack roll.
- **What you never spend Fear on:** looming, cold gusts, spooky flavor. Canon guardrail — the dread is the arithmetic. It is *incapable of menace on purpose.*

### *(superseded)*

> It leans down — all that height, folding, careful — and ties a cord around your wrist. Six loops. Loose. Its movements are the same ones you watched it use on the ledger: unhurried, exact, *practiced.*

> It bows. And it's a heron again, on a piling, at the edge of sight. Where it always is.

## 🚫 Hard Guardrails

- **Courteous, patient, unmoved. Never spooky, never comic.** The delivery tic carries everything.
- **Not a fight — ever.** If the table forces one, the veil itself strains: run it as an environment/event, no HP, per [[the-tallykeeper]]. Nobody wins a fistfight with the tide.
- **Terms, never whether.** No roll tonight ends in collection. Bad rolls end in worse *terms* — that's the pressure.
- **Don't rescue her later.** The grace is revocable arithmetic, not kindness. When loops tighten, they tighten. The cord is only real if the table believes it.
- **The player's fourth option:** if she invents a lever nobody planned, the ledger hears any honest offer — price it truly, in entries, and let her have it.

## 🎯 Win Condition

The player walked in braced for a monster and walked out with a *creditor* — and liked it better and worse at once; the physical cord is on the table where everyone can see it; at least one dial got argued for and paid for; and nobody at the table can say the words "the office does not pause" casually anymore.
