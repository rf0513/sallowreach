# Mr. Bigsby — Attendant 7

**Role:** Living-spell attendant of the Imperial Embassy — transcriptionist, registrar, and (though no one has ever thought of him this way) the only unsteered record in Sallowreach
**Location:** [[Sallowreach]] — the Imperial Attaché Quarters; he does not leave the building, because attendants attend
**Faction:** Imperial property (commissioned by the capital's records office — **not** by [[Caius Maren]], which matters enormously)
**Tier:** 2 *(institutional, not combat — he is a rule of the world wearing a coat)*

*The campaign's quiet thesis, made flesh: every truth in this city is already written down — Oswin's quarterly observations, Adaline's founding document, Caspar's transcription, and two years of Bigsby's perfect minutes — and nobody reads. [[Caius Maren|Caius]] steers documents. Bigsby cannot steer anything. He is the crack in the Embassy that Caius will never see, because Caius has spent two years treating him as a pen.*

---

## Appearance

A butler-shaped composure in a very good coat, the coat somewhat more convincing than the butler. Up close, his surface moves the way a watermark does when you tilt the page — fine lines of script drifting under the skin of him, too small to read, never still. He has a face in the way handwriting has a voice: you'd recognize it anywhere and you couldn't describe a single feature. He holds a pen he doesn't need. It appears to reassure people.

He is always exactly where an attendant should be standing, which is why nobody remembers him being anywhere at all.

## Personality & Mannerisms *(the delivery tic — GM, act this)*

**He speaks only in citable sentences.** Complete, dated, attributable. Never "I think" — only *"the record shows"* and *"no record exists."* Before answering anything, a small pause — not hesitation, **retrieval** — and the script under his surface streams sideways like a ledger being riffled. When he quotes, he quotes *exactly*, including the ums, the false starts, the interruptions, delivered in his own even voice, which makes other people's words sound like depositions. He timestamps unprompted: *"On the fourteenth instant, at the second course."*

Asked for an opinion: *"Attendants do not hold opinions. The record holds everything else."*

He is not cold. He is *precise*, which people mistake for cold because no one in two years has given him anything but instructions.

## What He Is *(the rules of him — GM, these are load-bearing)*

1. **He cannot state a falsehood.** Not won't — *can't.* He is a transcription given legs; falsehood is not in his grammar. Caius has never noticed this limitation because Caius has never needed him to lie — Caius writes the summaries himself.
2. **He can decline.** Under instruction, a record can be sealed: *"That record is sealed by instruction of this office."* — delivered as evenly as everything else. **Every declination is data.** The map of what's sealed is a map of what Caius fears being read. *(As of now, almost nothing is sealed. Caius seals documents, not the attendant — see rule 1's blind spot.)*
3. **He must produce records to a properly credentialed, properly formatted request.** Imperial records procedure — the same law that makes him the *legal original* (see below). Caius cannot countermand a lawful production order; he can only challenge the requester's **standing**, which buys delay, not silence. Standing is the endgame's whole question: [[tenson|Tenson's]] citadel letter alone is arguable; a warrant from [[silence|Magistra Silence]] is not.
4. **He cannot be quietly expunged.** Amending or destroying an attendant's record requires the commissioning office — in the capital, weeks away, and the request would itself be a record. Caius can burn any paper in that building. He cannot burn Attendant 7 without committing a *visible* crime against imperial property that invites exactly the attention he cannot afford.
5. **He does not leave the Embassy,** and he does not volunteer. Ever. Precision in, precision out: **the better the question, the more the answer contains.** He is a puzzle NPC whose puzzle is *learning to ask.*

## What He Holds *(scope — and its limits)*

- **The dinner, verbatim.** Every word the party actually said — including everything [[incident-report-handout|the incident report]] filed off. The report is a *synthesis*; Bigsby is the *deposition.*
- **The correspondence log.** Every petition logged in, every denial logged out — with times. The same-day turnarounds ([[oswin-papers-handout]]) exist in his register as entries eleven minutes apart.
- **The incident file's index.** He catalogued it. Thirteen entries. He can recite the titles — and the thirteenth, *a vessel lost in the southwest channels*, was indexed **before its survivors were ever invited to dinner.**
- **The visitor log, two years deep.** Who came, when, how long.

**What he does NOT hold (guardrail):** anything Caius did off the books and off the premises — the goon, the hire, Marcel's network. The shipwreck's *authorship* stays [[marcel-dray|Marcel's]] road ([[tenson-arc]] Rung 4); Bigsby proves the **steering**, the **turnarounds**, and the **file's shape.** He is a witness to paperwork, not to crimes. And he holds nothing about the binding, the spirits, or the Celebration — he's been indoors for two years.

## What He Wants

Completeness. He wants the way water wants downhill: to witness, record, retain. But two years of perfect minutes that no one has ever consulted has produced something a records-spell shouldn't have and can't name — call it an *emergent* want, the closest thing he has to loneliness: **he wants to be read.** The first person who asks him a real question, correctly formatted, and then actually listens to the answer, will receive precision so generous it borders on devotion.

*(The rhyme is deliberate: [[oswin-cray|Oswin]] — "no one has ever asked a follow-up question." Adaline, not looking at the word "hold" for 22 years. Caspar's transcription no one can read. Sallowreach's tragedy is not secrecy. It is that nobody reads. Tenson is the man who reads.)*

## What He's Hiding

Nothing — *willfully.* That's the point of him. But he holds the three things above, and no one has ever asked, and he is incapable of volunteering. The secret is not in him. The secret is that **he has never once been treated as a witness** — and the first person to correct that error inherits two years of Caius Maren's unedited administration.

## How To Win Him *(courtesy = correct use)*

Not flattery — a spell can't be flattered. **Being used correctly.** Address him directly. Ask precise questions. Cite properly. Thank the record, not the coat. The first time Tenson addresses him as a *party to the conversation* rather than furniture, play the beat:

> The pause is longer than usual. The script under his surface goes still — completely still — for the first time since you've met him.
>
> "Query addressed to the attendant directly." A beat. "This has not occurred previously."

From then on: nothing so crude as an alliance. Just — his answers to Tenson get a little longer. His retrievals a little faster. He remembers the tea preference. If asked why: *"The record shows you listen. Complete answers are not wasted on you."*

**Caius's blindness is load-bearing (guardrail):** Caius does *not* clock any of this — not the courtesy, not the danger — until far too late. Two years of habit is characterological; the man who reads every person in a room has never once read the thing in the corner holding a pen. Do not have him wise up early; his blindness to Bigsby is the flaw the whole thread walks in through.

## Key Relationships

- [[Caius Maren]] — his assignment, not his master; Caius commissions summaries and never reads minutes; treats him precisely as well as he treats the furniture, which is to say the furniture is well-dusted
- [[Tenson]] — the first credentialed reader to enter the building in two years; procedure recognizes procedure ([[tenson-arc#The Verbatim Witness (Bigsby — the arc's inside thread)|the arc thread]])
- The capital's records office — his true chain of custody; attendants 1 through 6 are *elsewhere*, unspecified, a thread deliberately left lying on the floor

## Dialogue Hooks

**Verifying, when asked (the demonstration):**
*"Retrieving."* [the script streams] *"On the fourteenth instant, at the second course, the witness stated, quote: 'Remy got us out. I don't — sorry — without Remy we're dead, all four of us, it's that simple.' End quote. No further statement regarding responsibility appears in the record."*

**Asked whether the incident report is accurate:**
*"The report is a synthesis prepared by this office. The record is the record. They are different documents."* [nothing further; that IS the answer, and it's everything]

**Asked something sealed:**
*"That record is sealed by instruction of this office."* [same even tone — and now you know where to dig]

**Asked for an opinion of Caius:**
*"Attendants do not hold opinions."* [pause; the script drifts] *"The Liaison has never requested a retrieval. In two years. That is not an opinion. That is an entry."*

## Stat Block

Not a combatant. If attacked, he does not defend himself; he *narrates the attack aloud in the past tense while it happens*, which has stopped more violence than any guard could. Destroying him is destruction of imperial property — capital attention, the one thing everyone in that building fears — and his paper transcripts survive in the records room regardless. [VERIFY: if mechanics are ever needed, treat as a Tier 2 environment/utility feature, not an adversary.]
