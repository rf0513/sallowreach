# Set Piece — What the Water Keeps *(a missing girl, the stillness, and the one the Mire kept)*

*Built 2026-07-24. **Mined, not invented** — [[old-patience]] and [[odile-verret]] have both been in the repo for months, are two of its best files, and neither has ever been staged. They also live in the same water: the Still Pool sits off the **southwest** Wending channel, and the reed-channels are **southwest** of the Wending. Both files hinge on a **nine-year-old girl.** Nobody had noticed. This is the scene where they collide.*

***Roughly one session. Zero combat. Every die is in the water — both meetings are conversations.*** *Delivers the campaign's first hard evidence that the tether can change, the Mire-from-above vision, and Odile Verret on screen at last.*

## 🧭 In Plain Words *(no jargon, no shorthand — read this if you're cold)*

An old woman on the outer edge of town has a granddaughter, nine years old, who walked out to a little family shrine at the swamp's edge that morning to leave an offering. She never came back. The family asks the party for help, and the innkeeper takes them out in his boat.

They find the girl three hours southwest, sitting at the edge of a very still pool, completely unhurt. She just sat down and lost track of the day. The pool is not ordinary — there's something ancient living in it, made of the light that moves in swamp water, and it is curious about the party specifically, because they're the four people in this city who went under the black water and came up cursed. If they behave correctly around it — which mostly means shutting up and waiting — it makes the curse *hurt less*. Not gone. Less. That's the first time anything has ever moved it.

On the way home the swamp goes wrong on them. The boat swamps, or the channels turn, and they end up much deeper than anyone meant to go. And out there a woman finds them and pulls them out of the water. Twenty years ago this city walked her to a far channel at first light, took her boat and her salt, and left her there, because she had drowned a man with her own hands — the ferryman whose carelessness had drowned her nine-year-old daughter. The swamp was supposed to finish her. It didn't. She's still out there, and she pulls people out of the water now, and she will tell the party exactly what she did without softening a word of it.

Then they take a nine-year-old girl home, and they have to decide what story to tell about who found her.

## 📍 What You See *(three places, in order)*

**1 · The porch.** *Outdoors, low water, the far end of the outer boardwalk.* A house on stilts with the water at the second step. A porch two doors down that is **entirely underwater**, and an old woman standing in it to the shin, sweeping it. A stack of tide journals on a dry chair, forty years deep, in one hand.

**2 · The Still Pool.** *Outdoors, three hours southwest, an unnamed break in the mangroves maybe sixty feet across.* Black water with no ripple on it at all. Offerings collected at the near edge — grass twists, buttons, carved things, some of them very old. A nine-year-old sitting on a root with her back to you.

**3 · The reeds.** *Outdoors, night or near it, deep water, reeds taller than the boat.* No channel you can name. Green light under the skin of the water and a smell of iron and crushed stems. Somewhere out of sight, someone humming — badly, absently, the way you hum when you're working.

## Beat 1 — The Porch *(10 min; the ask, and the stillness starts here)*

**Old Sera Lethaby.** Matriarch of the oldest holdout family on the outer boardwalk — the one that refused the relocation order and never stopped refusing ([[the-drowning-edge]]). She sweeps a porch that went under last spring because the porch is hers and the sweeping is the refusal. She has kept the tide, in writing, for forty years.

> She doesn't stop sweeping when you come up. The broom goes on under the water — scrape, scrape — and it isn't doing anything, and she's been doing it a long time.
>
> "Lenne went out to the shrine before breakfast. She's nine. She's done it a dozen times."
>
> *[the broom stops]*
>
> "That was six hours ago."

**Play her still, not frantic.** She does not cry, does not rush them, does not beg. She says the facts in order and waits. **The whole family is standing behind her being calm at the same volume.** That's what a family that lives over water looks like when it's afraid.

- **She hands over the journals** if anyone asks what she knows about that direction — forty years of water levels in a tight, unlovely hand. *(Free. Nobody in Sallowreach has ever read them. They say the same thing [[caspar-renne|Caspar]]'s tide records say, and nobody has read those either.)*
- **⭐ Sera's ask, said plainly — Secret #1:** *"The water comes up from the southwest now. It didn't used to. I've got forty years of it on paper and I've told three people and none of them wrote it down."*
- **She will not come.** She won't leave the porch. Don't make this a negotiation; it's a characterization.

**🎣 Remy takes it personally, immediately.** He's already untying the boat. Do not stage a decision here.

## Beat 2 — Southwest *(20–30 min; ⭐ the Bilge's first real outing)*

**This is where the whole session's dice live.** Run [[the-living-mire]] §Travel With Teeth exactly as written — and run it *honestly*, because this is the trip the teeth kit was built for.

- **Size the Bilge before you push off.** Daylight **6**. If they dithered and it's late, **4**. Draw the boxes where everyone can see them.
- **Difficulty 11**, guided by Remy. **13** without him.
- **Say the water level out loud every single tick.** *Ankles → shins → knees → thwarts.*
- **Say the cost before every roll.** That's the one rule.
- **Fear results draw from [[mire-complications]]** — each entry's bold **Teeth:** line is the price, pre-decided.

**Name the wet things before you start.** Lenne's family sent food. Somebody's carrying dry blankets for a nine-year-old. Say that out loud in Beat 1 so it's a *thing to lose* by Beat 2.

🎣 **Fletcher's, free — Secret #2.** No roll. His tether pulls one way ([[tether-manifestations]]) and the channel they want runs the other, and every mile southwest the argument gets louder. **Let his player say it to the table.** NPC fallback: Remy, cheerfully — *"Funny bit of water, this. Always feels longer going than coming."*

## Beat 3 — The Still Pool ⭐⭐ *(30–45 min; the quietest scene in the campaign)*

### Finding her

> The mangroves open up and the water just — stops. Sixty feet of black water and not one ripple on it. There's stuff piled at the near edge, offerings, some of it old enough that you can't tell what it used to be.
>
> And there's a kid sitting on a root with her back to you. Little twist of dried grass in one hand and a bright button in the other. She hasn't put them down.
>
> Nobody has said anything yet. Nobody wants to be first.

**Lenne is fine.** Not entranced, not hurt, not touched by anything hostile. The pool is just very still and she sat down in it. When someone speaks to her she blinks, looks around, and is *embarrassed*:

> "Oh." *[she looks at the light]* "How did it get so late?"

⚠ **Resolve her safety immediately and completely.** The scene that follows only works if nobody at the table is still worried about the child.

### The four rules *(they are not told these — this is your card)*

| Rule | What breaks it | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Wait** | Speaking first | It waits them out. Indefinitely. |
| **2 · Don't lie** | Any lie, at all, out loud | The conversation stops. The liar gets a quality of attention that is very unpleasant to be under. |
| **3 · Refuse once** | Accepting the first offer, or angling for a third | Decline the first (courtesy), accept the second (exchange). A third offer never comes. |
| **4 · When it's done, it's done** | Following, or calling after it | The light disperses and the Mire gets harder afterward. |

**Remy knows.** He felt this pool's attention once, six years ago, and held still. **He will not tell them** — he'll go rigid and stop talking, and *that's the tell.* Give the table a beat to read him. A PC with strong Instinct can intuit rule 1; the rest they discover by playing.

**⚠ Withdrawal is loss, not punishment** ([[old-patiences-hollow]]). They still have the girl. That part is always complete. What they lose is the gift — say nothing about it, ever, and let the silence do it.

### It arrives

> The little lights in the water start to — organize. Not brighter. *Organized.* Lines and drifts pulling into something with a direction to it, and then something with attention.
>
> And then it's a face, the way a cloud is a face. Once you've seen it you can't stop seeing it.
>
> It's only ever in the water. Never above it.
>
> The air gets a degree cooler. Not cold. Still.

### The three things it says *(canon — [[old-patience]]; Remy translates about half, the rest arrives directly)*

**1 · The marks.**
> "You carry two marks. The city's mark, and the water's mark. You are the first in a long time."

*If asked what happened to the last ones:* **"They found what they were looking for."** ⚠ **Never clarify. Never invent a story for it.** Let the table decide if that's hope or a warning. It is possibly both.

**2 · The pressure.**
> "Something in the deep water has been answering something in the city for longer than I have attended to it. It is very close now. It makes the water feel like waiting."

**3 · The map** — it *shows* this rather than saying it. The light in the pool briefly lays out the whole Mire from above: every channel, every pool, the lines of current. **The southwest is unmistakably brighter.** Everything flows toward Sallowreach from there, and has been.

🎣 **Fletcher's, and it's his wonder moment.** He's the one who reads a map from above and knows what he's looking at. Hand it to him and let him describe it to the others.

### ⭐ The gift — partial tether relief

Offered on the **second** offer, after the protocols hold. It is made of the same water the tether is made of; it understands the mark.

**What it is:** not a cure. The binding is untouched. But the wall at the Mire's edge goes *quieter* — they can get closer to the line before it becomes impassable.

**Why it matters more than it sounds:** this is the first concrete proof the tether **can move.** They have been chasing something they had no evidence was reachable. Now they do.

**⚠ Say nothing about what it means.** Play the physical change and stop talking. *(Iron Rule 2: recognition, never exposition.)*

**Remy has no framework for what just happened. He does not speak for a while.** Play that — it's the single best thing in the scene.

### Per-PC, at the pool *(one each, no rolls)*

- **🐸 Fletcher** — the map, above. Plus: the direction his tether has been arguing with is the bright one.
- **🪓 Tenson** — **rule 2 is his knife.** He has just filed (or is about to file) a page of true sentences arranged to mislead. The spirit is not a lie detector for the past — it only knows what's said *here*. **But have it look at him one beat longer than the others, and say nothing.** He gets to wonder about that for the rest of the campaign. Don't resolve it. Ever.
- **🍃 Leavy** — it examines her longest and doesn't comment. Later, once, as the light goes: *"You are the quietest thing that has ever come here."* ⚠ **True, eerie, and explains nothing.** She reads as a smooth blank to everything that looks ([[leavy-arc]]) — this is that fact, said by something ancient, with no interpretation attached. Do not connect it for the player.
- **🐈‍⬛ Isotta** — a spirit of the Mire, ancient, in the water, and it does not react to her as anything unusual. The one place in the world where being what she is doesn't require a note.

### 🎣 Lenne's image — the one she carries home

She tells them on the boat back, unprompted, still not frightened:

> "I saw something while I was sitting. Like looking through glass. Two lights, close together — almost touching, but not quite." *[she thinks about it]* "It's the most beautiful thing I've ever seen."

⚠ **This is [[delphine|Delphine]]'s vision, word for word.** Do not connect it here. Bank it for the scene where it hurts most — Delphine hearing a nine-year-old describe her own vision back to her, in a week where she is grieving a man who is not dead.

Tonight he *is* at the tiller, in water he swore he'd never take clients into, with a child in the bottom of the boat, and the four people who are lying to him. **Say nothing. Let him be competent and frightened.** *(This is [[world-in-motion]] Front 11 doing exactly what it says it does: the lie wears down under someone being kind.)*

## Beat 5 — The Reeds ⭐⭐ *(30–40 min; no rolls, none, at all)*

### She finds them

> Reeds taller than the boat. Green light moving under the water, and it isn't the little lights from the pool — this is slower, and it's *under someone's skin.*
>
> Somebody's humming. Off-key, not paying attention to it, the way you hum when your hands are busy.
>
> Then there's a woman standing in water up to her thighs with her hand on the gunwale, and she's already turning the boat.
>
> "Sit down. All of you. That's it."
>
> *[to Lenne, without looking up]* "You're all right. Come here, you're freezing."

**Play her competent first and strange second.** She is drying a child and checking the boat before anyone gets a word out. The bioluminescent tracery under her skin, the green things growing where they shouldn't, the eyes that hold light — the party notices all of it *while she's being kind to a nine-year-old.* That's the register.

**Remy knows who she is and goes white.** He will not say her name. **Lenne says it** — nine-year-olds have no discretion:

> "You're the Saint in the Reeds." *[not scared — thrilled]* "Grandmother says you're not real."

### Her four answers *(canon — [[odile-verret]] §Dialogue Hooks; use them verbatim, they're already right)*

Do not make them pull these out of her. **She volunteers everything.** She has had twenty years to decide how she says it.

| They ask… | She says |
|---|---|
| **Who are you / what happened** | *"I drowned a man. Pascal Comeau. He put my daughter in the water to save himself a turn at the pole, and the city called it the river's will and brought him supper."* [perfectly steady] *"So three months later I put him in the same channel. With my hands. I'd thought of nothing else since they pulled her out."* |
| **The city was wrong to do this to you** | *"They cried when they walked me to the edge. People I'd delivered. They did it anyway, and they were right to."* [a small, terrible shrug] *"You can't live over water that kills and let grieving mothers drown the careless. Let me go and every loss in this swamp becomes a debt. The city would come apart by winter. I understood it then. I understand it now. I'd still have done it."* |
| **Do you regret it** | *"He had two of his own."* [the only time she looks away] *"The water took my one. I left his without a father. Ask me if I'm sorry and I won't have an answer you'll like — because the honest one is* not enough, *and that should frighten you more than the reeds."* |
| **What is it like out here / what is happening to us** | *"You went under. The water gave you back. It doesn't always."* [the light moving under her skin] *"It didn't give me back. It* kept *me. There's a difference, and you'll learn which one you are."* |

**⚠ The one she'll offer unasked, if anyone mentions the tether or the pool:**

> "Something out here lost something, a long time ago, and it has never stopped looking. I know that song. I lived it." *[flat]* "I just can't tell you what it's reaching for."

**That is a woman's reading of the water, not the water's own account.** She has no idea about the binding, the Celebration, or what's under the Hall — she's been gone twenty years and lives in a swamp. She points; she cannot name. *(Guardrail restated below.)*

### ⭐⭐ Isotta's beat — the best thing in this file, and only she can do it

Twenty years of drowned people and cast-outs in these channels.

**There are no ghosts here. Not one.** The reeds are completely empty in the way only Isotta can see.

Which means the girl isn't here either. Manon crossed — a long time ago, cleanly, the way the dead are supposed to. And **Odile has spent twenty years in this water not knowing that**, because there has never been anyone to tell her.

**Hand it to Isotta's player and get out of the way.** No roll. No prompt. Just describe the emptiness to her privately or plainly, and let her decide whether to say it. If she does, Odile does not thank her, does not weep, and does not become a different person. She goes quiet, says *"Ah,"* and looks at the water for a while.

**Then she gives them the channel home**, which she was going to do anyway, and everyone pretends that isn't the payment.

> *(⚖️ **GM's call, the darker fork** — if you'd rather Manon* be *there, uncrossed, seen only by Isotta, standing near a woman who cannot see her: that's a legitimate and devastating alternative, but it's **new canon** and it makes Isotta the keeper of someone else's twenty-year secret. Recommendation: **don't.** The empty version costs nothing, invents nothing, and the mercy is the better scene.)*

### Per-PC, in the reeds

- **🪓 Tenson** — a woman who states the worst thing she has ever done in flat declarative sentences, refuses every softening he offers, and will not let him call it anything but murder. He spent last week learning to write true things that mislead. **She is the anti-version of his compromise and she is at peace and he is not.** ([[twist-the-knife]] — softest version first; one per PC.)
- **🐸 Fletcher** — she cuts Gault's nets and frees his catches, and Gault avoids these channels because of her. If Brin or the 2H-3 come up, she'll say so plainly: *"There's a man out here who takes and takes and calls the Mire a warehouse."* An ally he did not expect, in the worst water on the map.
- **🍃 Leavy** — Odile was a *midwife*, and traded preparations with Delphine for fifteen years. She knows old remedies nobody in the city makes anymore, and she will trade. Also: the only person alive who has been changed by this water longer than Leavy has been alive in that body.
- **🐈‍⬛ Isotta** — above.

## Beat 6 — The Return *(10 min; a menu, never a script)*

They take a nine-year-old girl home to a woman sweeping an underwater porch. **The only real question of the night: what story goes back?**

**Do not pick for them. Do not say the word "again."**

| The door | It costs | It gives |
|---|---|---|
| **Tell the truth — the Saint in the Reeds saved her** | The city has to look at something it decided twenty years ago and has never revisited. Some of it lands on Delphine, hard. Pascal Comeau's widow and two children are alive in this city. | The first crack in a twenty-year story. **Delphine gets word of Odile after twenty years** — one of the campaign's heaviest quiet beats ([[odile-verret]] §Key Relationships). |
| **Tell a version — "we found her"** | Nothing tonight. **Everything later:** this is the second false account they've built in a week, about the same water, for the same reason. And Odile *expects* it, and forgives it in advance, which is worse than being caught. | Simplicity. Nobody has to explain anything. |
| ⭐ **Let Lenne tell it** | It leaves their hands entirely. Nine-year-olds do not edit. She will tell her grandmother, and the boardwalk, and everyone. | The cleanest and most in-character option, and it makes it **Sera's** to carry — a forty-year holdout who now owes the bogey in the reeds. |
| **Say nothing to the city, but tell Delphine privately** | The secret becomes a thing they hold *for* someone instead of *from* someone. | The heaviest beat, on their terms, chosen. |

**Sera, whichever way it goes** — she does not thank them where anyone can hear it. She goes back to sweeping. Then she stops, and hands over the journals *properly* this time, all forty years, and says:

> "You'll want these, then. Somebody ought to."

**Cut there.**

## 🚫 Guardrails

- **No rolls at the pool. No rolls in the reeds.** Both encounters are conversations. Every die tonight is in the Bilge.
- **Old Patience has no agenda, no lore, and no opinions.** It says true things and never editorializes ([[cosmology]]). It does not know the party's story, their names, or what the city does on festival nights.
- **Odile cannot name what the Mire is reaching for.** She's been gone twenty years and lives in a swamp. She points at the feeling; she has no access to the binding, the Tree Spirit, or the Celebration. **Clue-holder, not a shortcut.**
- ⚠ **The register lock holds** ([[STATUS]], 2026-07-18): the Mangrove Spirit is **weather and tide, never a mourner.** Odile is allowed to hear a song in it because that's what a woman who lost a daughter hears. **The text never confirms her.** Let *"grief"* stay the players' word and hers — never the narration's.
- **The girl is safe from the first moment.** Resolve it fast and completely. This is not a rescue with a countdown.
- **Withdrawal is loss, not punishment.** If they blow a protocol, they still have Lenne. They lose the gift, in silence, forever.
- **Nobody drowns in the Swamping.** Ever. It's a cost, not a TPK.
- **Do not say the rhyme out loud.** The city gave a woman to the water so it wouldn't have to hold what she did. Five days ago the party gave a man to the water so the city wouldn't have to hold what they did. **This never gets stated by anyone, including you.** If a player says it, that's the whole scene, and you say nothing back.

Anybody's."* — [[set-piece-the-wrong-skin]]).
