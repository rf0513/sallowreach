# Set Piece — The Wrong Skin *(the boat's maiden commission)*

*Fletcher's Side-Quest 1 ([[fletcher-arc]]), promoted table-ready. Runs as slot 3 of [[session-05-run-sheet]]. Rewritten 2026-07-15 for cold-open readability — read-alouds and mechanics unchanged.*

## In plain words *(read this first — no jargon)*

The party's boat gets its first paying job. Brin — the Hunters' Guild ecologist — hires all four of them for a day trip into the middle swamp: survey a dying bird rookery (count the nests), and on the way, collect a freshly shed snake skin from a known shedding spot. The day runs: dawn send-off at the docks → swamp channels, where lizardfolk silently watch the boat's hull markings → the abandoned camp of two hunters who vanished chasing the snake, which is eerily *tidy* — something has been keeping it → the shedding hollow, where they extract a twelve-foot shed skin while the water goes dead silent → the rookery, where a giant guardian spirit shaped like a spoonbill stands up, explains the colony is dying, and asks for help: big predatory fish have moved in and are eating the eggs, and the spirit is too starved to drive them off. The party fights the fish — the trip's one real fight. Then, sailing home at dusk, they unroll the shed skin on deck and see it: two different snakes' scale patterns meeting in a dead-straight seam down the spine. Someone *built* this animal. And Fletcher checks his own journal — three years of pressed skin samples — and finds a faint brand at every jaw-hinge: **2H-3**. The proof has been in his pack the whole time. The snake itself never appears today; its absence is the horror.

One more thread: the spirit looks at the boat and says the pale one — Isotta — "is on another keeper's ledger." The heron that's been following her is *owed* her. If she confronts the heron directly, a separate scene fires (see below).

## Why this scene matters *(the payoffs, with the codenames explained)*

- **Fletcher's hunt advances one rung.** His arc gates the truth about the snake in steps ([[fletcher-arc]]). This trip delivers **Rung 1: the snake is an abomination — made, not born.** The seam is shown, Leavy says it out loud, and the brand lands the gut-punch. (Rung 2 — "it was shipped cargo" — is the next trip: [[set-piece-the-cold-cache]].)
- **Isotta gets named on a ledger.** The heron following her is the **Tallykeeper** — the spirit that collects the dead, which is owed *her*. The spirit at the rookery says so out loud. If Isotta directly, insistently questions the heron, run **the Introduction** from [[the-tallykeeper#The Grace Period]] — the collection notice, "convince me to wait," the grace cord tied at the table. Her other saved-up scene (witnessing a ghost cross over — her arc calls it the banked jewel) is deliberately NOT this trip; it's reserved for her next town case ([[isotta-arc]]).
- **Tenson falls in love with fieldwork.** Brin's survey protocol is structurally an audit — hand him the tally-book. Bonus, free of charge: from the water the city's southwest ward-wall is visible and visibly thinning (a tell for the wall-maintenance thread, Front 2 in [[world-in-motion]]).
- **Leavy owns the reveal.** Her brewer's read on the skin is what proves it was made — the line is hers. Side bonus: the hollow grows a rare reagent her potion quest needs ([[leavy-arc]]).
- **The table meets its first real spirit.** **Old Plenty**, the rookery's guardian, is the campaign's spirit-lore showcase: spirits *keep* things, they spend themselves doing it, and they honor each other's ledgers. That's the whole cosmology lesson in one scene ([[cosmology]]).

**Who's aboard:** all four PCs plus Remy at the tiller (he knows the channels, he's wanted back on the water since the wreck, and he cooks). Brin pays Guild rate afterward.

> **Canon note (2026-07-15):** an earlier draft had the dead hunter Gravois's *ghost* at the camp. That broke the ghost rule — ghosts stay in town and only Isotta sees them; the swamp belongs to spirits ([[cosmology]]). So the camp holds only the hunters' gear, and Old Plenty carries their story. Gravois stays the dead hunter whose tally-book comes home.

## The Water Between — voiceovers for the trip *(grab one per leg, ~30 seconds each)*

Seven short scripted narrations, one per leg of the trip, so the swamp feels alive without improvising. Read them near-verbatim; pause where the dots are. Don't stack them — one per leg, and skip any leg the table fills with their own noise.

**1 · Casting off** *(dawn, leaving the Mooring — right after Beat 1)*

> It's that hour where the water's the same color as the sky, so the whole city looks like it's floating. Boards creak. Somebody's baking bread. Then Remy pushes off — and all of it just… slides away behind you. The last thing Sallowreach gives you is woodsmoke. The first thing the Mire gives you is quiet. Not empty quiet. *Full* quiet. Like walking into a room where everyone was just talking about you.

**2 · The Wending** *(the braided channels — with Beat 2)*

> The channels out here don't go anywhere. They braid. Remy stands up at the tiller and reads the water like you'd read a crowd — that channel's lying, that one's sulking, this one'll do. Herons everywhere. Something big rolls off to the left, once, and doesn't come back up. And the trees — the roots come up out of the black water like knuckles. Like the whole forest is holding onto the ground so it doesn't float off.

**3 · Midday** *(the deep Mire, loud and alive — between Beats 3 and 4)*

> By midday the Mire stops pretending to be quiet. It's LOUD out here. Frogs arguing. Bugs like somebody shaking a jar of coins. A thousand birds having a thousand disagreements. The sun comes down through the canopy in long green blades, and where it touches the water, the water steams. Everything out here is eating, and being eaten, and *singing about it.*

*Remy, grinning over the tiller:* **"People call the Mire a graveyard. Nobody alive out here agrees."**

**4 · The lantern rays** *(the wonder beat — spend it anywhere the table needs a breath)*

> Leavy — you see it first. Light. *Under* the boat. Soft blue-green, moving slow. Then another. Then ten. Lantern rays — wide as doors, gliding under the hull in a loose line, each one glowing like a lamp under a sheet. For a minute the whole channel is lit from below, and you're sailing on sky. Remy doesn't say a word. He takes his hat off. And when they're gone, nobody remembers agreeing to hold their breath.

**5 · The squall** *(the chaos beat — anytime; it costs five minutes of daylight, nothing else)*

> Weather out here doesn't arrive. It ambushes. You get one minute of warning — the birds stop, and Remy says "hats" — and then the sky just falls on you. Rain so hard the water jumps. You can't see the bow from the stern. Then it's done. Sun's back like nothing happened, everything smells like green, and the Mire shakes itself off and goes right back to talking.

**6 · Dusk, homeward** *(before the deck reveal — sets the lantern light Beat 6 needs)*

> Going home, the Mire does its dusk thing: gold, then grey, then dark. Bats first. Then the frogs change key. The heat finally lets go of your shoulders. Remy lights the stern lantern and the water hands it back to you in pieces. And behind the boat, the channel closes up quietly — like it's already forgetting you were ever there.

**7 · Night water** *(the last leg — leads straight into the run sheet's heron beat)*

> Full dark now — and the water starts making its own light. Faint. Blue-white. Moving in slow patterns that don't match the wind. The boat slides through it and leaves a glowing line behind you that takes a long, long time to heal. It's beautiful. It's the kind of beautiful where you keep your hands inside the boat without anyone telling you to.

*Then Remy, quiet at the tiller — his wreck-tic, played straight: he counts you. Out loud. By name. All five.* **"Good. All right. Good."** *(If you're spending the Fear: this is the moment someone looks at the bow-post, and the heron is already there.)*

## Beat 1 — The Send-Off *(dawn, the Mooring)*

Brin meets them at the dock to hand over the job: the map, the two tasks (the skin and the count), and — finally said out loud — the clue about the maker's stamps she's been sitting on since Session 4.

> "Rookery's here." She taps the map. "Count nests, count eggs, count what's guarding them. Season over season, that's the arithmetic that tells me how bad this is. And one more thing — you'll pass the shedding hollow on the way. If the water's quiet, bring me one fresh casting. Whole. Don't cut it off a snag, don't fold it. Whole."

**The stamps line — she nods at the crates on their deck:**

> "Been meaning to say. The cage your cub broke out of, and those crates on your boat — same maker's stamps. I've seen those stamps before, cached out in the deep channels. Whoever built that snake's road built your boat's road too. Sit with that out there."

**The Whimsy button** *(60 seconds, warm-then-cold — Whimsy is the rescued owlbear cub healing at the Ranging House)*: she's eating like a landslide and still hates everyone but Fletcher. Then Brin, quieter: *"One change since your Witch talked to her. She used to cry at night. Now she does it in daylight too. Whatever's coming — it heard the conversation."* That's the mother owlbear drawing closer (Front 9 — [[set-piece-the-mother-arrives]]). Plant it. Sail.

## Beat 2 — The Wending *(the way out — one complication, then the watchers)*

🎲 One roll on [[mire-complications]] — let the Mire say good morning. Then, whatever it was, the lizardfolk make their silent appearance:

> Somewhere past the third channel mouth, Fletcher — you feel watched, and then you find it: heads. Three of them, just above the waterline, dead still, spaced like fence posts. Tessak. They're not hunting you. They're *reading* you — and they're reading the boat longer than they're reading the people. Those carved marks on your hull. Then the heads go under, all three at once, no ripple.

*(Why they care: the hull markings identify this as a smuggler's boat — one of Gault's. Say nothing today; it pays off at [[set-piece-the-cold-cache]].)*

## Beat 3 — The Kept Camp *(quiet beat, 10 minutes — the book, and the wrongness)*

The two hunters who vanished chasing the snake left a camp behind, and the camp is *wrong*: two seasons out here, and it looks freshly kept. The party doesn't learn why until the rookery — the guardian spirit has been tending it.

> It's been out here two seasons, and it's wrong in one specific way: it's *tidy*. Bedrolls rolled. Kit stacked — dry, square, like a shop display. Nobody stacked it. Fletcher — two seasons in the Mire and not one thing here is chewed, molded, or moved. Weather doesn't do this. Scavengers don't *skip* a camp. Something has been keeping it.

- **What's here:** the **tally-book** — the dead hunter Gravois's survey log, wrapped in oilcloth, bone dry, squared on a stone like it's waiting for pickup (Tenson will already have his hands out) · **six broadheads** etched with tally-marks ([[items/loot-ledger|Gravois's Tally-Broadheads]]) · two bedrolls. Two men. No bodies.
- **Isotta's read — no roll, hers by right:** nothing lingers here. No ghost, no pull. Whatever these men were owed, it was paid; the dead crossed clean. **Say why that matters:** out here, that's the norm — the swamp is spirit country; ghosts are a *town* problem ([[cosmology]]).
- **The question the camp asks — don't answer it yet:** who's been keeping it? The answer stands up at the rookery. If someone guesses "a spirit," reward it — the offering etiquette they've seen in town ([[the-kept-root]]) suddenly matters in the field.

*No roll needed. If the table wants one: Instinct 11 to reconstruct the hunters' last day from the kit — it ends at a shooting blind facing the water, and the blind is the part that should have worked.*

## Beat 4 — The Hollow *(the casting — tension is the dice)*

The shedding spot. One fresh, intact shed skin is here for the taking — and the extraction happens in total, unnatural silence, because the thing that shed it could come back.

> The shedding hollow is a bowl of slack water ringed in root-arches, and it's *littered* with castings — years of them, gone to lace. One is new. Draped over a root like a shirt over a chair. Entire. And here's what your ears notice before your head does: the water's gone quiet. Not calm. *Quiet.* Frogs, bugs, birds — all of it, stopped.

🎲 **Finesse 13, group** — extracting twelve feet of shed skin, whole, off wet roots, while everyone whispers without agreeing to whisper. Help with Hope works; so does Leavy's steadiness. **It is not there. It could come back.** Fail forward: the casting tears (Brin gets most of it; so does the reveal) or the extraction costs time and the light for Beat 5.

**Leavy's bonus:** the hollow's slack water grows the rare reagent her potion quest needs ([[leavy-arc]] — the Draught). One quiet handful; no roll unless she's greedy.

## Beat 5 — The Rookery *(the spirit, the count, and the trip's real fight)*

Three things happen here, in order: the party meets **Old Plenty** (the rookery's guardian spirit — a giant spoonbill, old, proud, and starving, because a spirit is only as strong as the thing it keeps, and its colony is dying); they do Brin's nest count; and the fight breaks out — the gar that have been eating the rookery show up to feed.

**What you see:** a bend of root-shelves rising out of the shallows, stacked with nests — **spoonbills and egrets**, pale birds on dark wood. Or there should be. Half the shelves are bare. The birds that remain are packed onto the high ledges, too quiet.

**Old Plenty stands up before anyone lands:**

> Something you took for a snag unfolds — and it's a spoonbill. The size of a punt. Standing on water that doesn't dip under it, feathers the grey of old salt. It doesn't threaten. It doesn't greet. It counts. "Forty nests, four winters gone. Nineteen, two winters. Six, this one." A beat. "I am what keeps this place. Look how much keeping is left in me."

**Playing it:** delivery tic — **it speaks only in tallies and counts.** Grief comes out as arithmetic. It never begs. Meet it with the offering etiquette from town: never ask its name, never thank it directly, offer it a *first* (the first ladle of Remy's supper works — [[the-kept-root]] rules, field edition).

🎲 **The parley — Presence or Instinct 12,** or pure roleplay if the table honors the etiquette. **What it wants:** the old bull gar dead — it can no longer drive the gar off, and the bull eats a nest-shelf a week. **What it gives:**

- **The camp, explained** *(shown at Beat 3, said here)*: *"Two hunters. Mine to keep, once they ended in my water. I kept their camp until someone came. You came."*
- **The testimony — said plainly by a starving spirit:** *"Your hunters were never hidden. It watched their blind before they chose the blind. I count everything in my water — and that thing would not stay counted."* *(Meaning: even a spirit can't track the snake. Sit with that.)*
- **The partner** *(open thread)*: *"The second man went into it. I counted him going in."* No body. Ever.
- **The Isotta line — spirits honor each other's ledgers.** It counts the boat, once: *"Your boat carries five. My water counts four. The pale one is on another keeper's ledger. I do not touch another keeper's ledger."* Then it says nothing more about it, no matter who asks. *(This is the likeliest trigger for Isotta confronting the heron — if she does, open [[the-tallykeeper#The Grace Period]] and run the Introduction as written there.)*

🎲 **The count — Knowledge or Instinct 12,** fading light — Brin's protocol, Tenson keeping the tally like it's scripture. The numbers ARE the story: nests, yes — **guarded, no.** And the reason is feeding as they count:

> Something rolls in the shallows under the nearest nest-shelf — then three more somethings. Gar. A whole boil of them, working the rookery like a market stall. And under the boil, longer than your boat is wide, an old bull grown fat on all of it comes around slow, and looks at the lantern.

**Run the fight:** 6–8 **Mire Gar** as Minions ([[mire-bestiary#Mire Gar]] base) plus **the Old Bull** — full stat block: [[mire-bestiary#The Bull Gar]] (**Difficulty 13**, hits like a slammed door, dives after every attack; SRD-scale-checked). **If the parley landed, Old Plenty spends itself to help:** its wing-shadow pins the boil — run **3–4 minions instead of 6–8**; the bull is the party's. The ecology lesson, in teeth, for anyone who asks why the rookery's dying: **the snake emptied the nursery pools, the fish that guarded the rookery starved out, and the scavengers moved up to eat eggs.** The party is fighting the *consequence* of the snake two sessions before they meet the snake. Brin pays a bonus for the bull's carcass — and if they offer the **first cut** to the rookery, Old Plenty inclines like a falling tree deciding not to.

**After the fight — the cost, shown** *(one line, then move — helping cost the spirit part of itself)*:

> When it folds its wings, there's less of it than there was. Noticeably. It counts one more time — "Six" — and seems to find the price fair.

## Beat 6 — The Deck at Dusk *(the reveal — then cut to black)*

Homeward, last light. The skin gets unrolled, the seam gets seen, Leavy says the line, and Fletcher's journal delivers the session's final image.

> It's beautiful, is the thing. Twelve feet of ghost-skin going amber in the lantern light. And then Leavy — you see it. Right down the spine. The scale pattern on the left side and the scale pattern on the right side… don't match. Two different animals' skins. And they meet in a *straight line*, nose to tail, like a seam. Like tailoring.

**Leavy says it** *(her mouth, her line)*: *"This animal has been… worked on. Brewed. Fletcher — someone MADE this."* Then the brew-read, if she processes a sample: fixative residue, preservation alchemy worked years deep. You can taste the workshop.

**And the gut-punch — no roll, costs nothing, worth the whole day:**

> Fletcher. You go to your journal — three years of pressed castings, your whole hunt in one book — and you hold the lantern close, and you look at the jaw-hinge on the oldest sample. It's there. Faint. It was always there. A brand: **2H-3.** Every casting. Every jaw-hinge. It's been in your pack for three years.

Cut to black. *(If the [[session-05-run-sheet|run sheet]]'s night-water beats — Remy's quiet question to Leavy, the heron on the bow-post — want a home, they live in the quiet minutes just before this.)*

## After-Scenes *(next morning, 5 minutes — don't skip the first one)*

- **The tally-book, delivered.** Brin takes Gravois's book like it's a casket flag. Reads a page. Closes it. Then — the emotional twist reserved for Fletcher this session ([[twist-the-knife]] Fletcher #1): *"Two years I've written that man as 'missing, presumed.' You brought my hunter home."* She means Fletcher. She looks at him while she says it. In front of everyone. Watch the professional try to receipt an emotion.
- **The count and the casting, paid.** Guild rate, like people the Guild intends to hire again. When she sees the seam: she goes very still, then — *"I need to write some letters. And you need to not talk about this at the Mooring."*
- **Threads opened:** who brands an animal? *(→ Tenson's paper chase begins)* · why won't the Tessak fish past the Double Knot? · the second hunter went *into* the snake — no body, ever · even spirits can't count the snake · "another keeper's ledger" *(Isotta — if she didn't confront the heron, the confrontation is still coming)* · three sets of eyes have now read the hull markings.

## 😨 Fear Menu — the boat day *(scripted moments — say "I'm spending a Fear" out loud, move the token, then read)*

Today's dread rule: **the snake never appears, at any price — its absence IS the horror.** Spend Fear on the Mire being older, hungrier, and more awake than the party. (Legal spends per SRD, verified in [[STATUS]]: interrupt/extra GM move · adversary or environment Fear feature · add an adversary Experience.)

**The Wending** *(transit — spend at most one)*
- **1 · The Mire re-braided** *(interrupt):* the channel Remy chose yesterday isn't there. Roots where water was. He stares at it a long two seconds, then: *"…Fine. We go around."* Costs daylight, not blood — but let the table watch their guide be wrong about his own water.
- **1 · The second look** *(extra move — only if Beat 2's watchers already fired):* the Tessak heads come up again. Closer. Still reading the hull marks. One of them taps the water twice — flat palm, deliberate — and all three go under. Nothing follows. Do not explain the taps.

**The Kept Camp** *(one spend max — the camp stays quiet)*
- **1 · Squared again** *(extra move, as they pole away):* Fletcher — you look back, the way you always look back. The kit you moved. It's squared again. Already. Nobody saw it happen. *(This is Old Plenty's housekeeping — eerie now, warm in hindsight at Beat 5. Say nothing.)*

**The Hollow** *(the extraction — dread on a budget)*
- **1 · The hull-touch:** something touches the boat once, from below. Unhurried. Like a knuckle checking a melon. It does not come back.
- **1 · The minute:** every frog and bug within earshot stops at once — one full minute of held breath — then starts again mid-note, like nothing happened. The rhythm out here belongs to something, and it skipped a beat.

**The Rookery — the gar fight** *(where the pool earns its keep)*
- **⚡ The bull's Fear feature** *([[mire-bestiary#The Bull Gar]] — **Breach**):* it dives after every attack — spend to have it resurface where nobody's weapon is pointing, under the count-keeper's shelf (Agility Reaction 13 or into the water).
- **2 · The nest-shelf collapse** *(environment):* the shelf under a mid-count PC lets go — Agility **Reaction 13** or into the water with the boil.
- **1 · Grown fat on the vacuum** *(add Experience):* the bull has done this to boats before — add its Experience to one attack, and narrate *why*: it goes for the lantern-hand first.
- **1 · The bull turns on Old Plenty** *(interrupt — only if the parley landed):* mid-fight, the bull ignores the party and lunges at the spirit spending itself above the boil. Nobody has to save Old Plenty. But the table gets one round knowing they *could.* What they do is who they are.

**The Night Water** *(one spend, then let the beauty stand)*
- **1 · Already there:** when someone finally looks at the bow-post, the heron is *already there.* Has been. Say nothing else — voiceover 7 hands you this moment.

**Never tonight:** the snake, at any price · Old Plenty as a menace (the dread lives in what's eating its rookery, not in it) · a second spend on the camp. **Banked for S6+:** whatever touched the hull gets a face the day they run the Wending at night.
