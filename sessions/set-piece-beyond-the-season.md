# Set Piece — Beyond the Season *(the Double Knot convergence)*

*The campaign's martial climax — the final battle of the [[gault]] arc, fired after [[set-piece-the-bad-season]] (~S9–10). **Stats live elsewhere:** Gault in [[gault]], the Season-Eater in [[set-piece-double-knot]], the mother in [[the-mother-owlbear]], the crew in [[sallowreach-roster]]. This file is the convergence: the doors, the phases, the three-force machine, and the ending menus. [[set-piece-double-knot]] remains the party-first door (they hunt the snake on their own terms); this is the race-lost door — he has the map, and everyone arrives at once.*

*The title is the Tessak's word made flesh: **tessavrak**, taking beyond the season. Tonight is what it looks like when a man finally does it all the way.*

---

## 🧭 In Plain Words *(the whole night — no jargon)*

After the raid, Gault is rich and homeless: the town knows his face now, so his smuggling routes through the city are dead, and everything he owns — the stolen ray haul, the stolen maps, the crated owlbear cub, eight years of profit — is loaded onto three barges heading southwest. He is going to collect the biggest contract of his life: the two-headed serpent he lost in transit years ago, which a laboratory will pay double for, captured alive. He has hired the largest crew of his career, and he has the dead hunters' maps to the nest. What he doesn't know: the serpent has spent three years learning his crates, his caches, and his men — to it, his expedition is the most legible prey in the swamp. And behind him, coming through the water and the dark, is the mother owlbear, tracking her stolen child — with the party following her. Four forces arrive at the nest ground on the same night: the poacher and his crew, the serpent that cannot be surprised, the mother who cannot be stopped, and the four people who love each other. Everything ends here. He bet all of it.

**Two goals:** the party gets the war they were promised — waves to cut through, a monster to face, a villain to finish — and every arc's payoff (the shot, the reunion, the arithmetic) lands *inside* the battle, not after it.

**Invariant in every door:** Gault is present, committed, and cannot walk away — everything he owns is on the water tonight. For the first time in the campaign, *he* is the one who can't leave.

## 🧳 What He Brings *(the expedition — his whole fortune, floating)*

- **Three barges + a cage-scow:** the basin haul in sealed jars, the crated cub, the caging rig ("nothing that punctures the skin"), and his ledger — eight years of buyers, routes, and manifests in one waterproof case.
- **The crew — his biggest ever, and rented:** run **6–10 Wending Runner Lackeys** (Minions) + **3–4 Wending Runners** (Standards) + one **foreman** (a Runner at Difficulty 13 with *Keeps the Crew Working +2*). Port-town muscle; no loyalty, good pay. [[sallowreach-roster]] canon: *"they don't ask questions and don't stay loyal."* Tonight that's a mechanic (the Morale clock, below).
- **Gault himself** — [[gault]], Tier 2 Solo, run with his full kit. He is not managing an exit tonight. There isn't one.
- **The plan:** bait lines rigged from the dead hunters' maps, the crew as beaters driving the snake toward the cage-scow. It is a good plan against any animal that can be surprised. *(Say that sentence to yourself before Phase 3.)*

### ⏱ The Morale Clock *(Countdown 4 — the fun one; draw it visible)*

The crew is rented loyalty. Tick it when: the snake kills a crewman · the party drops the foreman · a sabotage from Phase 2 detonates · the mother screams her arrival · Gault visibly loses control of the take. **At 4, the crew routs** — they seize a barge and run, and the battlefield halves. The party can fight the expedition — or they can *bankrupt* it. Fighting smart shrinks the enemy roster in real time; say the clock's name out loud so they know it's a lever.

## 🌊 The Phases

### Phase 1 — The Follow *(the approach; ~15 min)*

Door A, following the mother:

> *"You don't track her. You just keep the sound in front of you — that low note under hearing, the one you felt in the hull the first time. She doesn't stop at night. She doesn't feed. Remy said it two days ago and nobody's argued since: 'I've guided everything in this swamp. I've never followed anything that doesn't rest.'"*

At the old Tessak markers — one beat, no roll: the clan surfaces in a silent line and watches the party pass into the water they themselves won't fish. No words. *(If the Weighing was won, one of them raises a spear-butt — not a salute; a* marker. *You are past the line. What happens past the line is yours.)*

### Phase 2 — The Yards *(his staging camp; the heist DNA — reward planning)*

His camp sits on the last dry ground before the Knot: barges moored, bait lines going out, the cage-scow winched and ready. The party arrives with hours. **Sabotage menu** (each: one roll, Diff 13, one PC's lane; each success = a scripted detonation later + a Morale tick when it blows):

- **Spike the cage** (Fletcher/Finesse): the winch jams at the worst moment — the Take fails one beat earlier.
- **Salt the bait** (Leavy/Knowledge): her wrong-smell craft, inverted — the bait lines read as *trap* to a tongue that tastes air. The snake ignores his plan entirely.
- **Walk among them** (Isotta/Presence): the crew are port-town hands who've heard the raid story by now. One quiet conversation seeds the question: *"Did he tell you what happened to the last town that worked for him?"* — Morale starts the night at 1.
- **Read the manifest** (Tenson/Knowledge): the barges ride low and wrong. Fires the clue (ledger below): this isn't an expedition, it's a liquidation.

**Or they skip all of it and attack at dawn.** Fine. The phases still run; they're just louder.

### Phase 3 — The Take *(his trap springs; the party chooses their moment)*

> *"The bait lines sing, the beaters start their sweep, the cage-scow's jaws stand open — and for one long minute it is all working, and you watch him allow himself one half-breath of the smugness you remember. Then the water where the nets are goes still. And a crewman on the* far *barge — behind everyone, outside the sweep, nowhere near the plan — just leaves the deck. Straight down. No scream. The plan is pointed at where the snake was. The snake is where the plan came from."*

**The party strikes whenever they want** — during his setup, at the spring, or in the half-breath after it fails. Whenever they move, Phase 4 begins.

### Phase 4 — Loose *(the three-force battle; the long middle)*

**The battlefield rule — one free enemy beat per GM turn:** besides normal spotlights, narrate ONE thing another force does to another force — the snake takes a beater; the crew's line breaks; Gault re-rigs the winch alone. The war moves *around* the party without costing your Fear. Fear spends stay for moments aimed *at* them.

- **The snake hunts the legible.** Three years of reading hunters, and his crew is a *textbook* — downwind creeps, bait lines, panic. It eats the expedition in order of legibility. If the party carries Leavy's brews ([[set-piece-double-knot]] prereqs), they are *nothing* to it while they fight the crew — the arc's whole thesis staged as spectacle: they watch what they would have been. If they never brewed, they're prey like everyone else — say so with its first look.
- **The crew fights until the math breaks** (Morale clock). Minions die in sweeps; Standards hold until the foreman drops.
- **Gault fights for the score, not for the crew.** Run his features ([[gault]]); at half HP, **When He Commits** — perform it, the exit-math finally gone.
- **Fletcher's freeze fires here** — the arc's load-bearing image, ported intact from [[set-piece-double-knot]]: first Close contact with the snake, GM spends a Fear, he cannot act toward it until his anchor holds him — his name, said plainly, while a war happens around them. Someone holds. Someone covers. Stage it, don't rush it.

### 😨 Fear Menu *(performed; spend 6–9 across the battle — the biggest night of the campaign)*

- **1 — The Learning Head reads someone** *(the snake's star move — [[set-piece-double-knot]])*: second use of any tactic = countered, disadvantage. Say "learned" every time.
- **1 — The net, one last time:** Gault pins the PC who has cost him most. No line. He's done talking forever.
- **1 — The light dies** *(his old trick, failing)*: he moves through a dying patch of glow — but this deep, the light isn't his either, and it comes back *wrong* and shows everyone where he is. His tricks are running out of country.
- **2 — Inventory** *(his darkest move — once, and the table should gasp)*: the strike meant for him — snake, talon, or arrow — and he hauls the nearest crewman into it by the collar and is already moving. Hired hand number twelve. *This is the man, on-screen, at last.*
- **2 — Drag Under, staged** *(the snake's kill move — [[set-piece-double-knot]])*: if it takes Isotta, give her the moment — the thing built to drown people meets the one person in the Mire who can't.
- **3 — Her arrival** *(door A only — this spend IS Phase 5)*: see below.

### Phase 5 — The Mother *(door A; the fourth force)*

> *"Everything stops hearing itself. From the treeline: wood — big wood — giving way, in rhythm, like a drum coming closer. The crew stops fighting you. The snake's spare head comes up off the water. And through the last screen of mangroves she arrives like weather with a heartbeat — and she is not looking at any of you. She is looking at the scow with the crate on it."*

- **She is aimed at the crate.** Everything between her and Whimsy is an obstacle, not an enemy ([[the-mother-owlbear]] rules: lead with the want; she never presses a retreat off her line). Morale ticks immediately.
- **Clear her path and she is the party's battering ram** — not controlled, never commanded, but *aimed*: pull crew aside, drop a net-line, shout the lane open, and she goes through Gault's operation like the raid went through the boards. Let the players feel the raid inverted — this time the unstoppable thing is on their side of the ledger.
- **Free the cub and the Calm-Down opens mid-battle** ([[set-piece-the-mother-arrives]] countdown, ported live): weapons down in the middle of a war, the crate cracked, the cub pushed forward. If they land it, the reunion read-aloud plays *while the battle's wreckage burns behind them* — the campaign's loudest night folding into its smallest sound.
- **Guardrail, absolute:** no cub harmed by accident — telegraph twice, always. That outcome is chosen or it doesn't happen.

### Phase 6 — The Reckoning *(the menus — the table picks every door)*

**Gault** *(his invariants hold to the end — no begging, no bargaining, no explaining)*:
- **They finish him.** He gets no speech. If the party carries Brin's grievance, the Name beat is here ([[set-piece-the-last-inventory]] — *"...Which one was that?"*). Then it's done, and the Mire is exactly as quiet as it was going to be anyway.
- **The Third Knife** *(the arc's crown — [[fletcher-arc]])*: the snake takes him — and it is not going his way — and Fletcher has one clean shot *to save Gault.* Brin's creed says take it. Three years say lower the bow. **Do not rescue Fletcher from this choice, and do not tell him what it makes him either way.**
- **They take him alive.** The only cell that holds him is paper: Brin's three years of files get a face, and [[caius-maren|Caius]] gets very courteous about a courier who moved unmarked imperial cargo. Handing Gault to Caius is handing Caius a lever — say nothing, let them notice.
- **He slips the field.** Dice happen. Then [[set-piece-the-last-inventory]] stops being a door and becomes the epilogue: a man with no crew, no fortune, and no buyers, alone in the deep water, too stubborn to stop. Run it within a session or two — small, human, and ugly, exactly as written.

**The snake:**
- **The shot is Fletcher's.** Engineer nothing. When the opening comes, give him all the time he wants; say nothing after the roll ([[set-piece-double-knot]] — the image the whole arc aims at, now with a war holding its breath around it).
- **It takes the expedition and goes deeper.** It eats what it came for and vanishes past the Knot with the campaign's coldest lesson: it was never anyone's cargo. Front 10 stays open — the mound is still out there.
- **It's already in the cage** (they struck late): now the party owns a caged abomination, a dead man's contract, and a decision with no good doors. That's not a failure state. That's a session.

**The mound** — the clutch decision stays where it lives ([[set-piece-double-knot]] §The Mound): a scene, not a roll, chosen out loud, never during the fight.

## 🌅 After *(short scenes; take the whole session's last half hour)*

- **The jars.** The basin haul is aboard, alive. The trip home stops at the brightfin basin, and the party pours the light back into the water — the raid's emptiest dawn, reversed by their own hands. Remy counts. Out loud this time.
- **Whimsy and the mother** go home — up the channels, one cub riding between her shoulders, *(if Marcel's brokerage ever lands)* maybe two. Fletcher watches until you stop describing it.
- **The castings come home.** Brin re-pins her map wall in silence, then: *"Did he say which one?"* — and whatever the answer, she nods once, like a ledger balancing.
- **The ledger.** Eight years of buyers, routes, and redacted stamps — leverage on Caius, ammunition for Tenson's report, and the epilogue war's paper trail (2H-1 and 2H-2 are *in it*). Route it via [[items/loot-ledger]].
- **The crew scatter** carries the story down the corridor: the Mire towns learn what happened to the man who took beyond the season. The standing order dies with no one to pay it.
- **The letter.** Fletcher finally writes home — not *job complete*, something longer. Slide him paper, or just ask: *"What's the first line?"* End the campaign's biggest night on his answer.
- **Fronts:** close or recut 9 and 10; tick 6 if Caius got his lever; the Tessak file the whole night under *the law held* — and if the party passes the old markers on the way home, **now** comes the head-dip.

## 🎯 Win Condition

It felt like a war and ended like a hunt: the table spent the night making real choices between four fires; every arc paid inside the battle (the freeze held, the shot was his, the reunion happened with wreckage burning behind it); and when it was over, nobody said "boss fight" — somebody said *"the basin,"* and everyone got in the boat.
