# Tenson — Arc: The Reconciliation

> **Status: ACTIVE** *(designed 2026-07-02).* Everything player-facing on [[tenson|Tenson's sheet]] and in [[tenson-handout|his handout]] stays true — this is the GM half. The arc is welded to the main plot on purpose (Fronts 2, 6, 7 in [[world-in-motion]]), which means less room for invention and **no room for error**: this is the one arc where the campaign's spine is the personal quest. The design compensates the only way that's safe — by putting every reveal **on paper the player can hold.** Tuned to what the player has actually shown at the table: strict, polite, procedural, allergic to signing anything he hasn't rewritten. The arc's thesis in one line: ***a true report can be a weapon for the wrong side — and the counter-weapon is also a report.***

## The Answer (GM only — the whole arc in one paragraph)

Silence sent Tenson to answer one question: *why does the wall keep asking for money, and why does the money keep being refused?* The audit, followed honestly, finds that **the wall is being killed from both ends by two men who have never compared notes.** [[Oswin Cray]] — the petitioner, the victim, the man begging for funds — has been quietly diverting two coins in every five of the budget he *does* receive, through fabricated "research disbursements," to service a gambling debt held by [[Marcel Dray]]. [[Caius Maren]] — the fiscally prudent liaison — is denying the supplemental funds **on pretext**, because he wants the wall to fail: every denial, every drowned house, every incident report (including the one in Tenson's satchel right now) is a page in an unauthorized annexation file that needs one good catastrophe to be complete. Document thirteen in that file is the party's own shipwreck, which Caius purchased. And underneath both crimes sits the finding no ledger can hold: **even at full funding, honestly spent, the wall is losing** — the Mire's growth outruns two hundred years of budget assumptions, for reasons the money cannot explain. The audit ends with a thief who is also the only man holding the wall up, a saboteur whose paperwork is immaculate, and a remainder that doesn't reconcile. What Tenson *does* with a finding like that — who he reports, in what order, in what words — is the arc. The title is the accountant's word for it and the city's: **reconciliation** — making two records agree.

**In plain table language:** the further the audit goes, the more it proves — and the more dangerous the truth gets to say out loud. Filing the honest report gets Oswin destroyed, Marcel paid, the wall orphaned, and Caius his crisis. The arc's endgame is Tenson learning what Caius already knows: *documents are written toward conclusions* — and then writing better ones.

## The Auditor's Dilemma (the emotional engine)

The two gut-punches [[tenson|his sheet]] loaded are both scheduled here:

1. **The broke wizard begging.** The ledgers, read right, convict Oswin — and Oswin is a lonely, kind, weak man who will make the party tea, teach them everything he knows, and, if he learns they mean to expose him, go very still and say *"Please don't."* The first time Tenson's competence produces a true finding, the finding has a face.
2. **Document thirteen.** The incident report Tenson is currently redlining was commissioned to sit in a file *beside the record of the party's own attempted murder.* The night he understands what Caius's file is *for*, he also understands what it already contains. The most polite man at the dinner table bought their deaths, then asked them to co-sign the paperwork.

And under both, the quiet one: **the pivot.** This assignment is Tenson's proof he can be brains instead of brawn — and the assignment, done perfectly, produces a truth that hurts everyone it touches. The desk-life was supposed to be *clean*. That was the whole point of wanting it. The arc honors the accountant dream, then makes it expensive, per house rule.

## The Reveal Ladder (GM — gate the truth in this order)

Rung 0 already fired at the dinner: **the incident report** taught him that a document can be built from true statements and still be a lie ([[incident-report-handout]] — every finding steered). Every later rung is that lesson again, at higher stakes. Each rung lives on **paper the player is handed** — never in an NPC's mouth first:

1. **The denials are pretext.** The refusals are procedurally wrong on purpose: a misapplied provision, same-day turnarounds, an office that shouldn't hold fiscal authority at all — and not one denial ever disputes that the wall is failing. *(Fires in S4 — [[oswin-papers-handout|the correspondence]]; hardened into **pattern** by the Kit Room stack, [[sable-crue|Sable's]] payoff: the same hand throttling a *different* channel.)*
2. **The petitioner is also the thief.** The salt arithmetic doesn't balance: labor at zero, "research disbursements" from a man who says out loud he stopped researching, books that balance too perfectly. Roughly two coins in five never touch the wall. *(Fires whenever the player cracks [[oswin-papers-handout|the expense summary]] — S4 if he's sharp, later with help. Oswin never confesses; the paper does. **Whiff-proof by design:** if the summary never cracks, the Pale Room rumor — "someone connected to the failing ward gambles here and owes badly," [[session-03-prep]]'s casino tease, already canon — is this rung said aloud by the whole city, and Marcel's copy of the evidence is the confession-grade backstop.)*
3. **The money's grave is the Pale Room.** Where do two coins in five go? Sinope's casino tip (S2, live), the too-high laugh, and the discovery that [[Marcel Dray]] holds both the debt **and** a copy of the evidence — and has been waiting, holstered, for an instrument. *(Front 7 opens its door.)*
4. **The file has a purpose — and the party is in it.** Caius's two-year incident file is an annexation petition in embryo; the denials feed it; the shipwreck was bought. *(Late. Best source: Marcel — but players will not spontaneously ask a crime boss who blew up their boat, so **the bridge is written down, two channels:** shown — the scorch-patterning on the Ridgeback's salvaged timbers reads as a **rigged charge** to a professional eye (Fletcher's hazard-read, or [[Théo Rouchard|Théo]] mid-build if the shield gets commissioned — [[items/loot-ledger|loot ledger]]); said — by [[Remy Duval|Remy]], whose boat it was and who wants the name: "A hired job? On my water? Nothing gets* hired *on this water without the Pale Room hearing the price." That line is what sends the party to Marcel with the right question. The Truth-Teller's Ink is the corroborator, not the road. The moment this lands, Front 6 becomes personal for all four PCs — Tenson is just the one holding the proof.)*

Rungs 1–2 make the audit real (two culprits, opposite motives). Rung 3 gives it a bank. Rung 4 gives it a war. **Order within 1–2 is flexible — it's a sandbox** — but hold 4 until the party has met Marcel or done the paper-chase legwork; it's the arc's midpoint detonation, not its opener.

## The Reports to Silence (the arc's recurring instrument)

The audit has a **deliverable**, and the deliverable is the character. Every few sessions, Tenson owes his patron a dispatch — and what he chooses to put in it is the arc's moral instrument, played in his own handwriting:

- **Does he name Oswin before he understands him?** A single accurate sentence — *"the maintainer's books do not reconcile"* — sent early, ends Oswin from a thousand miles away. Silence has Guild reach. Tenson should feel the weight of that pen every time.
- **Does he name Caius without proof?** Silence is wary of exactly the channels Caius operates through ([[silence|her file]], GM-eyes) — an unproven accusation up that chain warns the wrong people.
- **Does he say the word *tethered*?** The report that admits he can't come home is the report that triggers [[silence#The Gut-Punch|her fork]] — the kind version or the sharp one. His call when to send it. Let him sit on it as long as he dares; her letters get more pointed as he does.

**GM procedure:** when he writes one, ask for it *in brief, in character* — three lines of what he includes and what he leaves out. That's the whole scene. Her replies (forwarded, late, warm, precise) are the screw: use them to tighten, humanize, or — only if the table earns it — turn.

## The Verbatim Witness (Bigsby — the arc's inside thread)

The Embassy has a crack, and it's the thing in the corner holding a pen: **[[bigsby|Mr. Bigsby, Attendant 7]]**, the living-spell transcriptionist. Caius's entire method is *framing* — and he shares his house with a construct that is **constitutionally incapable of it.** Bigsby cannot state a falsehood, only decline; his transcript is the *legal original* under imperial records procedure; and he must produce records to a properly credentialed request — a production order even Caius cannot countermand, only delay by challenging the requester's **standing** (Tenson's letter is arguable; a warrant from [[silence|Silence]] is not). Caius has spent two years treating him as furniture, and that blindness is load-bearing: it's the door the thread walks in through, and it stays shut to Caius until far too late.

**The heavy hint (players will never invent "subpoena the furniture" — so it's delivered at three brightnesses, all designed in [[session-04-redline-meeting]]):** shown — Bigsby verbatim-quotes the dinner mid-meeting, stammer and all, contradicting the report while Caius nods along unbothered; said — Tenson's free citadel expertise, no roll: *the attendant's transcript IS the legal original*; said again — Lucien at the Long Room, if the table hasn't bitten within a session: *"The spell is the record, man. Ask what else the attendant heard."*

**The relationship is won by correct use, not charm:** precise questions, proper citations, being *read* for the first time in two years — the courtesy beat and the five rules of him live in [[bigsby|his file]]. Scope guardrail: he proves the **steering**, the **same-day turnarounds**, and the **file's index** (thirteen entries — and the thirteenth predates the dinner invitation). He holds no crimes: the shipwreck's authorship stays Marcel's road (Rung 4), and he knows nothing of spirits or bindings. He also sets up the arc's missing set piece: the late-arc **Embassy records production** — the lawful caper, standing secured, the whole party in the building — designed as its own file when the table points at it.

What he offers the arc, if Tenson finds him: **professional confirmation, not evidence.** He read the room-grab as a legacy case being built, wrote the capital one anonymous letter, and got the silence that tells Tenson exactly what naming-Caius-upward is worth (it sharpens the L73 caution into a person: *"I did it properly. — No. I did it* quietly. *Properly implies it was read."*). ## The Auditor's Mercy (the Oswin resolution — never yes/no)

When Tenson finally holds proof of the skim, the obvious moves are both traps: *report him* (Oswin destroyed, Marcel collects, the wall loses its only maintainer, Caius files the failure) and *bury it* (the auditor becomes what he came to indict). Per house rule, the real resolution is negotiated in **time, terms, and collateral** — and it's the most in-character move in the campaign: **Tenson can restructure the debt.**

- **A restitution schedule.** The skim repaid into the ward, quarter by quarter, documented — Tenson holding the iron box as collateral and the deadline as leverage. Not forgiveness. *Amortization.* Oswin would weep at the mercy of being treated as an account instead of a criminal.
- **Terms:** the ward back to full application immediately (labor rehired, real salt grade); the gambling stops (verifiable — Marcel's door is watchable); the quarterly *observations* keep coming, because someone at the Citadel actually reads them.
- **The Marcel problem is the collateral's collateral:** the debt to the Pale Room still exists, and Marcel wants Oswin *fired*, not solvent ([[marcel-dray#What He Wants|his file]]). Any plan that saves Oswin runs through, around, or into Marcel — which is exactly the pressure that keeps this from being tidy. Marcel will respect a well-structured counter-offer more than a moral appeal. He's said so, in his way: *he finds people who are honest about wanting something refreshing.* **The full negotiation space — what he holds, what's free, what stacks, and what snaps back — is mapped in [[marcel-dray#The Table — the Marcel Decision Tree (GM reference)|his decision tree]].**
- **Or Tenson doesn't.** He's Orderborne. The rules exist for a reason. Filing the true report *is on the menu* — see Endgame — and the campaign should never punish him for integrity, only show him, in full, what it costs. That's not a trap; that's the theme.

## The Remainder (guardrail — the audit's edge)

**Tenson's arc explains the human half of the wall's failure and no more.** The skim and the sabotage are his to prove; the grief, the binding, the Interlock are the *campaign's* (per the no-lore-conduit rule — [[isotta-arc]] precedent). His thread touches the mystery exactly once, and it's the best kind of touch: **the honest numbers don't close.** When the skim is quantified and the denials are mapped, a competent auditor can show that even a fully funded, honestly spent wall is losing ground against a growth curve fifty years of flat budgets never anticipated. Oswin has been writing this in his quarterly observations for four years — *"always from the southwest… something in the city has its attention"* — and Silence is the one person who's read them ([[silence|her file]]). Tenson's contribution to the campaign's central question is an accountant's: **he proves the size of the thing the money can't account for.** He hands the party a *quantified anomaly* — then the spirits, the wall, the Celebration belong to Isotta's sight, Fletcher's water, Leavy's craft, the whole table. The auditor certifies that the mystery exists. He never gets to be its narrator.

## The Pivot (the dream, honored then priced)

- **Sable is the standing counter-argument** ([[sable-crue#The Foil|the foil]]): brawn, at peace, thriving. Every Drillfield scene quietly asks Tenson *what if it was never something to escape?* Never say it. Just play Sable happy, close by.
- **The Sweat First payoff pays the arc twice:** winning Sable takes the sports-medicine skill Tenson buried (Perrin's beat) — the *body* opens the door to the *records.* The Guardian gets to be the brains only after being generous with the brawn. The dented whistle ([[items/loot-ledger|loot ledger]]) makes it permanent.
- **Schedule at least one scene where the desk and the shield conflict directly:** the audit needs him at a table with documents at the exact hour someone needs a wall of muscle between them and harm — the wall breach, the mother owlbear, the Warden, Gault's people. The point is never that the dream is wrong. The point is that *he's both,* and pretending otherwise is the only actual mistake on the table.
- **The quiet victory condition** (hold for endgame, let the player find it): Sallowreach has no bank, no imperial credit, and one lender ([[marcel-dray|Marcel]]). A city like that doesn't need another guardian. **It needs an accountant.** The tether closed the road home — it may also be the only place on the continent where the dream has a vacancy.

## The Clients Runner (the dream, paid en route)

Sallowreach has no bank, no imperial credit, and exactly one lender — [[marcel-dray|Marcel]]. So once word spreads that the shipwrecked stranger *reads ledgers*, the city starts bringing Tenson things: a disputed tally, a contract nobody understood when they signed it, a season's books that won't close. **One small client vignette every couple of sessions.** Each one honors the accountant dream in the present tense instead of deferring it — and each one teaches Tenson, case by case, exactly how this city bleeds, so the endgame vacancy (*a city like this doesn't need another guardian; it needs an accountant*) is earned instead of announced.

### Client Zero — "The Berth Contract" *(whole party, table-ready)*

**The hook:** [[Remy Duval|Remy]] — still boatless since the wreck, quietly humiliated by every rental ([[The Mooring|his file and Sabine's]]) — has finally been offered a way back: **Sabine Aucoin's rent-to-own contract** on a used punt. He's going to sign it.

**The unspoken thing (the scene's real subject):** the party's boat is tied to **Remy's own personal dock.** He walks past a working boat every morning to go rent from Sabine — and he will never, ever ask for it. Not just pride: the **Honored Guest tradition.** It's his inn's name; it's the code he's kept his whole life ([[Remy Duval|his file]] — he takes it with genuine weight). *A host does not reach for what belongs to his guests.* He'd sooner sign Sabine's paper. He **is** signing Sabine's paper. The contract isn't only a trap — it's the proof of how deep the code runs, and the party finding that out is the gut-punch the job is built around.

**Triggers (never depend on Remy volunteering — he won't):**
- **Primary, pride-compatible:** he asks Tenson to review the paper, embarrassed, like it's nothing — *"Just — look it over first? Ten minutes. You're a numbers man. I'll feed you for a month."* Asking for *expertise* isn't asking for *help*; that's the loophole in his own code, and he doesn't notice he's using it.
- **Shown (the image, if they haven't caught on):** any morning at the inn — Remy stepping past their moored boat, pole and coin-purse in hand, headed for the rental shack. Let a player ask why.
- **Said aloud (the fallback mouth — Sabine, who else):** *"You want to know the best part, cher? He's got a working boat tied to his own dock — I can see it from my shack. And he's down here signing paper for a lesser one. 'Guests' boat,' he says. Honored guests."* [she taps the contract, not unkindly] *"I don't have to be a predator in this city. Pride does half my work for me."*

**The contract's three teeth** *(Tenson reads them in minutes — this is a small handout if the table wants one, or narrated redlining if not)*:
1. **The payments buy the rent down, never the boat.** No amortization — the principal never shrinks. Remy would pay for that punt three times over and own none of it.
2. **The Wardens are the sole assessors of damage.** The deposit-forfeiture scam, formalized in ink.
3. **The default clause converts arrears into "obligations transferable to the contract's holder."** A debt *built to be sold* — and everyone in this city knows who buys debt. *(A third channel into Rung 3, from a direction nobody expected: the Mooring's paper drains toward the Pale Room.)*

**The weld, as played (S4 broke it — the good way):** the original weld — Sabine's offered punt *was* Anselme's — died at the wall: Solène withdrew the sale and Anselme crossed ([[session-04-recap]]). Sabine's rack offer is now **ordinary Warden stock**, no mark on the transom, no story in the hull. The new weld sits across town: **the A.T. punt is in Solène's hands, and she can't work it.** She strips drowned houses for salvage while her father's boat sits under a tarp — selling it again is unthinkable, working it needs hands the family doesn't have, and every relocation notice on her row makes the tarp heavier. That boat is the Lease branch below.

**Why each PC is at the Mooring:**
- **Tenson** — the redline, aimed for the first time at someone he loves owing nothing to.
- **Isotta** — the coda: she closed the *man* at the wall ([[session-04-salt-wall]] As Played); this job can close what he *meant*. "I built Solène that boat so she'd never owe the Mooring a copper" — she's the only one at the shack who heard him say it. Casebook, final entry, in ink.
- **Fletcher** — leverage: his pipeline evidence is the thing Sabine *least* wants looked at (the Wardens skim for Marcel through the night berths — [[The Mooring|canon]]). A quiet card. Spend it carefully; burning Sabine costs standing the party needs later.
- **Leavy** — payment in passage: Sabine can put a name on a **Consortium-bound supply run** — the clean way into the gloamcap errand ([[session-04-gloamcap-run]]). The Mooring's favor is a door, not coin.

**The Sabine beat (the scene's engine):** she is not a villain — she is *exact*, and when Tenson redlines her contract at her own shack she is **delighted.** Nobody, in forty years, has ever actually read it. Respect unlocks negotiation; she warms to straight dealing (her file, canon). Per house rule the bargain is never yes/no — negotiate **time, terms, and collateral**: the amortization schedule, the assessor clause (independent assessment — Remy's own expertise, deliciously), what the transferable-debt clause gets replaced with, whether Fletcher's card stays in the pocket. *"Remy Duval's friends, redlining my paper. Sit down, cher. This is the best part of my* year.*"*

**The real question under the paper — a menu, not a script** *(the contract is the trigger; what the party does about the* boat *is the job)*:
- **The Partnership (the flagship — pure Tenson).** He can't make Remy accept a gift; Remy's code won't bend for kindness. But Remy *can sign terms.* Tenson papers dignity: a charter — the party owns the boat, Remy berths, maintains, and captains it; his guiding fees square the ledger; everyone's name on one honest page. The guest-code is satisfied because nothing was *given* — it was **contracted.** The accountant beats the tradition with its own weapon, and the counter-contract to Sabine's trap is the first document Tenson authors instead of audits. *(If he drafts it, let him actually draft it — terms at the table.)*
- **The Gift, made acceptable.** If the party insists on giving: the code cuts both ways — *guests may honor the host.* Framed as the debt of four lives, it can land — but the boat is a pipeline boat, covered in Gault's marks, and Remy's *"we don't do business with Gault"* is three sessions old. The gift that works is the **scrubbed hull** ([[items/loot-ledger|loot ledger]] — Théo strips the cache-marks; the disguise-vs-identity choice made for love), a new name on the transom, and a boat that has stopped being anyone else's. He will cry. He will pretend it's the varnish fumes.
- **⭐ The Thouvenel Lease** *(replaces "The Clean Punt" — S4 took Anselme's punt off the racks the good way; re-cut 2026-07-16, [[coin-in-sallowreach]]).* Solène holds her father's boat and can't work it; Remy needs a boat and won't be given one. **Tenson drafts the charter that lets two prides sign the same page:** Remy hires the A.T. punt from the Thouvenel family — schedule, terms, a named guarantor, *authored* this time instead of demanded (the Marcel Branch-2 rhyme, twice as loud) — and his guiding fees, topped up by party coin through work orders, become the rehousing money Solène would never take as charity ([[world-in-motion]] Front 3). The boat Anselme built so his daughter would never owe the Mooring a copper ends up **earning her family off the drowning row** — and never touches the Wardens' Book at all. Sabine, when she hears why Remy's off her racks: a long look, then — *"You didn't beat my paper, cher. You wrote better paper. That's called joining the trade."* (If the renegotiation never fired, "the reader" respect fires here instead.) And because a bargain must keep costing: every installment is a scene candidate, and Solène watching another man pole her father's boat out each morning is comfort *and* grief — give her one beat of each before the season turns.
- **The redlined rack punt.** If the table just wants Sabine's paper made fair for the Warden punt, run the renegotiation table in [[berth-contract-handout]] — a fair hard deal is a real win in this city, and Sabine walking away respecting Tenson is worth more than any clause.
- **Nobody acts.** He signs the original terms. The world moves: Remy under Warden paper, the debt transferable to where debts in this city go — a quiet, sad front-tick, and a lever someone colder than Sabine may someday find.

**GM note — no arc breaks, whatever they choose:** the boat keeps sailing with the party aboard in every version (Remy is their guide; the crew is the crew), so [[fletcher-arc]]'s pipeline-marks plot and the deep-Mire trips ride along regardless of whose name is on the hull.

**Rewards:** Remy restored, not rescued — his pride rule honored to the letter, by whichever road. Sabine's respect, which is Warden standing the party will want when the city has to decide about outsiders (her file's own secret). Anselme's case closable. And Tenson's shingle is out: **the next client finds him.**

## The Ladder *(S4 →; bend to the table, ~1 strong beat + 2 clues per session)*

- **S4 — The Maintenance House.** *(Table-ready run file: [[session-04-maintenance-house]]; handouts: [[oswin-papers-handout]].)* Tenson meets Oswin: tea, the flinch under the delight, the volunteered correspondence (Rung 1 material), the pried-loose expense summary (Rung 2 material, uncracked or cracked — player's pace). No confession; the box stays shut. Meanwhile the session's other rails: Caspar's note (the rehearsal-ask), Solène's notice at the wall, the Ranging House. Don't force all of it — the correspondence alone is a full Tenson beat.
- **S4–5 — The Redline Meeting.** *(Table-ready run file: [[session-04-redline-meeting]].)* The marked-up report returned; the fencing match (Caius concedes everything except attribution-to-a-person); and the arc's most important plant fires at three brightnesses — Bigsby's verbatim demonstration, the free expertise, Lucien's fallback. The Embassy now contains a *known* unsteered record, and the slow burn toward **standing** (Silence's warrant) begins. If they stall on signing, Caius's credentials-verification clock starts.
- **S5 — Sweat First, paid.** Perrin's turnaround wins Sable; the Kit Room opens; **Tenson finds the guard-channel denial himself** in a boring stack (the arc's spotlight rule — Sable slides it over ungrasped: *"Knock yourself out, brain-boy"*). Rung 1 hardens from anomaly into **pattern.** First report to Silence due about now — what does he say?
- **S5–6 — The Pale Room night.** Sinope's tip cashed: the party sees the casino, the too-high laugh, learns whose establishment it is. If Rung 2 has fired, the skim now has a destination; if not, this is where the question *"where does a broke wizard's money go?"* gets its answer-shaped hole. Marcel knows their names. Marcel is patient. **Party jobs, so it's never a one-man show:** gambling dice for everyone (Finesse/Presence/Knowledge, per the S3 tease) · **Isotta reads Marcel — the one-liner, do not forget: [[marcel-dray#What Isotta Sees (the one-liner — do not forget)|his file]]**, primed by Caspar's rumor (two-stage, stage 1 in [[session-03-caspar-encounter]] Beat 5) · Leavy's tongue on the house Mire Spirits (a brewer knows contraband distillation when she tastes it) · Fletcher reading the room like terrain — exits, sightlines, and which "patrons" are working.
- **S6+ — The right question.** Marcel, asked directly, hands over what he holds — the Deal (Oswin's ruin for the Tidebreaker) and, to the *right* question, the Caius connection (Rung 4 / Front 7 discharge). This collides Tenson's arc with the whole party's cure quest **on purpose:** the price of the curse-breaking spear is the auditor's integrity. The table's best argument of the campaign lives here.
- **S7+ — The Reconciliation.** Tenson holds everything: the box (or its location), the pattern, the file's shape, the shipwreck proof within reach. The endgame menu opens. The counter-report gets drafted — with the Truth-Teller's Ink, the Long Room favor, and every redline the player has fought for since the dinner.

**Running underneath, all arc long:** the fronts don't wait ([[world-in-motion]]). Every session Tenson spends *not* resolving the wall, Front 2 thins and Front 3 posts another relocation order — and Caius files it. The audit has a deadline nobody will announce.

## Endgame — a menu, not a script

*(Per the no-hard-coded-endings rule: live options, chosen at the table. Most tables will braid two or three.)*

- **The Counter-Report.** The signature move. Tenson authors the document Caius never sees coming: a Citadel-bound audit — provable, redlined, inked, witnessed through the Long Room, and **corroborated by compelled production from Attendant 7** (the lawful caper: standing secured, the verbatim originals served into the record in front of witnesses Caius can't manage) — that restores the ward funding, exposes the denial pattern, and lands *before* the annexation petition exists. Caius's whole method turned back on him: he built a file; the auditor built a **better** one, and the Embassy's own pen signed it. If [[silence]] takes her kind fork, she's the artillery that makes it land.
- **The Restructuring.** The Auditor's Mercy in full: Oswin on a restitution schedule, the ward refunded, Marcel bought out or boxed out, the box burned or banked. Quiet, unglamorous, and the closest thing to saving everyone — it costs the party the easy road to the Tidebreaker and gains them a devoted, terrified friend on the wall.
- **The True Report, Filed.** He reports it all, straight, to everyone entitled to it. Oswin falls; Marcel collects; the Guild scrambles a replacement it doesn't have; Caius's file swells — and Tenson keeps the only thing he came here still owning. Play every consequence honestly and let it be tragic *and* respectable. If the player chooses this with open eyes, it's not a failure state — it's the campaign's hardest-won piece of characterization.
- **The Signature.** Rung 4 weaponized: the shipwreck evidence from Marcel plus the unauthorized mandate, assembled and served. Caius cornered is Caius mask-off ([[caius-maren#Campaign Function|his file]]) — he makes the desperate play, and the party gets the fight they've been owed since the water closed over their ship.
- **If ignored, the gravity wins.** The wall breaches on schedule ([[world-in-motion#The Gravity Well]]); Caius files mid-catastrophe; Oswin drowns trying to hold a line the money left years ago; and somewhere in the wreckage, an unsigned incident report goes soggy in a satchel. The world moves.
- **The unwritten one** the player finds when he realizes the audit was never about the wall — it was about what a report is *for.*

## What this arc deliberately doesn't do

- **No lore conduit.** The audit stops at the Remainder. Tenson quantifies the anomaly; he never explains the spirits, the binding, or the Celebration. If a paper trail ever threatens to reveal the metaphysics, the trail was drawn wrong — redraw it.
- **No interrogation crack.** Oswin never confesses under questioning — not in S4, not ever. He's practiced at avoidance, and the *paper* is the confessor. The day he says it out loud is the day someone shows him the evidence and he goes very quiet — his file's terms, unchanged.
- **No early mask-off.** Caius stays patient, warm, and useful until *cornered by assembled proof* — no villain monologue on suspicion. The wrongness stays a low hum as long as possible; it's scarier that way.
- **No pre-decided Silence.** Her fork (kind/sharp) stays live until the table's relationship with her letters tips it — [[silence|her file]] governs.
- **No coined names.** The arc runs entirely on the existing cast — Oswin, Caius, Marcel, Sable, Perrin, Solène, Sinope, Lucien, Bigsby, Silence. The paper is the only new character it needs.
- **No punishing the redline.** The player's instinct to rewrite documents is the arc's superpower, never an obstruction. Every time he fights for an edit, something downstream should be different because he did.

## Secrets & Clues — the audit file

*(Ten, abstract from discovery, per the Lazy DM rules. ~2 per session via the handouts, Sable, the Pale Room, Marcel, Solène, the letters.)*

1. Not one of the five denials ever disputes that the wall is failing — they refuse on procedure alone, which is what an office does when the facts are against it and it doesn't care.
2. The provision cited in every denial governs new construction, not the maintenance of standing works — a misapplication no one upstream ever checked.
3. Petitions take weeks to cross the city's channels; the denials come back stamped within a day. The decision exists before the reading.
4. A cultural liaison holds no fiscal authority anywhere in the Empire — except, somehow, here, where every coin bound for the wall crosses his desk.
5. The Maintenance House books balance to the exact copper in the very years the wall got visibly worse — and honest books never balance perfectly.
6. The maintainer bills the Guild for field research every quarter, and will tell anyone who asks, with a sad little laugh, that he gave up research years ago.
7. The wall's day-labor line went to zero eighteen months ago — which is why one round-shouldered wizard hauls salt alone, twice a week, like penance.
8. Someone connected to the failing ward gambles at the Pale Room and owes far more than coin — and the man who holds the debt also holds papers that could end him.
9. The liaison's incident file runs two years and thirteen documents deep, every one of them true and every one of them framed — and there is a slot waiting for document fourteen, with four signature lines.
10. Even funded in full and spent honestly, the wall would still be losing: the growth outruns fifty years of budget arithmetic, and the only person who ever wrote that down has been ignored every quarter for four years.
11. The Embassy's attendant cannot say a false thing — only decline to answer — and in two years, no one has thought to ask it anything at all.
12. The attendant catalogued the liaison's incident file: thirteen entries — and the thirteenth, a vessel lost in the southwest channels, was indexed before its survivors were ever invited to dinner.
