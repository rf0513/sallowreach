## 🧭 In Plain Words *(the whole session — no jargon)*

The whole night is one visit to the crime boss's underground casino, then two fights. The festival is still tomorrow.

It opens just inside the door. Across the room, the auditor hears a laugh they know: the salt-wall wizard they investigated two days ago is at a card table — and he's cheating, with a pair of magic glasses that see through the backs of the cards. In a casino famous for honest games. He owes the crime boss a fortune and has lied about paying it back, and he has no idea the crime boss already knows about the cheating. A stack of six chips on the table counts down how long the wizard has before he's thrown out. If the auditor wants something from him, the time is now.

Then the crime boss, Marcel, sees them. He's polite, cold, and never tricks anyone. First he asks, plainly, what really happened to the museum curator the party "lost" on their boat — the party faked that death, and Marcel wants to know if they're the kind of people who cheat him. Then he names his price for the spear that can cut their curse: get the cheating wizard fired from his guild. Once the wizard loses that protection, Marcel can collect from him however he likes. The auditor can take that deal, or bring a better one.

In the middle of the negotiation, a mother owlbear comes crashing down the boardwalk toward the casino — following the scent of her cub, which is Marcel's pet. The party can fight her or calm her, and the fastest way to end it is to hand her the cub. Marcel will let it go, because the arithmetic says so, and he'll remember who saved his door.

Walking home, someone on a dark boat shoots an arrow at the ranger — his own arrow, with a shipping tag tied to it. This time the shooters climb up onto the boardwalk. They've been hired to take the ranger, alive. End on the walk home.

## 🎭 The Theme *(say nothing, feel everything)*

**There are two kinds of people: the ones who pay, and the ones who cheat.**

- **Caspar paid.** Every month. That's why he was never in danger.

- **The mother** has come to collect what's hers.
- **Gault** is collecting too. He's put the frog on his books.
- **Marcel** spends the whole night finding out which kind the party is.

Nobody says it. Marcel's the only one who comes close.

## 🎲 Dice First

*The reflex: the moment a PC tries to* get *something — read a person, find a thing, push past a "no" — call the roll* **before** *you narrate the result.*

**Action roll** = 2d12 (Hope die + Fear die) + trait vs **Difficulty.** Hope die high → PC gains **Hope.** Fear die high → **you** gain **Fear.** Matching dice = **Crit** (auto-succeed, gain Hope, clear Stress).

- ⭐ **Spend a Hope → +2 from a relevant Experience.** Every roll below names which one. **Say it out loud when you call the roll.**
- **Help an Ally** → helper spends 1 Hope, rolls +d6 (highest d6 counts).
- **Advantage** → +1d6. Spotting the cheat gives it on the napkin (Beat 1). Fletcher has it on the Calm-Down (Beat 4).
- ⭐ **Under Marcel's eye** *(the Pale Room's Composure passive)*: any roll that lies to Marcel, pushes him, or tries to read him — **mark 1 Stress first, or roll with disadvantage.** Player's choice. Say it the first time.

**Four answers, not two:** success-with-Hope · success-with-Fear · failure-with-Hope · failure-with-Fear.

**Diff ladder:** 11 baseline · 13–14 hard · 15 a real reach.

### 🎰 This session's one new mechanic: Oswin's Stack *(say this to the table)*

> "See these six chips? That's Oswin's stack. Every time anybody rolls in here, I take one away. When they're gone, so is he — the house walks him out. If you want something from him, you're on the clock."

That's the whole rule. It's a **standard countdown** — it ticks on **every action roll**, success or failure, anywhere in the Pale Room ([[STATUS]] §SRD Facts, verified). The notice roll in Beat 0 takes the first chip. A full game of Wake can eat four on its own, so the table has to choose: play, or go talk to the wizard. **Reaction rolls don't tick it.** At zero, run **the walk-out** (Beat 1). It costs you no Fear — the chips already paid for it.

**Every counter tonight is visible:** Oswin's Stack (6) · the Calm-Down (3/3, Beat 4) · Isotta's Slipping Clock · her tally sheet.

> So the girl's still smiling at you. She's already got Tenson's coat over her arm — and honestly, nobody saw her take it.

**Put the six chips on the table now** and say the Stack rule (Mechanics Menu, above).

🎲 **The notice — Instinct 13** *(Eye For Danger)* **or Knowledge 13** *(Budding Bureaucrat — the odds are wrong; the auditor counts)*. **Takes the first chip.**

⭐ **Said aloud** — this is Tenson's line, out of their own memory: ***"He doesn't wear glasses."*** If the player doesn't get there, **Octave** says it at the bar (Beat 1): *"Wizard got new spectacles. Started winning the same week. I'd lay four to one they're not for reading."*

**Run Marcel grey** ([[marcel-dray]] §Playing Him Grey): never cheats, never word-traps, never springs what he knows. He corrects a misread term *before* they agree, even against himself. He listens to every answer all the way through.

#### 👁️ Isotta's sight *(fires the moment she looks at him — before the drinks)*

Hand her the card, or whisper it. **To her alone.**

> Isotta — every ghost you've ever met is an *unfinished* thing. Open. Aching to be tied off. That's what your sight is for.
>
> He reads **finished.** Tied. Knotted — the knot every spirit at that wall would drown the world to get. A closed account.
>
> And the closed account is standing behind the card table, dry in a room full of sweating people, asking if anyone would like another drink.

He notices her noticing. He finds the question uninteresting. That's the whole exchange. **What she does with it is hers.**

#### 3a · The Caspar question

**Why he asks (GM only):** he doesn't suspect a thing. He lost an asset, and the people across his table were the last ones to see it. He's about to give them his word, and he's checking one thing — **do these people cheat?**

> "Before the spear." *[beat]* "The curator."
>
> "You took Caspar Renne out on your boat, and you came back without him. I'd like to hear it from you."

**Then shut up and let them answer.** He doesn't interrupt.

- **They tell the truth — no roll.** *"Thank you."* [beat] He's honestly refreshed. He doesn't chase Caspar — *"A man can run a long way from a closet of vases. That's his business now."* **Their word is good tonight** — a promise from them counts as collateral.
- **They lie — 🎲 Presence 14, under Marcel's eye** *(mark a Stress first, or disadvantage)*:
  - **Success with Hope:** he takes it. *"I'm sorry for your trouble."* Their word stands.
  - **Success with Fear:** he takes it — but the room filed whoever went quiet when the name came up. No cost tonight. It's on the ledger.
  - **Failure:** no accusation. Ever. *"That's not all of it."* [beat] *"That's allowed. It isn't my business until it touches the terms."* **Their word is worth nothing tonight** — anything they promise needs real collateral behind it.

**Either way, then — the quiet knife.** Say it like it's small talk:

> "Caspar thought I wanted him dead. I wanted him *nervous.* He tried to cut me out once. Nervous was the lesson."
>
> *[beat]*
>
> "And he paid. Every month. Men who pay don't have accidents."

Let the table sit in it. The fake death was never needed. **Delphine is grieving a man who was never in danger.** Don't say that. They'll get there.

🔒 **Held in reserve — the Caius consequence.** Marcel knows Tenson's report on the drowning was never signed by the Embassy. **He never brings it up.** Only if somebody says the Embassy has it on file, he answers, mildly: ***"Not yet, it doesn't."*** Then moves on.

**Then, if they ask** *(he answers every direct question, plainly, and volunteers nothing)*:

- **"Why him? Why now?"**
  > "He owes me a great deal, and he's lied to me about paying it." *[beat]* "And three weeks ago he started cheating at my tables." *[beat]* "In my house. Where the games are fair."

  >
  > *[beat]*
  >
  > "Take the coat off him, and he's just a man who owes me money."
- **"What happens to Oswin after?"**
  > "Whatever collecting takes." *[beat]* "I don't decide that in advance."
- **If they spotted the cheat and say so:** *"Three weeks. I wondered if anyone else would see it."* — the closest thing to a compliment he gives.

### 4 · ⚔️ She Comes for the Casino — *everyone* (40–50 min)

#### The trigger

> The cub picks her head up off his knee.
>
> She makes a sound — high and thin, like a kettle right before it starts to whistle.
>
> Every glass on the table starts shivering — like, all of them, at once.
>
> A little line of dust comes down from the ceiling, right onto the felt.
>
> And out on the floor, somebody comes running down the stair. First time all night anybody's hurried.
>
> Marcel's gone completely still.
>

**If Fletcher already played his card:** *"Your information was good."* **If not:** this is the moment someone says *"That's her mother."* Marcel: *"…Ah."*

#### The deal in the back room *(30 seconds — but it's the whole fight's price)*

The Calm-Down needs **a cub there, unharmed.** The only one in the building is on Marcel's knee. **Someone has to ask him for it.** When they do:

> He doesn't answer right away.
>
> He picks the cub up. She's heavy, and he doesn't make a show of it. He just holds her for a second.
>
> He says something to her — really quiet. You don't catch it.
>
> Then he holds her out to you.
>
> "Go on. Before she finds the stair."

Arithmetic beats fondness, and he knows it about himself. **Say the price plainly, his way, as they go:** *"Keep her off my stair and we'll discuss what that's worth."*

*(If nobody asks for the cub, she's still on his knee when the fight starts. The Calm-Down can't run without her. Somebody will figure it out — or Camille comes up the stair carrying her, sent.)*

#### The arena — the top of the stair

> You come up the stair and it's *loud* up here.
>
> The whole boardwalk's done up for tomorrow. There are lanterns strung between every post — not lit yet — and folding tables leaned up against every door.
>
> People are standing in their doorways in their nightclothes.
>
> And down at the end of the boardwalk, something big is coming. The planks are popping up in front of her like loose floorboards.
>
> Marcel's two men are at the top of the stair with crossbows, already shooting. A bolt sticks in her shoulder and she doesn't even slow down.
>
> She's coming straight for the stair. Straight for what you're holding.

**Toys:**
- **The stair** — narrow, one body wide. **Tenson's lane:** the wall between talons and the door.
- **The lantern strings** — 🎲 **Finesse 13** to drop one across her: **one clear round** while she tears free.
- **The leaned tables** — shields, barricades, something to shove into her line *(Strength 13)*.
- **The crossbows** — Marcel's men keep shooting. **While the Calm-Down runs, every bolt that hits her ticks Consequence.** Stop them: 🎲 **Presence 13** — or someone yells down the stair, and Marcel says one word: *"Enough."* They stop.
