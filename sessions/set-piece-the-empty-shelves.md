# Set Piece — The Empty Shelves *(Adaline takes possession, and does the inventory by accident)*

*Built 2026-07-25 for [[session-06-run-sheet]] Slot 0, replacing the "what would Caspar have wanted" version. **This is also the campaign's countdown tutorial** — the scene where you stop the game, put two dice on the table, and teach your players a mechanic you've never used. Both jobs are in here.*

***30–45 minutes. Zero combat. Four to six rolls, all social.*** *Delivers Front 11's museum inventory a full front-dial early — in the room, in front of them, unstoppable.*

---

## 🚨 Panic Panel *(open here — you can run the whole thing off this box)*

**What this is:** the day after the funeral, the woman who runs the city lets herself into the dead curator's museum to take possession of it. The party is already inside. While she's there she notices the collection is a mess and a lot of it is **gone** — and she comes to the obvious, devastating, completely wrong conclusion: that Caspar Renne was robbing this city for years.

**She is right about every fact and wrong about the man.** The party knows why the shelves are empty. Saying it out loud means naming **Marcel Dray** to the city's steward — which is the one thing they can't afford before they've even met him.

**Say this if you need a beat:** *"She's not looking at you. She's looking at the wall behind you, and she's been looking at it for a while now."*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who walks in** | **[[adaline-roux\|Adaline Roux]]** — runs the city, twenty-two years. Key in her hand. Caspar was a real friend. |
| **Why she's here** | To take possession of a city-funded building and find a new operator. **Not** to investigate anyone. |
| **What she finds** | Gaps. Years of them. *(Real ones — Caspar genuinely was fencing to pay Marcel.)* |
| **What she concludes** | Theft. Sad, certain, and stated plainly. |
| **What she does NOT know** | **Marcel Dray exists as a factor in her city.** Never hint otherwise. |
| **The clocks** | 🔴 **The Gaps — 6** · 🟢 **The Other Story — 4** *(race; first to zero is the verdict she leaves with)* |
| **Difficulty** | **13** for everything here *(house ladder: 11 baseline / 13–14 hard / 15 reach — [[STATUS]] §SRD Facts)* |
| **The free door** | Tell her the truth about Marcel → 🟢 hits zero instantly, no roll. It works. It costs. |

**The one rule:** she never accuses anybody in the room. Every gap she finds, she says **sadly**, about a man she loved. The party's dread is entirely self-inflicted and that's the whole design.

## 🧭 In Plain Words *(no jargon, no shorthand — read this if you're cold)*

The party is standing in a dead man's museum with his two-hundred-year-old journal open on the table. The front door opens and Adaline Roux walks in. She runs this city. She has a key because the museum is city-funded, the curator died two days ago, and somebody has to take charge of the building and find someone new to run it. She was not expecting to find four foreigners in here.

She's polite about it. She's polite about everything. But while she's talking to them she starts walking the room the way anyone would walk a room they're now responsible for — and she starts noticing that things are missing. Not one thing. A lot of things, over a lot of years.

The party knows exactly why. Caspar had been selling off the collection for years to pay a crime boss who was squeezing him. They know that because he told them, right before they helped him fake his own death and float away on a balloon.

Adaline doesn't know any of that. So the conclusion she reaches — out loud, standing in the room, grieving — is that her friend of twenty years was stealing from the city the whole time. And she's going to be very calm and very certain about it.

The party can let that stand. They can invent something better and try to sell it to her. Or they can tell her the truth, which clears him, and which points the most powerful person in Sallowreach at the man who's holding the one object that can break their curse.

There is also a bow missing, and the reason it's missing is that Fletcher stole it the night before last.

## 🎓 Teaching the Countdown *(the out-of-character bit — this is why the file exists)*

You have never run a countdown at this table. This scene is where you teach it, and the teaching is **staged**, not front-loaded.

**⚠ Do not explain the clocks when she walks in.** She hasn't found anything yet, so the numbers would mean nothing and it'd feel like homework. You run Beat 1 in character, let her find the **first** gap, and *then* stop the game. Now the dice mean something, because they just watched the thing the dice measure.

### The script — say it roughly like this

> "Okay — hang on, I'm stepping out for a second. I want to show you something, because we're going to use this a lot from now on."
>
> *[put the first die down where everyone can see it, showing 6]*
>
> "This is a countdown. It's at six. Every time one of you rolls in this scene, this number goes down — and how far it goes down depends on how the roll went. When it gets to zero, Adaline decides something about Caspar out loud, and she doesn't take it back."
>
> *[second die, showing 4]*
>
> "This one's yours. This is whatever version of events you can get her to believe instead. Same deal — your rolls move it."
>
> "They're racing. Whichever one gets to zero first is what she walks out of this building believing."
>
> "One thing you should know, because it's the mean part. If you succeed with **Fear** — you move *both*. You'll get what you wanted, and she'll notice something anyway."
>
> "That's it. The numbers aren't secret. Ask me about them whenever, and I'll say them out loud every time they change."

### Then, every time after this

You don't re-teach it. One line, and only if it's a new clock:

> "New countdown. Six. When it hits zero, [the thing] happens. You know how they work."

### If they ask *(plain answers — none of this is secret)*

| They ask                       | You say                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Can we just not roll?"        | "Sure. If nobody engages, she just keeps walking the room and I tick it myself. That's the worse option, but it's yours." |
| "What counts as a roll here?"  | "Anything where you're actively steering what she thinks. Talking to each other doesn't cost anything."                   |
| "How do we know what to roll?" | "Tell me what you're doing and I'll tell you the trait. Difficulty's 13 across the board."                                |
| "Can we help each other?"      | "Yes — **Help an Ally**. Spend a Hope, hand them an extra die. Do it out loud so I can see it."                           |
| "Is the red one bad for us?"   | "Yes. I'm not hiding that."                                                                                               |
|                                |                                                                                                                           |
|                                |                                                                                                                           |
|                                |                                                                                                                           |

### 🎲 The tick values — the actual verified table

*Official SRD Dynamic Countdown Advancement. Cross-checked against two sources 2026-07-25; matches [[STATUS]] §SRD Facts.*

| Their roll | 🟢 Progress ticks | 🔴 Consequence ticks |
|---|:--:|:--:|
| **Critical success** | **3** | 0 |
| **Success with Hope** | **2** | 0 |
| **Success with Fear** | **1** | **1** |
| **Failure with Hope** | 0 | **2** |
| **Failure with Fear** | 0 | **3** |

⚠ **Know which parts are house.** The SRD table above ticks on **player action rolls**. The two free ticks below (her walking the room) and the Fear spend that finds a gap are **our extension** — they're the right call for this scene, they just aren't in the book. Don't teach them to your players as rules; they're you moving the world.

## ⏱️ The Two Clocks

**Put both dice on the table where everyone can see them.** The visible-dial rule: a clock they can watch beats a clock you describe.

### 🔴 The Gaps — starts at 6

She notices what's missing. **You tick this twice for free** — once in Beat 1, once whenever the pace sags — so the dice only drive it from **4 → 1**.

**⭐ At 1, the bow. Always. Locked to that number.**

This is the single most important mechanical note in the file. A failure with Fear moves the Gaps by **three**, which means the dice could otherwise jump straight past your best beat to the verdict. Hard-wiring the bow to the 1 means the dice decide *when* you get there, never *whether* it lands. Steal this trick for every countdown you ever build.

**0 = the verdict.** She names it. She does not take it back.

### 🟢 The Other Story — starts at 4

Whatever explanation they build. **It does not have to be true.** At 0, that's what she believes instead — and the Gaps stop mattering tonight.

*Optional, and it's a good idea: set this to **5**. Then the lie is measurably harder than the truth, since the Marcel confession wins it instantly and free. The mechanics quietly say honesty is cheaper and costs more, which is this campaign's whole argument. 4 is fine if you want them to win it.*

### What they roll — Difficulty 13

| Trait | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **Presence** | Selling her an account of Caspar's last month that holds together |
| **Instinct** | Clocking which gap she's walking toward and getting in front of it |
| **Knowledge** | ⭐ **Tenson.** Reconciling the collection *for* her — deaccessions, loans, bad recordkeeping |

⭐ **Do not miss what Tenson's roll actually is.** He is a professional imperial auditor using his real expertise to help a grieving woman reach a **false conclusion about a record.** Offer him the option. Say absolutely nothing about it. That's his arc detonating and it costs you zero prep.

## 📍 What You See

*Indoors. Two rooms and an office, low ceilings, late afternoon light coming in sideways.* Glass cases along both walls, about half of them lit. Hand-lettered placards, some of them decades old. The wizard's journal still open on the table where they left it. Dust in the air where somebody's been moving things. **And if you're looking: pale rectangles on the walls where things used to hang.**

## 🎙️ Playing Her *(everything you need; her full box is in [[session-06-run-sheet]] Slot 0)*

**Warm the way a wall is warm in the sun.** Measured, unhurried. She looks at people *completely* — hold eye contact one beat past comfortable. She says **"we"** for the city and means two hundred years of it.

**The one physical thing:** don't fill silences. She stopped needing to announce herself decades ago. If a player trails off, let it sit.

**Her register tonight is grief, not suspicion.** Every single gap she finds lands as *sad*, never as *gotcha*. She is not building a case against anyone standing in front of her. She's watching a friend she trusted turn into someone else, in real time, and being very composed about it.

## Beat 1 — She Has a Key *(10 min)*

Open where you cut last session. Don't recap — re-enter.

> The journal's still lying open where you set it down. Nobody's said anything for a minute. Outside, it's getting late.
>
> And the front door opens.
>
> You know her. Everyone does. Deep green, silver in her hair, a black cord knotted round her left wrist. Adaline Roux — she runs this city, and she's got a key to this building in her hand.
>
> She stops when she sees you. Looks at the four of you for a good three seconds.
>
> "Well," she says. "You're the last people who saw him."

**Why she's here, said plainly if they ask:** the museum is city-funded, the curator is dead, and somebody has to take charge of it and find a new operator. She means today. She brought the key for exactly that.

**She grieves, plainly.** One short story about arguing with Caspar. Keep it small and specific.

⭐ **Tenson's knife** ([[twist-the-knife]]) — he refused the funeral, and now he's standing in a room while somebody mourns for real. Say nothing about it. Let him sit there.

### 🔴 First free tick — 6 → 5

She's talking, and she stops, because she's looking past them at a wall.

> "There was a landscape hanging there." *[she's not upset yet — she's just placing it]* "Awful thing. Brown. I hated it for twenty years."
>
> *[a beat]*
>
> "Where's it gone?"

**→ This is your cue. Stop the game and run the teaching script above.** Put both dice down. Then pick the scene back up exactly where you dropped it, with her question still hanging.

---

## Beat 2 — The Race *(20 min — the body of the scene)*

She keeps walking the room. They keep talking. Call for a roll **when a player commits to a claim that could be examined** — roughly four to six times. Not every sentence.

**Say both numbers out loud every time they change.** *"Gaps down to three. Other Story's at two."* That's the whole reason the dice are on the table.

### 🔴 Second free tick — use it when the pace sags: case seven

> She's stopped at the empty case. The placard's still in it. *Object on Loan.*
>
> "On loan to who?"
>
> *[she looks round at them, honestly asking, like they might know]*
>
> "He'd have told me. He told me everything about this place. He told me when a hinge went."

**They know what's in that slot and who has it.** Let the silence be theirs.

### 🟢 If The Other Story is landing

She *wants* to believe them. Play that. She nods. She offers them the charitable version herself and half-builds it for them — *"He was always lending things out. Half of it never came back and he never chased anyone."* Let them feel it working.

### 🚪 The free door — the Marcel confession

If anyone tells her the truth — Marcel was extorting him, that's where the collection went — **🟢 hits zero instantly. No roll.** It works. It's the only thing that actually clears him.

She goes very still. Then:

> "Say that once more. Exactly the way you said it."

**What it costs, and say it at the table so the choice is honest:**
- The most powerful person in Sallowreach now has **Marcel's name** and a reason to move on him — before the party has met him or asked him for anything.
- She will want to know **how four people who'd been in the city a month knew this**, and that question doesn't stop being asked when the scene ends.
- It's a thread that pulls on a drowning that didn't happen ([[world-in-motion]] Front 11).

⚠ **She does not act on it tonight.** She takes it in, she asks that one question, and she goes quiet. Log it and turn the front after the session.

---

## Beat 3 — ⭐ The Bow *(fires at 🔴 1, always)*

She's in the second room. She stops. This is the first time all night her voice does anything.

> "…Where's the bow?"
>
> *[nobody answers]*
>
> "There was a bow in that case. It's one of the founding pieces. It's been in this building longer than the building's been a museum."
>
> *[and here she gets quiet, and it's much worse than angry]*
>
> "That one he'd have had to carry out himself."

**Fletcher stole it two nights ago.** He is standing in the room watching a grieving woman attribute his theft to a dead man she loved.

**Do not look at his player. Do not prompt him. Do not roll anything.** Say the line, let it land, and move on to whoever speaks first.

⚠ **No punishment on the bow** (standing GM call, 2026-07-24). She never looks for it, never suspects him, and [[theo-rouchard]] already handled the other half in Slot 1a. **The consequence here is emotional and it is completely sufficient.**

---

## Beat 4 — Whichever Hits Zero *(5 min)*

### 🔴 The Gaps reach 0 — the verdict

She's stopped moving. She's not looking at any of them.

> "Twenty years." *[quiet, and completely certain]* "I signed his budget every year. Every year. And I never once walked the floor."
>
> *[a beat]*
>
> "He was selling it. Piece at a time, for years, and I paid for the lamps he did it under."
>
> "I'd like you to leave now, please. That's not — " *[and this is the only time she loses the thread of a sentence]* " — I'd just like the room."

**That's the scene.** Don't soften it, don't give them a last roll, don't have her reconsider at the door.

### 🟢 The Other Story reaches 0 — she holds it

> "Alright." *[and she lets out a breath she's had for a while]* "Alright. That sounds like him. That sounds exactly like him."
>
> *[she picks up the key, turns it over]*
>
> "Thank you. I'd have got there wrong on my own, and I'd have said it out loud, and it would have stuck."

**They have just successfully protected a dead man's name with a story they made up.** Don't comment. They'll feel it.

⚠ **The Other Story is now canon in Adaline's head, and the party has to keep it true.** Write down exactly what they told her. Front 11 holders table.

## 😨 Fear Tonight *(quiet scene — Fear buys pressure, not damage)*

- **1 Fear — she finds one they didn't plan for.** An extra 🔴 tick, your pick, no roll. Something small and specific off a shelf.
- **1 Fear — she asks one of them directly.** *"How did you know him?"* Aim it at whoever's weakest on the lie. Warm, curious, no edge.
- **1 Fear — she picks something up and holds it.** An object one of them touched two nights ago. She doesn't say anything about it. She just holds it while she talks.
- **1 Fear — [[sinope]] is in the doorway.** Has been. Run his four-branch box ([[session-06-run-sheet]] ★) and get out.
- **Banked, not spent:** the actual formal inventory. That's the front, later. **Do not fire it tonight** — tonight she just stands in the room with a list in her head.

## 🚫 Guardrails

- **She does not know about Marcel.** Not a hint, not a "something in her expression," nothing. Her blankness is a clue in its own right (ledger #3).
- **She never accuses anyone in the room.** Not once. Every gap is grief aimed at a dead man.
- **She is not investigating the party.** She's taking possession of a building. The dread is theirs, not hers.
- **No punishment on the bow.** Standing call. Emotional consequence only.
- **This scene does not burn [[set-piece-show-me]]** — it builds the Standing that scene needs. If she warms to them, log it.
- **She doesn't know what the Celebration holds** and won't learn tonight. If they steer there, use the run sheet's satisfied non-answers.
- **Don't argue her out of the verdict once it lands.** A countdown that hits zero and then gets talked back was never a countdown.
