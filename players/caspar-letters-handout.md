# Handout — The Unsent Letters *(Caspar to Delphine, forty years)*

*The party found these in the museum archive in Session 5 and the GM narrated them into existence at the table. They have never existed as text. Here they are, printable. **Canon anchor:** Caspar's own farewell letter already says it — "Be kind to Delphine. She has been being kind to me for forty years and I never once made it easy, and she came anyway. Some collections you do not deserve" ([[caspar-letter-handout]]).*

---

## 🧭 In Plain Words *(no jargon — read this first if you're cold)*

Caspar Renne ran the city museum for forty years and was in love with Delphine, the city healer, for most of them. He wrote her letters. He never sent a single one. Instead he filed them — with catalogue numbers, in his own museum's accession system, the way he filed everything else he owned and couldn't part with.

The party found the bundle after they helped him fake his death and float away on a balloon. So four foreigners are now holding forty years of a love letter that was never delivered, while the woman it was written to grieves a man who isn't dead and doesn't know either of those things.

The reason he never sent them is the whole tragedy: he was quietly fencing stolen goods out of the museum, and a crime boss owned him for it. Sending one letter meant letting Delphine close enough to see what he actually was. So he wrote, and filed, and went to her house for tea for forty years, and said nothing.

This file is two things. First, the letters themselves, to print and hand over. Second, the scene where the party decides what to do with them — give them to her, tell her the truth, or keep both.

---

## 📄 The Prop *(print this — a close curator's hand; the numbers are in the same ink as the museum's placards)*

*Five letters, tied with cord that's been retied many times. They are in accession order. **He catalogued his own love letters.***

---

> **ACC. 0119 — Correspondence, personal. Provenance: this room. Condition: poor.**
>
> Delphine —
>
> I have begun this four times and each time it comes out as an essay. You will laugh at that. You laugh at me approximately once a week and I have started arranging my week around it, which is the kind of admission a man should make in person and not in ink, and here I am, in ink.
>
> What I want to say is that you were right about the roof and I was wrong about the roof, and also that when you came up the stairs on Thursday I forgot what I was holding and put it down somewhere and have not found it since. It was a ledger. It was, in fact, *the* ledger.
>
> I am going to bring this to you tomorrow.
>
> — C.

---

> **ACC. 0447 — Correspondence, personal. Provenance: uncertain.**
>
> Delphine —
>
> You came out of that house at four in the morning with the boy's blood still on your forearms and you sat on my step and you did not say one word for an hour and neither did I, and I want you to know that was the best hour of my life, and I am aware of how that sounds.
>
> I have no skill for the thing you do. I keep dead things in cases and tell stories about them. You keep living ones and sometimes you lose. I don't know how you carry it. I know you shouldn't carry it on a step by yourself.
>
> Ask me. Any hour. I am nearly always awake and I am nearly always alone and those two facts have a relationship I would rather not examine.
>
> — C.

---

> **ACC. 0812 — Correspondence, personal. Not for display.**
>
> D. —
>
> There is a thing about me you don't know. I have written it out three times now on this page and blacked it each time and you can see where, and I'm not going to write it a fourth.
>
> Here is what I can say. The museum is not entirely what it looks like. I am not entirely what it looks like. And every single time you are kind to me — which is constantly, which is *relentlessly* — I go home and I sit with the fact that you are being kind to a version of me that I have manufactured and maintained the way I maintain everything else in this building.
>
> You would not think less of me. That is the unbearable part. You would look at it, and you would say something flat and correct, and then you would put the kettle on. I know exactly how you would take it. I have rehearsed it for years and I cannot stand it.
>
> So I keep coming for tea. Forgive me for that. It is the only honest hour in my week and it is built on a lie.
>
> — C.

---

> **ACC. 1206.**
>
> Delphine —
>
> A man came to the museum today, and he was very polite, and I have not stopped shaking since about the second bell.
>
> I am seventy-one. I have run out of clever. Everything I own I got by being the cleverest man in a small room and the room has been getting smaller for a decade and today it got very small indeed.
>
> If something happens to me, go into the archive. Second floor, the cabinet under the window, the drawer that sticks. Take the whole drawer. Don't read it in the building.
>
> I should have brought you the first one of these. Forty years ago. It was a bad letter. It was about a roof.
>
> — C.

---

> *(no number — this one was loose, on top, in fresh ink)*
>
> Delphine —
>
> I have decided to stop being careful, which at my age is not courage, it is arithmetic.
>
> I love you. I have loved you since the roof. I did not say it because I was a coward and then because I was a criminal and then because I was old, and each of those was a real reason and not one of them was a good one, and I have spent forty years cataloguing the difference.
>
> There are four strangers in my museum who cannot leave this city and I have been thinking all week about the fact that I could have left any time and never
>
> *(the sentence stops there. The pen is still in the fold.)*

## 🎬 The Scene — What They Do With Them

**When it fires:** whenever the party goes to Delphine, or she comes to them. She is grieving in public and working through it the way she does everything: by not stopping. **This is a quiet scene. No rolls. Don't build one.**

**Her register — the thing to get right:** Delphine does not weep and does not perform. She is blunt, warm underneath, dry sideways, and she has buried more of this city than she'd like to count. **Her grief looks like working.** She'll keep sorting jars while they talk. That's what makes it land.

### The four doors

| They… | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Give her the letters and say nothing** | The warmest scene and the worst outcome. She reads them at her table, in front of them, taking her time. Then: *"Well."* [a long pause] *"Forty years. Man could catalogue a shipwreck and not one of these."* She thanks them, honestly, and grieves twice as hard from that night on — **and the lie now has her holding it too.** Front 11 advances a rung without anybody doing anything wrong. |
| **Tell her the truth AND give her the letters** | ⭐ The big one. She believes them — she's read their bodies since day one and she knows what four people lying badly look like. **She does not shout.** She goes very still, the way she goes still with a patient. Then she asks the only question she cares about: *"Is he warm? Wherever he is. Is he warm."* Then she decides whether to forgive them, and it should take her more than one scene. **Cost:** she now carries the lie in a city she's spent sixty years being honest to. **Gift:** the party has an ally who is genuinely unshakeable, and Front 11's most dangerous holder is now on their side ([[world-in-motion]] Front 11, the holders table). |
| **Keep them / hide them** | Legitimate, and it has an address. The letters are museum property with accession numbers in them, and **Adaline is counting the museum** ([[world-in-motion]] Front 11, the inventory). Four numbered items missing from a catalogue is a pattern. Fire it later, gently, from a direction they didn't aim at. |
| **Burn them** | Fine. Let them. Then never mention it again for three sessions, and have Delphine say, apropos of nothing, that she keeps thinking he was about to tell her something. |

### ⭐ The vision beat *(fires only if she physically handles the loose one — the last one)*

Canon: her visions come by **touch**, arrive as impressions, and are opaque to her until after the fact ([[delphine]] §What She Wants). So:

> Her thumb goes across the fold where the pen is, and her eyes drift half a second to the left of the page, and she stops.
>
> It's short. Maybe two seconds.
>
> Then she's back, and she folds the letter closed, and she says — flat, to nobody:
>
> "A small light. Going up, and away, over black water." *[she puts the letter down]* "That's all. That one felt like the ones that matter." *[a shrug that costs her something]* "I don't know what it means. I never do."

**⚠ She has just seen the balloon and cannot read it.** The players can. **Say nothing else. Move on to something practical.** She hates being treated as an oracle and will get short with anyone who presses her ([[delphine]]).

**This does not out the party.** It just makes them sweat, which is the entire point.

## 🧠 GM Notes

- **The reason he never sent them is the spine.** ACC. 0812 is the load-bearing letter: he couldn't let her close because the museum was a front and [[marcel-dray|Marcel]] owned him for it. Every other letter is weather around that one. *(Consistent with [[caspar-renne]] §What He's Hiding and the fencing that [[world-in-motion]] Front 11's inventory is about to expose.)*
- **The catalogue numbers are the gut-punch, not the prose.** Read the accession headers aloud. A man who filed his own love letters in his own museum's system is a whole character in one detail — and the numbers thin out and then stop, because by the end he'd stopped pretending it was a collection.
- **Do not let the letters be smarter than Caspar.** No lore, no spear, no Celebration, no Mire. He's a frightened old man in love. The last one gets within one clause of the campaign's theme (*I could have left any time*) and **stops mid-sentence**, which is as close as he gets. Same calibration law as the journal and the old words: baffled, never right ([[caspar-letter-handout]] §GM Annotations).
- **ACC. 1206 is a live lead.** *"The drawer that sticks."* If the party never worked the archive properly, that's a free, in-fiction reason to go back into a building **Adaline now has the key to.** Put something small in it. Don't put a plot in it.
- **⭐ The Lucien crossover.** They're carrying letters that were meant to leave and never did, and there is a man in this city whose entire profession is sending words out — and whose one private crack is *the message he never sent* ([[the-long-room]]). If they ever put this bundle in front of Lucien Aubert, he is undone, quietly, and he'll do nearly anything they ask afterward. **Spend it once** ([[set-piece-the-long-room]] §The Paper Fuse).
- **And the door that isn't shut.** If they ever reach Caspar again, it's through the Long Room, and the price is telling Lucien who they're writing to. *A reply to these letters is the single most valuable object in the campaign that nobody has to fight anybody for.*
- **Front 11 wiring:** Delphine is the holder whose break costs the most and whose **gift-if-told-first is the largest.** This handout is the object that forces the choice. Log the outcome in the wrap-up ([[world-in-motion]] Front 11, Wear Ladder).

## 🚫 Guardrails

- **Delphine is not an oracle and hates being used as one.** One vision, unexplained, then she's a healer again.
- **She never guesses he's alive.** Not from the vision, not from their faces. If they don't tell her, she doesn't know. Ever.
- **No rolls in this scene.** Nobody deceives Delphine at a difficulty number. They either tell her or they don't.
- **The letters contain no campaign information.** Zero. They are about a roof, a boy who died, a drawer, and forty years.
- **If they confess, she does not punish them.** She's disappointed, practical, and she asks about his comfort before her own. That's worse and better.
