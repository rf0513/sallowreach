# Set Piece — Monsieur Dray Has Time *(the Pale Room night)*

*The campaign's third social climax, beside [[set-piece-show-me]] (Adaline) and [[set-piece-the-tessak-camp]] (the Weighing). This file is the **scene**; the room's mechanics live in [[the-pale-room]], the man and his decision tree in [[marcel-dray]] — **keep the tree open at the table** — and the girl at the door in [[camille-babineaux]]. Three movements: **the floor** (20–30 min), **the door** (5), **the table** (30–45). Fires any night after the museum truth (S5 slot 0) — the party knows who Marcel is and what he holds.*

> ▶ **Runs in S9 — staged in [[session-09-run-sheet]]** *(prepped 2026-10-02)*. Three changes ruled at that prep, swept in below: **Oswin is reachable** on a six-chip countdown and cheats with enchanted spectacles · **the Closed Account is now a question** (Marcel asks what happened to Caspar; he never suspected) · **the mother owlbear interrupts the table** before the terms close.

## 🧭 In Plain Words *(the whole scene — no jargon)*

The party finally walks into the Pale Room — the invitation-only underground casino run by Marcel Dray, the crime boss who owns the spear that can cut their curse. The Empire executed Marcel twelve years ago; he is somehow alive, and the party knows the rumor. The night has three parts. **First, the casino floor:** fair games, perfect service, and a sixteen-year-old hostess named Camille who quietly files everything guests say straight to Marcel — and who, the party may realize mid-drink, is the kidnapped girl their owlbear cub remembered being caged with in the hull of the boat the party now sails. **Then the door:** a drink nobody ordered arrives, and Camille says, *"Monsieur Dray has time."* **Then the table:** Marcel already knows their names and why they came. He offers exactly one deal — get the ward wizard whose debt he holds fired and stripped of guild protection, and the spear is theirs — and everything else is a counter-offer the party must bring, because he never negotiates against himself. Nobody fights. Nobody raises a voice. The whole scene is prices.

**Two goals tonight:** the party leaves knowing the spear's exact cost — and Isotta's ghost-sight finally shows her what Marcel actually is.

## Movement 1 — The Floor *(20–30 min — let the room be wonderful first)*

Read:

> Down a stair you'd never find twice, and the swamp just — stops. White tablecloths. Low amber light. Card-felt green as deep water. Somebody takes your coat like it's an honor, and the air smells of cedar and something sweeter underneath — the liquor here doesn't exist anywhere legal.
>
> A girl meets you at the rope. Sixteen, maybe. Dark hair pinned up too neatly. She smiles like she's genuinely glad you came.
>
> "Welcome to the Pale Room. House rules are short: the games are fair, the drinks are better, and everything you say here is worth something — so say what you mean to spend."

- **Let them play.** Run the games from the menu below — the perfect service, the floor-work roll, the fair felt. The trap is comfort, not deceit. Every courtesy is real, and every courtesy opens an account.
- **The bar: Octave Cormier** *(anyone who drifts to the bar meets him — third stool from the end, a row of untouched free drinks)*. The only man who ever beat Marcel and got paid in full — the lifetime tab hollowed him out, and he's the party's ghost-of-winnings-future: loud, kind, quoting odds on everything, and handing out the negotiation's meta-rule as bar wisdom (*"his word is iron once given; before it's given is the only part of him that moves"*). Full card: [[octave-cormier]]. **Remember: talking to him is broadcasting** — the floor files it all with Camille.
- **The recognition (the beat this movement exists for).** Leavy heard it from the cub: *"a girl, tied up"* in the same hull the party now sails. Camille is that girl. Play it from the **party's** side — the hostess taking their coats is the face from Whimsy's memory. If they say the boat's name, or describe the bow, watch her: the floor-calm cracks for **one visible second**, her hand going to something small in her apron pocket *(the cage's broken padlock — she keeps it like a saint's medal)*. What she does next is a dial ([[camille-babineaux]]): quietly asks if they've repainted the bow yet — or their drinks are simply never charged again, and no one explains why.
- **She will never say Marcel's name about the cage.** Her true channel, if asked about the smuggler: *"The cold men opened the cage. One of them gave me his coat because I was shaking. Nobody said anything the whole way home."* Warmth and dread in one breath — that's the register of the whole room.
- **Oswin dial.** If you want Front 7 standing in one room: the ward wizard is at a far table tonight, into the house deep, laughing slightly too high. Tenson's Sinope tip (S2) fires on sight — *the money's grave is the Pale Room.* ~~Don't let them reach him; see the Fear menu.~~ **Re-cut 2026-10-02:** let them reach him, on a clock — **Oswin's Stack**, six chips, one off per action roll, and at zero he's walked out. He's **cheating** with enchanted spectacles ([[oswin-cray#The Spectacles (canon 2026-10-02 — the Pale Room cheat)|his file]]), and Tenson can try for his signature on a payback schedule before the chips run out. ### The Games *(the floor menu — let them lose a little before the door opens)*

*Stakes doctrine ([[coin-in-sallowreach]]): the pot is never the point. Coin here is chips and house credit; the real winnings are markers, favors, and the room's respect — and everything a PC does at a table is a tell the floor files with [[camille-babineaux|Camille]]. Every game is dealt straight. That's the trap: nobody made them keep playing.*

| Table | The game | The roll |
|---|---|---|
| **Wake** | The house card game — the long felt | Finesse (the cards) or Presence (the players) — Progress Countdown (4) of hands |
| **The Count** | The dealer's quick game — shells and a pearl | Instinct 13, or Knowledge 13 for the regulars' version |
| **Pins** | The nerve game — a spire of carved bone | Agility, climbing: 11 → 13 → 15 → keep going if you dare |
| **Markers** | The high table — you bet things that are true | Presence (bluff) or Instinct (read), Diff 13, best of three hands |

- **Wake** *(both meanings — the boat's and the funeral's; the room never says which)*. Local deck: suits of **Salt, Reed, Rope, and Lantern**, and the high trump nobody wants to draw late is **the Drowned Man**. Run it on the Fair Game engine ([[the-pale-room]]): a countdown of hands, and on any roll **with Fear** the PC may *raise into debt* to keep their seat, or fold and lose the felt. Winning a full game buys the table's respect in ways money can't — and the room's first real look at you. *What it teaches the house: how you handle losing slowly.*
- **The Count.** Three lantern-shells and one black pearl, swept across the felt faster than eyes should follow. Tourists bet on where the pearl stopped. Regulars bet the real question: *how many shells were ever on the table.* One roll. On a crit, the dealer inclines their head — the room's highest compliment, and the room noticing you back. House color: **Camille, off-rotation, is unbeatable at it, and everyone knows it.** *What it teaches the house: how you watch.*
- **Pins.** A spire of carved bone pins on a dead-level brass plate; pull one clean, the pot doubles, the next pull is worse. Walk away any round with everything you've won. Topple it and the nearest three tables go gently, briefly quiet — which costs more than the stake did. *What it teaches the house: when you stop — its favorite fact about anyone.*
- **Markers** *(invitation only — Camille seats it)*. Nobody bets coin. Each seat stakes **something true**, written on house paper, folded, face-down. Three hands; win two. Win: your paper back unread, plus theirs — and handing a stranger's secret back *unread* is the classiest move in the room. Lose: the house files yours. On any hand rolled **with Fear**, the table read something true off you regardless of who took it. This is the purest thing the Pale Room does, and playing it well is the fastest version of **"be interesting"** — the drink may simply arrive.

**The house is watching, not hunting.** Never cheat them — the games are fair, and nobody at this house ever pushes a guest to keep playing; the danger on this floor is the guests' own appetite. Any Fear result on the felt means the room learned something true: log one tell per game. **What Marcel does with a tell is offer it back, plainly, as a courtesy** — *"You play carefully until you're behind. I mention it because the deal we're about to discuss will put you behind."* Never an ambush, never a twist of their own words — an observation laid on the table like a card face-up. That's when the party understands the room was reading them all night, and that the man reading them refuses to use it as a weapon. Both facts should be unsettling.

## Movement 2 — The Door *(5 min — nothing is announced)*

Nobody "meets Marcel." Three ways through, all of them Camille: **ask her plainly** (the *about* matters — vague asks get a lovely evening and no door) · **be interesting** · **be weird.** The party asking about the spear, by name, is plainly. When the answer is yes:

> Nobody comes to get you. A drink arrives that nobody ordered — the good one, the one you can't buy — and the girl sets it down without a word about it.
>
> "Monsieur Dray has time."
>
> Present tense. Like he's been holding it open for you.

He already knows why they've come. The floor files everything with Camille; Camille files with the ledger, nightly. The negotiation began the moment they gave up their coats.

## Movement 3 — The Table *(30–45 min — the campaign's coldest conversation)*

He opens with their names — all of them, correctly, no explanation. Offers something to drink. Asks what they **need**, not what they want. Then listens to the whole answer before speaking. When he shakes a hand, the cold spreads up the wrist before he lets go. He does not apologize for it.

**Isotta's stage-2 reveal fires here — the first time her second sight rests on him.** The canon read, exactly as written in [[marcel-dray|his file]]:

> Isotta — every ghost you've ever met is an *unfinished* thing. Open. Aching to be tied off. That's what your sight is for.
>
> He reads **finished.** Tied. Knotted — the knot every spirit at that wall would drown the world to get. A closed account.
>
> And the closed account is standing behind the card table, dry in a room full of sweating people, asking if anyone would like another drink.

Say it to her alone — a note, a whisper, however your table does private information. **What she does with it is hers.** He notices her noticing. He finds the question uninteresting. That is the entire exchange.

**The cub (optional — the grey beat, worth its two minutes).** Sometime mid-negotiation, the owlbear cub pads out from the private quarters and puts its head on Marcel's knee. He feeds it from his own hand without pausing his sentence — the one soft thing in the Pale Room, and the sibling of the cub sleeping on the party's boat. Say nothing. Let the table clock that the cold man has a pet he genuinely loves, and that they know something he doesn't: her mother is coming. *(Plants Branch 3 without a word; [[set-piece-the-mother-arrives]].)*

**Then run [[marcel-dray#The Table — the Marcel Decision Tree (GM reference)|the decision tree]] as written.** The felt-map, one line each — full branches in his file:

| They bring | It buys | One-line note |
|---|---|---|
| The stated price (Oswin ruined) | **The Tidebreaker** | He honors it to the letter. The cost lives on Tenson's sheet |
| A restitution schedule — numbers, collateral, a guarantor | The debt lane opens | Structured honesty is the one thing he finds *refreshing*. Say the snap-back plainly: if the schedule breaks, the original price returns |
| The cub brokered home, *before* the mother arrives | Real credit in his ledger | He'll be put out. He'll pay anyway — arithmetic beats fondness, and he knows it about himself |
| The right question about Caius | **Free** | ⚠ Don't let them overpay — he's been waiting two years to be asked |
| A theft attempt | The door closes | Known within hours. No hit squad — just a city where he *is* the door |

- **Stack the currencies.** The branches combine. A party that arrives with the mother brokered, a schedule drafted, and the right question asked can walk out with the spear, Oswin breathing, and Caius's death warrant — never once raising a voice. That night is *available*, and it should cost everything they've built to afford it.
- **Bargain, never yes/no** (house rule): terms, timing, wording, collateral. His *word* is fixed once given; his *opening* is not — negotiations left dangling get costlier while their urgency teaches him the spear matters.

### The Closed Account *(only if Caspar's death was faked)*

- ~~**The leash dissolves — and Marcel says so first.** Before anyone asks: *"You'll have heard about the curator. A dead man's debts close. His account is settled."*~~ **Re-cut 2026-10-02 (GM):** Marcel has no reason to suspect — but he's thorough, so **he asks.** *"Before the spear. The curator. You took Caspar Renne out on your boat, and you came back without him. I'd like to hear it from you."* Truth is free and buys the night's discount; a lie rolls Presence 14 under his eye; a failed lie is met without accusation. Then, always: *"Caspar thought I wanted him dead. I wanted him nervous… Men who pay don't have accidents."* Full branches: [[session-09-run-sheet]] Beat 3a · [[marcel-dray]] §Caspar, As Marcel Sees Him.
- **He does not accuse.** But remember who he is: the Empire certified *his* death too, thorough and official — **Marcel Dray is the one man in Sallowreach who knows exactly what a certified death is worth.** The file stays open behind his eyes. He asks no questions about it tonight.
- **The price of the lie is the discount they lose.** Candor is the only currency that buys one here. A party maintaining a fraud at his table plays every hand under the Composure passive, and if their nerve cracks around Caspar's name, the room notices — and the room files everything. Don't punish the plan; *price* it.

### Fear Menu *(performed — never a blade; one, maybe two, never three)*

- **The envelope.** Mid-negotiation, a host slides a sealed envelope face-down across the felt — a secret the party thought buried, arriving as a courtesy. Marcel doesn't look at it. *"For later. It's not why you're here."* *(The Marker Comes Due, [[the-pale-room]] — spend the Fear.)*
- **The walk-out.** Two enforcers materialize at Oswin's far table and *courteously* escort him out, mid-laugh, before the party is finished needing him in the room. Nobody hurries. That's what makes it land. *(S9: no Fear — it fires when Oswin's Stack hits zero.)*
- **The refill.** A PC bluffs or withholds — Marcel refills their glass himself, unasked, and says it plainly, without heat: *"You're holding something back. That's allowed. It's not my business until it touches the terms — and if it touches the terms, tell me before we agree, because I will hold you to exactly what we say."* No cat-and-mouse; the dread is that he's being **fair**, and everyone at the table knows it. Mark the Stress and move on.
- **The stillness.** Something displeases him. He goes very still and very quiet, and the whole floor drops half a register without a signal anyone saw. Hold it three full seconds. Resume.

## Walking Out *(not endings — states; Front 7 turns after the session)*

- **A closed deal.** Terms fixed, to the letter, forever. Log the exact wording — he will honor it with a lawyer's cruelty and a gentleman's grace.
- **A dangling negotiation.** Legal, and expensive: his opening hardens while they shop. The room is always glad to see them; the price is never again tonight's.
- **The disappointed door** *(theft, or a lie that surfaced)*. Quiet, total, and priced: the Deal withdrawn, the spear moved beyond a second attempt, every future price doubled. No one is ever rude to them again. That's the punishment.

**Threads opened:** the deal's shape forks the endgame (log it in [[STATUS]]) · Isotta holds what she saw — who does she tell? · the Camille recognition, if it fired, is now a live debt of its own (the boat, the bow, the family in Thornside) · if the Caius question got asked, Front 7 discharges — the holstered gun is in the party's hands · Marcel's open file on Caspar, if the fraud stands.

## 🚫 Hard Guardrails *(compiled from the files)*

- **Combat with Marcel is not an encounter that exists.** If blades come out, the room handles it ([[sallowreach-roster#Pale Room Enforcer|Marcel Is Watching]]) and you re-read his file before next session.
- **He never negotiates against himself.** The Deal is his only offer; every other lane arrives in the party's hands or not at all.
- **No gotchas, ever.** He never cheats, never word-traps, never springs what he knows. If the party misreads a term of *his* deal, he corrects them **before** they agree — even against his own interest. His menace is candor and arithmetic ([[marcel-dray#Playing Him Grey (the register — read this before every Marcel scene)|Playing Him Grey]] — read it before this scene).
- **He volunteers nothing that isn't asked.** The collectable truth, the Caius evidence — the right question is the price.
- **Never explain what he is.** Not tonight, not ever. Isotta's sight deepens the question; nothing answers it.
- **Isotta's read fires the moment she sees him — not before, not narrated to the room.** Hers to spend.
- **Camille never says Marcel's name about the cage.** Her channel is the coat, the cold men, the silence.
- **One meeting, one deal.** Closed terms never reopen; dangling ones get costlier.
