## 🧭 In Plain Words *(the whole session — no jargon)*

One split day. After tonight: Long Rest → Full Downtime/Town Day → Long Rest → Daytime Celebration (Session 8).

Two of them are already out in the swamp when the session opens — the ranger and the witch, on a one-day boat job for the hunters' guild, counting bird nests at a colony that's been dying. A huge old spirit shaped like a spoonbill is standing guard over it, starving, and it wants help killing the predatory fish eating the eggs. It's also been keeping something for four years that nobody ever came for: a shed snakeskin from an animal that has never been in its water. That skin is the thing the whole day is for.

Meanwhile the half-ghost is in town, walking an old man to his own funeral. He's a spirit who's been passing as human for a hundred and fifty years, his memory is going, and the one thing he still wants is the full name of the little girl who adopted him when he arrived. The family who has her belongings doesn't remember she existed. Three women in that family had names starting with M, and their things are all in the same trunks. She has to work out which one.

And the auditor, suspicious of why the embassy is starving the city's perimeter, visits the wizard who maintains the salt wall. He gets tea, a tour, a stack of paperwork, and — because the maintainer is sweating to hide his own embezzlement — two hundred years of the answer handed over as a distraction, along with a heavy crate of ancient journals.

Then everyone comes home, and the thing that collects the dead comes to collect the old man, leaving the half-ghost with a relic of peace, and says something about a number it can't get right.

## 🎭 The Theme *(say nothing, feel everything)*

**Everyone tonight is holding something for someone who never came back for it.**

- **Old Plenty** kept two dead hunters' camp for two seasons *"until someone came."* Nobody came. It also kept a skin it couldn't even count, because nothing else would.
- **The Morels** kept three women's trunks and never once opened them. *"You don't sort a thing like that, you just carry it."*
- **Basile** kept himself in this city for a hundred and fifty years for a girl who's been gone for seventy.
- **The Tallykeeper** has been carrying nine entries it cannot close since before the pilings were cut.
- **Oswin** keeps the wall. And keeps one drawer shut.

Nobody says the word. Don't let them. It's in the shape.

## 🎲 Dice First

*The reflex: the moment a PC tries to* get *something — read a person, find a thing, push past a "no" — call the roll* **before** *you narrate the result.*

**Action roll** = 2d12 (Hope die + Fear die) + trait vs **Difficulty.** Hope die high → PC gains **Hope.** Fear die high → **you** gain **Fear.** Matching dice = **Crit** (auto-succeed, gain Hope, clear Stress).

- ⭐ **Spend a Hope → +2 from a relevant Experience.** Every roll below names which one. **Say it out loud when you call the roll.**
- **Help an Ally** → helper spends 1 Hope, rolls +d6 (highest d6 counts). ⚠ **Only two PCs on the boat** — Help matters more than usual tonight.
- **Tag Team** → 3 Hope, two PCs on one action. Still never used at this table. **Offer it in the gar fight, out loud.**
- ⚠ **Reaction rolls** make no Hope/Fear and can't be Helped.
- **Gate the cost, not the fun.** They always learn *something*; the die sets the price.

**Four answers, not two:** success-with-Hope · success-with-Fear · failure-with-Hope · failure-with-Fear, and the Fear spend lives inside the failure-with-Fear row.

**Diff ladder:** 11 baseline · 13–14 hard · 15 a real reach.

### 0 · ⭐ The Rookery — *cold open, on the water* (30–35 min)

**Run from [[set-piece-the-wrong-skin]] — the S7 panel, then Beat 5.** Everything below is the index, not the scene.

**The read-aloud is printed in the file.** Open on it. It ends on *"then one of the snags stands up"* — go straight into Old Plenty.

- **Old Plenty stands up and counts.** *"Forty nests, four winters gone. Nineteen, two winters. Six, this one."* Tic: it speaks only in tallies. Grief comes out as arithmetic. **It never begs.**
- ⭐ **The count of the boat.** *"Your boat carries two."* [beat, looking at Chip Chip] *"My water counts three."* If Leavy pushes: *"It is not born and it is not dead. I do not have a column for it."* **A chill and a joke, not a hook.** Nothing is wrong with the familiar.

- ⭐ **The Kept Skin.** Show it first — something pale, high in the root-arches, folded over a root like a coat over a chair arm. Then its four lines, ending on *"Take it down. I have no use for a thing I cannot count."* **No roll.**
- ⭐ **The camp, offered back.** *"It is an hour back the way you came. I have kept it two seasons. I would like to stop."* **That's a real decision for the return leg** — Gravois's tally-book and Brin's gut-punch, against the last of the light.
- 🎲 **Shore up the shelves 12**, then 🎲 **the count 12** — Fletcher's tally now.
- 📖 **Leavy reads Orna's 5th Notebook (Spoken Read-Aloud):**
  > Leavy, you crack open that fifth notebook looking for the night-vision brew, and it’s… chaos.
  >
  > The leather is warped from swamp water, the margins have dried beet stains, and every page is in three different inks. Half of it isn't even alchemy—it’s grocery lists, arguments with herself, and sketches of frogs with hats.
  >
  > But right in the middle, the handwriting gets frantic, pressed hard enough to tear the page:
  >
  > *“Year six. The grey-rot girl from the docks. Three drops in boiled well-water and the skin knit clean in thirty seconds. SHE BREATHED. It worked. It actually worked.”*
  >
  > And right under it, scribbled out four times in black ink:
  >
  > *“Why won't it repeat? The sap? The soil from the southwest tree? I'm forgetting the third step. My hands keep turning green and the third step is GONE. Write it down before the head goes soft. WRITE IT DOWN.”*
  >
  > And then the next page is just a recipe for pickled radish.

**Cut here, mid-water, and go to town.**
