# The Tether, Made Personal — Manifestations

*GM only. The reserve item from [[STATUS]] ("held until PCs are known"), now built against three sessions of play. This is how the shared curse shows up differently in each of the four — the recurring sensory signatures, the costs, the gifts, and the read-aloud fragments to deliver them with.*

## The One Rule — it welcomes, it doesn't punish

The hidden explanation that unifies every symptom (house pattern): **the tether is a small binding cut from the great one, and the great binding doesn't restrain — it *keeps*.** It holds the way the city holds: by adoption. So each PC's manifestation is a **welcome, mis-shaped to fit the person** — the cage speaking to each of them in the language of what they already long for. Home, for the man who won't admit he misses it. Memory, for the one who lost hers. Belonging, for the one caught between worlds. Duty, for the one who can't put it down.

None of it hurts *as its first move.* That's the horror, and it's the same horror as the city's warmth: being loved by something that will not let go.

**Guardrails:**
- **Never say Celebration.** These are chain-#3 priming beats ([[the-celebration-clue-map]]); they feed the click, they never pre-empt it. Recognition stays earned.
- **Distinct from personal curses.** Isotta's Slipping clock, Leavy's clank-curse, the ghost-state — separate conditions, separate files. If a symptom could belong to a personal arc, it's the wrong symptom.
- **One manifestation beat per session, at most** — same rationing as the coin beat. These land by being rare.
- **All four go quiet on Celebration nights** (canon since [[session-05-first-celebration]]'s night button — the best sleep in Sallowreach). That correlation is *theirs* to notice. Say nothing.

## Fletcher — the compass that bends

*His register: direction, trails, home. The welcome says: **you live here now.***

**The Signature** *(recurring — establish it, then repeat without comment)*: the smell of his home spawning-pool — mud-warm water, reed pollen, the specific green of it — arriving on a wind that cannot be carrying it. Always faint. Always from the **wrong direction: deeper in.**

> Fletcher — hold on. Stop. That smell. That's home — that's *exactly* home, the pool, the reeds, all of it. And here's the thing your ranger brain won't let you un-notice: the wind's coming off the deep channels. Southwest. Home is northeast, four hundred miles. The smell is coming from the wrong way.

**The Cost:** his Wayfinder's sense — never lost, never wrong — now *bends at the edge.* Routes that lead out of the Mire read dim and cold on his internal compass, like a map with the margins burned; pressing against the boundary anyway costs a Stress and a wave of vertigo his body files as *wrongness* `[homebrew — Stress grammar, GM's call]`. He is the party's navigator, and the one direction he can no longer navigate is *away.*

**The Gift:** pointing inward, he's better than he's ever been. The deep Mire reads to him like a marked trail — channels legible, sign fresh, the whole southwest laid out like it *wants* walking. Grant advantage on navigation and tracking deeper into the Mire `[VERIFY: advantage grammar]`. The campaign needs them sailing toward the Heart; his curse greases the road and he should eventually notice that it does.

**The Knife under it:** the trail home going cold in real time was always his quiet dread. Now it's literal — and the Mire keeps offering him a *warmer* trail, pointed the other way.

## Leavy — the water that almost remembers

*Their register: taste, memory, self. The welcome says: **what you lost is here.***

**The Signature:** charged Mire water, on the tongue — and they taste everything; it's the profession — carries a flavor they can never quite place. Not an ingredient. A *when.* Like tasting a kitchen from a childhood you didn't have.

> Leavy — you dip a finger, out of habit, taste-testing the channel like you'd test a brew. And it's there again. Under the silt and the tannin and the faint char of the bioluminescence — something *familiar.* Familiar like a name on the tip of your tongue. You almost have it. You almost—
>
> It's gone. It's always gone. Mark a Stress or spit — your choice, every time.

**The Cost:** the almost-memory, on demand and never delivered. Standing too long in charged water, the vessel feels *porous* — like the Mire is proofreading a document it didn't write and finding the seams. A Stress when the almost-name slips away at a bad moment `[homebrew]`. The cruelty is precision-built: the one thing they want is memory, and the water deals in *almost.*

**The Gift:** everything they brew on Mire water is stronger — the charged water is a transformation-engine and their craft runs on it. One die step, a longer duration, a brighter effect, GM's call per brew `[VERIFY]`. And quietly, arc-serving: the water is *training their tongue.* Every almost-taste sharpens the palate that will someday read what [[seven|Seven]] carries ([[leavy-arc]] — the taste-vault). The tether is tutoring them for their own reveal. Neither of you says this.

**The Knife under it:** their reflection in still Mire water occasionally holds a *posture* that isn't the clank's — a set of the shoulders, a way of tilting the head, someone's old habit. Never a face. **Never a face** — the face is endgame, and it isn't this file's to give.

## Isotta — the water that answers back

*Her register: the between, and being seen. The welcome says: **you belong to us.***

**The Signature:** on still nights, the black water lights — faintly, blue-green, a single slow pulse — **only where she's looking.** Not where the others look. She turns her head; the light follows a half-second late, like an eye tracking an eye.

> Isotta — everyone else sees black water. You know that, because you checked, twice, without making it obvious. But everywhere you rest your eyes tonight, the water breathes one slow pulse of light back at you. Just once. Just where you look. The Mire has noticed you're looking — and it's looking back the way you look at something you recognize.

**The Cost:** the under-hum. There is a chord in the deep water — low, constant, beneath hearing — and it *harmonizes with her.* Opening her second sight near open water sets the tether resonating: mark a Stress, and the deep pulls at her attention like a conversation she's rude to be ignoring `[homebrew — interacts with, but is not, the Slipping clock; keep the files separate]`.

**The Gift:** she can feel the weave. Thin veil, worn stretches, spirit-stuff under strain — she reads it the way Fletcher reads a trail. **Once per session, free:** the GM hands her one true directional impression (the wall is thinner *there*; something crossed *here*, recently; the thing in the deep is *that* way). She's the early-warning system her sheet promises — this is the dial that delivers it.

**The Knife under it:** of the four of them, her welcome is the only one that feels like *recognition* — and some nights, in the worst quiet way, like the only place that's ever offered it without conditions. The dead revere her, the water greets her, the city feeds her. Being claimed feels good. That's the trap the whole campaign is made of.

## Tenson — the post they can't stand down from

*Their register: duty, the unclosed file. The welcome says: **you have not been relieved.***

**The Signature:** at the Mire's edge — and only there — a sentry's bell. Faint, behind them, from the direction of the city. The sound of a watch changing. Nobody rings it. It rings anyway, and their body answers it before their mind can object: shoulders square, weight shifts, *on duty.*

> Tenson — you're at the boundary markers, and you hear it. A bell. A watch-bell, the change-of-shift kind, the one you've responded to your whole working life — coming from behind you. From the city. There is no bell out here. Your body has already turned around. You didn't decide that.

**The Cost:** the pull wears a uniform. The others feel homesick or haunted; Tenson feels **derelict** — the tether reads as a post they haven't been relieved from, a report undelivered, a duty with no discharge date. Contemplating leaving — really contemplating it, at the edge, bags notionally packed — costs a Stress and a night's sleep `[homebrew]`. The auditor's itch, colonized: an unclosed ledger-line in this city now keeps them physically awake.

**The Gift:** the Stalwart's watch, steadied. When Tenson stands guard over someone else inside the Mire — the wall between a person and harm, the exact work they're trying to retire from — the tether holds them like a braced footing: **once per session, clear a Stress when they take a hit or hold a line for another person** `[VERIFY: Stress-clear pacing]`. The thing they want to stop being is the thing the curse rewards. Let them notice. Let it complicate the pivot.

**The Knife under it:** the assignment was supposed to end — investigate, report, return. The tether deleted the last verb, and Tenson's manifestation is the deletion made audible: a bell that calls them back to a post nobody assigned. Whether [[silence|Silence]] would call that a tragedy or an asset is already on their sheet. The bell doesn't care.

## The Comparison *(stage this — a campfire scene, S5–7, whenever they finally trade symptoms)*

Sooner or later they'll compare notes — engineer it gently if they don't (Remy asking "so what's it actually *like*?" over a fire does it in one line). When the four symptoms are on the table together, the pattern is the clue, and it fires at exactly legal brightness:

- Four different symptoms. One grammar. **Every one of them is an invitation, not an injury.**
- Let a player say it — the table's version of: *"it's not keeping us out of the world. It's keeping us* in *a place. Like it… wants us?"*
- The word someone should almost reach and not find: not *imprisoned.* ***Kept.*** *(The same word the salt wall, the honored guests, and the founders' chants all orbit. Say nothing. File it. It pays off at the Reading — [[reveal-read-alouds]] §4.)*
- **The host has a face now** *(2026-07-18)*: the being doing the welcoming is met in [[set-piece-the-honored-guest]] — and its *"did you curse us?"* answer is this file's four symptoms replayed in one image: a host setting a table for four guests it never meant to take. When that scene fires, every manifestation the party has ever felt retroactively becomes dialogue.

## The Edge *(if they physically test the boundary — pairs with Vane's boundary-attempts entry, [[the-first-maintainers-journal]])*

Each manifestation spikes in its own register, in this order as they press: Fletcher's compass whites out (every direction reads as *away from home*) → Leavy tastes the almost-name **continuously**, unbearable → the water lights everywhere Isotta looks, pleading → Tenson's bell rings without stopping. Then the physical hold (the established canon — the tether does not negotiate). Walking back, all four symptoms go quiet at once — *satisfied* — and that's the detail they'll hate most. Give it to them straight and let nobody sleep well.

## Fear-Spend Hooks *(upgrades the [[panic-button]] "tether ache" line — spend 1, pick the register)*

- **Fletcher:** the home-smell, mid-scene, from deeper in — right when he's choosing a route.
- **Leavy:** the almost-name surfaces during a brew and takes the measurement with it — re-roll or mark a Stress.
- **Isotta:** the under-hum swells during a quiet human moment — the deep, jealous of her attention.
- **Tenson:** the bell, in the city, once — during the exact conversation where they're being told they're only muscle.

## When It Ends *(one line each, banked for whichever ending lifts it — menu-agnostic, per the no-scripted-endings rule. Delivery is staged: [[ending-read-alouds]] §1 plays first, then these, one per player, around the table.)*

- **Fletcher:** the wind turns, and home smells like it's coming from home.
- **Leavy:** the water finally tastes like nothing at all — just water — and they cry about it, probably.
- **Isotta:** she looks at the black water and it does not look back. She gets to decide how that feels.
- **Tenson:** silence where the bell was. Relieved of post. Both meanings.
