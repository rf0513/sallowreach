# Twist the Knife — the emotional pressure menu

*The per-PC companion to the [[panic-button]]'s Fear menu: that page escalates* danger, *this one escalates* meaning. *Grab one when a scene is mechanically fine but emotionally flat.*

**The rules of the knife:**
1. **Be a fan first.** A knife exists to let a character *shine under pressure*, never to punish a player. If the twist won't produce a moment the player retells later, put it back.
2. **One knife per PC per session, max.** Same rationing as the coin beat and the tether beat — these land by being rare.
3. **Twist on strength, not on weakness.** The best knives arrive when a character is winning, comfortable, or loved — not when the player is already down.
4. **Softest version first.** Every knife below is written soft → hard. You can always escalate later; you can't un-stab.

---

## Fletcher — the man who decided not to care

1. **The thanks he can't file.** Someone he helped — Brin, a dockhand, a kid — thanks him *sincerely*, at length, in public. Not for the job. For *him.* Watch the professional try to receipt an emotion.
2. **Ribbit kids at the feast.** The edge tables, a swarm of them, climbing on him like he's furniture. He counts continuance in clutches. He's counting right now. Say nothing.
3. **The journal, needed.** Something only works if he hands over the most personal object he owns — to be borrowed, cited, or ([[fletcher-arc]], S7) cut up and boiled. He treats it like ammunition. Make him spend it.
4. **The letter from home** *(the heavy one — canon device, [[session-01-prep]])*: the town grieving a clutch *and* the hunter it sent, in his sister's handwriting, forwarded after the ship was reported lost. What does a man who measures himself in jobs completed write back?
5. **Needed for love before revenge:** the owlbear crisis wants his exact skills and has nothing to do with his snake ([[set-piece-the-mother-arrives]]). The knife is that he's *better* in that fight than he's ever been on the hunt — and someone tells him so.

## Leavy — the person shaped like an absence

1. **"It."** A stranger — polite, no malice — refers to Leavy as *it.* Once. Nobody at the table corrects them fast enough. Move the scene along; let the silence do it.
2. **The face-slip** *(sheet canon)*: in a moment of exclusion they wear a human face without deciding to — and [[delphine|Delphine]] or [[orna-tessant|Orna]] *notices, and says nothing.* The kindness is the devastation.
3. **The waste.** Orna, who could fill libraries, waves off another untranscribed miracle — in front of the documentarian who knows exactly what it is to lose what was never written down. Let Leavy be the one who argues.
4. **Seven remembers** *(arc canon)*: the magpie does something only possible if it knew them *before* — and cannot say a word of it. One head-tilt. One object placed just so. Then dinner as usual.
5. **The question, asked gently** *(hold for a quiet high-trust scene)*: someone who loves them — Remy's good at this — asks: *"If you get the old you back… what happens to the you we know?"* Do not resolve it. It isn't resolvable. That's the knife.

## Isotta — the one the water recognizes

1. **The cat.** A child's pet takes one look at her and won't come out from under the table. The child asks why. Kids ask *loudly.*
2. **Guest-night kinship** *(S5 canon)*: she counts the spirits passing as human at the feast — fed, toasted, welcome — and realizes she's been *passing* at every meal since she died. The city's warmth works on her kind. Both facts are true at once.
3. **The crossing she conducts** *(docket work, [[isotta-arc]])*: she ushers someone across, and it's beautiful, and the door that opens for them has never once opened for her. Play the pride first. The ache after.
4. **The best sleep** *(arc canon, the wonder-note)*: after a Tallykeeper beat, she sleeps *better* — the debt-holder's presence steadies the fraying. The knife is the implication she works out alone: the thing that's owed her death is also the thing holding her together.
5. **The living need her too** *(the inversion — spend it on a big scene)*: someone alive, hurt, scared, reaches for *her* — not the Order's tools, her. She's spent the campaign managing the dead. Let the living be the thing that undoes her composure.

## Tenson — the guardian who wants a desk

1. **"Look at the size of you."** Mid-sentence — *their* sentence, about ledger irregularities — someone admires the shoulders. Warm, well-meant, total erasure. Their whole life in one compliment.
2. **Sable's peace** *(the foil, never mockery)*: Sable, gloriously content being average, catches Tenson watching them laugh and says: *"You could just be happy, you know. It's allowed."* It isn't a taunt. That's the problem.
3. **The desk, dangled** *(poisoned — Caius's move)*: the Empire always needs clean auditors; a liaison can arrange a clerkship, a real one, papers and everything. The dream, offered by the man whose books they're secretly unpicking. The price isn't stated. Prices never are, with him.
4. **Silence's letter** *(canon: "I have spent a week being a person who believes that, and I find I refuse")*: her letters keep arriving — warm, brisk, *expecting.* Each one is a report due to the person whose kindness put them in the cage. When the redline lands, ask what they tell *her.*
5. **Perrin, healed** *(the buried skill honored)*: the guard Tenson patched up wins something — a race, a posting, a spar — and dedicates it, publicly, to "the auditor who fixed my knee." The room applauds the *body-work.* Tenson wanted to be seen for the brain. They got seen for the hands. Sit in it. *(Best room for it: [[the-last-board|The Last Board]] — Perrin is Maud's grandson, and Thornside applauding an imperial-adjacent outsider is worth three fronts of goodwill.)*

## Universal knives *(whole-party, use sparingly)*

- **Remy goes without** *(the coin engine's face — [[coin-in-sallowreach]])*: the boots, the tobacco, the good lamp oil. No lecture. Just the sight.
- **The warmth aimed at whoever holds apart** *(the Celebration's Fear feature)*: the city adopts the most guarded PC — a family drum pressed into their hands. Warmth as weaponry; it makes the endgame heavier.
- **The tether, in register** — the per-PC menu lives in [[tether-manifestations]]: the home-smell, the almost-name, the water that looks back, the bell.

## The Late Knives *(Acts 2–3 — each is gated on a reveal; check the gate before you reach for it. Same four rules apply, and rule 3 doubles: by now every PC is strong, so every knife lands on strength.)*

*These only cut after the truth starts landing. Before the Reading ([[reveal-read-alouds]] §4), they're just odd sentences — hold them.*

### Fletcher
- **The steward's word for it** *(gate: the Reading)*: his whole profession is creatures out of place — culling what shouldn't be here, protecting what should. Then someone asks him, professionally, hypothetically, what you do with a population *held* out of its range for two hundred years. He knows this answer. He's given it about snakes. Don't say it for him; leave the silence where the answer goes.
- **The job with no client** *(gate: the Double Knot resolved)*: the first morning of his adult life with no commission. Brin pays the fee, correct to the coin; he counts it twice. Before he can decide what he's *for* now, somebody's kid needs walking home through the dark — and he goes. Let the player notice there was no job number.

### Leavy
- **The same manners** *(gate: [[set-piece-the-honored-guest]] met)*: the spirit was rearranged by someone else's yes and has been a perfect guest about it for two hundred years. Leavy has been polite about theirs too. One observer — Orna's the mouth for it — says only: *"You two have the same manners."* Nothing else. Do not explain it. It is not explainable; it is only true.
- **The cure's provenance** *(gate: late cure work — the essence/charged-water thread)*: the draught that could hand back the old self draws on the held presence itself. Getting Leavy back means taking one more thing from the being that lost most. Terms, not yes/no — [[marcel-dray]] rules: state it plainly *before* anything's brewed.

### Isotta
- **The door policy** *(gate: the Reading)*: the next time the welcome verse goes up — the children's verse, hands over hearts — she hears it as what it is. She has stood at a wall that refused her. At the honored-guest toast, she genuinely cannot tell whether she's the guest or the door. Give her the line and let the player pick which.
- **What she'd be asking to lose** *(gate: endgame doorstep — resolving the binding is actually on the table)*: her steadiness sits on the debt-holder's ledger; the arrangement holding her fraying together is part of what "resolving everything" resolves. The knife is that she's *comfortable* — freedom is the disruptive option. If it needs a mouth, the Tallykeeper has one, and no contractions: *"You are aware of what you are asking to lose."*

### Tenson
- **The unfileable finding** *(gate: the Reading)*: the audit of a lifetime — a city whose books balance perfectly because one account has never been paid, two hundred years running. Textbook. Career-making. And there is no office anywhere that accepts the filing. They finally out-brained everyone, and the only honest ledger left is themself. Ask what they write down. Ask where they keep it.
- **The seal, wanted clean** *(gate: late — after the seal's been fought over all campaign)*: someone *good* — Adaline, Solène, Maud — asks Tenson to certify a thing that's true but unprovable, on their authority alone, as a kindness. Caspar wanted the seal for a lie; the campaign taught them what it costs. Now it's wanted for something right, and it costs the same. Sit in it.

### Universal, late
- **The bread didn't change** *(gate: the Reading)*: the next Homecoming plate arrives hot and free, pressed into their hands by somebody's grandmother, and every kindness in the city now has the last line of the Reading underneath it. Serve dinner. Describe it well. Say nothing else.
- **The children's choir** *(gate: the Reading)*: kids on a stoop, learning the welcome verse by sound, the way every generation has. It's adorable. Let it be adorable. Count three, move on.
- **The warm question** *(gate: the party owns the truth)*: an NPC they love asks — no suspicion, just fondness — *"So what did you find out there?"* The party owns the answer now, and every friendly mouth is a fork in the road. Never force the telling. Just keep the question coming, from people they can't lie to comfortably.
