# The Eddy

**Type:** Residential quarter — the immigrant pocket; where [[Sallowreach]]'s ~3,000 kept outsiders live
**Region:** [[Sallowreach]] — mid-city, two boardwalk tiers in from [[The Mooring]], built around a slow canal loop
**Controlled By:** Nobody on paper. **Cassia Fontaine's** kitchen in practice.

*Everyone in this quarter is someone the city kept — without magic. The party's possible futures live here, with faces and cook-smoke: year two, year six, year forty. This is where the do-nothing ending goes to be looked at. It is also where [[Caius Maren|Caius]] is quietly fishing for witnesses, and where [[items/the-first-maintainers-journal|the journal quest]] physically happens (Reille's house is two streets off the plaza).*

---

## What You See

Outdoors. A canal loop, not a canal street — the water here circles a small market plaza instead of going anywhere, and flotsam rides the loop for days before it leaves. That's the name: things the current catches, going around. In view: stilt-houses with *wrong* details — an imperial brass door-knocker on bayou blackwood, window boxes of homeland herbs sulking in the humidity, a prayer-flag line strung the way no local family strings washing. Twelve architectures of homesickness on one boardwalk. At the plaza's edge, under an awning patched with old sail: **the Year Table** — Cassia's cookshop, one long communal table, always at least half full.

Locals say it with a smile that isn't unkind: *"The Eddy caught you yet?"*

## The Year Count

Every arrival says the same sentence: *"One year. Then home."* The Eddy's standing joke — and its whole sociology — is the question **"What year are you on?"** Use this table as instant-NPC fuel (names: [[improv-ammo]] §1, outsider bank; the longer they've stayed, the more local the surname sounds):

| Year | What staying looks like | Say it like |
|---|---|---|
| **1–2** | Rotation-brain. Counts days, hates the damp, writes home weekly. Still says "one year" like a promise. | Defensive, fast |
| **3–6** | Has a favorite stall, a nickname, a chair at the Year Table. Says "one year" like a habit. Letters home have slowed. | Sheepish |
| **7–15** | "Almost local." Kids with bayou vowels. Went home once to visit; came back early and won't discuss it. | A laugh, a shrug |
| **20+** | Doesn't answer. Asks what year *you're* on, and means it kindly, and waits. | Level, warm |

*The pipeline is canon ([[Sallowreach]]): attachés and guild reps who stop finding supplication humiliating stop wanting to go home. The Eddy is where they land when they cross over.*

## Cassia Fontaine

**Role:** Keeper of the Year Table and the quarter's temperature; arrived forty-one years ago as an Alchemists' Consortium junior clerk — the most miserable posting in the city. Married a poleman named Fontaine. Buried him. Stayed.
**Tier:** 2 *(social — the Eddy's Maud, and the two women know of each other with professional respect)*

Late sixties, small, quick, always mid-task. Serves every newcomer their **first meal free** — the Eddy's one sacrament — and asks the question while they eat.

**Delivery tic (GM — act this):** she never says "home" for a place. She says *there* and *here*, and lets you hear the capital letters. *"You'll say 'home' again someday, cher. First it stops meaning There. Then a long while it means nothing. Then one morning it means Here, and you'll cry into perfectly good rice."*

**What she wants:** an **Eddy table at the Celebration.** Two hundred years of family roles ([[Sallowreach]]) and the 3,000 have none — they're forever guests at other people's tables. She wants one feast table, Eddy-cooked, homeland dishes offered *to* the city: *"Two hundred years this city has fed its guests. Someone should feed it back."* It's a completely achievable ask that nobody with standing has ever carried to [[Adaline Roux|Adaline]]. *(GM — what this buys: the warmest scene available on [[world-in-motion]] Front 4, and one of the few places the party can straightforwardly make the city better. Three thousand people finally get a seat at the thing that defines the place they live. ⚰️ **2026-07-25:** this is no longer an amplitude play — attendance is not a lever, and the binding is not part of this scene. It's just a good thing to do.)*

**What she's hiding:** **the sea-chest under her bed.** Packed for the crossing home — and repacked, carefully, every spring for forty years. Petition drafts to the Council are in there too, fifteen of them, none ever sent: if it's refused, the hope dies, so the hope stays in the chest. The woman who teaches the whole quarter to stop counting never stopped. The day her table is granted is the day she unpacks it. If the party ever helps her do that, play every minute.

**Voice:**
- *"Sit. First meal's mine; that's not kindness, it's law. Now — what year are you on?"*
- *"Nobody comes to Sallowreach on purpose, cher. They come for a year. The Mire does its arithmetic after."*
- *(the crack, if trust is earned)* *"Forty-one years. I could go. I know the boats better than the polemen."* [wipes the clean table] *"I repack it in spring. The spring air There smells like — I don't finish that sentence anymore."*

## Severin Calder

**Role:** The Eddy's letter-writer; formerly the Empire's senior trade attaché in Sallowreach — **the man whose rooms [[Caius Maren|Caius]] had reassigned on arrival.** When his recall finally came through two years ago, he read it twice and walked his effects to the Eddy instead. The Embassy filed it as a breakdown. The Eddy filed it as Tuesday.
**Tier:** 2

Fifties, precise, imperial posture going soft at the edges like everything here. Runs a scribe's table by the plaza: the man who wrote **forty-four quarterly reports nobody read** now writes letters home for neighbors who can't — *"and every one of these gets answered."* He says that flatly, like a finding.

**Delivery tic (GM — act this):** he proofreads his own speech, correcting word choice mid-sentence. *"I resigned. — No. I stopped. 'Resigned' implies somebody read it."*

**What he wants:** **someone to take Caius off his hands.** Severin read the room-reassignment for what it was — the opening move of a man building a legacy case — and a year ago he sent one anonymous letter to the capital saying so. No answer. He can't tell whether that silence means *sanctioned* or *unread*, and it gnaws. He won't go to the Council — crossing over means you're done doing Empire business; that's the deal he made with himself. But a certain kind of visitor, one who reads documents the way he used to… he'd talk. *(Wire: this hands [[players/tenson-arc|Tenson]] the one person in the city who can professionally confirm that Caius is **freelancing** — see [[caius-maren]], "none of it has been sanctioned.")*

**What he's hiding:** **his attaché seal.** Never surrendered; the Embassy's inventory says *lost in transit.* A live imperial seal in a defector's desk drawer — which matters enormously the day anyone needs imperial paper to say something the Empire didn't authorize. *(GM: yes, that's a second seal Caius doesn't know exists — a quiet backstop or escalation for the false-report gambit in [[session-03-caspar-encounter]] and everything downstream of the [[session-04-redline-meeting|redline]]. He would need a very good reason. "Good" by *his* ledger, not the Empire's.)*

**Voice:**
- *"Letters are my trade. The Empire taught me. — Taught me half. The Eddy taught me the half where someone writes back."*
- *(on Caius, if trust is earned)* *"He took my rooms the day he arrived. Not needed them — took them. That's not comfort, that's flag-planting. I wrote as much to the capital."* [beat] *"You'll notice I now write other things."*
- *(to Tenson, watching them actually read a document to the end)* *"You read the whole thing. [beat] Sit down."*

## The Arrivals Ledger

At the Year Table, chained to nothing, guarded by everything: a fat ledger older than Cassia. **Every arrival who stays a season gets written in. Nobody is ever crossed out.** The moved-away get a small wave-mark — *gone to water.* The dead get an underline — the Eddy's word for it is ***kept.*** When the party has eaten their first meal, Cassia opens it and writes their four names while they watch, unhurried, and blots it, and says nothing about it at all.

*(GM — for [[isotta|Isotta]]: a book where no one is ever removed, in the hands of a woman on [[players/isotta-arc|a docket]]. It's paper and love, not power — it rhymes with the Tallykeeper's ledger and must never touch it. Don't explain. Let the player feel whatever they feel when the pen keeps her name.)*

## A Scene If You Need One (d8)

1. **A send-off.** Someone's rotation ended; they're *actually going home.* Feast, songs, tears — and a betting slate on when they'll be back. (Spring. It's always spring.)
2. **A first meal.** A shattered new Consortium clerk, day four, being fed. Cassia seats the party beside them: *"You're newer than anybody. Tell them it gets—"* [waves a spoon] *"—tell them something."*
3. **Severin's queue.** Three neighbors deep. He's reading an answered letter aloud to a weeping poleman, flat-voiced, kind.
4. **Caius's aide** in the plaza, buying dinners, collecting "statements on conditions." The Eddy being elaborately, collectively vague at him.
5. **A homeland feast-day.** One family keeps their old country's festival; the whole quarter shows up; nobody knows the steps; everyone dances anyway.
6. **The question turns on the party.** *"And you four — what year are you on?"* The honest answer is *weeks.* The question lands anyway.
7. **A ledger dispute.** Did old Andrée get her wave-mark or her underline? Nobody's sure she's dead. Letters are being written. Severin has opinions about the addressing.
8. **A window-box funeral.** A homeland herb finally died after twenty years of sulking. Its owner is devastated out of all proportion. *(A [[the-witch|Leavy]]-shaped problem — [[The Verdant Margin]] could graft the rootstock, if someone thought to ask.)*

## Tensions & Hooks

- **Caius is fishing here.** His annexation case needs credible, sympathetic witnesses ([[caius-maren]]) and he's misread the Eddy as *his* people — fellow imperials who'll want out someday. They won't, and they sandbag him beautifully. But the newest, loneliest arrivals are exactly as vulnerable as he thinks. **If ignored:** he gets his three signatures from year-one clerks — names on a document that will someday be read aloud in the capital.
- **Cassia's ask is a quest sitting in the open:** carry the petition she'll never send, or get her in a room with [[Adaline Roux|Adaline]]. Payoff: an Eddy table at the next Celebration — three thousand people with no family role in the city's biggest night, finally given one, and a second matriarch who owes the party a table. *(Pairs with Maud's ask — one wants the city to come out, one wants the Eddy let in. A party brokering both reshapes [[world-in-motion]] Front 4. ⚰️ **2026-07-25:** this is no longer a "counterweight to emptying benches" — there is no walkout and attendance is not a lever. It's just a good thing to do for people nobody thought to invite.)*
- **The journal quest lives here.** Reille's house — Natta's two floors of hoard, the polite man who'll be back — is two streets off the plaza ([[items/the-first-maintainers-journal]]). Natta Preis was Eddy to the bone: year fifty-one when she died, underlined in the ledger. *Kept.*
- **[[Remy Duval|Remy]] is the Eddy's favorite native** — the one local who seeks strangers out ([[remy]] "collects" them; the Eddy finds this hilarious and correct). The quarter watches his generosity toward the party with a landlord's eye: when his charity starts wearing thin, it's Cassia who notices first, and a quiet word at the Year Table is how the party finds out — the city's way of saying *the free year ends; what year are you on?*

## Secrets

- **The sea-chest** (Cassia, above) — repacked every spring for forty years.
- **The seal** (Severin, above) — *"lost in transit."*
- **The coast-and-back pattern.** Eddy folk *can* leave — they visit There, some stay away a year — and they come back, and their reasons never quite cover it. **GM guardrail:** this is *not* the tether. Nobody in this quarter is bound; in two hundred years exactly one person shared the party's condition, and he's in a grave behind the Maintenance House ([[items/the-first-maintainers-journal|GM only]]). If the party interrogates the pattern, the Eddy's answer is the [[session-05-first-celebration|satisfied non-answer]] register: *"The Mire catches people for reasons, cher."* Never let anyone here claim a magic they don't have — the truth is lonelier and more human: the city is simply very good at keeping people, and it had two hundred years of practice before it ever needed a spell.

## Dungeon Alchemist Notes

Small plaza over a canal loop: one long communal table under a patched-sail awning (cookshop against the adjacent house), scribe's table with stool and letter-box opposite, mixed-style stilt-houses ringing the water — vary the door and window dressings per house, add window boxes and strung flags. One fat ledger on the communal table. Day lighting, warm. The playable focus is the plaza, the Year Table, and Reille's cluttered two-floor house two streets out.
