# Fletcher — Arc: The Season-Eater

> **Status: ACTIVE** *(designed 2026-07-02; lore recut same day — lab origin locked).* Everything player-facing on [[fletcher|Fletcher's sheet]] stays true — this deepens the GM side and welds the hunt to the party and to [[gault|Gault]]. Supersedes the amber-civet "Haunt of Thornside" material in [[brin-sevarre|Brin's file]] (overtaken at the table: the owlbear cub *was* the trust-test, and he passed — see [[session-03-recap]]). Tuned for the sheet's lever: **validate the competence, then make him love something.** The arc's thesis in one line: *the hunt is only completable by a man who lets people flank him.*

## The Answer (GM only — the whole arc in one paragraph)

The amphisbaena is not an animal. It is a **product.** Somewhere upriver, an imperial research facility — the kind whose name is redacted on every page Tenson will ever find — spent years learning how to fuse two serpents into one body that **never sleeps**: a living sentry, one mind always waking while the other works. Specimen brand **2H-3** — which means there were at least two before it. It was fed cheap: eggs and pen-raised young, by the crate. Three years ago the facility needed it moved — quietly, off the books, to a buyer whose name also doesn't appear anywhere — and for freight like that you hire the one courier who moves things with no paper: **[[gault|Gault]].** At a barge stage half a day upriver from a Ribbit spawning-town, the crate went into the water. Neither the lab nor Gault reported the loss — both had their reasons for silence — so **no one warned the towns on the river.** The thing that came out of the water was starving, foreign, and did exactly what the lab had taught it to do since hatching: it found the nursery pool, and it fed, three nights running, until the pool was empty. Then it followed the only road it knew — Gault's pipeline, every crate sweating the smell of the richest water in the world — cache by cache, cracking his goods, all the way to the source. The Mire: warm, deep, endless prey, and nothing in it that recognizes a made thing as something to fight. **Fletcher has spent three years tracking the snake. The snake was tracking Gault's supply line. The journal in Fletcher's pack is a map of the villain's smuggling route — and the specimen brand is on every casting pressed in its back pages. He has been carrying the proof since the first shed skin, and nobody at the table knows it yet.** Gault, meanwhile, wants his cargo back: the contract line is still open, the buyer still pays, and a recovered 2H-3 is worth double — *proof the product survives field conditions.*

**In plain table language:** kill the snake and you avenge the *deed.* Walk the trail back far enough and you find the courier who dropped the crate and said nothing — and behind him, the people who *built* the thing that ate the future of a town.

## The Reveal Ladder (GM — gate the truth in this order)

Fletcher cannot "fully track" the snake until he understands what he's tracking. Each rung is the **reward of a side-quest** (below), never an NPC handing over lore:

1. **It's an abomination.** Made, not born — the seam, the brand, the manufactured appetite. *(Reward of [[#Side-Quest 1 — "The Wrong Skin"]].)*
2. **It was cargo.** Crated for years, escaped in transit, homing down a freight road. *(Reward of [[#Side-Quest 2 — "The Cold Cache"]].)*
3. **The courier was Gault.** The authorship, from his own flat mouth. *(The Offer — later; don't rush it.)*
4. **The inversion.** It has been reading its hunters all along; the Double Knot is prepared ground. *(Late, on the water — the Delphine warning matured.)*

Rungs 1–2 are what let him *find* it: the brand and the freight stages turn three years of wandering castings into a legible route with a fixed endpoint. Rungs 3–4 are what let him *survive* it.

## The Snake — an abomination, not a spirit

*(House rule: one hidden explanation that unifies every symptom.)*

**It thinks in two — because it was built to.** One body, two minds, the product working exactly as designed:

- **The Waking Head.** They sleep in shifts; that was the *point* of the experiment — a sentry that cannot be ambushed. It has never once been surprised. Not by the two hunters, not by anything since the crate broke.
- **The Learning Head.** While one hunts, the other *studies.* It grew up watching handlers through glass; reading humans is its first language. It learned that crates hold food. It learned what a hunter's approach looks like — the downwind creep, the blind, the bait that's a little too easy. It learned faster the second time.

Every eerie thing about the trail flows from that one fact: castings **arranged to be found** (it edits its own sign); routes that double back through their own scent; caches torn open from the *outside* with the patience of a thing that knew the latch mattered; two experienced hunters baited past the Double Knot and never recovered.

### The Making — what a professional can read off it

- **The seam.** One skin, two lineages: the scale patterns of two *different serpent species* meet in a straight line down the spine. Nature doesn't draw straight lines. Fletcher has stared at that pattern for three years and read it as "unknown species" — because who looks at a wild animal for a *seam?*
- **The brand.** Faint, repeated at the jaw-hinge of each head, shed with every casting: **2H-3.** It is on every sample pressed in his journal. The abomination reveal doesn't come from a new clue — it comes from **someone else looking at his old ones with different eyes.**
- **The appetite is manufactured.** It was fed eggs and pen-raised young by the crate since hatching. It raids nurseries because *that is what food means to it.* The clutch, the rookeries, Brin's collapsing populations — even the crime has an author's handwriting.
- **The alchemy lingers.** Its tissues carry fixative residue — preservation alchemy worked deep, years of it. A brewer who processes a casting can taste the workshop in it. *(Leavy's channel — see Side-Quest 1.)*

**Say this in the GM's head before every scene it touches: flesh, venom, appetite.** No spirit, no curse, nothing to parley with. It can be killed — by a *party.* The horror isn't magic; it's that somebody *built* intelligence into a shape that owes nothing to anyone, then lost the receipt. And the reason the Mire hasn't checked it is Brin's own textbook: a made apex nothing here recognizes, eating **eggs and young** — the next season — the slowest, deepest damage an ecosystem can take.

**Brin's line when the truth lands — the arc's moral spine:** *"It didn't ask to be made. Doesn't change the arithmetic."* Pity is permitted. The cull is still required. That's the whole creed in nine words.

**Its names, by faction** *(each one is a clue channel):*
- **Brin:** *the amphisbaena.* Taxonomy. Respect through precision.
- **The Tessak:** ***the Season-Eater*** — their word for it is the living form of *tessavrak*, "taking beyond the season." It is the embodied violation of their oldest law. **They will not hunt it, and they will not fish the water past the Double Knot.** (A man-eating clan that *fears something in the water* — let the party feel what that means.)
- **The lab:** *2H-3.* The coldest name of all, because it's the true one.
- **Gault:** *"my cargo."* Three years loose, and he has never once called it anything else.
- **Fletcher:** refuses to name it. Naming is for things you keep. Log entries read *the specimen* — which will hit differently the day he learns that's exactly what it is.

## Gault — the courier, the rival, the proper introduction

- **He didn't make it. He just dropped it and said nothing.** That's his guilt, and it's colder than malice: the crate went in the water, and reporting it would have cost him the contract, the client, and questions he doesn't answer. So the river towns got no warning. The clutch died of two parties' silence, and one of them was paid for his.
- **The recapture is pure money.** The contract line has sat open in his ledger for three years; the buyer still pays; the facility pays *more* — a specimen that survived three years feral is proof the product works. Recovered alive, 2H-3 is worth double what it was in the crate. He does not think of it as redemption. It's an uncollected asset that has started eating his margin.
- **It's eating his margin.** The Learning Head knows his crates. His caches along the deep channels keep getting cracked and stripped. His own food-road feeds the thing that embarrassed him. He would not use the word *feud.* It's a feud.
- **The standing order.** Through the night-berth fence **Cauvin** (see [[brin-sevarre|Brin's file]] — that thread is still live) and every intermediary on the corridor: coin for word of the two-headed one, triple for a trail — **alive, never dead.** *This is how the race reveals itself in town, cheap and early.*
- **He can't take it with nets.** It sheds out of restraints; it reads a trap the second time it sees one; his hired hands won't go past the Double Knot twice. What he needs to collect his cargo is the one thing money hasn't bought him yet: **the best trail-read on the continent.** Which just walked into Sallowreach carrying a journal.

### The Ranging House Collision — S4 *(his reintroduction — the party walks in on him)*

When the party arrives to collect Brin's promised intel, the map room already has a visitor. The Guild leaks — it likes his reagents more than her reports (her file, canon) — so Gault has heard the Ranging House holds sign of the two-headed one. He has come, in person, a thing he almost never does, to buy **her four castings and the channel map.** The party walks in on the tail of it: two people who never raise their voices, hating each other at close range.

> **Gault:** *"Four skins in your drawer. Southwest junctions. Name a price."*
> **Brin:** *"There's no number."*
> **Gault:** *"There's always a number. Yours is just sentimental."* *(the exact word he uses for Remy — the table may catch the rhyme)*
> **Brin,** not louder, colder: *"Get out of my guild hall."*

And he goes — **unhurried**, the specific smug departure his file promises the party will learn to hate. On the way past them he stops for half a beat at Fletcher: the journal under the frog's arm gets the exact look freight gets. In lantern-light, his eyes hold the glow a half-second too long *(the wrong-eyes seed, if the S1 jar didn't land it)*. Last line, delivered to Brin but meant for the room:

> *"You'll sell eventually. Everyone in this city is one bad season from a number."* — a beat, at the door — *"And if your bounty boys stumble on it first: nothing that punctures the skin."*

That last instruction only makes sense after Rung 2 — *alive, never dead* — a two-stage clue in miniature. Then he's gone, and Brin gives the party the only explanation she will ever volunteer *(the hatred shown AND said, per the clue rule)*: ***"That man costs more than he pays. That's all you'll get from me about it."***

> **As played, S4:** the party watched the collision **from the doorway** — too polite to interrupt. They got the shape (Brin hates Gault; he wanted maps; she threw him out) but **not the parting line** — "nothing that punctures the skin" is still unfired and keeps for a later Gault sighting. Upside: Gault never clocked Fletcher or the journal *at all* — the guardrail held better than written. Commission as played: **the rookery count, middle Mire.** Freight-road and maker's-stamps clues both carry to the boat trip.

**What the scene does:** recognition (the jar man from the port tavern now has a context — he buys and sells the Mire) · says out loud that **castings are worth money to someone**, so the journal becomes a thing to guard · lands the map scene right after with real charge — *she refuses his coin, then opens the drawer for Fletcher for free; trust means something in this building* · plants the race with zero rungs fired.

**Guardrails:** Gault does **not** learn Fletcher has a trail here — he clocks a Ribbit ranger with a journal, nothing more; the rumor reaches him between now and the Offer. No authorship, no "my cargo," no explanation from Brin about the dead hunter. **Optional roll (one, or none — it's a cold open):** Instinct for Fletcher or Tenson to clock that Gault's sealed containers carry the **same maker's stamps as the broken cage on their boat** — a Rung 2 teaser, dropped abstract per the Lazy DM rules.

### The Offer *(the escalation — his third appearance, first negotiation; after rungs 1–2 have landed, don't rush it)*

Night berths or open water: Gault, unhurried, mid-load, flanked by hired hands who don't make eye contact. He's heard about the frog who's been showing castings around the Ranging House. He looks at Fletcher the way he looks at everything — appraising freight.

*"You've been walking my road for three years. Didn't know it was mine, did you."* A beat. *"Two-headed. Went into the river at the barge stage above some spawning-town. Wrote it off."* He says it the way other men say it looks like rain — and then, while the table is still breathing: *"I'll buy the journal. Or hire the hands holding it. You want it found — nobody gets it found faster than I do. Boats, channels, men. You'd be at the Double Knot inside a month. Only condition is it goes in a crate, not on a wall."*

**Per house rule, the bargain must not reduce to yes/no.** The offer has real teeth: he genuinely is the fastest road to the snake, and Fletcher genuinely is the best tracker Gault has ever needed. Negotiate terms, splits, who holds the trail, what *alive* means. Say yes and you're hired hand number twelve of eleven. The party should feel the pull — and hate that they feel it. *(And the cruelty under it: he does not remember the town's name. He remembers the manifest number.)*

**Guardrail:** Gault stays exactly as his file plays him — plain contempt, no bombast, no redemption arc. This arc gives him **screen time, not depth.** The table's hate should be earned by what he *says calmly,* never by mustache-twirling.

## The Side-Quests — one boat, four reasons

*(The cohesion engine, made of actual sessions. Design principle: every expedition is an **anthology trip** — one destination, and every PC has their own reason to be on the deck. Fletcher's reveal is the spine; it is never the only thing that happens. ### Side-Quest 1 — "The Wrong Skin" *(reward: Rung 1 — it's an abomination)*

**The hook (whole party, not Fletcher):** Brin commissions the boat's maiden expedition — Guild coin, real pay. Her maps flag a **shedding hollow** in the middle channels she's never been able to reach without a deep boat, plus a hit rookery she needs surveyed for her tally-book. She wants a *fresh, whole casting* — and she's hiring the party, not the frog: *"Four sets of eyes. It's killed people who went out there in ones and twos. Bring me the skin and the count, and I'll pay you like the Guild pays people it intends to hire again."*

**Why each PC is on the boat:**
- **Leavy** — the hollow's slack-water grows a Draught-grade reagent *(GM: this trip can double as the deep-Mire reagent hunt from [[leavy-arc]]'s Draught II — two arcs, one voyage)*. And processing the casting is a **brew scene**: her tongue and cauldron find what three years of Fletcher's eyes couldn't — fixative residue, preservation alchemy worked deep into the tissue. **She fires the reveal aloud** *(clue rule — shown once, said once)*: *"This animal has been… worked on. Brewed. Fletcher — someone MADE this."*
- **Isotta** — the two lost hunters' last camp is on the route — kept eerily tidy, and holding nothing that lingers (the dead crossed clean; ghosts are town business, [[cosmology]]). She carries the dead men's kit home with the right weight — and at the rookery, **Old Plenty** counts the boat and says the line that puts her on notice: *"The pale one is on another keeper's ledger."* *(2026-07-15: Gravois's ghost retired — his testimony now belongs to Old Plenty; the witnessed-crossing jewel banked → [[isotta-arc]].)*
- **Tenson** — Brin's survey protocol is, structurally, **an audit** (counts, season-over-season, evidence standards) — hand him the tally-book and watch the auditor fall in love with fieldwork. Comedy channel: the one person who respects Brin's paperwork as much as she does. And from the water, the southwest **wall is visible, and visibly thinning** — a Front 2 tell for his thread, free of charge.
- **Fletcher** — the read: fresh sign, the hollow, phobia beat #1 with anchors (below). His competence carries the approach; his fear tells the truth the whole way.

**The beats & rolls** *(✅ promoted table-ready → [[set-piece-the-wrong-skin]], SRD-audited 2026-07-16 — run from there; this list stays as the design intent)*:
1. **The approach** — a [[mire-complications]] roll on the Wending; the boat's pipeline marks get their first strange look from watching Tessak.
2. **The camp** — the kept camp (quiet beat, no roll): the tally-book bone dry, gear **neatly stacked**, and nobody stacked it. The answer stands up at the rookery.
3. **The hollow** — extracting a whole casting intact (Finesse, group aid) *while the water goes quiet.* It isn't there. It could come back. The tension is the dice.
4. **The rookery count — and the new tenants** *(the trip's real fight)* — a spoonbill-and-egret rookery, half its root-shelves bare, warded by **Old Plenty**, its starving guardian spirit (parley first — [[set-piece-the-wrong-skin]] Beat 5). Under fading light (Knowledge/Instinct), the numbers telling Brin's grim story. And the reason *this* season's count is so bad is chewing on it live: a boil of **Mire Gar** has moved into the vacuum the snake made — emptied nursery pools stopped feeding the rookery's defenders, and the scavengers came up after the eggs. Run 6–8 gar as Minions plus **one old bull grown fat on the vacuum** as a Tier 2 Standard (gar minions from [[mire-bestiary#Mire Gar]]; the bull is built and SRD-scale-checked — [[mire-bestiary#The Bull Gar]]). The thesis, in teeth: the Season-Eater's damage *cascades* — the party draws steel against the consequence two sessions before they ever see the cause. Brin pays a bonus for the bull's carcass; the count explains itself.

**The reveal, staged:** the casting unrolled the full length of the deck at dusk — *shown:* the **seam**, two scale-lineages meeting in a straight line down the spine; *said:* Leavy's line above. Then the gut-punch that costs no roll: Fletcher opens his own journal, checks the pressed samples by lantern-light, and finds the **brand at every jaw-hinge. 2H-3. It's been in his pack for three years.**

**Threads opened:** who brands an animal? *(→ Tenson's paper chase begins)* · why won't the Tessak fish past the Double Knot? · the second hunter went *into* the snake — no body, ever · even spirits can't count the snake.

### Side-Quest 2 — "The Cold Cache" *(reward: Rung 2 — it was cargo, and it escaped)*

**The hook (whole party):** their own boat betrays it. Their stolen smuggler's boat has *stops.* Brin wants the pipeline proven; the party wants to know what their boat was; and a cache in the deep channels means **contraband worth real coin.** Greed, justice, and curiosity all point the same bow southwest.

**Why each PC is on the boat:**
- **Tenson** — the cache's freight paper, half-eaten by damp: he reconstructs a waybill. Specimen designation **2H-3** · a freight-stage list running upriver past a Ribbit spawning-town · an imperial facility stamp with the name **redacted** · and one handwritten line: *"LIVE — DO NOT MANIFEST."* Off-books imperial freight — the exact species of rot he came here to prove exists. **He says the transported-reveal aloud:** *"This wasn't an animal that wandered in. - **Isotta** — one of Gault's drowned hired hands lingers at the cache (the dead find her; Gault's eleven are exactly her clientele). - **Leavy** — the cache's surviving crates hold **sealed Consortium-bound reagent jars** — loot with plot strings: leverage or entrée at the Alchemists' Consortium, where her gloamcap quest already points ([[leavy-arc]]). - **Fletcher** — the professional read, said aloud for the table: the sheds near the cache carry **cage-wear** — scale growth scarred in crate-straight lines. *"This thing spent years in a box."* And on the cracked cache lid: one casting, **arranged across it like a signature.**

**The beats & rolls:**

2. **The cache** — search scene (Knowledge/Instinct); the paper, the jars, the torn-from-outside crates.
3. **The complication** — a [[mire-complications]] roll or scavenger encounter while loaded down; the getaway choice (take the contraband? the Tessak are watching; so, maybe, is something else).
4. **The watch-beat** *(no roll — the arc's Stage-1 climax)*: on the return leg, a wake-ripple. **Two periscope heads**, motionless, watching the boat. Then nothing. It never attacks. That's worse.

**Threads opened:** who pays the standing order? *(→ Cauvin at the night berths → the Offer)* · where does the redacted stamp lead? *(Tenson's slow-burn — the facility stays offscreen, Bindweed-style: pressure, not a dungeon)* · what does 2H-**3** imply about 1 and 2?

## The Party Is the Counter-Build *(why the endgame can never be a solo boat trip)*

Delphine's warning has a flip side the party earns mid-arc, said aloud by her or Brin: **"A lone hunter is legible, cher. It's read every hunter that ever came. It has never read four people who love each other. Be illegible."**

- **Leavy — the venom and the smell.** The two hunters didn't drown; the venom took them first *(run as an escalating countdown — the SRD-legal framework; [[STATUS]] SRD Facts)*. The antivenin's key reagent: the **pressed castings in Fletcher's journal.** He hands over the most personal object he owns to be cut up and boiled (his sheet: *he treats it like ammunition* — now literally). Second brew: a **wrong-smell masking draught**, so the party reads as *nothing* to a tongue that tastes the air. Two session-sized brews on Leavy's set-piece skeleton.
- **Isotta — the one it can't drown.** Its deep-water kill: one head bites, the other **drags you under.** Isotta cannot drown. The drag-under becomes a hostage exchange it doesn't understand — and if the worst happens at the Double Knot, she's the one it *cannot finish.*
- **Tenson — the paper and the wall.** The redacted stamp, the procurement lines, the advance paid against freight that never arrived — a paper trail running adjacent to Caius's world, exactly where he's already digging. In the fight, he's the wall between the snake and the ritualists.
- **Fletcher — the read, and the shot.** His fear is the party's best instrument (below), his journal is the map, and the ending is his. But he only gets to take it because three other people made the ground safe to stand on.
- **Brin — the arithmetic.** The approach maps, the matched castings, what the two dead hunters did wrong. She still can't come, and she says why, once, plainly.

**Standing GM rule for this arc: never hand Fletcher a solo snake scene again.** His solo scenes are the cub, the letter from home, and Brin. Every snake beat runs through another PC's lane — that's the design, not a scheduling accident.

## The Phobia — costs & gifts

*(House pattern. Don't author its origin — that's the player's. Mechanics resolved vs SRD 2026-07-17: Stress marks, Fear-spend GM moves, and feature-granted Stress clears are all verified idioms — [[STATUS]] SRD Facts.)*

**Costs**
- **Fresh serpent-sign bites.** First contact with fresh sign (castings under a week old, a wake-ripple, the smell) — mark a Stress. In the thing's presence, the GM may spend a Fear to make the freeze real: he must be *anchored* (below) before he can act toward it *(a Fear-spend GM move — legal)*.
- **He will not say its name, and he will not describe it.** Social cost, played, not rolled: the party learns the shape of what they're hunting from Brin and the journal, never from his mouth.

**Gifts**
- **The fear is instrumentation.** Reframe *Eye For Danger:* a lifetime of terror built the finest snake-detector in the Mire. His body knows before his eyes do. GM channel: *"Fletcher — your skin goes cold"* is **always a true positive.** Never once a false alarm. The party learns to move when the frog goes quiet.
- **The anchor.** A named party member's voice — his name, said plainly — steadies him: clear the freeze, or clear a Stress *(features may grant clears — SRD-verified)*. *(Deliberate rhyme with Isotta's grounding technique — let the table notice the party keeps teaching each other the same trick: you hold, I'll aim.)*

**The image the whole arc aims at** (from his sheet): the frog who cannot be in a room with a snake, standing in the water in front of one, bodily afraid, doing it anyway — because this time there are people holding the line beside him.

## Endgame — a menu, not a script

*(Per the no-hard-coded-endings rule: live options, chosen at the table.)*

- **The Clean Cull.** The party build works; Brin's creed honored — understood first, killed without audience, used whole. The fear faced in the one image the arc exists for. After: he finally writes home. Not *job complete.* Something longer.
- **The Race Lost, Then Won** *(✅ BUILT 2026-07-19 as the arc's default finale → [[set-piece-beyond-the-season]])*. The party stalls or refuses; Gault takes the trail-read by force ([[set-piece-the-bad-season]]) and mounts the capture himself — and the war for all the marbles happens at the Knot: his expedition, the loose snake, the mother, the party, one night. Both villains in one arena; let the problems eat each other while the party threads it.
- **The Third Knife.** Deep channels; Gault and the Season-Eater finally meet, and it is not going his way. Fletcher has one clean shot — to save *Gault.* Brin's creed says take it. Three years say lower the bow. Do not rescue him from this choice, and do not tell him what it makes him either way.
- **If ignored, it nests.** Front 10 in [[world-in-motion]]: the mound at the Double Knot fills with eggs, and in a season the southwest is snake country — a made thing breeding true, which not even its makers knew it could do. Brin's quiet nightmare, the world moving.
- **The unwritten one** the player finds when he understands what the hunt has been teaching him.

**Epilogue hook only** *(player's call, post-campaign, Bindweed-style)*: 2H-3 implies 2H-1 and 2H-2 — and a facility that is still open. That's a war for another table.

## What this arc deliberately doesn't do

- **No sympathy flip on the snake.** Lab-made invites the "poor creature" turn — resist it, per the Tessak-recut precedent. Brin's line is the whole allowance: *pity is permitted; the arithmetic doesn't change.* The displaced-victim pathos lane already belongs to the owlbears and the Mangrove Spirit; the kill is allowed to be righteous. The tragedy belongs to the *makers'* ledger, not the table's conscience.
- **No spirit, no lore pipe.** The snake has nothing to do with the binding, the Turn, or the tether. Geography overlaps (the same southwest channels — his sheet's "the hunt points at the heart" stands); metaphysics never do. In a haunted swamp, the *manufactured* monster is the contrast — the one southwest horror with a fully mundane explanation and a paper trail.
- **No onscreen lab.** The facility stays a redacted stamp and a paper trail — pressure, not a dungeon. Naming it, staffing it, raiding it: epilogue material at most.
- **No solo endgame.** Structurally impossible: venom needs Leavy, deep water needs Isotta, the paper needs Tenson, the arithmetic needs Brin. If he sails alone anyway, he's the third stack of neatly arranged gear — and the table knows it in advance, in fiction, from three mouths.
- **No Gault redemption, no Gault depth.** He is the man who dropped the crate and said nothing. He ends the way his file says he ends.
- **No over-authoring home.** The town, its name, who's grieving there, what the letter says — the player's. The arc only guarantees the letter *arrives.*

4. Its tissues carry preservation alchemy worked years deep; a good brewer can taste the workshop in a single casting.

9. The Tessak's name for it is their word for taking-beyond-the-season made flesh, and no Tessak will fish the water past the Double Knot.

## Adversary design brief — the Season-Eater (2H-3)

*(✅ The full stat block now lives in [[set-piece-double-knot]] — Tier 2 Solo with a Tier 3 scaling note. This brief remains the design intent; its frameworks were resolved against [[STATUS]] SRD Facts 2026-07-17 — any remaining `[VERIFY]` debt lives with the numbers in [[set-piece-double-knot]].)*

- **Two Minds — Passive:** cannot be surprised or ambushed; hidden attackers gain no advantage against it. It acts with the relentless pressure of a Solo *(Relentless — exact SRD wording verified in [[STATUS]] SRD Facts)*. *(This is the product working as designed — the fight is the lab's sales pitch.)*
- **The Learning Head — Fear:** the second time a PC repeats a tactic against it (same weapon trick, same spell shape, same feint), spend a Fear — the tactic is *read*: it counters, and the roll is at disadvantage. Teaches the party mid-fight what the whole arc taught them: don't be legible.
- **Drag Under — Action:** one head bites and holds, the body rolls, the target goes into the channel — Restrained and Submerged, drowning countdown *(standard SRD countdown — Restrained is the verified condition; "Submerged" is fiction, not a rules term)*. *(Isotta's gift turns this from a kill move into a hostage exchange it doesn't understand.)*
- **Two-Headed Venom:** on a bite with Fear, venom takes hold — an escalating countdown the party can only stop with Leavy's antivenin *(countdown framework SRD-verified; no invented condition needed)*.
- **Shed — Reaction:** once per scene, it sheds out of a restraint, net, or grapple, leaving the empty skin in the party's hands. *(Also the canonical reason Gault has never caged it — and why he needs Fletcher.)*
- **Phobia interplay:** its presence within Close range of Fletcher = the arc's anchor mechanic (above), not a separate feature — the fight is designed so someone must *hold* him and someone must *free* him to line up the ending.
