# The Field Journal of Augury Vane

**Type:** Document (mundane appearance) — **a physical prop; hand it over**
**Status:** ✅ **FOUND.** In party hands since [[session-05-recap|Session 5]] — read aloud at Caspar's museum, at the exact moment the session ended.
**Origin:** Written by **Augury Vane**, first Mages Guild ward maintainer posted to Sallowreach, ~200 years ago
**Significance:** The document that made the party certain the Celebration and their curse are connected

> ⚙️ **Co-author note:** the four entries below are **the GM's own prose** (rewritten 2026-07-24). They are the prop, verbatim. Do not expand them, do not add entries, do not "improve" the voice. If new journal material is ever needed, it goes in a **later volume** (see §The Other Volumes), never inside these four.

---

## In plain words

Two hundred years ago the Mages Guild sent a wizard named Augury Vane to help build and maintain the salt wall — the magical barrier on the southwest edge that keeps something out of the city. In his first year he fell in the water during the Celebration and something happened to him. After that he could never leave Sallowreach again. He tried four times, got sick, turned back, and stopped trying.

He stayed thirty-one years in a five-year posting, refused to be recalled six times, and died here. The Guild wrote that he was devoted to his work. They gave him a commendation for it.

The party read this and understood immediately: it happened to him too, on a festival night, in the same water. That's their whole reveal, and it already fired.

---

## The Journal *(the prop — read verbatim)*

**What it looks like:** a leather-bound journal. The bottom of every page is water-stained. The handwriting is precise, with meticulous calligraphy. On the spine: **A. Vane.** The date implies it's almost two hundred years old.

---

**Entry #1**

> *The southwest expedition ended poorly. We were out there to scout the optimal location of the abjuration glyph the expedition has decided to crudely call "the salt wall". I misjudged the depth and fell overboard during the city's festival. The water went still as glass and became bright like a star. I felt a pressure inside my self, like a hand pressing in on my heart. A boatman pulled me out. I have not felt entirely well since, in a way I cannot quantify anatomically.*

*(GM — this is the party's own wreck in a dead man's hand: festival night, glass-still water, the light, the closing pressure, **a boatman pulled him out.** Read it and stop talking. Said-channel fallback if the table doesn't click: Remy, over a shoulder — *"...that's what happened to you lot, isn't it. That's it exactly."*)*

---

**Entry #2**

> *The guild writes asking me when I will return and present my findings. The truth is impractical: I have now attempted the journey out four times. There is a boundary after which my body starts failing. Pressure, pain, and a sense of doom. Recovery is quicker the nearer I return to the city. I have stopped attempting to leave.*

*(GM — the party has personally hit this boundary. The word "curse" appears nowhere; recognition does everything. Note "**the truth is impractical**" — that's a man deciding to lie to his employer, and it's why the Guild's institutional memory holds nothing about tethers.)*

---

**Entry #3**

> *The ward holds. The salt holds. But whatever force it is that we are keeping out is unfamiliar to my textbooks. The locals simply rumble about their swamp spirits and forces of nature. The glyph is theoretically simple but requires constant upkeep, it should stand for the foreseeable future if managed properly.*

*(GM — two jobs. It plants the ward as **maintenance-dependent** — which is Front 2's whole engine and [[oswin-cray|Oswin]]'s whole job — and it establishes that the Guild never identified what the wall holds back. "If managed properly" is doing a lot of work two hundred years later.)*

---

**Entry #4** *(in different handwriting)*

> *The Wizard Augury died at Sallowreach in his 31st year of a posting that was supposed to be five. He declined recall six times, and was commended for his devotion to his work. He is buried in the Guild plot behind the Maintenance House. He asked that his journals go to whoever holds his posting next.*

*(GM — **the arithmetic is the clue, and it's Tenson-shaped.** Five-year rotation, thirty-one years served, six refusals, death in post. The Guild read a cage as devotion **and gave him a commendation for it** — institutional blindness in one clause. Nobody found it strange in two hundred years. Four people who currently cannot leave will find it very strange indeed.*

*Two hooks live in this entry: **the grave** — a real place, behind [[oswin-cray|Oswin's]] Maintenance House, already in play since S4 — and **"his journals," plural.** See below.)*

## The Other Volumes *(the hook entry #4 creates)*

Entry #4 says *journals*, and says they go to **whoever holds his posting next**. That post has passed down for two centuries and currently belongs to **[[oswin-cray|Oswin Cray]]**.

This is already canon-compatible — Oswin's file has him volunteering, unprompted, that Vane's original notes are the one gap in his archive:

> *"There's a gap — Vane's original notes. First maintainer, two hundred years ago, obviously the most interesting source. Gone. Misfiled somewhere during a transition, probably."* — and **he has never wanted to pull on that thread.**

**So: the party is holding the volume that went missing. Oswin has the rest of the shelf.** Nobody has to invent anything for that to be true, and it points the Oswin thread and the journal thread at the same door.

⚠ **Unbuilt.** Nothing has been written for the later volumes. See the flag below before using them.

## Vane Was Tethered *(GM — canon)*

**The truth:** in his first year, siting the ward in the southwest channels, Vane went under the black water during a Turn — the party's accident, two hundred years early, one man instead of four. He never left again. The "permanent posting" the Guild believes it negotiated was one practitioner's captivity dressed as duty.

**He died of age, in year thirty-one, still asking.** Say that plainly if a player panic-reads the grave as *"the curse kills you."* The tether kept him; it did not consume him.

**Why he hid it:** pride, plus a colder calculation — a Guild that learns its practitioner is magically imprisoned quarantines the posting or writes it off, and either way the ward loses its maintainer. So the summaries stayed dutiful and the recalls were "declined."

**What it buys:**
- **The stakes get a headstone.** The do-nothing ending isn't abstract — it's a plot of ground behind the Maintenance House. *"He had the question. We have the key."* A player can say that sentence.
- **His obsession has a motive.** He wasn't researching for the Guild. He was investigating his own cage — the party's engine, proven to run once before, and to lose.
- **It rhymes with [[old-patience|Old Patience]]** — *"you carry two marks… the first in a long time"* — without resolving it. **Do not connect them explicitly.** Do not decide whether Vane is among the ones who "found what they were looking for."

## Hard Guardrails

- **No ghost. Ever.** Vane crossed long ago — not available to Isotta, not through the Tallykeeper, not at the grave, not ever. A speaking Vane is a lore conduit that breaks *nobody knows* and makes the journal worthless. **The journal is his only voice.** The grave is a place to stand, not a place to ask.
- **Oblique only.** The word *curse* never appears in his hand. He documents symptoms in a flat clinical voice and hides them from his employer in the same breath.
- **His tether teaches nothing about the binding.** He never learned what held him — that is the entire tragedy. His death closes nothing. Reveal-gates L4–L5 ([[the-celebration-clue-map]]) are untouched by anything in this prop.
- **Never let a future edit make the journal smarter than these four entries.** If new Vane material is needed, it goes in a later volume, gated behind Oswin.

## Why Nobody Ever Acted On It *(for when a player asks — and they will)*

1. **It's a private field journal, not the Guild record.** The official record was quarterly summaries filed to the capital — which is why [[caspar-renne|Caspar]] spent forty years "working from summaries." This book never entered any institution's shelves.
2. **His successors didn't read it carefully.** Canon ([[cosmology]]): every maintainer since has been consumed by their own posting — [[oswin-cray|the current one]] most of all. Shelf-furniture for generations.
3. **A posting turnover cleared the house.** Somewhere in the intervening decades a change of maintainer emptied the back rooms; a box of old papers went out to make space and drifted through collectors' hands until it reached Caspar's archive.
4. **And the deep reason, which IS the campaign:** the founders built the working as sacred, unchangeable, untranslatable tradition *on purpose* — tradition as error-correction ([[fen]], GM section). The failsafe doesn't just stop people from changing the song. **It stopped a trained Guild investigator from ever looking at it.** The journal isn't a hole in the secret. It's proof of how good the lock is.

## ⚠ What the 2026-07-24 Rewrite Cut *(design flag — GM decision pending)*

The previous draft carried two clue-map entries that **no longer exist in the prop**:

| Clue | What it was | Status now |
|---|---|---|
| **#8 — the attractor** | *"It is pulled. Something inside the city holds its attention… I cannot locate the source."* | **Cut.** Low cost — the party reached "the city is the cause" on their own in S5. L2's job is already done. |
| **#9 — the chant-hunch, struck through** | *"Four households, four answers… a hymn is not a mechanism. Strike this."* | **Cut.** ⚠ This one has a downstream cost. |

**The cost:** [[the-celebration-clue-map]] (Risk B, line 145) calls this journal *the only chant-pointer in the campaign* — the one thing telling the party to carry Caspar's transcription to the Heart for the L5 unlock.

**That risk note is now stale**, though, and worth re-reading before panicking: it was written 2026-07-06, and since then the campaign gained [[old-words-handout|the Old Words handout]] (the transcription as a physical prop, with Caspar's forty years of baffled marginalia) and [[fen|Fen's]] five contradictory translations. Both are live chant-pointers. The L5 road is not unsigned.

**What's genuinely lost** is the specific gut-punch: *a two-hundred-year-old expert wondered whether the answer was in the chants, and talked himself out of it.* That was the failsafe working, in a dead man's handwriting.

**Recommended fix — mined, not invented:** put it in **a later volume**, which entry #4 already created and which sits on Oswin's shelf. The GM's four entries stay exactly as written; the chant hunch returns as something the party has to *go get*; and the Oswin thread gets a reason to be opened. **Not built — say the word.**
