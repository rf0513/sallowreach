# Coin in Sallowreach

*GM doctrine. How money works at a table where nobody is motivated by wealth — and in a campaign where every villain is a money story. Companion to [[justice-in-sallowreach]]; supersedes the "rental bleed" money engine in [[The Mooring]] (obsolete since S3 — the party kept the smuggler's boat and the bleed never bit).*

---

## The Thesis — coin is never for you

Look at the campaign's rot: [[Oswin Cray|Oswin]] *skims*, [[Caius Maren|Caius]] *defunds*, [[Marcel Dray|Marcel]] *collects*, [[gault|Gault]] *buys*. Every antagonist is defined by what money does when it moves through him. Sallowreach's warmth runs the opposite way: the feast tables, Remy's charity, Marcel's one redeeming habit (anonymous contributions to the Celebration). **In this city, what you do with coin is who you are.**

So for a party with deep personal motivations, money is not a resource to optimize. It is a **moral instrument** — and it only comes on screen when it can do one of three things:

1. **Say something about a person** (giving, refusing, owing);
2. **Move a front** (fund the wall, feed the fracture, enter Marcel's ledger);
3. **Cost a choice** (whose problem gets the Bag — because there's only one Bag).

If a purchase does none of those, don't price it. Wave it through. The party never haggles for rope.

## The Floor — where the wealth actually goes *(the Homecoming; canon 2026-07-18)*

The quarterly lumber shipment is not paid in coin. **The barges go out loaded with timber and come home leading the Guild's deep-water freighter** — one huge ship, riding low with everything a swamp can't make: salt (the ward's own supply rides that hull), steel, glass, grain, wine, medicine, spice, silk. The city calls the return cargo **the Homecoming**, and it is how Sallowreach is actually rich. For two days the whole city collects at the warehouses, family book in hand — a care package for every house, at harbor scale; the closest thing to a second festival, and nobody thinks it's remarkable.

- **Distribution runs on the family books — "paper about trees."** Every household that feeds the export (and everything here feeds the export — cutting, milling, poling, drumming, cooking for the crews) holds a standing **draw** at the guild warehouses, kept by custom and contribution, never by price. You don't *buy* pepper in Sallowreach. You draw it.
- **The floor is high and it is free.** Nobody with a name in a book goes hungry, cold, or unhoused. Abject poverty is structurally impossible for insiders. What exists instead is **coin-poverty**: you can be *broke* — no boat, no lease, no exit — but you cannot be *poor.*
- **The seam is the doctrine.** The draw covers *living*; coin covers *changing your life* (a hull, a paper, a passage, a debt). That's the ground truth under "coin is never for you": coin here can't buy what everyone already has, so the only things it can do are moral — say something about a person, move a front, cost a choice.
- **Outsiders live coin-side.** The party has no book at any warehouse — which is why the city feels expensive to them and effortless to everyone else. [[the-eddy|The Eddy]]'s Year Count is, among other things, the slow growth of a book. The [[The Celebration|Celebration]]'s feast tables are the commons performed weekly, and the reason "coin into the feast" converts to standing so fast.
- **Front texture:** a late or short Homecoming is a city-wide event — and a quiet lever (the ward's salt is on those barges; [[Caius Maren|Caius]] knows exactly which line of the manifest to slow-walk).

**The show piece — how it lands at the table:** [[remy#The Homecoming at the Honored Guest *(the redistribution show piece; canon 2026-07-18)*|the Homecoming at the Honored Guest]] — one morning at Remy's inn that teaches the party the entire economy without a single number.

## Rule 1 — Weigh, don't price

Daggerheart's abstraction is the feature, not the bug. `[VERIFY: SRD gold tiers — handfuls / bags / chests]` Use the tiers as **narrative weights**:

| Weight | What it is | What it can do |
|---|---|---|
| **A handful** | a gesture | repay a table, tip a poler generously, bribe a dockhand, buy a round for a room |
| **A Bag** | a life changed | free a boat, rehouse a family, a season of ward salt, a debt closed for good |
| **A chest** | a legacy | not an Act 1 object; when a chest appears, someone is buying a *person* |

Never subtract coins mid-scene. Track the pooled purse in Bags-and-handfuls, glance at it between sessions, done.

## Rule 2 — Every sink is a person *(the menu — all already in canon)*

Gold goes fake the moment its destination is a shopping list. Every destination below has a face and a thread:

- **⭐ The Berth Contract → the Thouvenel Lease** *(the Act 1 sink — re-cut 2026-07-16, post-S4; 1–2 Bags, paid in installments)*: S4 closed two of the old three payoffs for free — Solène withdrew the sale, Anselme crossed, the A.T. punt went home ([[session-04-recap]]). What's left standing: **Remy is still boatless, Sabine still writes predatory paper, and now Solène holds a working boat she can't work.** The sink runs through **Client Zero** ([[tenson-arc#Client Zero — "The Berth Contract" *(whole party, table-ready)*|Tenson's arc]] — menu repaired there): Sabine's offer is a **lesser Warden punt** on the same trap paper ([[berth-contract-handout]], prop updated), and the flagship counter-move is **the Thouvenel Lease** — Tenson drafts the charter that lets two prides sign one page: Remy hires Anselme's punt from the family, and his fees (topped up by party coin, sideways, through work orders) become the rehousing money Solène would never take as charity. One sink, three payoffs, new edition: **Remy restored · the Thouvenels earning off the drowning row (Front 3) · Anselme's meaning turned right-side-out** — the boat he built so his daughter would never owe the Mooring a copper never touches the Wardens' Book again.
- **Salt for the southwest** *(handfuls to a Bag — Front 2)*: coin quietly turned into maintenance-grade salt for [[Oswin Cray|Oswin]]'s wall. Delicious wrinkle: done anonymously, it echoes Marcel's feast-table habit — and Oswin, who cooks his own books, will *notice* an unexplained surplus and be terrified of it.
- **Solène's row** *(handfuls — Front 3)*: rehousing help the Thouvenels would rather drown than ask for. Has to be done *sideways* — through Remy, through work orders, through [[fen|Fen]]'s tannery rent — or Solène refuses it. *(The Thouvenel Lease above **is** the sideways route, industrialized — if the lease lands, fold this sink into it.)*
- **The feast tables** *(handfuls — standing)*: the city has fed them since day one. Coin into a cook-stall, a widow's winter stock, the cleanup fund — this is how outsiders repay the adoption, and [[justice-in-sallowreach|the ladder]] sees it. Generosity is *visible* here; it converts to standing faster than any deed.
- **Orna's reagents** *(handfuls, recurring)*: gloamcap is controlled and priced; every ghost-page in Leavy's grimoire costs real coin at the Consortium. The one recurring "operating cost" worth keeping — because it's rationed *wonder*, not upkeep.
- **Caspar's exit** *(if the Ask lands)*: barge passage, a hand-off bribe, a dead man's travel fund. Smuggling a man out costs — and paying it makes the lie *theirs*.

## Rule 3 — No jobs. Paydays ride the quests.

*(GM decision, 2026-07-03.)* This table has too much fun quest material to spend sessions on side work — so **"earning money" is never an activity.** There is no job board. Instead, every arc the party is already pursuing has a payer standing inside it, and gold arrives as a *consequence* of the story, never as its reason. **This retires the old boat-fund "job fork" (Théo/Marcel/Caius odd jobs in [[The Mooring]]) — those NPCs still pay, but only inside threads the party is already pulling.**

## The Smuggler's Boat — the wrinkle they're already sailing

The party's boat is **dirty money made wood**: Gault's pipeline stamps on the crates, a trafficker's cage broken in the bow, an owner who met "an unfortunate end." Beats to bank:

- Someone at the berths **recognizes the stamps** and goes quiet — or names a price for not mentioning it.
- **Night-berth coin is offered for it**, no questions — Gault's people buying their own boat back.
- The party **renames and repaints it** — and realizes mid-brushstroke that they're laundering their own conscience. Let a dockhand ask the old name. Let them decide what to say.
- **Berthing fees** (coins, trivial) keep Sabine and [[The Mooring]] alive as a location without ever becoming a tax.

## If They Ignore Money Entirely — the default

**Remy pays.** Quietly, invisibly, the way he's been paying since the wreck — lodging, meals, berth fees, small bribes smoothed over. His charity was always meant to *wear thin as a pressure* ([[STATUS]]); now make it visible on a delay: he skips his own Berth Contract installments to cover them, Sabine starts circling the punt, and one day a PC catches the famous guide **going without something small** — new boots, his tobacco, the good lamp oil. No lecture. Just the sight. The party that notices has found the money system; the party that doesn't gets one more beat, wordless, worse.

*(The world moves: left alone long enough, Remy signs Sabine's rack-punt paper on the original terms — Client Zero fires without the party, and clause by clause his arrears become the kind of debt this city sells toward the Pale Room.)*

## The Once-Per-Session Coin Beat *(the whole mechanic)*

One beat per session, **maximum**, chosen from:

1. **Coin arrives** — a payday lands *on screen*; ask the table what they do with it before the scene ends.
2. **Coin is asked for** — a sink surfaces wearing a face (an installment due, a reagent priced, a family's shortfall visible).
3. **Coin is refused** — someone won't take what they're owed; play the why.

**Optional house rule** *(matches the Outer Market's warmth economy)*: when a PC spends a handful **on someone else** with nothing asked in return, they gain a Hope. `[VERIFY: Hope-award pacing vs SRD; mirrors "Eat What You're Given"]` Generosity literally powers the party — in this campaign, that's not a bribe to roleplay, it's the thesis with dice attached.
