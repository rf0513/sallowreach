# Session 4 Set Piece — The Maintenance House

*Tenson's arc-opener ([[tenson-arc|The Reconciliation]]). The party meets [[oswin-cray|Oswin Cray]] — mid-labor, lonely, delighted, hiding something in plain sight. Paper-forward: the payload is the two documents in [[oswin-papers-handout]].*

## Cold-Open Box

**What happens:** Tenson (any tagalongs welcome; Oswin is zero threat) visits the lone ward maintainer. Tea, a flinch at Silence's letter, two handouts into the player's hands, a hard pivot if pressed, and a doorway line. **Oswin does NOT confess tonight** — suspicion and paper, not proof. The iron box stays shut.
**Slots:** any time of day; best after the wall scene (same southwest line — Solène's denial notice points here).

## Beat 1 — The Man on the Wall *(approach)*

> You smell the Maintenance House before you see it — salt, strong enough to taste. It's a squat stone tower right up against the wall itself, crusted white to the waist like a kettle that's boiled over for two hundred years. Stone, in a city built on stilts and timber. Somebody, once, thought this building mattered.
>
> And there's a man out front. Fifties, grey-and-blue robes with the hem gone stiff and white, pushing a barrow of salt along the base of the wall. He stops every few feet, packs a handful into a seam, says something under his breath, moves on. It's careful work. It's *good* work — you can see that. You can also see it's a two-man job, minimum, and he's one man, and he's been one man for a while. There's no crew. There's no apprentice. There's just him and the barrow and about a mile of wall.

- **Let them watch a moment before he notices.** The image is the thesis: *unrelenting effort, alone.* When he spots them — strangers, worldly-looking strangers — he straightens up too fast and smiles too wide. His file's first-meeting energy: tea, questions, *sit, please.*
- **Fletcher's job (free, no roll — his whole skill set):** the green shoots coming through the wall's cracks. *"That regrowth is days old. Not weeks — days. He's re-salting ground he already salted. Whatever he's fighting doesn't stay fought."* *(Clue C shown — the wall is losing faster than neglect explains.)*
- **The salt shed** stands open by the tower door: **half empty**, and it's early in the season. Show it; don't explain it. *(Clue B, first channel — file it for the expense summary.)*

## Beat 2 — Tea, and the Flinch *(the letter of introduction)*

He talks the whole way up the tower stairs — where are you from, what do you do, follow-up questions, *actual* follow-up questions, the hunger of a man four years starved for conversation. The tea is real and it's good and he apologizes for it twice.

Then Tenson presents the letter from [[silence|Magistra Silence]].

> He goes still with the letter in both hands, and for a second — you'd swear he's about to cry. "The Citadel read it," he says. "My petition. Somebody actually—" and he laughs, once, and it's about ninety percent relief.
>
> And then the other ten percent happens. You watch it happen. Something crosses his face — fast, gone again — and when he looks back up at Tenson the smile is still there but it's *working* now.

- 🎲 **Instinct, Difficulty 13 `[VERIFY]`** — read the flinch. On a hit, say it plainly: **he's overjoyed you're here, and something about you being here scares him — and both of those are completely true.** *(This is the encounter's emotional headline. An auditor answers his prayers; an auditor reads* everything. *He summoned the thing that can destroy him, and he's just now doing that math.)*
- On a miss: Tenson just gets the joy. The fear resurfaces on its own in Beat 4 — fail-forward.
- **He asks nothing about the party's curse-business** unless offered — he's heard shipwreck rumors, he's sympathetic, but the letter has eaten his whole attention.

## Beat 3 — The Volunteered File *(Handout 1 — the correspondence)*

He doesn't wait to be asked. He *wants* to be audited — pointed the right direction.

> He's up and rummaging before your tea's half gone, and comes back with a folder tied in cord, worn soft at the corners from handling. "Five petitions," he says, laying it in front of Tenson like it's evidence. Which — fair. It is. "Five denials. Every word of mine, every word of theirs. Read the denials. Read them twice. They're *beautiful*. Not one fact in dispute, not one coin released. I've stopped being able to tell if I'm being refused or being *managed*."

**→ Hand the player Handout 1 ([[oswin-papers-handout]]) and let him actually read it.** Don't summarize. This is the shit he came for.

- 🎲 **Knowledge, Difficulty 13 `[VERIFY]`** — the on-the-spot audit, *only if the player doesn't find the tells himself* (give him real time first; the anomalies are in the document). A hit confirms whichever tell he's circling: the misapplied provision, the same-day stamps, the routing itself.
- **Free clue, no roll — the routing (Fletcher-pattern; it's his whole skill set):** the player cannot know imperial administrative law, and [[tenson-handout|his handout]] calls Caius "the ambassador," so *"Office of the Cultural Liaison"* on a fiscal stamp won't register by itself. The moment Tenson reads a signature block, hand him the character's knowledge plainly: *"Tenson — you've routed citadel paperwork for years. A cultural liaison holds no fiscal authority anywhere in the Empire. This channel shouldn't exist. Someone built it."* The deduction that follows — *built by whom, for what* — stays his. *(Standing rule: [[tenson-arc#The Paper Trail — a map of every document in play|Budding Bureaucrat is free procedural knowledge]].)*
- **The stamps get a mouth too (clue rule):** while Tenson reads, Oswin, refilling the tea, almost to himself: *"You'll notice they never take long. Eleven days for a petition to crawl across this city — the refusals come back before my tea's gone cold. Efficient office."* *(Shown in the registry logs, now said. The* meaning *— the decision exists before the reading — stays Tenson's to name.)*
- **The said-aloud channel (clue rule):** if Tenson asks the natural question — *why does the* cultural *liaison sign Guild fiscal paperwork?* — Oswin blinks at it like it's the first time anyone's said it out loud:

> "I… never asked. It came stamped. The first one, I assumed it was a clerical detour. The third one, I stopped asking." A beat. "You stop asking a lot of things in year four."

- **What Oswin believes (and says, if drawn out):** Caius is a *budget-line man*, an Empire that doesn't care, prudence as cruelty. **He does not suspect sabotage.** His theory is indifference. He's wrong in the exact way that makes him useless as a witness and precious as a file cabinet.
- **GM note — the steering:** everything he volunteers points *outward*, at Caius. It's all genuine. It's also curated. Tenson learned the word for this at the Embassy: a document assembled to reach a conclusion. If the player clocks that his helpful host is doing softly what Caius's report did sharply — say nothing, smile inside, and award the insight in Beat 4.

## Beat 4 — The Tour *(Handout 2 — the expense summary)*

His one domain of authority — he shows the work and he's *good* at explaining it: what the salt does, why the intervals matter, why the southwest face eats twice the attention. Give him two minutes of being excellent. Then the paper.

- **Getting Handout 2:** Tenson asks for the books — phrased however a polite auditor phrases it. 🎲 **Presence, Difficulty 12 `[VERIFY]`.** On a hit, Oswin produces the annual expense summary — because refusing a Citadel auditor looks worse than complying — **and his face does the thing again**, harder this time, and he over-narrates while Tenson reads: *"It's all — you'll see it's all quite standard, salt and freight mostly, the labor situation is — well, you'll see."* On a miss: he produces it anyway, tomorrow, "once I've tidied it" — and if the party later compares the tidied copy against the maintenance logs, the tidying *is* a clue. Either way the player gets the handout; the roll buys *tonight and unlaundered* or *late and suspicious.*
- **→ Hand the player Handout 2 and let him work.** The tells are in it (labor at zero, the research line, the too-perfect totals — GM key in [[oswin-papers-handout]]). **Do not flag them. This is the puzzle. It is allowed to not crack tonight.**
- **The contradiction, said by his own mouth (clue rule — plant it, never connect it). Time it: fire this while the player is physically holding Handout 2.** Twenty minutes of table time between the line and the ledger row is how this connection dies — deliver it as commentary *over his shoulder while Tenson reads*, apropos of his shelves:

> "Research? Gods, no. I stopped in my second year. You need a reason to research, and the reason is somebody, somewhere, reading it." That small laugh that doesn't land. "I file my quarterly observations. That's the whole of my scholarly output. Nobody has ever asked a follow-up question."

  *(The expense summary in the player's hands bills the Guild for field research every quarter for eighteen months. If the player connects the line to the laugh, that's Rung 2 cracking — **let it be his**, never an NPC's. If he doesn't, it keeps.)*
- **Party jobs on the tour:**
  - **Leavy** — the brewer's eye, free: the salt in the recent barrels is **coarser, cheaper grade** than the older stock crusted on the shed floor. Same invoice line, worse salt. *(Clue B, physical channel.)*
  - **Isotta** — she can feel the wall from here ([[session-04-salt-wall]] rules): the *thin* stretches she felt map to the **stretched intervals** in his maintenance log, if she and Tenson compare notes. Party glue: her senses corroborate his paperwork.
  - **Fletcher** — already fired in Beat 1; on the tour he can add the grim rate math: at this regrowth speed, the re-salting cycle is losing ground, not holding it.

## Beat 5 — The Hard Pivot *(if pressed — no confession)*

If Tenson pushes toward money, debts, the Pale Room, or *"where does the difference go?"* — Oswin does what his file says he does: the pivot so hard it's practically audible.

> Whatever was open in his face closes like a ledger. "Is there—" he starts, and resets. "The figures are with the Guild, of course, if the Citadel wants the full accounting, that's — that's a formal process, I'd welcome it, truly." And then, before anyone can answer, he's somewhere else entirely: "Do you want to know the strangest thing about this posting? Genuinely the strangest thing?"

And the deflection is a **gift**, because it's his best material and it's true:

> "The Mire comes from the southwest. Always. Two hundred years of records — always the southwest. It's not spreading, you understand. It's *reaching*. There's something in this city it wants, and I have written that in my quarterly observations every quarter for four years, and no one at the Guild has ever asked me a single follow-up question." He looks out the window at the wall. "I've stopped being surprised by that."

- 🎲 **Instinct, Difficulty 13 `[VERIFY]`** — optional, if a player wants to read the pivot: it lands as **avoidance, not innocence** — a man steering *around* something with the practiced motion of somebody who's steered around it a thousand times. That's as far as tonight goes. *(Per [[tenson-arc]] guardrails: no interrogation crack, ever. The paper is the confessor.)*
- **If someone tries to snoop the office** (the desk, the drawers): the locked iron box is *findable as an object* — Difficulty 14 Finesse to even get a private moment `[VERIFY]` — but breaking into a Guild wizard's lockbox mid-tea, on day one, in front of him, is a bridge the table should feel the weight of. If they do it anyway, [[oswin-cray#If He Realizes the Party Intends to Expose Him|his file governs]] and the session goes somewhere very different. The design intent: **tonight plants the drawer; a later visit opens it.**

## Button — "Say What's True" *(as they leave)*

The last beat, at the door, and he can't stop himself:

> "When you write to her — the Magistra." He's holding the door and not looking at anyone. "May I ask what you intend to—"
>
> He stops himself. Shakes his head. "No. Forgive me. That's not a thing you ask an auditor." And then he looks up, and for just a second the pleasant face and the tired face are the same face: "Say what's true. Whatever you've seen — say what's true. It'll be the first honest paperwork this wall's had in years."

*(He means every word, and he's terrified, and he's including himself in the indictment and hoping nobody notices. That line is the arc's whole dilemma handed over in a doorway — the player just doesn't know it yet. First report to Silence is due soon; see [[tenson-arc#The Reports to Silence (the arc's recurring instrument)|the reports device]].)*
