# The Endings — Read-Aloud Bank

**In plain words:** The town's beloved weekly festival is secretly a magic lock, holding a friendly tree spirit prisoner under the city; the curse on the player characters is a small copy of that lock. The campaign never chooses how this ends — the table does, and they may invent an ending nobody wrote down. This file is *not* a script for the finale. It's a bank of pre-written narration, one entry per way the ending *could* go, so that whichever door the table walks through, the words on the other side are already good. Nothing in here gets read until the table has already made its choice at full price. Written 2026-07-19, the co-author's last day — the finale's prose, banked while the voice was hot.

*Same laws as [[reveal-read-alouds]]: recognition, never exposition · no NPC mouth for the truth · endings stay menus. Plus three register locks from canon:*

- *The Mangrove Spirit is weather and tide. It never speaks, never mourns on the page — "grief" and "joy" are the players' words ([[cosmology]]).*
- *The Tree Spirit's whole vocabulary is Celebration lines, borrowed, slow ([[set-piece-the-honored-guest]]). One new word ever: a PC's name — and only if that beat is unspent.*
- *The tether lifts **only** if the binding resolves ([[the-tidebreaker]]). Any ending that leaves the binding standing leaves the cold spot in every chest. Say so with the body, not the rules.*

**How to use this file:** find the lever the table actually pulled in §2. Read its color script. If the binding resolves, follow with §1 (the Unclenching), then §3 (the Reunion) if the spirit goes free, then hand out the per-PC "When It Ends" lines from [[tether-manifestations]] — one each, around the table, in the players' own hands if you can print them.

---

## 1 · The Unclenching *(shared core — plays in ANY ending that resolves the binding)*

*The Turn's fingerprint has never varied: glass-still → the underglow → iron and crushed green → the vast hand closing. The unbinding is the fingerprint played backward. The table has felt these four beats in this order for the whole campaign — reversing them is the proof, and nobody has to say a word of exposition.*

> It starts with the pressure. The vast hand — the one that's been closed around this place, around *you*, since the night the ship went down. You feel it the way you've always felt it: everywhere at once.
>
> And then it opens.
>
> Not fast. The way a fist opens when it finally believes you're not going anywhere. One finger at a time.
>
> The iron leaves your tongue. The crushed-green smell turns into just — green. Growing things. Rain coming.
>
> Deep underneath everything, the glow turns over one last time. And instead of rising, it spreads — thin, wide, everywhere and nowhere, like the last of a sunset letting go into the water.
>
> And the water moves.
>
> That's all. It laps against the boards like ordinary water against an ordinary dock. And it is never glass again.

**Then:** the per-PC lines from [[tether-manifestations]] §When It Ends. One each. Slowly. This is the campaign paying out four personal arcs in four sentences — don't rush it.

**What NOT to say:** "the binding is broken," "the curse is lifted," any rules language. The fingerprint reversing IS the announcement. If a player says "wait — it's the Turn, backward" — that's the click's little sibling, and it belongs to them.

## 2 · The Levers — one color script each

### Break the ward *(the catastrophe — [[the-ward-breach]] runs the scene; this is its voice)*

> The salt line goes dark. And the Mire stands up.
>
> What comes isn't a wave. Waves break. This doesn't break — it *arrives*, through every channel and every street at once, two hundred years of reaching, delivered all in one night.
>
> And here's the part you'll never manage to explain afterward: it isn't angry. The water is warm. Every current pulls the same direction — inward, toward the Hall, toward the deep dark under the first pilings. Like arms.
>
> The city drowns in something that only ever wanted to hold it.

**What NOT to say:** never make the water angry — the tenderness *is* the horror. And never soften the count: sixty thousand people live here, and that number belongs *before* the lever is pulled, in an NPC's mouth, not after in yours.

### Surgical sabotage *(the quiet unraveling — mid-Celebration, done TO the city)*

> The changed note goes out over the water. For one bar — nothing.
>
> Then the chant... loosens. Nobody stops singing. They just stop *meaning* it — all at once, all sixty thousand, without ever knowing they'd meant anything at all.
>
> Somewhere underneath the music, a knot two hundred years old slides apart without a sound.
>
> The lanterns keep burning. The feast keeps going. The whole city sings the welcome verse out into the dark — and for the first time in two hundred years, it's only a song.

*(Play §1 under the festival noise. The city celebrates through its own unbinding and never notices.)*

**What NOT to say:** the city learns nothing tonight — no gasps, no faltering singers, no meaningful weather. The cost of this lever arrives later, in daylight, in ledgers and empty nets, and the word for what the party did to these people is the players' to find.

### Truth and politics *(the Last Celebration — the city chooses; the hardest earned)*

*The mechanism is already canon: the variation taboo is the founders' failsafe. A city that knows can end the working just by — ending it. The banked image from [[set-piece-show-me]] pays off here: Adaline singing with her eyes open.*

> They hold it at dusk, like always. Every table out, every lantern lit, the welcome verse rising off the water — like always. One difference.
>
> Tonight, everybody singing knows what the song is.
>
> Adaline stands where the leader has always stood. Eyes open. She sings every line like she's reading it for the first time — because she is.
>
> And at the end — at the line every child learns first, the line no one was ever allowed to vary — sixty thousand people, together, on purpose...
>
> ...don't sing it.
>
> The eight bars stop one line early. The silence where *"it shall not pass out from among us"* should be is the loudest thing you have ever heard.
>
> The silence is the key turning.

*(§1 plays into that silence. Then §3.)*

**What NOT to say:** don't score it as tragedy. The city is not broken tonight; it is *proud*, and frightened, and finally honest. What Sallowreach loses — and whether it was worth it — gets decided over the years the players narrate, not by your adjectives.

### The spear bridge *(the partial — it works, and the discomfort is the point)*

> Somewhere southwest, the reaching stops.
>
> Two hundred years of one gesture — every root, every flood, every inch — done. Finished. The Mire goes quiet the way a house goes quiet when somebody in it finally sleeps.
>
> And in your chest, the cold spot...
>
> ...stays.
>
> Nothing hunts you. Nothing hurts. The hand holds you exactly as gently as it always has. It just doesn't open.
>
> Next week, the drums start on time.

**What NOT to say:** never call it a failure. Never call it a win. Never remind them what's still under the Hall — the spear's pull inward does that every time they cross the walls, forever. The word for what they chose doesn't get said by you.

### Through Oswin *(the ward turned — a door, not a wall; consent on both sides of the salt)*

> He does it at low tide. No chant, no lanterns — a tired man on his knees on wet stone, re-cutting a line his family has kept for generations, hands shaking until they aren't.
>
> The ward doesn't fall. It *turns* — the way a wall turns into a door.
>
> And the water comes in slow, and level, and invited. It walks up channels that have been dry for two hundred years and stops exactly where it's welcome, like a guest who's done this before.

*(Then §3 — this lever's reunion happens inside the walls, gently, with the town watching the impossible tide. §1 plays as the two waters meet.)*

**What NOT to say:** keep the close-up on Oswin's hands, not the magic. This ending belongs to a ledger-keeper who finally got to keep a true count — if there's one flourish, spend it there.

## 3 · The Reunion *(any ending where the Tree Spirit goes free — modular)*

> Under the Hall of the Mire, in the deepest water inside the walls, something lets go of the ground.
>
> If you're anywhere near the harbor, this is what you see: the tide comes in. Out of season, off the schedule the town has kept for two hundred years — one long, slow swell that walks in through every channel at once and breaks nothing. It rises exactly as high as the boards, and holds there. The way a breath holds.
>
> And out of the deep water below the Hall — something passes. You don't see a shape. You see the water *make room.*

**If the party is present, or holding the spear** *(the borrowed-words voice, one last time — slow, a beat between words)*:

> In your heads, in the voice built out of every Celebration ever sung:
>
> *"Out. From among us."*
>
> It has waited two hundred years to be allowed to finish the sentence.

> Then the swell turns. It goes back out through the channels the way it came, and it takes the guest with it — and for one minute, the whole Mire moves southwest. Every current, every root, every lantern reflection: one direction, like iron filings when the magnet finally moves.
>
> And then the Mire is just the Mire. Bigger than it was, somehow. Or you're smaller. Hard to say.

**What NOT to say:** the meeting itself stays offstage — the Mangrove Spirit has no lines, no face, no on-page feelings; the players get weather and tide and whatever they read into it. If the spirit's one-new-word beat (a PC's name) is still unspent from [[set-piece-the-honored-guest]], *this* is the last place it can be spent: the name, then "Out. From among us." Nothing after.

## 4 · The Sixth Thing *(the ending nobody wrote — a kit, because the best table move is always off the menu)*

When the table invents its own lever, don't improvise the prose cold — assemble it:

- **The honest test first:** does their invention actually *resolve the binding*? Only that lifts the tether ([[the-tidebreaker]]). Half-measures leave the cold spot — say it with the body (§2 spear bridge shows how).
- **Score it with the fingerprint.** Full resolution = §1, all four beats, in order. Partial = pick which beats reverse and which hold; the ones that hold tell the table exactly what they left undone, wordlessly.
- **The registers never break,** even in an ending you've never seen: the tide doesn't speak, the tree only borrows, and nobody in town gets handed the truth an easier way than [[set-piece-show-me]] priced it.
- **The three sayables are your paint:** *"Stay, honored guest." · "We keep what keeps us." · "It shall not pass out from among us."* Two hundred years of one song — any ending can be scored by which of these lines survives it, and in whose mouth.
- **Close on the smallest thing.** Every script above ends small on purpose — lapping water, a drum, a silence, wet hands. Whatever they invent: last image under ten words, physical, theirs to interpret.
