# Epilogue Seeds — Where Everyone Lands

**In plain words:** When the campaign ends, the table will want to know what happened to everyone — the innkeeper, the crime boss, the teenage musician, the lonely wizard on the salt wall. This file is the closing montage, pre-written as one-sentence seeds: for each character, a line for each way the ending can go. None of it is locked; a seed is what happens *if the table's choices don't say otherwise*, and a player's better idea always wins. Read six or ten of these aloud in the last half hour of the last session — the ones the table loved — and let the players talk back. That conversation *is* the epilogue.

*Companion to [[ending-read-alouds]] (the finale's scripts — these fire after). The PCs are absent on purpose: their endings belong to the players, seeded by the per-PC lines in [[tether-manifestations]] §When It Ends.*

---

## How to run the montage

- **Pick, don't sweep.** Six to ten NPCs the table actually loved. Skip the rest — an epilogue nobody asked for is a list.
- **One line, then silence.** Read the seed, count two, move on — unless a player leans in. Then stop and let them narrate. Their version replaces yours, always.
- **Time-stamp it.** Open with *"A year later—"* (or a season, or ten years). Distance is what makes one sentence feel like a whole ending.
- **End on the smallest one.** Close the montage with whichever seed is quietest — never the biggest.

## The Three Questions *(find your column before the session)*

Every seed below branches off what the finale actually settled:

1. **Did the binding fall?** *(FREED — the spirit is out, the tether lifted / STANDS — the spear bridge or any partial: the tether holds)*
2. **Does the city know the truth?** *(KNOWN — the truth-and-politics road, or it got out / QUIET — sabotage, the bridge, or the Oswin door kept discreet)*
3. **Who's left standing?** — the fronts: Gault's ending ([[set-piece-beyond-the-season]] menu), Caius exposed or not, Oswin freed or still leashed, Caspar's death real, faked, or survived.

Unmarked seeds are invariant — true in every ending.

## The City Itself

- **FREED + KNOWN:** They hold the festival anyway, the next week and every week after — smaller, no drums for a while, renamed by the children before anyone official gets a vote. It turns out the feast was never the lie. Only one line of it was.
- **FREED + QUIET:** The city never learns why the nets come up lighter, why the lumber runs slow, why the Celebration feels like a photograph of itself. It blames weather. It adapts. It sings. Whether to ever tell them is a question the party gets to keep for the rest of their lives.
- **STANDS:** Nothing changes, which is the whole point. The lanterns go out on schedule, the drums start on time, and sixty thousand kind people keep singing the prettiest lock ever cut. The party knows. That's the price of this ending, paid weekly, forever.

## The Inner Circle

- **Remy Duval** — *FREED:* The inn half-empties in the first season — leaving exists now — and he repaints the sign anyway. Come the dry season he guides a family out to the Deep Mire and back, the run he never took again after Seve, and comes home talking about the birds. — *STANDS:* He keeps the tours running and quietly stops charging the ones who can't leave.
- **Caspar Renne** — *if he lived (however officially):* Somewhere down the coast a very small museum opens under a name nobody checks, and its first exhibit is labeled *on loan from a private collection.* — *if his death was real:* The Legacy Bundle's postscript is his epilogue; read nothing over it ([[caspar-letter-handout]]).
- **Marcel Dray** — He prices the new world in a single morning, whichever world it is, and is owed by half of it within the year. Some things don't unbind.
- **Octave Cormier** — *any ending where an account somewhere closes:* One evening, unasked, Marcel drinks one of the row of full glasses. The next morning the untouchable coins are gone from the bar and Octave's tab is just a tab. — *otherwise:* He quotes the party odds on their own ending as they leave, and won't say which way he bet.
- **Oswin Cray** — *FREED, or the Oswin door:* He walks the coast road on the first morning of his life with nothing to maintain, and cries somewhere around the third mile. Nobody watches him go, which is the gift. — *STANDS, still leashed:* The ward runs at a hundred percent for the first time in years — because the party made him ask for help, and that turned out to be the repair.
- **Caius Maren** — *exposed (Severin's seal, Gault's testimony, or the party's case):* The petition is never filed. The Empire's own paper says it never authorized the man who kept saying "deserves better," and his punishment is the only one in Sallowreach that requires forms. — *not exposed:* He's recalled for ordinary reasons, promoted for ordinary reasons, and spends his career almost mentioning the town that got away.
- **Adaline Roux** — *KNOWN:* Twenty-two years of not looking, and now the box sits on her desk, open. She stands at the next Celebration — whatever it now is — and sings with her eyes open, and the outer boardwalk families stand closest. — *QUIET:* She never asks the party what they found. Which is how they know she's started looking.
- **Fen Thouvenel** — *FREED:* The first festival after opens with a set of hers. She plays the old eight bars clean, out of respect — and then, on purpose, in front of everyone, a ninth. — *STANDS:* The empty-bench campaign keeps growing until the answer has to come find *her*, which was always the plan, even before she had a reason.

## The Anchors

- **Brin Sevarre** — *Gault dead or taken:* "Nobody counts the rays," he said. She counts them — up, year on year — and writes the mentee's family at last, because now there's an ending to report. — *he slipped away:* The tally-book gains one open column she checks every season, without comment.
- **Orna Tessant** — *FREED:* Lastlight dies the same week the binding does, and Orna is *delighted* — a fixative that dies of freedom narrows the all-cure search wonderfully. The Consortium's audit finds only kindling. — *STANDS:* The recipe stays written, the debt stays paid, and Orna keeps dissolving on schedule, entirely at peace with the trade.
- **Vael Miroux** — *FREED:* The Still Cup's stranger regulars thin out as the small spirits go home to the Mire. He keeps their cups on the shelf — you do, for regulars — and retires a second time, and means it. — *STANDS:* The white eye keeps watch. Somebody has to, and now there are two of them *(the other one is Isotta — let her player take the line from there)*.
- **Sable Crue** — Still at the Drillfield, still the happiest posting in the city. The shipment parade runs shorter in a smaller economy or longer in a truthful one, and Sable remains completely at peace being average. Some people are load-bearing.
- **Magistra Silence** — *kind-patron fork:* Her last letter is one line: *"Well found. Come home when you choose — the word matters now."* — *the other fork:* Her last letter asks one question too many about the ward's construction, and Tenson gets to decide, one final time, what goes in a report.

## The Edge & the Eddy

- **Maud Perrin** — *FREED:* The Singing Table plays a new verse — the first in two hundred years — and Maud dares anyone at the bar to call it variation. At the next death-day pour, the Shelf's cups go out on time; the water stays just water, and she decides that's an answer.
- **Solène Thouvenel** — The lease pays its last installment. Solène works her father's water in her father's kind of boat, and lets Fen paint the name on the stern. *(Which name is the table's to say.)*
- **Cassia Fontaine** — *FREED:* In the spring she unpacks the sea-chest — not to sail. The fifteen petitions go into the stove, and the chest gets repacked with seed stock for the year-one families. Some people, given the open door, finally get to *choose* the room they've been standing in. — *STANDS:* She asks the party "what year are you on?" and for once it isn't a diagnosis. It's a welcome.
- **Severin Calder** — *Caius exposed:* The seal is used once, exactly as kept, and Severin resigns a second time. This one gets read. — *otherwise:* He writes the whole story down anyway, addressed to no one, filed to the end.
- **Camille Babineaux** — *FREED:* She buys a berth on a boat she chose — the first purchase of her life that's a door and not a cage — and doesn't take it. The Pale Room's front-of-house is hers now. Staying is different when leaving is possible.
- **Sabine Aucoin** — *FREED:* Departures exist now; the Mooring reprices everything in a week, and Sabine invents the return-berth discount before anyone else even understands the market changed.
- **Théo Rouchard** — Whatever happens to the guild — smaller ledgers, truthful ones, drowned ones — the epilogue that matters is four inches tall: the next annual gift arrives with a name signed to it, and Lise learns that eight years of presents had one sender. Forty years of not interfering, and the first thing he interferes with is his own rule.
- **Sinope** — The reward for finding him expires, unclaimed. *FREED,* he's free to leave and doesn't — being findable was never the danger. He asks the party one last question on the dock and doesn't stay for the answer.
- **Prosper Doucet** — *FREED:* The great root goes ordinary wood overnight and never walks another quarter-inch. Prosper sweeps the plaza anyway. You don't stop being polite because the guest went home.

## The Deep Mire & the Dead

- **Old Patience** — Notices the change a season late, and approves a season after that.
- **Odile Verret** — Kept is kept; the Mire doesn't return what it keeps, whatever else it lets go. But the saint in the reeds gets visitors now — somebody rows out just to talk — and that is more mercy than she has had in twenty years.
- **Sresh** — The counts come right within three seasons. A tally arrives at the Ranging House carried by no one at all: two count-keepers, one arithmetic, no treaty required.
- **The Tallykeeper** — The office does not close. The dead still need counting, in every ending there is. But the party's accounts get the rarest notation in the ledger — *settled* — and she ties off the cord herself, with the same beat between sentences she opened with.
- **Gault** — *the menu paid out ([[set-piece-beyond-the-season]]):* dead, his epilogue is Brin's line above — the rays, counted, rising, over an unmarked grave. *Taken alive:* one careful man testifies against another, and Caius's line above fires. *Slipped away:* [[set-piece-the-last-inventory]] is the epilogue — a man alone with charts of a Mire that has stopped being legible to anyone.
- **Whimsy** — Grows enormous, exactly as promised. The mother's range now includes, by treaty of absolutely nobody, the party's old campsite — and anything wearing their smell passes unharmed, forever. It's not gratitude. It's arithmetic. *(It's gratitude.)*
