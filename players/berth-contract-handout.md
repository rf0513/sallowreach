# Handout — The Berth Contract *(+ GM annotations)*

*The paper Remy hands Tenson at breakfast. Print the boxed text as the prop; everything below it is yours. The design: the contract is **Warden-standard predatory** — not villainy, just forty years of boilerplate that grinds people who don't read it. Tenson gets to be the first person in years who reads it. This is his tutorial for [[marcel-dray#The Table — the Marcel Decision Tree (GM reference)|Marcel Branch 2]]: same skill, higher stakes, later.*

> ⚠ **Re-cut 2026-07-16 (post-S4).** The Vessel is no longer Anselme's A.T. punt — Solène withdrew the sale and Anselme crossed ([[session-04-recap]]). The prop now names ordinary Warden stock, and the A.T. clue channel is **spent** (it paid off at the wall directly, as played). The counter-move menu — including the flagship **Thouvenel Lease** — lives in [[tenson-arc#Client Zero — "The Berth Contract" *(whole party, table-ready)*|Client Zero]].

## The Prop *(print this)*

> **AGREEMENT OF HIRE-TOWARD-REDEMPTION**
> *The Mooring — Berth & Vessel Office of the Punt Wardens*
> *S. Aucoin, Berth-Mistress, for the Wardens' Book*
>
> **Vessel:** one flat-bottomed punt, cypress-built, twelve foot, patched hull, Warden stock, **no builder's mark.** *("the Vessel")*
> **Hirer:** R. Duval, guide. *("the Hirer")*
>
> **1.** The Hirer shall pay to the Wardens' Book one installment each Celebration-week, for twenty weeks, at the rate posted in Schedule B.
> **2.** All installments constitute **fees of hire.** Title passes to the Hirer upon receipt of the twentieth installment in full, and not before.
> **3.** Should two installments in sequence go unpaid, this agreement is void; fees of hire are retained by the Book; the Vessel returns to the racks.
> **4.** The Hirer bears all maintenance, fitting, and betterment of the Vessel for the term. **Betterments accrue to the Vessel.**
> **5.** Upon the twentieth installment, redemption shall be assessed at the Vessel's **fair value on that day, as the Book assesses it;** any difference between fees paid and assessed value falls due before title passes.
> **6.** For the term, the Vessel berths at the Mooring at posted rates.
>
> *Signed and witnessed on the Wardens' Book —*

## What Tenson Finds *(free, no roll — this is his entire profession; let him shine)*

Give him these as he reads, one at a time, or let the player find them and confirm. His spoken lines are drafted so you can read them straight if the player wants the words:

- **Clause 2 — the rent trap.** Nineteen payments buy nothing. Ownership arrives only with the twentieth.
  *Tenson:* "This isn't a purchase, Remy. It's rent with a prize at the end — and the prize is behind the last door. Miss week nineteen and you've paid twenty weeks of rent for a boat you never owned."
- **Clause 3 — no cure window.** Two missed in sequence, everything's forfeit — and this is the *Mire.* One fever, one storm week, one client who doesn't pay Remy on time.
  *Tenson:* "There's no way to make a late payment good. None. In a city where the weather decides your income. That's not an oversight — that clause is where her profit lives."
- **Clauses 4 + 5 together — the one that should make the table gasp.** Remy maintains and improves the boat; improvements "accrue to the Vessel"; then at the end, the price is the boat's assessed value — *assessed by her, on that day.*
  *Tenson:* "Read four and five together. You repair it, you fit it, you improve it — and then you buy it back at whatever it's worth *after* your work, at *her* assessment. Remy — every hour of carpentry you put into this boat raises the price of the boat. You would be buying your own hands back."
- **Clause 6 — the captive berth.** Twenty weeks of posted-rate fees on top, at rates that change "with the weather and her mood."
- **The honest total:** face price around one Bag; as written, closer to two.

**The quiet beat that sells it:** Remy, when Tenson starts marking clauses — *"I've signed three of these over the years."* [beat] *"Nobody ever read one to me before."* That sentence is why the famous guide has never owned a boat since the Ridgeback. Pride doesn't read fine print.

**The A.T. mark** *(retired — the channel fired S4)*: the original Vessel was Anselme's punt, mark **A.T.** at the transom; that clue paid off at [[session-04-salt-wall|the wall]] as played, before this scene ever fired. The current Vessel carries **no mark** — and if anyone asks, that's the quiet tell, and Remy's the one who'd say it: *"Warden stock. Nobody built this boat* for *anybody."* A boat with no story, offered to a man whose whole trade is stories — let it sting a little.

## The Renegotiation *(if Tenson takes it to Sabine — and he will)*

**No roll to find the problems; one Presence roll (Difficulty 12) for the negotiation itself — advantage if he cites clauses by number.** Sabine is unmoved by warmth, charm, or appeals to Remy's years of service. She is moved by *exactness* — it's the only language she respects ([[the-mooring|her file]]: she is not cruel, she is exact).

What she gives, and how:

| Tenson demands | Sabine's answer |
|---|---|
| **Fix the redemption price now, in writing; strike clause 5** | Haggles once, then takes a fixed figure slightly above face. *"Finally somebody read the thing. Fine. A number's a number."* |
| **A cure window** — a missed week can be made good within seven days, plus a late fee | Takes it without argument. It's standard for locals; she just doesn't *offer* it. *"You didn't ask, cher. Nobody pays me to volunteer."* |
| **Credit installments toward the price** | Flat refusal. *"That's called a sale. Different form, bigger deposit, and the frog doesn't have it."* |
| **Strike 'betterments accrue' / credit improvements** | Partial: she'll credit *documented materials*, never labor. Take it or leave it. |
| **A named guarantor** — default triggers the guarantor, not forfeiture | She looks at Tenson for a long moment, then writes it in. *"And who signs as—"* [he already has the pen] *(This is the foreshadow. The same structure — schedule, collateral, named guarantor — is exactly what cracks Marcel's Branch 2 later. Say nothing. Let it rhyme when it rhymes.)* |

**The win is real but partial** — the contract goes from a trap to a fair hard deal, which is the correct outcome: Sallowreach doesn't do happy paper, and Sabine walking away *respecting* Tenson is worth more than any clause. From here on she calls him "the reader." It isn't warm. From her, it's a title.

## GM Notes

- **The financing is clean.** Tenson may (correctly, in character) suspect Marcel's money behind the Wardens' Book — the audit finds it's Sabine's own ledger, all of it. Give him the relief beat: for once in this city, the paper is only greedy, not sinister. *(The "quietly a Marcel arrangement" dial in [[the-mooring]] stays available for some other deal, some other season — never this one. This sink stays warm.)*
- **The scene budget:** breakfast read = 5 minutes; the Sabine renegotiation can happen this session or next — it keeps. *(The old before-the-wall timing rule is dead — the A.T. channel already fired S4; this scene now floats freely.)*
- **Payment reality:** with the [[coin-in-sallowreach|Payday Map]] flowing, the party can cover installments as they land — remember: **Remy earns alongside them or not at all.** Their cleanest structure: the guarantor clause plus Remy's own guiding income, party coin filling gaps *through work orders, sideways.* If they just try to hand him a Bag, he walks. That's canon and it's load-bearing.
- **One coin beat per session, max.** This is S4's. The stamps at the berths, Sabine circling, all of it keeps.
