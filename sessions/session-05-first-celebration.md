# Session 5 — The First Celebration: *Nobody Asks*

*A full session, dawn to dreams: the party's first Celebration, front and center. Three registers, braided all night — **awesome** (the most beautiful thing they've seen in this world), **wholesome** (games, food, NPCs with nothing to sell them), and **eerie** (two hundred years old, meaning lost, and nobody asks). Designed dice-forward — the mini-games ARE the dice — and every key clue fires **twice** (shown once, said aloud once).*

*Runs on [[the-celebration]] (Surface features only — the Hidden Layer does not exist yet). One hard rule over everything: **the Turn does NOT land tonight** ([[cosmology#The Recognition Reveal — what they can know, and when|reveal rules]] — first Celebration = swept up; the click is the second one's job).*

## 🧭 The Theme *(the eerie is an absence)*

Play three registers and never let one win:

- **Awesome:** scale and light. Sixty thousand voices landing together, a sky-river of lanterns, the Mire itself glowing back. Underplay the numbers, let the images do it.
- **Wholesome:** the city wants *nothing* from them today except that they belong. Games, food, kids, old women pressing seconds on them. No asks, no hooks pressed, no bill. (That IS the hook. Trust it.)
- **Eerie:** nothing spooky ever happens. **The eerie is that every question has a warm, loving, complete non-answer.** Two hundred years, not one torch moved, not one word changed, meaning lost — and *nobody asks.* The GM's one acting note for the whole day: when the party questions the ritual, NPCs are never cagey or defensive. They are **satisfied.** Cagey means there's a secret; satisfied means there's a hole where the question should be. Satisfied is scarier.

The session's quiet question — never said aloud: *how does a whole city keep something perfectly for two hundred years without a single person knowing why?*

## Where it slots — and why now

**Its own session, whole.** The previously loaded S5 spotlights (gloamcap run · redline meeting · "The Wrong Skin") keep beautifully and slide one session — they're different days anyway (the Calling needs a dusk, and this dusk is spoken for).

The bookkeeping: they wrecked *on* a Celebration night ([[session-01-read-aloud]] — Remy: "first one I'll have missed in... huh"). At least one more passed while they were half-drowned and neck-deep in other people's paper ([[world-in-motion]] Front 4 canon: households skipped "the last Celebration"). New-washed strangers get a season's grace — but **Remy will not let them miss another.** That's the trigger, and it costs nothing: he simply assumes they're coming, the way he assumes the sun is.

**Why this session exists (GM eyes):**
1. **The endgame is only as heavy as tonight is light.** "Attend it knowing" ([[the-celebration]] Hidden Layer) is the campaign's best scene — built entirely out of the memory of tonight. Every game they win and lantern they release is weight on that scale.
2. **It arms the click.** The recognition reveal needs a *second* Celebration. No first, no second.
3. **Front 4 gets its first faces.** The empty benches stop being a GM note.
4. **The table's tone, paid in full:** wonder and warmth after a session of salt and paper — with the mystery running underneath like a cold current.

## Beat 0 — Remy's Homecoming *(strong start)*

The only man in Sallowreach who's ever missed one. Read:

> You've never seen Remy like this. He's scrubbed. His beard's combed. He's wearing a shirt that was clearly folded in a drawer for years waiting for something worth it — and he's standing at the door with his arms crossed like a man herding ducklings.
>
> "No. Whatever it is, it keeps till morning. Everything in this city keeps till morning, tonight. You're my guests, and my guests don't miss a Celebration — *I'm* not missing another one as long as I live." [a beat, quieter] "Last one I missed, my boat blew up. Draw your own conclusions. Get your coats."

*(His grief and his joy in the same sentence — let them sit together. If anyone digs: the wreck night was the first Celebration he ever missed. He's not superstitious about it. He's exactly superstitious about it.)*

## Beat 1 — The Build *(morning — the city puts them to work)*

The Celebration isn't watched, it's **made** — and the fastest way to belong is to be handed a job. No one asks if they want one. Read:

> Morning starts with one reed flute somewhere in the outer districts. Then two. It doesn't stop all day — it just keeps getting *joined.* And the city starts moving like one animal: every thoroughfare, people carrying tables — not moving house, carrying tables, out into the street, end to end, till you could walk a mile on tabletops. Nobody's directing it. Everyone knows where theirs goes.
>
> And then somebody puts something in YOUR hands.

Deal one job per PC — each is a small scene with a face, a laugh, and **one eerie splinter buried in the wholesome** (deliver the splinter flat, never spooky):

- **Lantern-frames** (Isotta or Leavy) — an old-family matron ([[improv-ammo]] names) teaches the fold. Craft/Finesse **11**. The splinter: every frame is the same swept-wing shape. Ask why and she's *delighted* to answer: *"That's the shape, cher."* That's it. That's the whole answer, and she means it completely. *(Fletcher knows what shape it is — see Beat 6.)*
- **The table line** (Tenson) — hauling boards with big cheerful strangers; [[sable-crue|Sable]] is on the line, sweating and singing, thrilled to see him. The splinter, from Sable, apropos of nothing: *"Best day of the week, brain-boy. Best day of my* life*, every week."* — said by someone the party knows is *genuinely* happy. It should be lovely. It should also snag: *every week, his whole life, and it never once got old?*
- **Torch-setting** (Fletcher) — following a torch-warden with a two-hundred-year-old brass sighting-frame: each torch is set by *sightline*, checked twice. The splinter: Fletcher's tracker eye clocks it free, no roll — **these aren't decorations, they're positions.** Precise as survey stakes. The warden, satisfied: *"They go where they go. Always have."*
- **The children's rehearsal** (Leavy or anyone) — kids drilling the little welcome-dance, bored and giggling. The splinter: a girl of about six corrects a PC's hand position — *seriously*, the only serious thing she does all morning — then goes right back to giggling. Even the children keep the form without knowing it's a form.

## Beat 2 — The Festival Games *(midday — the wholesome core)*

**Menu — run 2–3, player's pick.** Prizes are never coin ([[coin-in-sallowreach]] doctrine): ribbons, the first-lantern honor, a kissed cheek, the eel itself. Every game has a face, **full stakes on the dice** (this is the session's dice-forward core — the S3 rule stands: the moment a PC tries to *win* something, call the roll before you narrate), and **one clue in its pocket.** Universal economy: participating at all = advantage per [[the-celebration]]'s *The Whole City Moves*; a Fear on a game roll is always a **comedy** beat with a real cost, never a threat. *(✅ SRD-verified: rolling with Fear still succeeds/fails normally — it gives the GM a Fear and the narrative turn; these stakes are legal as written.)*

- **The Polers' Dash** — standing punt sprint, a marked canal loop, best-of-three legs. **Roll:** Agility **13** per leg. **Win:** the first-lantern honor (the release platform at Beat 6) + **gain a Hope** — the roar of a whole district learning your name. **Lose:** Sable's rib-creaking hug and a standing rematch — and the district learns your name anyway. **On Fear (any leg):** into the canal, publicly; mark a Stress — it clears the moment someone hauls you out laughing, but the splash is a story the city tells about them now (it *will* come back). **Face:** [[sable-crue|Sable]], defending champion, delighted to lose. **Pocket clue, win or lose, gasping:** *"Guard the wall on the southwest leg — current's gone wrong there this year, pulls at the pole."* *(Front 2, said by a friend, mid-laugh.)*
- **The Eel Grab** — greased eels, a flooded barrel-ring, bare hands. **Roll:** Instinct or Finesse **12**. **Win:** the eel (dinner, honor, and a very good story) + **clear a Stress** — pure stupid joy. **Fail:** eel gone, dignity gone, crowd delighted — no cost, that's the game. **On Fear:** the eel goes down someone's shirt — mark a Stress *and* the fish-matriarch adopts you out of pity, which is worth more than winning (she's a Pull-of-Belonging hit, free). **Face:** a fish-matriarch who calls everyone *"minnow."* **Pocket clue:** she tosses the winner's eel to the cook-line — *"Fat this year. All of them fat — Mire's never fed them so well."* *(Front 1, said as good news.)*
- **The Drum Line** — learn a family's dance, join the round. **Roll:** Presence or Agility **12**. **Win:** folded in — the family teaches you *their* steps, counts as a Hope-success on the Pull of Belonging (adoption + the three offered secrets). **Fail:** wrong feet, right spirit — no cost, they teach you slower. **On Fear:** you improvise a flourish and the whole line *stops.* One beat of total silence — not anger, something older — then the gran laughs and it restarts. Mark a Stress. *(That silence is the taboo showing its face for half a second. Never explain it.)* **Face:** any adopted family; Fen on a rail, arms crossed, *rating them.* **Pocket clue:** every family's dance is proudly different — **except the second-movement steps, identical in every one.** Shown to whoever dances; Fen says it aloud if asked: *"Everything varies. Except the part that doesn't. Ask them why."* *(They can't.)*
- **The Gumbo Tables** — the cook-off; Leavy drafted as judge (the outsider tongue is the only honest one). **Roll:** Knowledge or Instinct **11** to judge with authority. **Win:** her verdict *sticks* — three aunties now owe her, respect her, and will feed the party forever; that's currency ([[coin-in-sallowreach]]: every sink is a person — this is the reverse). **Fail:** she picks wrong and two aunties unite *against* the verdict — she's now inside a feud that will follow her, warmly, for sessions. **On Fear:** she hesitates one beat too long and all three aunties decide she's judged *them* — mark a Stress; reconciliation requires seconds of everything. **Face:** three ferociously competitive aunties. **Pocket clue (her tongue, free, no roll):** the edge-row pots run on saffron and fortified wine this row can't buy. Somebody funds this feast and signs nothing. *(Marcel's food — [[marcel-dray]], never say so. Solène's said-channel waits in Beat 4.)*
- **The Counting Game** — kids' circle game, a rhyme and a thrown ring. **Roll:** Presence **11** to be terrible at it *charmingly.* **Win:** the kids claim the PC for the night — a small shadow-escort everywhere they go (and children see everything; one of them is tonight's best said-channel fallback for any clue that whiffs). **Fail:** the kids gleefully declare them the worst player in city history — beloved anyway. **On Fear:** the ring sails into a gumbo pot; see auntie feud, above. **Face:** a swarm of children; Seven orbiting the shinies. **Pocket clue:** the rhyme is the Mire's magpie count — *"…six for a debt, and **seven for a secret never to be told.**"* *(The banked true-name beat, [[leavy-arc]] — tonight is its natural home. Don't force it; it keeps.)*

**The prize that matters:** the Polers' Dash winner traditionally **lights the first lantern** at the release. If a PC wins it, they're up on the release platform at the centerpiece — prominent, honored, and *still swept up* (guardrail: even from the platform, the Turn stays buried — height, crowd, glare).

## Beat 3 — The Questions Strand *(all day, anytime — the mystery with no bottom)*

This is the one-shot-mystery layer, and it inverts the genre: **the party can investigate all day, and every road ends in a satisfied non-answer.** The discovery isn't a fact — it's the *shape of the hole.* Run it purely reactively: whenever a PC asks anyone anything about the ritual's origins, pull the matching card. Never volunteer; let them come to it. (Your mystery player will — the wall scene taught her the town rewards asking.)

**The answer cards** *(each one warm, complete, and empty — deliver satisfied, never cagey)*:

- **Any elder, on what the old words mean:** a proud, loving answer — that contradicts the last elder's. *(Fen's collection made live: "The promise holds." / "The water remembers." / "Keep the good year." / "Welcome the guests twice." / a recipe.)* Every family is certain. No two match. Nobody has ever compared.
- **The Accord, on why the form never changes:** *"It is not the traditional form"* is both the question and the answer. Push, and you get the closest thing to doctrine anyone owns: *"You don't improve a welcome, cher. You* keep *it."* Push harder and they get — not defensive — **puzzled.** The question doesn't parse. *(The founders' failsafe working exactly as designed — [[fen|Fen's file]], GM-eyes. The taboo IS the machine.)*
- **On when it started:** *"Founding year."* Which founding year? Records? The Accord's oldest ledger starts *mid-tradition* — page one already assumes the Celebration exists. There is no page where it begins. **Shown** if anyone actually gets ledger access; **said** by [[sinope|Sinope]] (below) if not.
- **The museum thread** *(conditional on the S4 fork)*: if Caspar's "Object on Loan" ask played out, the museum is shuttered tonight — and the one man who'd have engaged the question ([[caspar-renne]]; he holds the only transcription, canon) is out of reach or lying low. The party feels the *absence of the expert*. If he's still in play, he gives them exactly one thing before the guardrail: the old words **predate written Sallowreach history** — he told Fen the same once. Then: *"Ask me again when you bring me something I can read it against."*
- **[[adaline-roux|Adaline]], the one brush** *(she's everywhere tonight, running it — give the party ten seconds of her)*: warm, formidable, a black cord knotted at her wrist. If they ask her the why: the pause under her certainty, then — *"You just keep faith. That's what a covenant asks."* And she's moving again. *(Front 8's tell, verbatim from her file. The only NPC whose satisfaction has a crack in it — one beat of pause, no more.)*
- **[[sinope|Sinope]], the festival heckler** — the philosopher vagrant heckles the *content* while Fen fights the *form*; tonight he's on a barrel with a cup, delighted with himself. He's the strand's **said-aloud channel**, the one voice that names the hole out loud so the players can't miss it: *"Sixty thousand people. Two hundred years. I've asked every one of them the same question and gotten a different beautiful answer. You know what nobody's ever said to me? 'I don't know.' A city that never says 'I don't know' is a city that's forgotten it doesn't."* [drinks] *"Wonderful party, though. I come every week."*
- **[[delphine|Delphine]], if sought** — she works the feast like a healer works anything: present, watchful. Asked what she makes of it, quiet: *"It's the best night of the week. And every week, right when the lanterns go, I want to cry and I couldn't tell you why. Don't put that in your pocket, now. Some things aren't clues."* *(It is. Guardrail: shape, never answer — direction and dread only.)*
- **The inward verse** *(free, shown to everyone, once — the strand's one purely visual card)*: at full dark, the oldest chant — and the entire city **turns to face the same direction to sing it.** Not the water. Not the wall. *Inward*, toward the heart of the city. Sixty thousand people rotating like a compass needle, mid-song, without a cue. Ask anyone: *"You face the heart. That's how it's done."* Satisfied. *(No one will think of it again until the day the party learns what sits at the heart of the city. Then this image detonates retroactively. That's the plan. — And note for [[isotta-arc]]: the same heart [[the-tallykeeper|the heron]] has never once flown over.)*

### The Four Finds *(the eerie, delivered per PC — each in their character's own language)*

The answer cards above are reactive. These four are **planted** — one per PC, non-negotiable, the session's eerie payload. Each is discovered *through play* (a roll, a game, a choice — never narrated at them), each is phrased in the PC's native competence, and each gets the two-channel treatment. **The rule: the eerie find must be something only THIS character could have noticed.** The other three players hearing it secondhand is the delivery mechanism — four lenses, one wrongness.

- **Fletcher — the hunter reads the ground: *this is not a party, it's a formation.*** Interactive route: the torch-setting job (Beat 1) or any quiet moment he surveys the festival like terrain — **Instinct 12**, his trail-craft. What he gets: the layout *repeats*. Torch positions set by sightline off a two-hundred-year-old brass frame; tables to the same stations; the procession routes worn into the boards like game trails — **a camp that has been struck and re-pitched, identically, ten thousand times.** A hunter knows what repeated, precise, purposeful arrangement means: *something built this pattern to DO something.* He just can't see the prey. **Said-channel:** the torch-warden, satisfied — *"They go where they go. Always have."*
- **Isotta — the ghost counts the missing: *the dead are giving this night a wide berth — except the ones who are invited.*** Interactive route: her guest-night count (Vael's *"count who's eating and never swallows"*, Instinct 12) — and then the flip she finds on her own: the passing-as-human guests are *here*, feasting — but the ordinary dead, the street-corner shades she's seen every night since she arrived, are **gone.** Streets empty of ghosts for the first time. And no heron, anywhere, all night. Whatever tonight is, the dead treat it the way animals treat a flood coming. **Said-channel:** Vael, if she names it: *"Noticed that, did you."* — and drinks. *(Guardrail: that's ALL he gives. Shape, never answer.)*
- **Tenson — the auditor finds the missing ledger: *the only record this festival keeps is who wasn't there.*** Interactive route: let him try to find the books — his instinct after S4 is to trace any big operation's paper, so when he asks who *pays* for all this (the torches, the tables, sixty thousand meals), give him the free citadel expertise, no roll: an event this size runs on a treasury, a requisition chain, a roster. **The Celebration has none.** No budget, no accounts, no one in charge of money, nothing bought or sold all day — in a city where he's learned every coin says something, the biggest thing the city does is *invisible to paper.* Except one document: the Accord steward's little book, counting the empty benches. **The sole written record of the Celebration is an attendance ledger of the absent.** **Said-channel:** the steward himself, satisfied, if asked: *"Nobody needs minding to come, cher. Only to stay away."*
- **Leavy — the brewer tastes the timing: *every hedge-wife in the city sets her mother-brew tonight, and none of them knows why.*** Interactive route: at the gumbo tables or the herb stalls, an auntie presses a stoneware jug on her — *"Draw your brewing water tonight, love. Lantern-night water. Sets a mother-brew for the whole year."* Ask any of them why and it's the day's signature non-answer: *"It takes better."* Her own craft check — **Knowledge 11**, brewer's expertise — confirms it's not folklore: water drawn tonight IS different on the tongue, charged, *eager.* The whole city's kitchen-craft is calibrated to this ritual's clock, and not one practitioner has ever asked what winds it. **Said-channel:** the auntie's *"it takes better"* — said with total satisfaction, which is the point. *(Deniable-priming brightness only, per the [[session-01-prep]] clue chain — charged water is established canon; the curse/Turn connection stays untouched.)*

**Whiff-proofing (the heavy-hint rule — players are obtuse at the table even when they're brilliant on the couch):** each Find has a **forcing line** that fires *at* the PC if they haven't found it on their own by dusk. Don't wait hopefully; don't hint gently twice — deliver the forcing line, then let the said-channel repeat it in plain words. Subtle died in Session 2.

- **Fletcher, forced:** the torch-warden *hands him the brass sighting-frame* — *"Eyes like yours — check my line, boy, third torch to the water-post."* Now the pattern is literally in his hands: survey marks, two hundred years worn into the brass. No roll. He can't not see it.
- **Isotta, forced:** on any quiet walk between beats, she catches herself *relaxed* — and then catches **why**: nobody's watching her. For the first night since she arrived, the street-corner shades are gone. Every ghost in Sallowreach has somewhere else to be tonight — or somewhere to not-be. Then Vael's line lands as the said-channel whether she visits him or he flags her down: *"Count who's eating and never swallows. And then count who* isn't here at all.*"*
- **Tenson, forced:** he reaches for his purse — reflex, feeding four people all day — and **Remy physically stops his hand.** *"Owes? Cher, there's no purse tonight. There's never been a purse."* Then the free citadel expertise fires, no roll, stated plainly to the player: *an event this size runs on a treasury, a requisition chain, a roster — and this one has none. The only written record in sixty thousand people is that steward's little book of absences.*
- **Leavy, forced:** she doesn't have to seek the auntie — the auntie seeks *her*, presses the jug into her hands mid-conversation, already talking: *"You brew, girl, I can smell it on you. Draw your water tonight. Lantern-night water sets a mother-brew for the year."* If Leavy shrugs it off, the water in the jug **hums against her palm** — her craft senses it, free, no roll. She can ignore a folk custom; she can't ignore her own hands.

**The strand's win condition:** a player says some version of *"nobody KNOWS?"* out of character. When that lands — and Sinope's speech is your guarantee it lands at least once — the eerie register has done its whole job. **Do not reward the strand with facts.** The reward is the hole. And the Four Finds compound at the table: four players comparing four different wrongnesses that all point the same direction is the session's real mystery engine. **If they don't compare notes on their own, force that too:** at the feast, Remy asks the table, innocently, *"So — first Celebration. What'd you make of it?"* — and lets four players answer each other. Confirm nothing.

## Beat 4 — The Seat at the Table *(dusk — the Thouvenel row)*

The city seats them where the campaign needs them: **Solène Thouvenel** ([[session-04-salt-wall]]) spots them drifting and waves them down to the edge tables like it's already settled. They know her from the wall; she feeds them like it's an apology for the state they met her in.

> The edge tables are louder than the fancy ones, the food's simpler and better, and Solène introduces you around like you're cousins from upriver. But here's the thing you notice once you're sitting down: the benches aren't full. Not "people are late" — there are *set places* with nobody in them. Plates put out and going cold. Down this whole outer row, gaps, like missing teeth.

- **Front 4, shown.** Then **said** — Solène, following your eyes, dry as the wall was: *"Half this row's stopped coming. You're sitting in the Caillou family's spot — three little ones, they'd have been climbing on you by now."* [a beat] *"My daughter's doing, most of it. She'd tell you it's the city's doing. We don't discuss it at table."*
- **The city decides what they are:** over food, gently — *"what were you doing on that ship?"* Warm. Also an inventory. How they answer travels; Adaline hears a version of it by morning.
- **The food tell pays off** (if the Gumbo Tables fired): Solène, when someone mentions the rich pots — *"Food's the one thing that never runs short down here. Nobody knows who sends it. Been that way since before my mother."* *(Do NOT put Marcel on-screen tonight; the food arrives, the giver doesn't. Isotta's sight-gag keeps for the Pale Room.)*
- **Front 9's tell tonight** *(90 seconds, a story not an ask — same rule as Sable's current line)*: an old margin-trapper down the table, three cups in — something *big* moving up the southwest channels, and kills found torn open, **not eaten.** The read-aloud and Fletcher's free read ("that's searching, not hunting") are scripted in [[set-piece-the-mother-arrives|the run file]]. One tale, then the feast rolls on; the near sign is S6's strong start.

## Beat 5 — Three Tables Down *(Fen, uninvited)*

She's not on a stage — the Accord doesn't give her one. She's standing on a bench at the outer arc with her grandfather's drum, playing an uninvited set to the tables the Accord never visits, and the edge row loves her for it.

Let her be **a person tonight, not a plot dispenser** — seventeen, loud, funny, bruised knuckles. If the Drum Line game fired, she's already rated the party's dancing to their faces. One party trick, max, *if* someone engages her about the music — her collection, delivered with relish (and if the Questions strand already ran, the party realizes she's the *only other person who ever asked*):

> "Ask five families what the old words mean. Go on. I did. 'The promise holds.' 'The water remembers.' 'Keep the good year.' 'Welcome the guests twice.' And old Maud Perrin swears it's a *recipe.*" [beat] "Same words. Five meanings. Everyone certain. Two hundred years of certain. Nobody's *checked.*"

- **Her secret, shown — hand it to a perceptive PC, no roll:** later, during the oldest chant, catch Fen where the Accord can't see her — **mouthing every word.** Perfectly. In her gran's cadences. The girl leading the walkout, singing along where nobody can catch her. *(Don't explain. If a PC calls it out, she denies it with the fury of the truly caught.)*
- **The family friction, one exchange only:** Solène, across the tables, tired and proud in the same face: *"You want them to hear you, Fenella. People don't hear a door slamming."* Fen, not missing a beat: *"They're not hearing the singing either, Ma."*
- **The Accord steward** works the outer arc with a little book — counting heads, writing names at the gaps. **Shown.** Then Tenson's free citadel expertise ([[tenson-arc]] grand rule), **said, no roll:** *that's not a guest list — that's an attendance record. Someone is documenting who stopped coming.* File it; one day it's evidence in somebody's hands.

## Beat 6 — The Lantern Release *(the centerpiece — give it the full stop)*

At the turn of the evening, the drums change, and the whole city goes to the water. Read:

> The bell rings once — and sixty thousand people go quiet at the same time. That's the part that gets you. A whole city, mid-laugh, mid-song, just... hushes, all at once, like a held breath.
>
> And then the lanterns go up. Not a few. *All* of them. Every street, every window, every hand — thousands and thousands of blue-green lights lifting off toward the black water, till the sky over the Mire is one slow river of light, and the water underneath is another one, glowing back.
>
> Next to you, Solène lights one last lantern off the table candle. She doesn't say anything. She holds it a second longer than she needs to — and lets it go, out toward the southwest, toward the open water past the wall.

- **The Isotta-only gut-punch (say nothing — let her player do the math):** she knows *exactly* who's standing out there past the salt line, hat in his hands, thirty yards from where that lantern is headed. The welcome-lantern, sent every week, to an honored guest who cannot cross. Solène doesn't know he receives it. He does — every week, for three years. *(If Isotta looks southwest with her sight: a far pinprick of a man on the water, head tipped back, watching the lights come. That's all. That's plenty.)*
- **Any PC holding a lantern:** Instinct Roll on the release, per [[the-celebration]] — success: a clean moment of belonging, **recover a Stress**. Failure with Fear: the lantern catches the wind wrong, drifts back against the current — and for half a breath something vast out on the black water turns its attention *through* them, toward the city's heart. They will not forget it. *(Front 1's one fingerprint tonight. One PC at most.)*
- **THE TURN — buried, as designed.** As the lanterns settle onto the water, the canals go glass-still and one slow pulse of light swells up from underneath — *and it reads as part of the show.* Fold it into the release imagery, one clause, no emphasis: *"—and the water goes still and glows up to meet them, like the Mire's answering."* The crowd sighs; the moment's gone. **A tethered PC's curse stirs** — one deniable line to ONE player, privately if you can: *"for a second, the cold spot where the curse sits feels... warm. Like it heard something."* If anyone chases it: the crowd surges, the singing peaks, there is nothing to find. *(First Celebration = swept up, seated away from the water's edge, even the race-winner on the platform. Maybe one PC half-notices and shrugs. The click is the SECOND Celebration's job — [[the-celebration#The Turn — Recognition Beat]].)*

## Beat 7 — Night Button *(walking home, lights-down voice)*

> It's late when you walk back, and the city walks with you — families carrying sleeping kids, someone still singing three canals over, lamps going out window by window. Nobody locks anything. Remy's humming the second movement under his breath, badly, and doesn't know he's doing it.
>
> And here's the strange thing you notice, each of you, as you drift off: the curse — the cold, stuck, tethered feeling you've carried since the water closed over you — is still there. But tonight it's quiet. Almost... settled. Like whatever holds the leash is holding it gently, just for tonight.
>
> You sleep like the drowned. All four of you. No dreams at all.

*(True, and awful, and they can't know why: the binding was performed at full power tonight, and they are a small binding — freshly reinforced. The best sleep in Sallowreach is the sound of the cage being maintained. Say nothing. It's a data point they'll assemble at the click.)*
