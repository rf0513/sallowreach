# Set Piece — The Boat Ambush *(drop-in)*

**In plain words:** The party is out on the water in their flat-bottomed boat and something attacks them. This is the file you open when that happens and you had nothing prepped. It has no plot and no location — it's a fight you can drop into any channel, in any session, in about a minute. You pick one of three sets of animals depending on how deep the water is, you run the boat itself as the battlefield, and the rules for falling in are already written down here. **Nothing in this file is a new monster** — every creature is already statted in the bestiary. The whole point is that a fight in a boat should feel different from a fight on land: cramped, tippy, and with the worst place in the world three feet away over the side.

> **House-rule compliant.** No new environment — this runs on [[the-living-mire]] (rule 18). No new adversaries — everything comes from [[mire-bestiary]] (rule 19). Built 2026-07-24 as the combat half of the Mire-danger kit.

---

## 🚨 Open Here — the 60-second setup

1. **Which water?** → pick a composition below. Shallow/known = **A**. Somebody's bleeding = **B**. Deep = **C**.
2. **Keep the Bilge running.** The fight ticks the same clock as the trip ([[the-living-mire]] §Travel With Teeth).
3. **Read the *what it wants* line out loud to yourself.** Nothing here is malicious. It's hungry, territorial, or afraid.
4. **Name what each PC is holding** before the first roll — pole, lantern, bow, the report. That's your cost, pre-loaded.

## The Three Compositions *(all pre-statted — [[mire-bestiary]])*

### A · Shallow water — *the log that wasn't*

**1× Ridgeback Crawler** (Diff 12) **+ 1× Bloodmidge Swarm** (Diff 13)

- **It wants:** one meal, then to disappear. It is not defending anything.
- **Opens with:** *Mistaken for Driftwood.* Instinct **12** to clock it, or its first bite comes with advantage and an extra 1d6.
- **The shape:** the Crawler drags one PC under, the swarm makes the rescue miserable. Fighting the swarm is the wrong instinct — smoke or submersion drives it off for good.
- **It ends when:** the Crawler marks HP and takes *Back Beneath.* **Let it go.** It got hurt; it's done. Chasing it is the party's mistake to make.

### B · Blood in the water — *the boil* ⭐ *(the Session 1 callback)*

**1× Old Gar** (Diff 13) **+ 6× Mire Gar** minions (Diff 10)

- **Trigger:** someone's bleeding, something got dropped in, or a Bilge tick already put a PC in the water.
- **It wants:** the easy meal. Every one of them leaves the moment it stops being easy.
- **The false light:** the Old Gar's glow reads as a lantern ray in dark water. Instinct roll to tell predator from wonder — *the Mire's beauty and its teeth are the same light.*
- **The shape:** minions drop to **any** hit. Let the party feel capable — the danger is the numbers and the water, not the individual fish.
- **It ends when:** the Old Gar marks HP twice, or nobody's bleeding anymore.

### C · Deep Mire — *the water goes quiet*

**1× Gloamjaw** (Diff 15, Tier 2 Bruiser). Solo. Add 3 Mire Gar as noise if you want the party busy.

- **It wants:** to take one thing, in one motion, and sink back down. It has never needed to hurry.
- **The tell:** the channel falls silent first. **Remy or Fletcher gets a roll to feel it coming** — without that tell it attacks with advantage. Give somebody the chance.
- **⚠ This one is genuinely dangerous.** Restrain → *Death Roll* → *Take It Down* is a real chain that removes a PC from the surface. Run it straight; it's the only composition here that can actually kill someone.
- **It ends when:** it takes what it came for, or takes real damage and decides the arithmetic is bad.

> **Reserved — don't spend him here:** the **Bull Gar** belongs to the rookery fight in [[set-piece-the-wrong-skin]]. Keep him.

## 🛶 The Boat Is the Battlefield *(the rules that were missing)*

**1 · The punt holds four, badly.** Everyone aboard is **Very Close** to everyone else. There is nowhere to spread out, and anything that hits the boat hits everybody's footing at once.

**2 · Standing to swing costs you.** A PC who makes a melee attack from the boat, or moves along it, **and rolls with Fear**: Agility Reaction Roll (**13**) or go over the side. *(Same idiom as the Bull Gar's Breach — [[mire-bestiary]].)*

**3 · In the water is the bad place.** A PC in the water is **Vulnerable**, and every gar attacks them with **advantage** (*Drawn to Blood*, already on the block). Climbing back in costs an action — theirs, or an ally's.

**4 · The boat itself can be Restrained.** Roots, a jaw, a weight on the rail. Clear it with Strength or Agility, or somebody goes over the side to cut it free. *(Precedent: [[the-wending]] §Root-Choke.)*

**5 · ⭐ The fight ticks the Bilge.** Every Fear result during combat ticks it **1**, on top of everything else. The fight and the trip share one clock — that's the whole design. **If it fills mid-fight, the Swamping happens during the fight.** That is the best possible version of this encounter. Don't flinch from it.

**6 · Poles and lanterns are objects.** A dropped pole drifts away. A dropped lantern goes out, and now you're fighting in the dark. This is why you named what everyone's holding.

## 😨 Fear Menu *(performed)*

- **The Rock** *(1 Fear)* — something big passes underneath. Everyone: Agility Reaction **13** or the boat pitches and one carried item goes in the water.
- **The Second One** *(1 Fear)* — there was never only one. A Crawler or gar arrives at Close range.
- **The Light** *(1 Fear — composition B)* — the false light rises off the stern, genuinely beautiful. Whoever reaches for it is who the Old Gar takes.
- **The Quiet** *(2 Fear — composition C)* — the Gloamjaw's *Take It Down.* A PC leaves the surface scene. Say nothing after.
- **The Bilge** *(1 Fear)* — the tick they can *see.* Say the water level out loud, then stop talking.

## 🎒 Walking Out *(states to log)*

- **What went in the water, and who was holding it.** It's not gone — it's at [[set-piece-the-long-anchorage|the Long Anchorage]] now.
- **Whether the Bilge filled**, and whether they limped home or spent the night out.
- **Whether Remy got hurt.** He's aboard on his own promise ([[session-05-recap]]) — if the trip maims the man who begged to come, that's the knife, and it's free.
- **Whether they chased something that was leaving.** Log it. The Mire remembers what's taken from it.

## 🗺️ Dungeon Alchemist Notes *(build this once, reuse it forever)*

One exterior swamp map serves all three compositions and every future boat scene.

- **The channel:** running corner to corner, **30–40 ft wide**, water tiles. Not a pond — a road.
- **Three root-islands:** one big enough to stand and fight on *(the "get out of the boat" option — the party should be able to find it)*, two deliberately too small.
- **A choke point:** a half-sunk wreck or fallen trunk crossing the channel at one end. Doubles as the thing the Ridgeback Crawler is pretending to be.
- **Overhanging canopy** along one bank — that's where the Bloodmidge Swarm drops from.
- **The boat is a loose token,** roughly 5×10 ft, that you physically move and rotate on the map. Print one or cut a card. **This matters more than the map does** — the party watching their boat drift off the heading is the entire feeling.
- **Lighting:** dark, low blue-green from the water itself, no moon.
- **No AI-generated art** — DA's own assets only.

## 🚫 Guardrails

- **Nothing here is malicious.** Hungry, territorial, or afraid — always. The Mire is wondrous first; you reach for this file when it needs teeth, not as its default mood ([[mire-bestiary]] header).
- **Predators leave.** A, B, and C all have an exit written in. An animal that fights to the death is a monster, and these aren't monsters.
- **Lantern rays are never adversaries.** They flee. Don't stat them, ever.
- **One clock.** If you're inside a set piece that brought its own (the Anchorage's tide), the Bilge stands down.
- **This is weather, not a front.** A drop-in ambush advances nothing and reveals nothing. Don't hang a clue on it — the clue files are where clues live.
