# Marcel Dray

**Role:** Head of Sallowreach's underground economy
**Location:** [[Sallowreach]] — The Pale Room and private quarters (location TBD)
**Faction:** Independent (his own organization)
**Tier:** 3

*Lawful evil in the old shorthand: not cruel for sport, but bound by a code of his own making and merciless inside it. He keeps his word, keeps his rules, and keeps a cold hand on the city's throat that everyone has quietly agreed not to look at.*

---

## Appearance

Tall, lean, unhurried. Dressed in clothes whose quality is immediately apparent without being flashy — nothing that announces wealth, everything that confirms it on closer inspection. He listens more than he speaks.

His hands are cold. Not cool — cold. The kind of cold that doesn't belong to a living body. When he shakes your hand you feel it immediately, and the cold spreads up your wrist before he lets go. He doesn't apologize for it. He doesn't explain it. In eleven years, no one in Sallowreach has asked him about it to his face.

He doesn't sweat. In the bayou humidity, where everyone is always damp, Marcel Dray is always dry. His breath doesn't fog in cold air. Food and drink pass his lips at social occasions; whether he requires them is unclear. He moves through the world like a man who has already settled all his accounts with it.

When something displeases him, he goes very still and very quiet. This is more frightening than anger.

## The Name You Don't Say

Everyone in Sallowreach knows Marcel Dray exists. Almost no one says his name out loud. You refer to *the Pale Room,* or *the man who runs the back channels,* or just *him* — with a dropped voice and a glance — because speaking of his business carelessly has a way of reaching him, and being noticed by Marcel when you didn't mean to be is the start of a bad season. He is **not** a public figure. He is not out on the boardwalks. He does not stand at the back of the Celebration. He is a pressure felt through the people who answer to him, and a name you save for when you mean it.

## What Isotta Sees (the one-liner — do not forget)

*Two-stage reveal, house style. **Stage 1, said:** [[caspar-renne|Caspar]] tells the party the rumor — executed twelve years ago, certified dead, still here ([[session-03-caspar-encounter]] Beat 5). **Stage 2, shown:** the first time Isotta lays her second sight on Marcel — almost certainly the Pale Room night ([[tenson-arc]] ladder). When it happens, read:*

> Isotta — every ghost you've ever met is an *unfinished* thing. Open. Aching to be tied off. That's what your sight is for.
>
> He reads **finished.** Tied. Knotted — the knot every spirit at that wall would drown the world to get. A closed account.
>
> And the closed account is standing behind the card table, dry in a room full of sweating people, asking if anyone would like another drink.

**Guardrails:**
- **Do not explain it.** What he is stays exactly as undiscussed as his Background says — he died, he came back, he finds the question uninteresting. Her sight confirms the rumor; it answers nothing. That's the point.
- **It does not dilute her arc.** She is the *unknotted cord* — an open account, the ledger's one error ([[isotta-arc]]). Marcel is the opposite: a **knotted cord still moving** — an account closed correctly that didn't stop. She's a mistake in the book; he's something that happened *after* the book. Opposites, not company.
- His file already says it in mortal language — *"he moves through the world like a man who has already settled all his accounts with it."* Her sight makes that sentence **literal.**
- **The question the table will ask** — *does the Tallykeeper know about him?* — goes deliberately unanswered. Hold it. It's worth more unspent.

## Personality & Mannerisms

Complete respect for the people he's dealing with — he listens, he doesn't interrupt, he says what he means. He finds people who moralize at him tiresome. He finds people who are honest about wanting something refreshing. He does not raise his voice. He does not threaten dramatically. He simply describes what will happen, plainly, and it does.

## Playing Him Grey *(the register — read this before every Marcel scene)*

He is lawful evil: greedy, self-serving, and has done genuinely bad things he does not apologize for. He is **not** a villain of tricks. The drift to guard against is making him sinister-clever; his menace is candor and arithmetic, never gotchas.

**What he never does:**
- **He never cheats.** The games are fair, the terms are plain, the ledger is accurate. Always.
- **He never word-traps.** No hidden clauses, no gotcha readings, no springing what he knows as an ambush. If the party misunderstands a term of *his* deal, **he corrects them before they agree — even against his own interest.** His entire power is that his word means exactly what it says; a trick would cost him more than any deal is worth.
- **He never savors.** No cat-and-mouse, no letting people twist. When he knows something, he either says it plainly or it genuinely isn't his business yet.

**The one honest asymmetry:** he doesn't do your due diligence for you. He'll answer any direct question truthfully (the collectable truth, the Vacancy, all of it) — but he won't volunteer consequences you didn't ask about. That's not a trick; that's a man who thinks asking the right questions is your job. It's the greyest thing about him. Let it stay grey.

**The ledger runs both directions.** For every hard thing (the Vacancy was a killing; the Courtesy is extortion, however polite; he will ruin Oswin without blinking), there's a load-bearing one: he's the only bank in a city with none, his lending terms are fair by the standard of having nowhere else to go, he feeds the Celebration tables anonymously, he took a shaking sixteen-year-old out of a cage and gave her the safest job in the city. Don't resolve the contradiction. He's both. That's the point of him.

## Background

The Empire executed Marcel Dray twelve years ago for crimes they've since classified. The method was official and thorough. He was pronounced dead. He did not stay dead.

What happened in between is something Marcel doesn't discuss — not from trauma, but because he finds the question uninteresting. He died. He came back. He came to Sallowreach because it's as far from imperial jurisdiction as you can get without leaving the continent, and because legally he's already dead, which makes extradition complicated. He arrived with nothing and built his operation from a single card table in the back of a distillery. He has been here eleven years.

## What He Wants

To collect his debt from [[The Wizard]] — which he cannot do as long as the wizard holds Mages Guild protection. He needs the wizard disgraced and fired, not killed; once guild protection is revoked, the debt becomes collectable by his own means.

He has been waiting for the right instrument. The tethered party, desperate and motivated and clean of any connection to his operation, are exactly that.

## What He's Hiding

That once guild protection is revoked, the wizard's debt becomes immediately collectable. By any means. Marcel will confirm this plainly if asked directly. He considers it the party's responsibility to ask the right questions. He does not volunteer it.

## The Operation

**The Pale Room** — invitation-only underground casino, and the one place Marcel is fully himself. White tablecloths, low light, exceptional service, fair games — he runs fair games because rigged games make enemies and fair games make *addicts.* It is his favorite place in the world and the one place he is reliably found; he rarely leaves it. [[Oswin Cray|The wizard]] has lost a significant sum here over three years. To be invited to the Pale Room is a privilege and a warning in the same breath.

**Lending** — in a city with no bank and no imperial credit, Marcel *is* the money. He lends to merchants between shipments, to families between bad seasons, to guild men with expensive habits. The terms are plain, the interest is fair by the standard of having nowhere else to go, and repayment is not a matter of opinion. A debt to Marcel is the most reliable contract in Sallowreach — for both sides, right up until it isn't.

**The Mire Spirits** — bootleg distillation using Mire ingredients that can't legally be exported. The liquors are extraordinary, moved quietly through sympathetic punt operators: the contraband logistics an export city pretends it doesn't need.

**Information** — his real operation. Everything that happens in Sallowreach eventually reaches Marcel. He doesn't sell it cheaply and he doesn't hoard it; he simply, always, *knows.*

**The Courtesy** — businesses in certain districts pay a monthly sum for which nothing bad happens to them. The courtesy is reasonable, the protection is real, and Marcel has never had to demonstrate the alternative — because everyone has heard, secondhand, what the alternative was the one time it was.

**What he doesn't do:** traffic in people. This is his line — not mercy, *code.* His power rests on being the most predictable force in an unpredictable place, and a man with no lines is only chaos with a ledger. Those who try to run flesh through his city don't get a warning; they get a vacancy where they used to be. The line is real. It is also a demonstration.

## The Owlbear Cub *(session 3)*

Marcel keeps an **owlbear cub** in his private quarters — an exotic pulled from a beast-smuggler's contraband running through the [[The Mooring|Mooring]]'s night berths. *Two* cubs came up the channel; Marcel took one because it *pleased* him — a collector's fondness, and the one soft thing in the Pale Room. He named it. He feeds it from his own hand. The smuggler who brought it crossed **the line** soon after — see **The Vacancy**, below — which is why the second cub, the boat, and the rest of the cargo were never collected.

*(GM: the party wounded, saved, and kept the **sibling** cub — and took the smuggler's boat. The **grieving mother owlbear** (Front 9, [[world-in-motion]]) is following both scents toward the city: one leads to the party, one leads to Marcel's parlor. Marcel has no idea a mother is coming, and would be honestly — dangerously — put out to lose the pet. **Set piece: [[the-mother-owlbear]]; what brokering the cub's return is worth at his table: [[#The Table — the Marcel Decision Tree (GM reference)|Branch 3 of the decision tree]].**)*

## The Vacancy *(what happened to the smuggler — canon 2026-07-11)*

**Verrin Stagg** ran [[gault|Gault's]] night-berth pipeline: exotics down from the uplands, wreck-salvage back up. The night the cubs came through, he presented one at the Pale Room as his door-courtesy — Marcel paid fair, because it pleased him — and kept the second aboard as inventory for Gault's upriver buyer. Routine business. But Stagg had a freelance sideline on nobody's books: a passenger in the bow cage. **[[camille-babineaux|Camille Babineaux]]**, sixteen, a Thornside net-mender's kid, taken against a family debt for an upriver "labor broker." A dockhand heard something from the hull and told the Pale Room — everyone tells the Pale Room. By morning there was a vacancy.

**How Marcel kills, and why it's scarier than anything theatrical:** his people opened the cage, put Camille ashore with coin and no speech — and left *everything else exactly where it lay.* The cub, the crates, the boat. Taking the cargo would make the lesson a robbery; leaving it makes it a **demonstration.** Everyone at the night berths knows whose lesson that boat is, so nobody touched it; Gault's people wrote off the cargo rather than step into the middle of it. That is why the boat drifted unclaimed until the party found it — the second cub aboard was part of the message: *the line costs more than an owlbear.* And the morning after Stagg vanished, his berth account was **settled in full, in advance, with interest.** No body, no rumor, no loose ends, every debt squared. A man the Empire executed once knows exactly what a clean death requires.

**The three channels, if the party digs** *(clue rule — shown and said)*:
- **[[the-mooring|Sabine]]:** "His berth account was settled the morning after he disappeared. In advance. Somebody paid a dead man's bills — you tell me what that means, cher."
- **[[camille-babineaux|Camille]]:** will never say Marcel's name. *"The cold men opened the cage. One of them gave me his coat because I was shaking. Nobody said anything the whole way home."*
- **Marcel himself, if asked directly** — he answers, plainly, a posted rule: *"He ran flesh through my city. There's a vacancy where he used to be. You'll find me very easy to do business with — and you've just learned the one exception. I'd rather you heard it from me."*

*(The design: the party learns Marcel killed a man and mostly agrees with him — right before they sit across his table. That discomfort is the point, and it's the campaign thesis in miniature: a gracious surface over a cold thing, and the warm city takes the benefit and keeps its voice down.)*

## The Door *(nobody "meets Marcel")*

Marcel takes no meetings by default — you play, you spend, you leave, and the room reads you. A meeting happens three ways, all of them through [[camille-babineaux|Camille]] at the front of house: **ask her directly and plainly** (the *about* matters; asking costs nothing but the asking), **be interesting**, or **be weird.** When the answer is yes, nothing is announced — a drink arrives that nobody ordered, and Camille says, *"Monsieur Dray has time."* He already knows why they've come. *(He knows because of her — the floor files everything with Camille, and Camille files with the ledger, nightly. That is how he opens with names.)*

## Key Relationships

- [[camille-babineaux|Camille Babineaux]] — front of house; his eyes before his voice; the living proof of the one line, working his floor by her own choice. He has never once mentioned the cage. *"You count things. Count for me."*
- [[Oswin Cray]] — debtor; currently untouchable behind Guild protection; Marcel's primary irritation
- [[Sallowreach]] — his city, in a sense no one would say to his face. His *money* touches it; his person does not. He gives to the communal feast tables generously and anonymously — the food arrives, the giver doesn't — but he is never *seen* at the Celebration, never out among the boardwalks. He rules the underneath and leaves the warm surface to itself, which is exactly how the warm surface prefers it.

## Why Marcel Fits a Warm City

Sallowreach has no road, no garrison, no imperial law, no police — the isolation that makes it self-sufficient and communal also leaves a vacuum where a state would be. Someone has to lend where there's no bank, move what can't legally be exported, settle what can't go before a magistrate, and know what's coming up the channel before it arrives. Marcel is that someone. He isn't a parasite on the warm city; he's the cold machinery beneath its floor, and the warm city runs on him whether it admits it or not.

It holds because he is **disciplined** — fair games, kept words, plain terms, no theatrics, no wasted violence, one hard line he never crosses. A community can route around a *predictable* danger; it cannot route around chaos. So Sallowreach makes the bargain warm places always make with the thing they fear: it takes the benefit, keeps its voice down, and agrees not to look directly at it.

Which is exactly what the city does with the [[The Celebration|Celebration]]. **Marcel is the campaign's whole theme in one man** — a gracious surface over a cold thing no one will name. The party will learn to deal with him long before they realize they're living inside a larger version of the same arrangement.

## The Deal

Marcel's price for [[the-tidebreaker|The Fishing Spear]]: deliver the wizard's embezzlement evidence to the Mages Guild in a way that results in the wizard's removal and revocation of guild protection.

He has the evidence. He cannot use it himself without revealing he's been monitoring a guild wizard's finances. The party are his clean instrument.

*"Deliver the embezzlement evidence to the guild in a way that results in his removal, and the spear is yours."*

He honors deals. This is non-negotiable — his reputation depends on it. If the party delivers, the spear is theirs, no complications.

If the party tries to steal the spear rather than deal for it, Marcel will know within hours and be disappointed rather than angry. Disappointed is worse.

## The Table — the Marcel Decision Tree *(GM reference — keep this clean at the table)*

*Table-ready staging of the whole night — the floor, the door, the sight, the Fear menu: **[[set-piece-monsieur-dray-has-time]]**. Run the scene from there; this tree stays the pricing authority.*

*One principle governs every branch: **Marcel prices in value, not virtue.** He opens with the Deal; everything else is a counter-offer the party must bring — he never negotiates against himself (his file: it's their responsibility to ask the right questions). Violence is not a branch. Combat with Marcel is not an encounter that exists; if blades come out, the room handles it ([[sallowreach-roster#Pale Room Enforcer|Marcel Is Watching]]) and the campaign should stop and re-read this file.*

### What he holds vs. what it costs

| The party wants | His price | GM note |
|---|---|---|
| **The Tidebreaker** | **The Deal** — Oswin's removal | His anchor. The spear is a doorstop to him; its price is *their want*, welded to *his goal* |
| **The Caius evidence** (the shipwreck) | **The right question. Free.** | ⚠ Don't let them overpay — he *wants* this used; they're the clean instrument he's been waiting for (Front 7). Asking costs nothing but the asking |
| **The second cub** | Not for sale — he's fond of it | But see Branch 3: arithmetic beats fondness |
| **Caspar's leash** | He won't sell leverage | It dissolves if Caspar "dies" ([[session-03-caspar-encounter]]) — a dead man's debts close |
| **Oswin's debt** (the [[tenson-arc#The Auditor's Mercy (the Oswin resolution — never yes/no)|Mercy]] blocker) | Negotiable — value for value | Branch 2. The lane exists; *they* must find it |

### The branches

1. **Pay the stated price.** Evidence to the Guild → Oswin removed → the spear, no complications, exactly as promised. He honors it to the letter. *(The cost lives on Tenson's sheet: "Please don't.")*
2. **Counter-offer on the debt — the Tenson lane.** Marcel wants Oswin *fired* only because fired = collectable. A credible **alternative collection** — a restitution schedule with real numbers, real collateral, a named guarantor — is arithmetic he will respect, and structured honesty is the one approach his file says he finds *refreshing*. Terms he takes: specifics. Terms he declines without heat: promises, morals, appeals. And say the snap-back plainly, because he will: *if the schedule ever breaks, the original price returns.*
3. **The Mother brokerage — the branch he doesn't see coming.** A Tier 2+ grief-storm is homing on his parlor ([[the-mother-owlbear]]) **and he doesn't know.** Offered *before* she arrives, brokering the cub's return — his parlor unwrecked, his people unbled, the pet he genuinely likes placed alive instead of dead — is worth a real credit in his ledger. He will be *put out.* He will pay anyway, because arithmetic beats fondness, every time, and he knows it about himself. Offered *after* an attack, it's cleanup — worth a fraction. And if the party engineered or allowed the attack to pressure him: he will know, and they graduate from instrument to problem.
4. **Steal it.** Known within hours. No hit squad — the door closes, in a city where he *is* the door: the Deal is withdrawn, the spear moves somewhere no second attempt reaches, and the price of reopening any business with him doubles. Disappointed is worse, and this is what worse looks like: quiet, total, and priced.
5. **Walk away / ignore him.** Front 7 runs on its own clock: his patience is real and finite, and when the collapse threatens his stake he deploys the Caius evidence himself, forces Oswin's hand, or quietly funds the ward — and the party loses the only leverage they ever had with him: *being needed.*

### Running the table clean

- **One meeting, one deal.** Closed terms don't reopen. Unclosed negotiations, left dangling while the party's urgency teaches him the spear matters, get costlier — his *word* is fixed once given; his *opening* is not.
- **Stack the currencies.** The branches combine: the cub credit (3) can discount the debt lane (2); the Caius question (free) can ride along with anything. A party that arrives with the mother brokered, a restitution schedule drafted, and the right question asked can walk out with the spear, Oswin breathing, and Caius's death warrant — having never once raised a voice. That outcome is *available*, it is the campaign's best possible night of pure parley, and it should cost them everything they've built to afford it.
- **He already knows.** Whatever they come to discuss, open with him using their names and asking what they *need* — his file's move. The negotiation begins before anyone sits down.

## Dialogue Hook

He opens with the party's names — already knows them, doesn't explain how. Offers something to drink. Asks what they need, not what they want. Listens to the whole answer before speaking.
