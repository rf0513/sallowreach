# The Field Journal of Aldric Vane

**Type:** Document (mundane appearance)
**Current Location:** Estate of Reille Preis — a box of miscellaneous papers labeled *OLD — UNCLEAR VALUE*
**Origin:** Written by Aldric Vane, first Mages Guild ward maintainer posted to Sallowreach, approximately 200 years ago
**Significance:** The earliest institutional record of the Mangrove Spirit's behavior — and the document [[Caspar Renne]] has been trying to locate for six years

---

## Description

A leather-bound field journal, water-damaged at the bottom third of every page (he lived here; it was humid). The handwriting is precise and compressed — a man trained to use paper efficiently. No illustrations. Margin notes in a different ink suggest he returned to earlier entries and added postscripts, sometimes years later.

To anyone without context: old papers. Guild notation at the front, dense technical observations, nothing exciting.

To Caspar: six years of questions answered, partially.

## Key Passages

The party doesn't need to read the whole journal. These are the excerpts worth staging.

---

**From his first year — the entry that reads differently once you know how his story ends** *(canon 2026-07-06: Vane was tethered — see the GM section below)*:

*"The southwest survey ended badly. I misjudged a channel depth on one of the city's festival nights and went under in the dark water. I record what I observed, because observation is the discipline: the water went still as glass around me. A light rose from beneath — slow, sustained, not the ray-shimmer. A taste of iron and green stems. And a pressure closing, with great patience, like a hand. My boatman pulled me out. I have not been entirely well since, in a way I cannot locate anatomically. I will not be recording this in the quarterly summary."*

*(GM — read this one word for word and then stop talking. It is the party's own wreck, two hundred years early: the Turn's exact signature, down to the festival-night timing — Remy's Session 1 line, in a dead man's hand. If the table somehow doesn't click, Remy reading over a shoulder is the said-channel: "...that's what happened to you lot, isn't it. That's it exactly.")*

---

**Early in the posting — dated about six months after arrival:**

*"The spirit does not wander. I have now observed its movement at sufficient intervals to be confident: it moves toward the city. Not randomly. Not incidentally. Toward Sallowreach, with intention that the word 'intention' barely covers — a force of nature that has chosen a direction and sustains it. My predecessors in the Guild literature called it 'encroachment.' Encroachment implies accident. This is not accident."*

---

**A frustrated entry — about three years in:**

*"Two colleagues have reviewed my observations and concluded there is no attractor. The spirit simply grows, they say, and Sallowreach happens to be in its path. I have run the trajectory calculations. I have consulted the channel-shift maps going back four generations. The spirit does not simply grow. It is pulled. Something inside the city holds its attention the way a lantern holds a moth's, and I cannot locate the source. The ward holds — the salt holds — but I am holding back a tide that wants to come in. This is different from holding back a flood."*

---

**About five years in — filed between ward-geometry notes, in the same flat hand:**

*"The Guild writes asking when I will present my findings in person. I have pleaded continuity of the work. The truth is unbecoming a practitioner of my grade: I have now attempted the journey out four times. There is a boundary — I could draw it on a map to within a half-mile — past which my body refuses. Pressure, then pain, then a turning-back that I do not entirely author. Recovery is quicker the nearer I return to the city. I have stopped attempting. The work is here in any case. The work is here."*

*(GM — the repeated last sentence is a man talking himself into his cage. The party has personally hit this boundary — Session 1, Secret #1: resistance, agony, back where you started. Recognition does everything; the word "curse" appears nowhere.)*

---

**Practical observation — undated, appears to be a reference note added later:**

*"Salt disrupts approach. Ward geometry must account for sustained directional pressure from the southwest quadrant specifically — not uniform perimeter pressure. If a future maintainer finds the ward skewing, reinforce the southwest. It will always be the southwest. The spirit is always coming from there."*

---

**Late in the posting — near the end of the journal:**

*"I have spent eleven years trying to identify the attractor. I have failed. What I am confident of: it is not us. We did not build it, and we are not maintaining it — it predates the Guild posting by at least as long as the city has records. Whatever draws the Mangrove Spirit to Sallowreach is something the city itself contains, and I believe the city does not know what it is.*

*I attended the Celebration again last week. I have attended it perhaps fifty times now. It is very beautiful. The chanting is older than the city's written history — when I asked the council where the chants originated, they had no answer. They have simply always been. I wonder, sometimes, if the answer I'm looking for is somewhere in those chants. I have not been able to read them — they are in an old dialect I can't fully parse, and Sallowreach's scholars have not seen fit to translate them for a Guild representative.*

*I am leaving these notes to my successor. The ward holds. The arrangement with the city holds. But the central question — why the spirit comes here — I cannot answer. I hope someone does, eventually. The answer matters."*

---

**Beneath that entry, added years later in the darker ink — and then struck through, one thin stroke, still perfectly legible:**

*"Revisited. I put the question to four households this winter: what do the old words mean? Four answers, each devout, no two alike. There is no translation being withheld from me — I no longer believe a translation exists at all. And a hymn is not a mechanism. An old man stares at a problem long enough, he begins finding it in the dinnerware. Strike this. The ward is my work; the singing is theirs."*

*(GM — this strike-through is the journal's real payload, calibrated on purpose: the hunch survives on the page, but the author himself retracted it. Why it matters: (1) it explains two hundred years of silence — the one man who nearly saw it talked himself out of it, which IS the founders' failsafe working as designed; (2) a crossed-out line is the first thing a player reads — the deletion is the beacon; (3) "four households, four answers" is [[fen|Fen's collection]] run two centuries early — when the party meets her party trick, let a PLAYER make that connection, never an NPC. **And (4), now that Vane's tether is canon, the strike reads differently and better: this is not a scholar dismissing a weak lead — it's a trapped man forbidding himself the one thread he couldn't afford to keep pulling.** "An old man stares at a problem long enough, he begins finding it in the dinnerware" is what hope-denial sounds like in a Guild hand. The player who disagrees with Vane — the first person in two hundred years to pick the struck line back up — has earned the campaign's central question honestly.)*

---

**And on the last leaf, in a different, younger hand:**

*"Aldric Vane died at Sallowreach in the thirty-first year of a posting the Guild rolls list as a five-year rotation. He declined recall six times, which was thought to be devotion. He is buried in the Guild plot behind the Maintenance House. His effects to the House; his papers herein. He asked that the journal go to whoever holds the question next."*

*(GM — the arithmetic IS the clue, and it's Tenson-shaped: a five-year rotation, thirty-one years served, six refusals of recall, death in post. "Which was thought to be devotion" is the institutional blindness in one clause. Nobody in two hundred years has found it strange. The party — four people who currently cannot leave — will find it very strange indeed.)*

---

**The arrangement, noted in the first entry of the journal:**

*"Terms of posting, as I understand them: the Mages Guild maintains a qualified practitioner here indefinitely, responsible for salt-ward maintenance and any spiritual containment the Mire requires. In exchange, Sallowreach provides agreed quarterly shipments of Mire lumber to Guild operations in the capital. The timber they provide is extraordinary — I had not believed the reports before seeing it. The arrangement, the council told me without apology, is payment. They pay for the protection they require and we provide the service for which we are paid. No one dressed it as anything grander, and no one called it anything grander. I respect that."*

## What Caspar Finds

He has read about Vane's posting in secondary sources. He has documented Vane's name, dates, and institutional role. But the primary record — Vane's own observations — has been missing from his archive for six years, and Caspar has suspected it contained something the secondary sources missed.

What stops him when he reads it: the attractor passage. Vane couldn't identify it. In sixty years of research, Caspar hasn't been able to identify it either. But Vane's certainty that it exists — that specific passage, *something inside the city holds its attention* — confirms what Caspar has believed and been unable to prove.

He reads it twice. He sets the journal down. He says, quietly:

*"He didn't know what it was. Eleven years, and he couldn't — I've been at this forty years, and I couldn't—"*

And then he reaches the struck-through lines, and the room goes quiet in a different way. One short laugh, no joy in it:

*"He struck it out. The one line worth the whole book, and he struck it out."* [a beat] *"I've been doing the same thing for forty years. I just used the word 'fairytales' instead of a pen stroke."*

Then, longer pause:

*"You need the spear. I know you need it. The man who has it is named Marcel Dray."*

He doesn't explain how he knows. He doesn't explain the complicated feeling on his face. He just says the name.

> **⚠ THE CASPAR FORK:** if Caspar is already gone (dead-on-paper, [[session-03-caspar-encounter]] Beat 6) when the journal surfaces, this scene re-homes to **[[Delphine]]** — his oldest friend, the one who told the party about his missing-journal obsession in the first place. She can't do the scholarship, and that's the point: she turns the pages gently, says *"he chased this for six years — he'd have wept to hold it,"* and hands the reading back to the players. The struck-through line stays theirs to find and theirs to disagree with (the design already puts the realization player-side, so nothing load-bearing is lost). If the party keeps a channel to hidden-Caspar, a copied page sent through it buys his reaction by letter — worth it for "You need the spear. The man who has it is named Marcel Dray" arriving in a dead man's handwriting.

## Vane Was Tethered *(GM — canon 2026-07-06)*

**The truth:** in his first year, surveying the southwest channels, Vane went fully under the black water during a Turn — same accident as the party's, two hundred years earlier, one man instead of four. He never left Sallowreach again. The "permanent posting" the Guild believes it negotiated was, at its root, one practitioner's captivity dressed as duty. He served thirty-one years, declined recall six times, and died old — **the tether kept him; it did not consume him** (say this plainly if a player panic-reads the grave as "the curse kills you": he died of age, in year thirty-one, still asking).

**Why he hid it:** pride, and a colder calculation — a Guild that learns its practitioner is magically imprisoned quarantines the posting or writes it off; either way the ward loses its maintainer. So the quarterly summaries stayed dutiful, the recalls were "declined," and the only honest record is this journal. His concealment is *why* the Guild's institutional memory holds nothing about tethers — the blindness is his own handwriting.

**What this buys the campaign:**
- **The journal quest becomes personal, not scholarly.** Players skim lore; nobody skims the diary of a man who had *their* curse and died holding it. This is the engagement engine for the campaign's single most load-bearing handout.
- **His eleven-year obsession finally has a motive.** He wasn't studying the attractor for the Guild — he was investigating his own cage. Chasing the cure IS chasing the secret: the party's engine, proven to run once before, and to lose.
- **The stakes get a headstone.** The do-nothing ending is no longer abstract: it's a Guild plot behind the Maintenance House. "He had the question. We have the key" — the difference between Vane and the party is the spear, and a player can *say* that sentence.
- **It rhymes with Old Patience** — *"you carry two marks… the first in a long time"* — without resolving it. **Do not connect them explicitly**, and do not decide whether Vane is among the ones who "found what they were looking for." That line stays unexplained, per its own file.

**Hard guardrails:**
- **No ghost.** Vane crossed long ago. He is not available for interview, not to Isotta, not through the Tallykeeper, not ever — a speaking Vane is a lore conduit that breaks "nobody knows" and makes the journal worthless. The journal is his only voice. The grave (behind [[oswin-cray|Oswin]]'s Maintenance House — already in play, S4) is a place to stand, not a place to ask.
- **Oblique only.** The word *curse* never appears in his hand; he documents symptoms in a physician's flat voice and hides them from the Guild in the same breath. The party discovers it by *recognition* — the Turn signature, the boundary — never by announcement. If a future edit makes Vane name his own condition, undo it.
- **His tether teaches nothing about the binding.** He never learned what held him — that's the whole tragedy. His chant-hunch stays struck through; his death closes nothing. The reveal-gates ([[the-celebration-clue-map]] L4–L5) are untouched.

## Why It Survived — and Why Nobody Ever Acted On It *(GM — the plausibility chain, for when a player asks)*

Because a player *will* ask: "this sat around for two hundred years and nobody put it together?"

1. **It's his private field journal, not the Guild record.** The official record was quarterly summaries filed to the capital — which is why [[caspar-renne|Caspar]] has spent forty years "working from summaries" (canon). The journal itself never entered any institution's shelves. Personal papers, personal hand.
2. **His successors didn't read it carefully.** Canon ([[cosmology]]): every maintainer since has been too consumed by their own posting to do the research — [[oswin-cray|the current one]] most of all. The journal sat in the Maintenance House as shelf-furniture for generations, and its one actionable idea is *struck through by the author.* A skimming successor sees eleven years of failed attractor-hunting that ends in a self-retraction. Case closed, shelf it.
3. **A posting turnover cleared the house.** Somewhere in the intervening decades, a change of maintainer emptied the back rooms — a box of "old papers, unclear value" sold to make space. It drifted through collectors' hands until **Natta Preis** bought it at an estate auction six years ago, for nothing, because it seemed interesting. Caspar caught the auction listing too late and has been chasing the trail ever since.
4. **And the deep reason, the one that IS the campaign:** the founders built the binding as sacred, unchangeable, *untranslatable* tradition on purpose — tradition as error-correction ([[fen|Fen's file]], GM section). The failsafe doesn't just stop peasants from changing the song. It stopped a trained Guild investigator from taking his own best idea seriously. The journal isn't a hole in the secret. It's the secret's near-miss — proof of how good the lock is.

**Calibration guardrail — what this handout must NEVER contain:** the words *binding, trapped, prisoner, spell.* No mention of the Turn, the tether, or the Celebration *doing* anything. Vane's chant-hunch is a struck-out question, not a finding. The journal's whole job is to weld *spirit + city + chants* into one suspicion and stop there — the answers (what the chants say, what is held, the love story) stay locked behind the Heart and the transcription ([[the-celebration-clue-map]], layers L4–L5). If a future edit makes the journal smarter than this, the mystery dies on a handout.

## The Mini-Quest: Tracking the Journal

### How the Party Learns About It

Three routes in, any of which works:

**From Caspar directly** — if the party returns to the museum and engages him about spirit research rather than just the spear's location, he'll mention it in passing. He's been trying to find Vane's original field journal for years. He tracked it to an estate auction six years ago but lost the trail. *"If it still exists, it would be the earliest first-hand account we have of the spirit's behavior. Forty years I've been working from summaries."*

**From Delphine** — she and Caspar are old friends. She knows he's been preoccupied with a missing document for years. She can point the party toward him as a way to build relationship: *"Caspar's been miserable about some old journal that vanished at auction. If you could find it for him, he'd — yes, that would help."*

**From the museum itself** — attentive players browsing the archive floor might notice Caspar's research files and find a folder labeled VANE — PROVENANCE INQUIRY with six years of dead-end correspondence.

### The Trail

Estate auction, six years ago. A single lot of miscellaneous papers went to **Natta Preis**, a retired merchant collector who bought things that seemed interesting without always knowing what they were. Natta died two years ago and left two floors of accumulated *stuff* to her niece, **Reille** — all of it in-city, of course; nobody's leaving the Mire. *(The house stands two streets off the plaza in [[the-eddy|the Eddy]] — Natta was one of the 3,000, year fifty-one when she died, underlined in the Arrivals Ledger. Anyone at the Year Table can point the party to Reille's door.)*

### Reille Preis

Mid-thirties, inherited a hoard she never wanted and a question she can't shake: *who was Aunt Natta, really?* Overwhelmed, a little sad, and warm to anyone who's kind — she'll happily turn the party loose to dig if they give her a plausible reason and maybe haul a few boxes while they're at it. She is **not** an obstacle. She's a person who'd be relieved to have some of this gone.

**The complication (use it or hold it):** a *polite man* came by last week offering to buy "Aunt Natta's old guild papers." Reille told him to come back. She doesn't know who sent him — but the party might wonder, and might want to dig *before* he returns. *(GM: it's [[Marcel Dray|Marcel]]'s quiet buyer, or a rival collector — your call. Since the spear and Marcel sit downstream of this quest, a Marcel thread here pays off.)*

### Digging the Hoard *(the fun part)*

Two floors of a dead eccentric's treasures. Let the party **dig as long as they like** — each real search turns up a find. Roll or pick; the journal is in here, but make them earn it past a couple of other things. The point is rooting through a weird, charming, slightly dangerous pile and seeing what turns up.

| d8 | What you turn up |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The prize** — Vane's field journal, in a box marked *OLD — UNCLEAR VALUE,* under guild correspondence and a stack of annotated fish-market receipts. |
| 2 | **The chest that's too nice** — a wardrobe of dark Mire blackwood, finer than anything around it. Natta couldn't resist it. *It is not furniture.* → [[mire-bestiary#Mire-Wrought Mimic|Mire-Wrought Mimic]] |
| 3 | **Quiet coin** — a string of deep-Mire pearls, wax-sealed reagent jars; a handful or two at the pawnbroker (boat fund). |
| 4 | **The mirror that isn't anyone's** *(filled 2026-07-12 — [[the-witch|Leavy's]] beat)* — a plain pewter hand-mirror backed in bog-oak, wrapped in a scarf at the bottom of a crate of nothing. Leavy's hands know its weight before they touch it. **GM guardrail:** there is no lore here — it was never theirs, and [[leavy-arc]] owns all real answers. This is their sheet's own instruction made physical: *"an object that means something they can't explain."* Grief with no object sometimes picks one up. Let it ache; explain nothing; let them keep it or set it down. |
| 5 | **Pure Natta** — forty identical brass spoons; a stuffed heron in a tiny waistcoat; love letters addressed *"my darling,"* all, it turns out, to the heron. She was a *person.* (Reille will need a moment.) |
| 6 | **The ledger** — Natta's purchase records, with a margin note in a fresh hand: someone asked after *"the guild man's papers"* recently. No name. (Feeds the complication.) |
| 7 | **Mind your hands** — a sealed jar of preserved Mire venom, *or* a spirit-stone that hums louder the nearer it gets to the blackwood chest. Mishandle it: minor hazard. (And — why does it hum near the chest?) |
| 8 | **A wonder** — a deep-water curio: a lamp of impossible design, a jar of water that still faintly glows. Valuable, or the perfect gift for an NPC. |

### The Mimic

When the digging's run a beat too long — or someone opens the beautiful blackwood chest — it **bites.** Full stats: [[mire-bestiary#Mire-Wrought Mimic|Mire-Wrought Mimic]] (Tier 1). It's a *surprise,* not a death-trap: low HP, it can't chase, it wants to feed not fight. Kill it and it disgorges everything it's swallowed over the years — coins, a locket, someone's good dentures, and one thing that matters. *(GM-secret: the chest is Mire blackwood half-woken by the [[Tree Spirit]]'s essence — a tiny echo of the campaign's whole secret. A Mire-attuned PC feels the kinship and won't know why. It lands later.)*

### Make the Clue Theirs

When a PC turns up the journal, **let them read it** — and make sure they hit the struck-through passage (the deletion draws the eye; describe the single thin stroke, the words still legible under it). *The players* should be the ones who go *"…wait — why did he cross this out?"* Don't save the realization for Caspar. The intended table-moment: a player picks up the line the author abandoned, and becomes the first person in two hundred years to take it seriously.

### The Return to Caspar

They bring him the journal **and a theory.** He opens it with careful hands, reads, goes very still — and when a player says *"he thought it was in the chants,"* Caspar looks up at them differently. Forty years of his own dead ends, and these strangers walked in and *saw* it. *That's* what unlocks him — not the delivery, the **engagement.**

Then, quietly: *"You need the spear. The man who has it is named Marcel Dray."* He won't explain how he knows, or the complicated look that comes with it. That's for another time. The name is out.

## GM Notes

- **⚠ SPINE RULE (2026-07-06): the journal must be in party hands BEFORE the spear is.** This is the only document in the campaign that welds *spirit + city + chants* into one sentence — without it, the post-spear road has no signpost. Three entry routes exist (Caspar, Delphine, the archive folder); if none has fired by the time the Marcel deal is closing, force the timing: Caspar asks them to find it as his condition for brokering the Deal, or the "polite buyer" complication puts it on a clock. See [[the-celebration-clue-map]] (Risk B / Patch P4).
- **It's a playground, not a puzzle.** No lock, no guard — the fun is digging through a weird hoard with a surprise in it. Let them root around as long as they're enjoying it; reward thorough searching with more finds.
- **The mimic is a surprise, not a threat to fear.** Spring it for delight and a jolt, not lethality. It's the chest that bites and then coughs up treasure.
- **Give the players the clue, not Caspar.** The win is a *PC* connecting Vane's chant passage; Caspar reacts to *them* having seen it. That flip is the whole point of the rebuild.
- **Reille and the "polite buyer" are threads.** Reille can become a recurring friendly face; the buyer is a string to pull toward [[Marcel Dray|Marcel]] (or a rival). A little urgency makes the dig a choice, not a chore.
- If a player browses the museum archive on another visit, let them find the *VANE — PROVENANCE INQUIRY* folder and pick up the trail there.
- **The double meaning still lands twice:** Vane hunted an *external* attractor and failed; the [[Tree Spirit]] was *inside* the city the whole time — in the Celebration, in the water, and (that chest!) in the wood. The mimic is that truth in miniature, hiding in a junk pile.
