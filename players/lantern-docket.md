# The Lantern Docket — Isotta's Casebook

**In plain words:** Isotta is half-ghost, and the dead of the town know it — she is a lit doorway, and they queue at it. Each entry below is one small ghost case she can work: a dead client with something unfinished, evidence that doesn't quite add up, and a solution that is never an exorcism — usually she carries a goodbye across a line only she can straddle. Every closed case secretly does two things: it teaches her one rule about how dying is *supposed* to work (evidence toward solving her own condition), and it draws the patient grey heron that's been following her one roof closer. This file is the case queue, table-ready. Run at most one knock per session; the dead are patient.

*The engine's rules, the crossing mechanics, and the Answer live in [[isotta-arc]] — this file is the cases. Ghosts stay in town and only Isotta sees them ([[cosmology]] principle 4). Case 1 (Anselme, the Drowned Poler) closed S4.*

## Running a case *(the fixed shape — five beats, ~20 minutes each)*

1. **The knock** — how it finds her. Ghosts don't wail; they *wait*, politely, where she'll pass. The creep is in the courtesy.
2. **The stated want** — what the client *says* is unfinished. It's always concrete. It's always slightly wrong.
3. **The snag** — the party grants the stated want and the ghost doesn't cross. The real anchor is one layer down; finding it is the mystery.
4. **The balance** — the bridge, not the fetch-quest: she relays what the dead can't say to the living who can't hear ([[isotta-arc]] §How a Crossing Works).
5. **The ledger** — closing pays out: clear her Slipping clock, grant a Hope `[VERIFY]`. *(⚰️ 2026-08-31: the grace cord is retired — closed cases no longer tick anything; they change what the Tallykeeper grants.)* ~~after the introduction, loosen one loop of the grace cord. The heron is one roof closer. Write the casebook line.

**The casebook lines** *(read each aloud as she writes it — the Order's flat clinical prose is the running joke and the running knife)* are at the end of each case.

## Case 2 · The Best Night — *Sidonie Lemoine*

**The knock:** on a Celebration night, out on the old-quarter boards, Isotta sees a woman dancing alone where the music barely reaches — in clothes sixty years out of fashion, glowing faintly, blissful. The same woman. Every Celebration. Slightly fainter each time.

**The stated want:** nothing. She's the one ghost who *doesn't* knock — she wants one more loop of the best night of her life, the night she danced till the boards shook and everyone she loved was still alive. She'd like Isotta to go away, please, before the song ends.

**The snag:** Isotta's casebook catches what memory can't — the loop is *editing itself*. Each pass the rain is gone, the stumble is gone, her partner's laugh is smoother. And she fades faster as the night gets better. The polish is the poison: a memory with the flaws sanded off holds nothing to hold onto.

**The balance:** somebody living was there. **[[the-last-board|Maud Perrin]]** was on the drums that night — and Maud remembers it *true*: it rained sideways, a drumhead split, Sidonie's partner stepped on her feet twice and laughed so hard he sat down in a puddle. Bring Maud to the boards. Isotta carries the old woman's telling across — and the real night, mud and all, is the one Sidonie crosses from, mid-dance, laughing at the true version.

**Teaches:** *the anchor was never the memory — it was being remembered right.* Being known beats being perfect. (Data point for the file marked *Subject: myself* — what is she polishing?)

**Said-aloud clue** *(Maud, after, plain as bread)*: "Funny thing about the good nights, cher. They only keep if you keep the rain in."

📓 *Casebook: Client departed during uncorrected recollection. The flawed original outperformed the improved copy. Query: what am I editing?*

## Case 3 · Eleven of Twelve — *Émile Thibodeaux*

> ⚠ **2026-07-25:** the jewel no longer lives here — it fires on the **next case that closes**, whichever one that is (see the compression note above). The Cold Cache has never been played and this case is gated on it. The scene below is intact and good; it just isn't holding the crown jewels hostage anymore.

*The setup rides in on [[set-piece-the-cold-cache]]: the drowned smuggler whose oilskin wallet Isotta carries out of the swamp — a pay-tally with eleven marks of twelve, bone dice, half an unsent letter. His wages sit with Cauvin at the night berths. His ghost waits there too.*

**The knock:** the night after the cache trip, a man stands at the end of the night berths, looking at the water, hat in his hands. He's been there, Cauvin's boys will tell you without knowing why they avoid that spot, "a while."

**The stated want:** *"I'm owed. Eleven of twelve, and a man crosses square."* He wants his wages tallied out. He is very reasonable about it. He has been standing there being reasonable about it since spring.

**The snag:** Cauvin pays — fences keep honest books, it's the whole point of a fence — and Émile counts it, nods... and doesn't cross. The money was never the anchor. The letter was. Half-written, never sent, and it opens *"Ma —"*. The twelfth run was the one that would've made the words in it true. He couldn't finish the letter without the money, and now he can't finish anything at all.

**The balance:** Isotta takes dictation. At the night berths, casebook open, she writes the letter's other half in her own clinical hand while a dead man finds the words — slowly, badly, the way real letters get written. The account balances when the letter is *finished*, not when it's delivered. He crosses with the pen still moving.

**The coda** *(the tether, inverted — don't skip it)*: Ma is upriver, past the wall. The letter and the wages go out through [[the-long-room|the Long Room]] — it costs most of the twelfth run's pay, and Lucien doesn't ask questions about senders. The party stands on the dock watching the courier-light go where they can't. *It leaves, even when you can't.*

**⭐ Then the jewel:** the first collection she witnesses — low tide, no wind, the bow, the one unhurried knot. Full script and staging: [[isotta-arc]] §How a Crossing Works (the midpoint scene banked since S4). And Émile, half-crossed, turns — the threshold line, re-homed here per the bank:

> *"Begging pardon, miss. Your cord. There's no knot in it — smooth as riverglass, worn from handling. It keeps yours near the top."*

*(Fires Secrets #4 and #10 from the arc file. Casebook entry after, per the arc: "Observed: a correct death. Subject felt: envy.")*

📓 *Casebook: Account settled in words, not coin. The stated debt is never the debt. Query applies generally.*

## Case 4 · Year One — *Galen Merrow*

**The knock:** in [[the-eddy]], on a feast day, a man queues at Cassia's Year Table who never reaches the front. Outsider's face, clerk's coat, forty years out of date. The Arrivals Ledger explains him: nobody in it is ever crossed out — and his line says *Galen Merrow, year one.* He's been on year one for forty years.

**The stated want:** *"I'm going Home. I was only ever here for a year."* He was saving for the return passage when the Mire took him — eight months in. He wants to be pointed at a boat. He is standing in a city he never once agreed to stay in.

**The snag:** there is no Home to point him at. Forty years — the street he means is gone, the family is gone, the *There* in his head hasn't existed for decades. You cannot deliver him and you cannot tell him that; grief isn't an anchor you cut, it's one you *settle*. What does a crossing need when the destination is gone?

**The balance:** **[[npc-quick-ref|Cassia Fontaine]]** — the woman who repacks her own sea-chest every spring — cooks the crossing meal: the food from There, the old recipes, laid at the Year Table with his name spoken in the old accent. Ghosts feed on significance ([[isotta-arc]] §Costs), and Isotta carries the taste and the welcome across. He crosses from a table that finally counts as Home — because Home was never the coordinates.

**Teaches:** *the account doesn't need the destination; it needs to balance.* A crossing can complete toward a place that no longer exists. (The data point that matters most to *her* case — and to four tethered people who were all going somewhere.)

**Said-aloud clue** *(Cassia, quietly, setting the plate)*: "You don't finish the journey, cher. You finish the *leaving.*"

📓 *Casebook: Destination nonexistent; crossing completed regardless. Conclusion: the door does not require the far shore. Subject rereads this entry often.*

## Case 5 · The Rounds — *Lazare Bellaire* ⚰️ *(re-homed 2026-07-25 → [[isotta-arc]] §Endgame, "take the taper")*

> **Not a mid-campaign case.** It hands her a nightly duty she keeps forever and quietly makes her belong to the spirit side in a way no cure undoes. That's an ending, and it's now the fifth door on her endgame menu. Everything below stays exactly as written — just play it at the finish, not on a Tuesday.

**The knock:** through her second sight only. Every dusk, Veilside, an old man with a taper walks the boards lighting lanterns the living can't see — the spirit-side lamps that burn the color with no name ([[isotta-arc]] §Veilside Sallowreach). One evening he stops below her window, looks up, and holds out the taper.

**The stated want:** *"Sixty years I've kept the rounds, and I'd like to sit down now."* He was the old quarter's living lamplighter; when he died, he saw that the *other* side's lamps wanted keeping too, and nobody had the job. So he kept it. He won't cross and leave the streets dark — the Veilside dark is deeper than ours, and things drift in where the lamps go out.

**The snag:** there is no successor to find. No living soul can see the lamps, and the dead don't linger — they cross, as they should. The only person in two worlds who can hold the taper is standing in her own window. This case cannot be closed by detective work. It closes by *inheritance* — and she has to choose it.

**The balance:** she takes the rounds. Lazare walks her through the route once — every lamp, every trick, which ones gutter in a river wind — the fussy, tender handover of a man who's loved a small job for sixty years. Then he crosses at dawn, at the last lamp, taper handed over, *"mind the third one on Cutter's Row, she sulks."*

**The gift with teeth:** the lit route is real. Wherever the rounds pass, Veilside is kind — and a lit lamp on her route counts as a **named anchor** against the dark-pull and the Slipping clock ([[isotta-arc]] §The Slipping Clock): the city itself becomes her lantern network. The cost is also real: it's *nightly*, it's hers, and the Tallykeeper — courteous as ever — notes that she now keeps an office. Offices are collateral too.

**Teaches:** *some anchors are duties — and a duty freely chosen holds better than any owed one.* Also, quietly: she has started belonging to the spirit side in ways no cure will simply undo. Let her player feel both.

📓 *Casebook: Closed by assumption of duties. Query, unresolved: whose docket am I on now?*

## The Pattern *(stage two's tripwire — let her find it)*

When the player reviews the casebook — and mystery players always review the casebook — the meta-clue is sitting in her own margins: **every closed case, the heron was one roof closer.** Plot it like she would: a line converging on her window. She has been doing the Tallykeeper's work, case by case, and it has been *approaching in step.* When she says some version of that out loud, [[set-piece-convince-me-to-wait]] is ready — and the introduction lands as her deduction, not your reveal, which is the whole house religion.
